## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Custom fence builders can design/build bespoke wood fences, panels and gates, fabricating to lot, architecture, slope, privacy/security and customer choice. Their loop is consultation/site measure → design/material/spec and itemized quote → property/permit/utility confirmation → materials/fabrication or stick-build → post/footing/frame/panel/gate construction → finish/operation/punch/warranty. Some custom woodworking firms fabricate fence structures but do not install; other contractors both design and install. This overlaps heavily with the separate broad Fence installation profile, so the niche candidate should not be merged absent confirmation that its original source describes a distinct business.

The profile treats project design/fabrication and installation as configurable, not universal. Existing fence CRMs market linear-foot takeoff, material calculations, quote, schedule and production; first-person fence-builder forum discussions say firms vary between general lead/quote/invoice management and fence-specific takeoff/production. Public software list prices exist, but actual WTP/purchases are not verified. Status is partial due source-label scope ambiguity.

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners commission privacy, decorative, pool, boundary, pet/security and driveway-gate structures. Architects/designers, builders, HOAs, property managers and small commercial owners can specify custom work. Custom woods/material combinations, arched/scalloped gates, privacy screens and integration with decks/pergolas are examples. A craft builder may fabricate offsite to drawings; a fencing contractor builds/installs on site; a broad fence installer may rely on prefabricated systems and mixed-material commercial/agricultural projects. Do not assume every fence business is woodworking or that every wood builder owns engineering/permitting.

## Terminology and durable records

Capture customer/property, boundary/survey, fence run/segment, elevations/slope, posts/spacing, panels/pickets/rails, style/finish/material/species, gate/frame/hardware, footing/soil, property utilities, permit/HOA/pool barrier, approved drawing, fabrication status, crew, change, warranty and completion. Installed perimeter/fence/gate persists and may support repair history; locate ticket, dimensions, design revision and marked alignment are job-specific. Boundary and neighbor details can be sensitive/disputed; store source and customer-confirmed limitations, not claims of legal survey.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Lead intake: custom build vs standard system, desired purpose/style/material, photos/sketch/survey, approximate linear feet, height, gates/hardware, slope/grade, existing fence/demo, property line/HOA/pool, utilities, site access, finish, timeline and fabrication vs install scope. Onsite measure confirms line and terrain. Quote can be itemized by runs/sections, posts, gates, material/finish, custom fabrication, footings/rock/soil, removal, hauling, utility hand-dig, staining/sealing and labor. Identify permit responsibility and boundary assumptions; approval of drawing/material before order/fabrication. Gate and property line changes require signoff.

Sequence: consult/measure → design/specification → quote/approval → verify owner/HOA/permit and utility locate → procure/fabricate → layout → posts/footings → panels/rails/gates → finish/cleanup → operation and alignment check → acceptance/warranty. A custom design form can qualify leads but site and property review still precede a committed schedule/price.

## Pricing and commercial model

Price inputs: lineal measure, height, material/species/grade, custom pattern, gate count/width/frame, terrain/slope, demolition, soil/rock, concrete, access, fabrication, finish, permits, utility conflicts, haul and crew hours. Fence operators in online discussions disagree with flat linear-foot pricing because gates, terrain, soil and style alter actual labor/materials; these are anecdotal but directly reflect estimator practice. Deposits/material and completion balance vary. **Software list-price signals, accessed 2026-09-24:** Fence Forger advertises a fence-specific plan at $200/month or $2,200/year; Jobber prices tier plans by size/features, with current public pricing changing by billing/offer. These do not demonstrate that an operator pays or would pay that amount. No verified WTP receipt/purchase statement found.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

New construction is one-time; repairs, gate adjustments, seasonal finish, warranty and replacement can recur. Schedule drawing approval, permits/HOA, utility locates, custom fabrication/material delivery, post setting/cure, crew and weather. Coordinate installation with landscape/deck/pool work if in broader project. Noncompletion: boundary uncertainty, locate not cleared, HOA/permit, weather/ground, access, custom material delay, wrong gate/fence revision, hidden utility or customer change. Record cause, action owner and revised expected date.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm layout, permit/owner authorization and utility locate → mark runs/gates → verify product and site → bore/dig/hand-dig as required → set posts/footings → fabricate/build sections → hang and tune gates → verify plumb/alignment/height/latch/opening and installed length → stain/seal where specified → remove debris, photo, customer walkthrough, invoice/warranty. AFA installer checklists for automated gates demonstrate that operator/gate safety requires system-specific checks; ordinary fence installation is not necessarily an automated-gate job. OSHA documents powered auger hazards including entanglement/hidden utility and overhead line risks. Utility location law, pool-barrier code, excavation, tool and equipment safety vary by task/jurisdiction. Rework includes wrong line/style, leaning posts, gate sag, insufficient clearance, finish failure and damage from utilities/ground movement.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need design/material options, site-measure date, permit/HOA and locate status, who marks property line, fabrication lead time, arrival/weather changes, change approval, progress, finish/care, gate operation, photos and warranty. Online drawing/approval can help, but field verification and boundary responsibility should be explicit. Do not imply that contractor establishes legal property boundary without survey authority.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Inputs include lumber/metal/vinyl/other fence material, posts, rails/pickets, gates, hinges/latches, concrete, fasteners, finish and disposal. Tools include auger/post-hole digger, saws, levels/laser, drills, compactors, trailers and fabrication equipment. Small crews may job-buy; larger contractors stock common materials and use supplier price books/takeoff. Team may include designer/estimator, fabricator, installer, helper, equipment operator, gate technician and licensed electrical subcontractor for powered gate work. Certifications/permits vary.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred: estimate speed/win, quoted-vs-actual length/material, fabrication lead time, feet/crew-day, material waste, gate first-pass, schedule slip, call-back, warranty, margin by style/material, change recovery and repeat/referral. Separate custom wood, standard install and fabrication-only; no standardized KPI benchmark found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Jobber markets general fencing workflows—estimates, optional line items, approvals, scheduling, field photos, invoicing and customer updates. Fence-specific products advertise takeoff/material calculations and production/dispatch. A 2026 first-person fence CRM thread contrasts general lead/quote/invoice needs with linear-foot takeoff and production scheduling, and calls out the small $1M–$5M contractor segment as challenging for SaaS; another commenter describes quick phone takeoffs and PDF quotes with detailed scope items. A separate older fence builder thread describes split estimating-first vs crew-management software. These are small samples, with possible vendor promotion, not prevalence or representative willingness to pay. Fence Forger's $200/$2,200 posted plan is an offer, not proof of purchases.
