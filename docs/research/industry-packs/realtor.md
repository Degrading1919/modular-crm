## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A Realtor's work is a relationship and transaction pipeline, with lead generation/follow-up, buyer or seller agreement, property/listing and showing activity, offer/contract, due diligence, escrow/closing coordination, document submission and commission disbursement. NAR's consumer guide describes post-contract escrow, earnest money, documents and contingencies; California DRE illustrates state-specific agency disclosures and escrow roles. Brokerage software exposes transaction-type checklists, document review and broker compliance separately from a contact CRM.

**Inference:** A pack should support both long-lived contact/property relationships and a time-bound deal record with parties, representation, deadlines, documents and status; residential purchase/sale, commercial sale and lease must use different templates. **Uncertain:** the candidate can include commercial brokerage, land and leasing. Evidence on operational software is stronger for US residential agent/brokerage work; commercial workflows differ. Complete status applies to the supported core patterns, with commercial detail explicitly called out as an open scope question.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential buyer/seller agents, listing agents, teams, independent brokerages and transaction coordinators help clients market, locate, negotiate and complete property transactions. Commercial brokers may represent landlord/tenant, buyer/seller, investor or occupier in lease and sale assignments; a long-term commercial deal may involve LOI, financial/due-diligence review and lease negotiation rather than a residential offer/escrow checklist. Agent may be solo or work inside team/brokerage whose staff review files and pay commissions. Leads come through sphere/referral, open houses, brokerage assignment, website/IDX and paid portal channels. The transaction cadence is uneven and market-dependent; referrals and later moves create repeat relationships, but closings are not a fixed recurring subscription. Agent represents a client under a defined relationship; they are not the buyer/seller, lender, appraiser or escrow holder.

## Terminology and durable records

Separate contact/household/entity, representation agreement, property/listing, showing, offer, transaction/deal, party role, brokerage, escrow/title contact, document, contingency, deadline, commission instruction and close. Store property address, parcel/MLS identifier where licensed, property type/use, list/offer price and status, owner/occupancy, marketing media, buyer preferences and geographic area. Store contact consent and relationship history. Deal-specific details include agency role and agreement term, transaction type (purchase/sale/lease), dates, offer/amendments, deposit/escrow holder, contingencies/due dates, inspection/appraisal/lender/title status, disclosure/document checklist, broker audit, close date and commission disbursement. Sensitive negotiation instructions, client financial facts and other party confidential information need role-based access. Brokerage retains legal/required file records under its policy and local law; customer-facing marketing CRM should not be treated as the formal file vault unless authorized.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Common services include buyer/seller representation, listing/marketing, buyer search/showings, offer and negotiation support, lease/sale brokerage, referral, and transaction coordination. Intake captures contact source, client goals and time horizon, transaction type/property interest, budget or asking expectations, representation status, geography, financing/preapproval status where voluntarily shared, property access, communication consent and decision makers. Written agency/buyer agreements and fee/compensation arrangements are a material early step; NAR materials emphasize written buyer agreements in relevant covered interactions after the settlement practice changes. Lead records should not imply an agency relationship before required agreement. Quote/proposal states agency scope and negotiated compensation; no universal commission rate is assumed. Public site/booking can accept consultation or showing requests, but listing/offer/agency and commercial lease scope need agent review. Brokerage forms, signatures and disclosure deadlines depend on state/association and deal type.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service pricing model:** Brokerage compensation is set by agreement and negotiated; NAR/settlement changes should not be represented as a standard rate. A transaction's sale price is not the agent's revenue, and any compensation/disbursement must follow written agreements and brokerage policy. Commercial leasing may use a lease commission arrangement; terms depend on parties, property and market and are not standardized by this research. Brokerage expense may include split/cap, franchise/desk fees, MLS/association dues, transaction fee and marketing spend, but these are operator-specific and not necessarily customer charges.

**Software price/WTP evidence:** In a 2025 Reddit post, one agent itemized a $224/month software stack, including Follow Up Boss at $69/month and transaction management through SkySlope at no charge via the brokerage. This is direct first-person spend/workplace-bundle evidence, not a market average or current vendor quote. Follow Up Boss's current public page is sales/trial oriented and does not expose an exact plan price in the page text reviewed; SkySlope is demo/brokerage-oriented. This supports that some agents pay separately for a lead CRM while a broker bundles transaction-compliance tools; it does not establish a single willingness-to-pay. Service compensation is not software WTP.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Activity includes prospect follow-up, consultation, property tours/open houses, listing photography/marketing, offer/inspection appointments and closing milestones. Showing tours can be route/territory-aware; transaction deadlines, access and negotiation matter more than dispatch. Contract deadlines are governed by executed agreement and local practice, and should be calculated/confirmed by authorized users, not generated as legal advice. Commercial site visits, tours and due diligence may span longer periods and multiple parties. A late contract/document, financing contingency, appraisal issue or closing date change triggers updates to parties and the brokerage checklist. Referral/contact nurture can recur as a CRM activity but not as a property service subscription.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

For a residential deal: capture inquiry and consent → consult and confirm representation/scope → create listing/buyer relationship → gather property/needs and market information → schedule showings/marketing → prepare/negotiate offer → execute contract → open escrow/title and track deposit/contingencies/inspections/appraisal/financing/disclosures → collect and submit required file documents for broker review → coordinate settlement and closing → reconcile commission/payout and archive. DRE sources explain agency duties and escrow participants in California; NAR notes state-level variation in closing practices. Commercial sale/lease should use a separate checklist and approvals. Completion evidence includes signed agreements/contracts, disclosures, deposit confirmation where available, inspection/access record, broker approval, settlement/closing confirmation and commission statement. Avoid collecting/recording confidential facts outside business need and protect client documents. Failure/noncompletion includes no agreement, rejected offer, failed contingency, missed access, title/lender delay, inspection renegotiation, lease negotiation ending or file returned for missing/incorrect documents. Rework is amendment, correction, missing signature/disclosure, reinspection or rescheduled closing—not an ordinary warranty callback.

## Customer communication and self-service

Clients need fast lead response, representation and fee explanation, showing confirmation, offer/counteroffer updates, deadline and access reminders, inspection/appraisal/escrow updates, status/next step and closing instructions. Use permissioned communication preferences and protect confidential negotiation content; do not send automated contract advice or promise a closing. A self-service portal may allow inquiry, preferences, selected property documents, task/deadline status and secure signatures through the licensed transaction provider. Public website/lead form, property alerts, consult/showing request and marketing consent may be configurable. Broker/compliance review, listing status, compensation terms and legally binding documents require authorized agent/broker input.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Common tools include phone/camera, MLS and association forms, mapping, lockbox/showing access, listing media vendors, e-signature, transaction management, CRM/lead routing, calendar, email/text, accounting/commission system and brokerage staff. Do not assume all agents pay for each: operator evidence shows at least one agent's transaction tool supplied by the broker. Team roles include agent, broker, assistant, transaction coordinator, marketing staff and outside title/escrow, lender, inspector, photographer and attorney. No physical stock inventory is typical. Brokerage commission splits and staff roles are configuration, not universal pack defaults.

## Reporting and operating measures

Incumbent brokerage tools report transaction status and agent/broker metrics. Useful measures include lead response and conversion by source, appointments/showings, signed representation agreements, listing/offer pipeline, accepted-to-close ratio, fall-through reason, cycle time, missing-file/compliance exceptions, commission volume/net payout and repeat/referral customers. Team leads need assignment and workload visibility; solo agents may need simpler nurture reporting. This list is inferred from vendor capabilities and transaction stages; no common industry definition/benchmark was established.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Follow Up Boss positions as a real-estate lead CRM and integrates lead sources/marketing tools; SkySlope positions as brokerage transaction management with transaction-type checklists, document upload/audits, e-signing and broker oversight. The agent-spend example differentiates an individually paid CRM from a brokerage-supplied compliance/transaction platform. California DRE and NAR sources document agency, escrow, agreement and transaction steps. Likely connected stack may include MLS, CRM, forms/e-sign, transaction file, calendar, email/SMS, accounting/commission, portal and title/escrow. Stacks differ by brokerage, MLS, residential/commercial focus and whether a TC is used. Operator posts include frustrations about CRM adoption/data entry and deals/docs across email and separate tools; anecdotes are not prevalence evidence. No exact public SkySlope brokerage list price was located.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** NAR's consumer guide describes contract-to-close tasks and notes state variation; California DRE provides state-specific agency and escrow detail. SkySlope's official setup requires checklist variation by listing/lease/commercial sale, while Follow Up Boss addresses lead CRM. A first-person agent reports paying separately for CRM but receiving transaction software through brokerage. **Inference:** the pack can recommend broad relationship/transaction records while default checklists must ask deal type and jurisdiction. **Uncertain:** commercial brokerage scope and economics were not independently researched to the same depth as residential; listing/transaction systems and brokerage-paid costs vary. The “Realtor” candidate may cover more than residential home sales. No common commission rate or universal software WTP is inferred. Confidence high for residential workflow separation and brokerage compliance role; medium for product fit; lower for commercial defaults.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask residential/commercial, buyer/seller/lease, individual/team/brokerage, lead/transaction tools, broker file requirements, jurisdiction/forms, representation status and direct-versus-closing commission flow. Prototype contact/property/deal separation, configurable deal checklists, leads and communications, deadlines with confirmation, and broker-facing file status. Keep MLS, forms, transaction audit and compensation handling tied to authorized systems; do not encode fee percentages or a universal residential-only pipeline.
