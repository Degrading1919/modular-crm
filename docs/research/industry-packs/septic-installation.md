## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Septic installation and replacement is project work shaped by property/site conditions, system design, soil evaluation, permits, excavation, tank and dispersal-field installation, inspections, and coordination with owners and sometimes separate excavation contractors. A work order can span weeks of approval and multiple site visits even when physical installation lasts only a few days. Repair/maintenance may lead into replacement; pumping can be an adjacent but operationally distinct recurring service.

The pack opportunity is a project workflow with durable property/system records, configurable system types and components, permit/inspection milestones, site photos/documents, estimates/change orders, equipment/materials, and post-install service history. The contract can represent steps, fields, services, checklists, pricing inputs, and recommendations. **Uncertain:** local code forms, design calculations, permit filing, and as-built diagrams vary; this evidence does not prove the shared product needs septic-specific core tables or automated regulatory logic.

## Business model and customer segments

New-home construction, failed-system replacement, upgrades, additions, and repair are sold to homeowners, builders/developers, property managers, and sometimes municipalities/programs. An installer may handle design/permit coordination and physical work or partner with soil evaluators, engineers, excavators, tank suppliers, and inspectors. **Observed variation:** a 2026 Oregon excavator’s explanation separates licensed septic installation (permit, tank, lines, inspections, signoff) from dirt work done by an excavation contractor; that is a local example, not a universal split. Demand is triggered by construction, failure, health/environmental concerns, or sale/inspection. Site access, weather/ground conditions, agency review, utility locates, and material delivery constrain scheduling. Owner-operated small crews and larger multi-crew contractors will differ in estimating, procurement, and project controls.

## Terminology and durable records

Use property/site, onsite wastewater treatment system, system design/site plan, soil/perc evaluation, tank, distribution box, dispersal/leach field, alternative/advanced treatment system, installer, excavation, permit, inspection, as-built, and change order. The durable property record should retain system type/capacity, design/as-built documents, tank location, dispersal area, installation date, approvals, installer/warranty, and later maintenance/service history. Job inputs include soil results, excavation dimensions, delivery and equipment access, utility marking, weather, inspection notes, and photo evidence. Permit numbers, owner documents, access notes, and site drawings may be sensitive or safety-relevant. Do not make fields nationally mandatory; terminology and system components vary by jurisdiction and design.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Typical services: new system, replacement, repair/upgrade, site evaluation/design coordination, excavation, tank/field install, final inspection, and follow-up. Intake needs property address/parcel, project trigger, building/bedroom/use assumptions as required locally, existing system records, site plans, well/waterbody/setbacks, access/terrain, soil evaluation, and permit status. Many quotes require site visit/design and are measured/engineered, not menu priced. Estimates should allow allowances, alternate systems, excavation/material assumptions, exclusions, and change orders for rock, groundwater, access, or unseen conditions. Deposits/progress billing may occur, but sources reviewed do not establish common terms. Self-service should collect basic property/project details and documents then route to a qualified estimator; it should not promise feasibility or permit approval.

## Pricing and commercial model

Prices vary substantially with system design, size, soil, terrain, excavation, materials, water table, local requirements, access, and restoration. SepticMind’s consumer education page gives a broad **US$3,000–US$15,000+** installed-cost range, accessed 2026-09-24; it is a vendor editorial estimate, not an installer quote and may not represent current locality or advanced systems. Iron & Earth in Oregon reports **US$3,500–$7,000** for excavation portion of full replacement jobs in its service area (2026-04-28), distinct from full system price. These are consumer project prices, not software willingness-to-pay. No actual installer quote/buying evidence for software was found. SepticSync lists $125/$349/$599 service provider plans; Septic BizMan lists a $250 minimum plus onboarding from $3,000; those are vendor list prices with very different product scopes, not a typical budget.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

This is project scheduling, not primarily recurring route work: design and approvals precede excavation; equipment, tank delivery, subs, inspections, and other construction work must align. Weather and site conditions can move dates; stages may need inspection before backfill. Capacity requires crew skills/licensing, excavator/crane/truck, tank availability, subcontractor windows, and access. Post-install records can support recurring inspection/pumping reminders, but those may be sold by a different service line. A route map is useful for crews and site visits, while project board/dependencies and calendar are more central. The current contract can encode workflow stages but not project dependency semantics explicitly; no evidence yet that a shared project scheduling capability is absent.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Qualify property → site meeting/evaluation and design → confirm permit and pre-work conditions → locate utilities/prepare access → excavate/set tank and construct piping/dispersal field → inspections at required stages → backfill/restore → final approval and customer handoff with records/warranty → create system service history. Pumper/install references emphasize interviewing the owner early and confirming buildability, access, and changed site conditions. The permit tech transcript shows incomplete submissions and authority follow-up can add days or weeks. Excavation hazards (trench collapse), heavy lifts, underground utilities, and wastewater exposure are relevant; apply actual local requirements and competent-person processes. Inspection timing and required as-builts vary by jurisdiction. Noncompletion causes include permit delay, failed inspection, weather/groundwater, utility conflict, site access, material/subcontractor delay, and scope change. Capture cause and commercial responsibility separately.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need clear pre-work scope, permit/status updates, site-preparation instructions, schedule changes, inspection coordination, change-order approval, completion documents, and warranty/maintenance guidance. Builder/developer projects may have multiple stakeholders and documents. Do not automatically assert a design is approved or system compliant before authority signoff. Portal can support document exchange, estimate approval, progress photos, invoice/payment, and final packet delivery; booking should hand off to an estimator.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Excavators, dump trucks, tank/pipe/distribution materials, stone/aggregate, pumps, compaction/laser/level tools, safety gear, and restoration supplies are job-specific. Track equipment and vehicle assignment, materials committed/received, supplier, and delivery date where the operator needs it. A small installer may rent or subcontract excavation; another owns a fleet. Crews need appropriate licensing/certification depending on jurisdiction and system. Time, equipment hours, subcontractor commitments, and job costing matter on projects; inventory defaults should be suggestions only.

## Reporting and operating measures

Observed vendor tools advertise estimates, service orders, job checklists, employee/vehicle assignment, inventory, cost/resale listings, photos, and time reports. Useful measures inferred from project sequence include estimate-to-approved conversion, days awaiting permit, project cycle time, inspection pass/rework, change-order value, labor/material/equipment cost vs estimate, warranty callbacks, and gross margin by system type. These are inferred and require tenant definitions; no universal installer KPI set was established.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Septic-specific software pages promote project estimates, install work orders, checklists, inventory, crew/vehicle assignment, and offline/mobile records. Septic BizMan presents broad ERP packaging with per-user price and substantial onboarding. SepticSync targets providers and municipalities with county forms, job costing, route tools, and migration. Pumper (trade publication; written by a University of Minnesota onsite-wastewater researcher/NOWRA president) advises owner interviews to verify site intentions, buildability, access, and changes during staging; this is professional guidance rather than owner complaint evidence. A Reddit founder/operator post seeks companies to shape software for site plans, permits, system data, and service history; it indicates a perceived problem but is self-interested and not independent customer validation. Evidence of installer-specific incumbent usage and direct software complaints remains thinner than pumping evidence.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources converge on site evaluation, permits, excavation, installation, and inspection, while showing that licensing and regulator roles differ locally. The Oregon source’s separation of installer and excavator may not match vertically integrated firms. Massachusetts assigns primary new-construction authority to local Boards of Health under state Title 5. System type and lot conditions materially change scope/pricing. Installation-specific operator software evidence is limited, so mark commercial patterns medium and software pain low confidence.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype project intake with document upload, configurable property/system records, a stage workflow for evaluation/permit/build/inspection/handoff, change orders, field photos, and a final system-record packet. Keep permit, form, system-design, license, material, and price rules jurisdiction/tenant-controlled. Ask what the company performs in-house, which agencies approve work, common system types, whether soil evaluation/design and excavation are included, how deposits/progress billing work, and whether post-install pumping is sold. Keep installation and pumping as separate profiles because their project sequencing, work duration, scope risks, and recurring patterns differ.
