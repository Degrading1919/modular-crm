## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A professional underground containment installation is a property-specific boundary design, utility-locate and wire/transmitter install, receiver/collar setup, boundary flagging, hands-on dog/owner training, test and follow-up/service workflow. Dealers sell a system-plus-training outcome, not simply a length of buried wire. Property layout, gates, driveways, landscape, buried utilities, dog count/temperament, collar fit, homeowner expectations, and service access shape the job. Ongoing work includes battery, wire-break, lightning/surge, collar, boundary adjustment, new-pet training and system transfer/service for a new owner.

**Inference:** Model the service around the property boundary/system asset and each dog’s collar/training record. Intake and quote need a site walk or detailed map/photos; installation completion should show tested loop/transmitter and training steps, with the customer acknowledging that electronic containment is not a physical barrier and does not prevent all escapes or outside approach. Keep it separate from physical privacy fence construction and general behavioral training.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential dog owners seek an unobstructed yard boundary, containment where a physical fence is impractical, indoor/no-go zones, or service/repair of an inherited installation. Some dealers are owner-operators or local authorized dealers selling a national manufacturer’s system; others bundle installation, certified training, equipment warranty and ongoing home service. Multiple-dog households can require separately configured collars and training. Property managers, rental units or farms may inquire, but are not established as a default segment.

The lifecycle is inquiry → property/pet consultation → boundary design and quote → utility marking/property permission → buried loop/transmitter installation → flags and equipment test → individual dog training and owner handoff → follow-up practice visits → warranty/service/repair. User-manual DIY retail products have separate economics and install scope; this candidate specifically names installation as the business.

## Terminology and durable records

Use client/owner, dog/pet, property/service address, containment boundary/loop, transmitter, receiver/collar, flags, training session, service call and repair. Durable property/system record should capture boundary map or annotated imagery, perimeter/loop length, wire/splice/repair positions, gates/driveway/drive-through sections, transmitter model/serial and indoor location, surge protection/grounding per manufacturer, tested signal/boundary settings, warranty, installation date, original dealer and service plan. Sensitive location/access data and private yard imagery require limited access.

Each dog is a customer-associated asset: name, breed/size/age as useful, collar serial/receiver and battery, settings as permitted, fit check, training session dates, observed response and owner instructions. Behavior or aggression history should be asked only when needed for safe service/training, held confidentially, and never converted to a guarantee of suitability. Site inputs include property boundaries, public sidewalk/road and neighbor context, existing fences/gates, landscaping/hardscape, visible utility marks, known private irrigation/lighting/septic lines, access and permission/HOA status. Distinguish service location from privately owned property and record whether the contracting person has authority to authorize installation.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Menu: new underground loop install, add-on/indoor zone, additional collar/pet, repair/wire break, system inspection/transfer, boundary re-flagging, battery/collar replacement, training/retraining, surge repair, landscaping pre-work/service plan. A vendor’s install package includes a custom boundary, buried wire, transmitter, collar, and training; terms and brands differ.

Intake minimum: address/property map; boundary request and exclusion areas; lot and landscape/driveway/gate details; house/garage transmitter placement and outlet; utility-locate status; known irrigation, lighting, septic or private services; homeowner/landlord permission; dog count, ages/sizes and relevant experience/temperament; existing collar/system/brand; desired date; access; and whether customer wants training/support plan. Photos or satellite/aerial view can prepare an estimate but do not replace site verification. Escalate unclear property lines, shared/HOA land, road/sidewalk crossings, utility conflicts, dog suitability/training needs, and unmarked private utilities for human decision.

## Pricing and commercial model

Quotes may be package plus perimeter/yard complexity, number of dogs/collars, training sessions, repair length, brand/warranty, and travel. Dog Fence Ontario (Ontario, Canada) lists $1,800–$3,500 for most complete installations, with a $1,945 + HST package including up to 500 feet of wire, one collar, installation and training; this cannot be treated as a US market rate. Payment plans, warranty, included service calls and cancellation terms are vendor/dealer-specific. These figures are customer service prices, not software subscriptions.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Installation is a one-time appointment with a consultation, possible utility marking wait, install crew/equipment allocation, then dog/owner training, sometimes as a return visit. Repairs are usually unscheduled or expedited when the loop fault alarm or escaping pet makes the system unusable. Annual service, battery delivery, warranty service, pre-landscaping check and new-pet training are optional contracts/cadence; vendor examples differ. Schedule by trained installer, property complexity, dog/owner availability, weather/ground conditions, call length, and install kit; never schedule a dog’s first training as a short unattended visit. Multi-dealer territories and multiple stops can justify geocoding/routing; solo dealer does not automatically need route optimization.

Late utility marking, rain/frozen ground, buried obstruction, property permission, incomplete customer training, dog ill/absent, wrong product/collar, or a discovered utility can delay or stop work. Repair dispatch should capture system brand/model and loop symptoms so the technician arrives with compatible locator/splice/battery equipment.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm legal property authority, system scope, marked public utilities and customer-disclosed private lines; do not assume utility locating covers private lines. PetSafe’s installation manual tells customers to obtain local utility marking before trenching; 811 coverage and process are local. Walk/map boundary with owner, identify roads, gates, pool or other no-go areas, property lines, landscape and utilities, then obtain approval before burying wire. Install transmitter/surge protection/grounding per product instructions and installer authorization; bury/test loop, verify warning/correction zones with test equipment, place flags, configure/collar-fit each dog, conduct manufacturer-aligned incremental training with owner, and leave written care/battery/training/service guidance. Record boundary drawing/photos, loop and transmitter tests, settings/serials, collar fit, training attendance and customer sign-off.

**Safety/limits:** PetSafe says proper training is essential and warns against leaving a collar on a dog for more than 12 hours a day; DogWatch warranty materials state its system may not contain every dog in all circumstances and specify training and aggression-related warnings. Phrase these as manufacturer/model-specific. Do not market any electronic system as a physical fence, escape-proof barrier, or protection from people/animals entering the property. Stop for unsafe animal/owner behavior, unknown utilities, disputed property line, wet electrical hazard, severe ground conditions, or dog distress. Record customer not ready for training, utility/conflict, loop damage, equipment fault, weather/ground, absent dog, or access/permission issue. Repair callback should relate to system record and warranty; refunds/retraining and billability remain contract terms.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs consultation/quote, property-prep and utility-locate instructions, install window, dog/owner training reminder, on-the-way/delay notices, completion diagram, system manual, training plan, test confirmation, warranty/service-plan terms, battery reminders, landscaping reminder and urgent repair channel. Self-service can submit request, dog count, address/map/photos and preferred time, but cannot confirm suitability or quote unverified utility/property work. Automations must not tell customers the dog is safe or system is functioning until technician records tests; significant boundary change, repair, dog behavior or warranty dispute requires office/trainer review.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Installers use trenching/wire-laying tools, hand tools, signal/wire-break locator, splices, boundary flags, transmitter/surge protection, compatible wire/collars/batteries, test unit, map/measure tool, PPE and vehicle stock. Tool assignment depends on surface and property. Manufacturers/dealers may train installers and trainers, impose approved setup/warranty rules, and supply proprietary hardware. Staff capability may include low-voltage/electronic device installation, animal training, locating tools, and familiarity with each brand; requirements must be verified with vendor, locality and insurer rather than assumed. Track serials, owner-supplied/existing units, compatible receiver, warranty and replenishment. No evidence supports a universal license; trenching and electrical/low-voltage rules are local and work may cross into licensed scope.

## Reporting and operating measures

Suggested reports: inquiry-to-site-survey and quote conversion; install duration/variance by boundary complexity; feet of loop and callbacks; first-pass loop test; training/follow-up completion; service-plan attach/renewal; wire-break repair response and repeat faults; revenue by install/repair/plan and brand; mileage and territory; dog/collar inventory and warranty claims. Vendor materials establish service, battery and warranty plans, not standard industry KPIs. Define denominator and warranty coverage; do not count a customer-reported alarm as confirmed wire break before diagnosis.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Manufacturer/dealer systems bundle professional installation, training, customer support, warranty and service calls. Invisible Fence’s support page lists annual service/battery plans and a home visit that tests transmitter, surge protection, collar and buried wire before repairs; DogWatch testimonials describe quick install, training and rapid repair after landscaping damage. PetSafe’s manual guides DIY layout, transmitter and buried loop and warns about training/utility marks. General contractor CRM or fence estimating tools can handle leads, appointments, quotes, crews, jobs and accounting, but fence contractor software is not proof of dog-containment-specific adoption. Dog-owner discussions mention wire break detection/repair difficulty and mixed experiences; those are anecdotal product-user reports, not installers’ complaint prevalence. No direct operator software stack/WTP statement was found.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Professional brands sell installation and training together; dealer warranties and service plans make post-install support operational. Manufacturer manuals require utility awareness and dog training; user/customer posts discuss mixed training outcomes, collar/wire failures and sometimes rapid seller support. **Inference:** Attach the core record to an installed boundary/system plus each dog; service planning is meaningful because wire breaks, landscaping and batteries can require return work. **Uncertain:** Exact electrical/trenching licensing, utility-mark coverage, permits, animal training practice, property permissions and animal welfare rules are local. The tool should enforce permission and stop conditions but must not encode manufacturer settings as universal. Canadian price example is not US pricing. High confidence in workflow distinction, medium in market/recurrence and low in prevalence/WTP.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a quote-driven home installation with site map and dog/system assets, utility/property permission checklist, technician test evidence, training handoff, and follow-on repair/service plan. Ask whether work includes trainer, repair/other brands, DIY devices, support plans, multiple installers and many daily calls. Keep boundary shape, brand, coverage, collar settings, warnings and warranties brand-specific and tenant-configurable. Do not show “contained/safe” automatically; require a technician-recorded loop test and retain explicit customer-facing system limitations. Regulatory review should be based on the operator's actual jurisdictions and trade scope.
