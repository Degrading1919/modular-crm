## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A pool alarm detects pool/perimeter access or water entry and sounds an alert; alarm types differ (surface, subsurface, perimeter/door and newer vision-based products). CPSC describes alarms as one layer alongside barriers and supervision, and ASTM F2208 covers residential pool/spa alarm performance ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Materials), [ASTM F2208](https://store.astm.org/f2208-07.html)). Alarm fitting may be a brief setup/calibration/testing job by a pool or security specialist, bundled with pool safety inspection or broader pool construction/service.

**Uncertain:** The inventory's “installation” label does not identify whether it means contractor-installed permanent alarms, device sales/setup, code-driven builder add-on, or a standalone niche business. Many consumer units are portable/self-installed and market evidence for dedicated installer firms is scant. Profile remains partial for business model, operator software and WTP; do not merge into pool maintenance or general alarm installation.

## Business model and customer segments

- Potential buyers include homeowners with young children/pets, landlords/short-term rentals, pool builders at handover, and managers of residential complexes. Safety-conscious parents and pool renovations are likely triggers; this is inference supported by CPSC pool safety guidance, not customer-segment survey evidence.
- Product/service scope can include alarm selection, delivery, installation, sensitivity testing, receiver pairing, user instruction, battery/service follow-up, and documentation for a local inspection. Poolguard describes a portable, battery-powered pool alarm and installation video; Canadian Orange Pools advertises an in-house installation team ([Poolguard](https://poolguard.com/alarms/), [Orange Pools](https://orangepools.ca/pool-alarm-systems/)).
- Providers may be pool safety consultants, pool-service firms, builders, home security installers or alarm specialists. A sole installer may have few visits/day; bundled firms share crews with other trades. No size distribution evidenced.
- U.S. federal guidance promotes multiple safety layers; local building-code requirements differ. New York guidance references ASTM F2208; confirm applicable code with local building official ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Materials), [NY Department of State](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/08/tb-1014-uc-pool-summary-rev-1.pdf)).

- **Service-location data:** pool type/shape, surface/deck, pool edge, house/door access, remote receiver placement, distance/barriers, outdoor noise, power/battery and Wi-Fi where connected.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Possible work: fit consultation, alarm supply, portable alarm setup, permanent perimeter/door sensor installation, receiver pairing, sensitivity/false-alarm testing, pool-safety review and repair/replacement. Do not assume electrical/wiring work is in scope.
- Intake asks residential pool/spa, pool geometry/type, existing barriers/alarms, reason for install, children/pets, model preference, local inspection/insurance requirement, address and access. Provider should hand off if electrical integration, code interpretation or incompatible surface needs a licensed specialist.
- Quote may be device price plus setup/installation, multiple alarms/access points, hardwire/receiver, travel and diagnostics. Angi cites consumer alarm device ranges $60–$400 and average $180 (published 2026; not installed-service price) and suggests professional help where installation/test uncertain ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/what-pool-alarm.htm)). Orange Pools says price depends on alarm type, number of access points, pool size and installation complexity; no public amount ([Orange Pools](https://orangepools.ca/pool-alarm-systems/)).
- Small installations may use appointment booking; site conditions and safety standards call for product compatibility check.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Inputs include sensor type, number of pools/doors/receivers, mount/deck work, Wi-Fi/power, distance, diagnostic and false-alarm troubleshooting. Device MSRP and install labor should be separate.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Often one-time appointment, with follow-up if receiver pairing/test fails or alarm needs replacement; seasonal opening/closing might create bundled service recurrence.
- Technician should match sensor type, pool surface/geometry and wiring ability. Route density matters only for pool-service routes or a region-wide installation operator.
- Reschedule for unsafe weather, closed pool, access or incompatible conditions. Customer notice and clear “test passed/failed” matter.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Intake/model compatibility → inspect site/pool → select alarm and installation point → install/mount/pair → test each relevant entry scenario per manufacturer procedure → verify audible receiver/alert in intended areas → explain limitations/maintenance → record model, test, photos and customer acceptance.
- ASTM F2208 states alarms are intended to supplement, not replace, supervision, fences, gates and locks ([ASTM](https://store.astm.org/f2208-07.html)). CPSC also describes door alarms and pool alarms among protective layers and notes false alarms can lead to users removing alarms ([CPSC letter](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/pdfs/blk_media_astmalarm.pdf)).
- Safety includes electrical/water proximity, working around a pool, child safety, and not altering barriers or bypassing alarms. Code obligations depend on jurisdiction and alarm type; the 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code references certified ASTM-compliant alarms but is only applicable where adopted ([ICC code](https://www.iccsafe.org/wp-content/uploads/ISPSC-2024.pdf)).
- Noncompletion: incompatible device, pool closed/unsafe, false triggering, receiver out of range, missing power, mounting issue or owner declines required additional protection. Do not promise drowning prevention.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Customer needs product explanation, clear limitations, booking/setup checklist, test result, battery/maintenance instructions, warranty, and pool-safety authority where code interpretation is requested.
- Online intake can capture pool photos and device preference, but no automated safety certification. Avoid phrasing that gives false reassurance or presents single alarm as sufficient protection.
- Follow-up reminders for battery/test may be optional and manufacturer-dependent.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Alarms, receivers, mounting hardware, batteries and test tools. Exact inventory follows chosen manufacturer; carry/stocking may be limited.
- Installers need product training and any electrical/security license required locally; crews may be pool technicians or alarm specialists. Certification claims should be verified.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype only as a small install/inspection configuration layered on a pool-service or security provider after market validation. Capture pool geometry/type, device model/certification, test steps/results, limitations, warranty and customer acknowledgment. Keep device manufacturer, code, install method and service bundling configurable. Ask operators whether alarms are a paid standalone service, who installs them, what code documentation customers request, and what callback/false-alarm handling costs. Never assert one alarm alone makes a pool safe or code-compliant.
