## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Mobile automotive glass service can repair a small chip/crack or replace a windshield, side/back glass or other vehicle glass at a customer/ fleet site. Replacement uses vehicle-specific glass, adhesive and safe drive-away time; windshields can integrate cameras/sensors that may require ADAS recalibration. AGSC/AGRSS standard calls for replacement/calibration records and specified calibration equipment/trained personnel when calibration is provided. Mobile installation additionally depends on suitable access and environmental conditions.

**Inference:** The CRM workflow must begin with triage: vehicle/VIN, damage location/size, glass part/features, repair-vs-replace decision, photos, location and insurance/claim status. Schedule after verifying part availability and mobile installation conditions, then link installation, adhesive cure/drive-away, required calibration, customer notification and documentation. Keep repair, replacement and calibration as separate linked work steps.

**Uncertain:** Candidate says “repair,” which could mean chip repair only or the broader glass replacement category. This profile includes both for discovery and emphasizes the exact service choice; it does not treat calibration as universally required on every vehicle.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include private vehicle owners, dealerships, fleets, insurers/claims networks and collision repair shops. Work is urgent when visibility, weather intrusion or vehicle use is affected, but appointment and part lead time can control response. Revenue includes chip repair, glass/part plus labor replacement, moldings/sensors, recalibration, mobile/travel or fleet account work; insurance billing may involve network rules and customer deductible. Solo technicians and small mobile fleets differ from multi-location auto-glass chains with centralized call centers, parts sourcing, dispatch and EDI claims.

## Terminology and durable records

Terms include **customer/vehicle owner**, **vehicle/VIN**, **glass position**, **chip/stone break**, **bullseye/star/combination crack**, **repair**, **replacement**, **NAGS/part number**, **urethane/adhesive**, **safe drive-away time**, **ADAS**, **static/dynamic calibration**, **claim/assignment**, **work order** and **leak/wind-noise callback**. Vehicle history may store year/make/model/VIN, trim/features, glass sensors, calibration outcome, prior repair and account; service location records address, garage/covered access, weather exposure, fleet/site contact and safe parking. Glass is single-job material; van stock, calibration frame/tooling, adhesive lot/expiry and reusable equipment persist. VIN, insurer claim, customer address, calibration report and vehicle safety record are sensitive.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services may include windshield chip repair, crack assessment, windshield replacement, side/rear/quarter glass replacement, regulator/window work, molding/trim, rain sensor/camera transfer, ADAS calibration or referral to a qualified calibration provider, fleet glass inspection and warranty callback. Intake: VIN or full vehicle detail, glass position, photos/measurements, damage age/condition, sensor/camera/heated glass features, customer/insurer responsibility, site and preferred time, part sourcing/availability, calibration needs and safe vehicle return. Quote can require physical assessment and part lookup; distinguish tentative estimate from insurance-authorized amount. Contract covers glass brand/part, repair limitations, damage discovered during disassembly, calibration/provider, adhesive cure time, weather reschedule, warranty, deductible/payment, supplemental insurer approval and customer safety notice. Self-service booking should hand off if VIN/part or calibration remains uncertain.

## Pricing and commercial model

Inputs include repair type, glass part/sensor content, make/model/year, OEM/aftermarket availability, labor/access/trim, adhesive/moldings, calibration, mobile distance, fleet/account price, insurer network, shop supplies and urgency. Some chip repairs are fixed/menu priced; replacement may require part quote and insurer/network approval. No service price is asserted. **Software list pricing:** eDirectGlass advertises Pay-Per-Invoice at $5 per invoice for qualifying low-volume shops and Essential at $89.95/user/month, with other per-user tiers and transaction/assignment fees. This is a software vendor list price, not auto-glass service pricing. **WTP evidence:** no operator purchase evidence found; list pricing alone does not establish what a mobile glass operator pays or is willing to pay.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Schedule by vehicle/site access, part arrival, technician skill, kit/van stock, job duration, insurer/network slot, weather and adhesive conditions. Mobile routing can cluster customer/fleet locations but must allow traffic, vehicle drop-off, calibration facility/site requirements and curing instructions. Replacement may need static calibration in a controlled layout or dynamic calibration drive; follow vehicle-specific requirements and qualified equipment. Rebook when wrong part arrives, damage type is not repairable, weather/site conditions violate installation instructions, customer/vehicle absent, or calibration fails. Fleet accounts can recur; private damage calls are usually one-off.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm scope, VIN/features, glass and customer/claim authorization; check part, adhesive, tools, calibration need and site conditions; document existing damage and vehicle condition; protect interior/body; assess repairability or remove old glass per procedure; install repair/replacement using product/OEM instructions; observe required safe drive-away time; recalibrate if required and equipped, or arrange qualified follow-up; complete post-install checks (retention, trim, wipers, leaks/noise, sensors, window initialization); provide calibration result, drive-away and aftercare instructions; collect customer acceptance/payment, close insurer documents and retain job records. AGSC industry standard and vehicle maker/adhesive specifications are source of procedures; laws and insurer obligations vary. Don't release the job as finished if required calibration, regulator reset or safety step is unresolved. Noncompletion/rework includes glass shortage/wrong part, crack not repairable, unsafe weather/parking, adhesive/cure issue, calibration failure, broken trim, leak/noise or claim denial. Capture photos and customer notification. Safety-related complaint escalates to responsible technician/manager.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs arrival window, part confirmation, insurance/deductible scope, mobile work space needs, cure/drive-away time, calibration plan, aftercare, proof/invoice and warranty path. Send arrival and delay messages; have an explicit human review for unexpected price/scope, unsafe conditions, calibration failure and insurer supplements. Avoid saying calibration is unnecessary or successful before the vehicle-specific process/result is confirmed.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Van stock may include common glass, adhesives/primers, molding/clips, repair resin/bridge, cutout tools, setting tools, interior protection, glass racks, PPE, calibration targets/frame/scanner and suitable workspace aids. Track part/lot, adhesive shelf life, tool calibration/inspection and reusable calibration equipment if operator workflow needs it. Roles include glass technician, calibration technician, dispatcher/customer service, parts/supply and insurer billing. Technician training/certification may be customer, standard or company requirement; AGSC certification represents industry program, not a universal legal license. Parts supplier, calibration partner, fleet account, insurer/EDI network and disposal provider vary by area.

## Reporting and operating measures

Possible measures: repair-vs-replace mix, quote acceptance, part fill/return, drive time, jobs/day, first-time completion, calibration completion/failure, safe-drive-away notices, leakage/wind-noise callbacks, warranty/rework, claim supplement, invoice aging and gross margin per service/job. AGSC standard requires certain records for replacement/calibration, but does not prescribe business KPIs. Treat operational measures as candidates until operator sources confirm use.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Auto-glass shop systems advertise vehicle/VIN and glass part catalogs, quotes, claims/EDI, dispatch, mobile work orders, supplier price/order, CRM, inventory, calibration and payment. eDirectGlass's own plan comparison illustrates both per-user plans and transaction/network add-ons. Operator discussions disagree over ADAS calibration necessity in particular cases, but the AGSC standard and OEM procedure stress proper equipment/trained personnel/communication when needed; operator anecdotes must not overrule technical procedures. Technicians cite calibration setup/space and glass availability as vehicle-specific practical issues. This profile uses community reports as examples, not official repair direction.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

The candidate says repair, while incumbent systems serve both repair and replacement. AGSC/AGRSS is an industry consensus standard; it is not described here as automatically a law in every location. ADAS calibration is vehicle-specific and required by manufacturer/procedure where applicable; operator forum disagreement demonstrates why a product must store an assessed requirement/result instead of hard-code “always” or “never.” **High confidence:** vehicle identity, damage/photos, part, mobile conditions and safety closeout matter. **Medium:** software integration across CRM/parts/claims/calibration reduces duplicate entry. **Low:** expected repair/replacement mix and WTP. Confirm jurisdictions, repair-vs-replace scope and in-house calibration capability.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype photo/VIN-first triage, repair/replacement branch, part status, mobile site conditions, cure/drive-away instruction, vehicle-specific calibration result and warranty followup. Ask which glass services are offered, whether claims/parts catalogs are integrated, whether calibration is in-house/subcontracted and what site/environment limits apply. Keep calibration, OEM/insurer rules, parts and warranties vehicle-specific; never treat a missing record as evidence the work is complete.
