## Executive summary

Auger boring is a trenchless construction method that advances a casing and rotating auger from a launch/drive pit toward a reception pit, often under roadways, railways or other obstacles, to install a casing or utility crossing. It is different from horizontal directional drilling (HDD): guidance, tooling and allowable alignment vary, and the sources surfaced here often use “boring” loosely. A contractor may specialize in auger boring, tunneling, casing, HDD, pipe jacking or several methods; research must not treat these as identical.

OSHA's HDD safety bulletin establishes the importance of utility verification and site-specific planning for guided drilling but is not an auger-boring procedure standard. A sample HDD contract spec requires daily drilling and guidance logs and as-built plan/profile, which is adjacent evidence and must not be presumed to govern auger boring. Direct owner/operator software evidence and auger-specific incumbent pricing were insufficient in this pass. Therefore this profile is explicitly partial; candidate pack behavior below emphasizes project records, permits, utility conflicts, equipment, safety and as-built evidence, with low confidence until auger-boring operators and primary technical guidance are reviewed.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include utility owners, municipalities/DOTs, GCs, pipeline/telecom/electric/water contractors and industrial facility owners. A boring subcontractor may install casing or carrier pipe beneath highways, rail, waterways, buildings or existing utilities. Project types, diameter, length, ground conditions, required line/grade/tolerance, pit construction, casing, spoil management, groundwater, settlement limits and access determine method and bid. Some operators combine auger boring with HDD or other trenchless technologies; keep method-specific project types separate.

Work is normally contracted per project or bid item, often as a subcontract with site-specific plans/specifications, permits, utility coordination and insurance. A small owner-led crew may work one rig at a time; larger firms may employ multiple drilling crews, welders, pit crews, survey/engineering and project controls. Work can be emergency/utility repair, but recurring service routes are not the baseline.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Jobs are project-based and equipment mobilization heavy. Match trained operators, locating/tracking or survey staff as method requires, rig/tooling, pit crew and support equipment. Utility locates, permits and closure windows are hard dependencies. Weather, ground/water, obstruction, utility conflict, equipment breakdown, spoil limitations, inspection and owner work windows can stop progress. Maintain reason, stop-work notification, measured installed length, equipment hours, remobilization and revised plan. Do not conflate planned production with accepted installed quantity.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Likely high-level lifecycle: design/spec and geotechnical review → utility ticket and field verification → approved pits/traffic controls → rig setup and alignment checks → boring/casing advance with monitoring → install/connection → inspection and as-built → pit backfill/site restoration → owner acceptance. Method-specific steps need an auger-boring technical source before being treated as defaults. OSHA HDD bulletin recommends use of state one-call utility locating and physical verification/potholing where possible for HDD; apply only as safety evidence for adjacent underground drilling and consult local law for each excavation method.

Hazards may include utility strikes, pit collapse, confined spaces, rotating machinery, lifting/rigging, traffic, groundwater and spoil/environmental release. OSHA excavation, machine guarding, lockout and other rules may apply based on operation. Noncompletion or rework can follow utility conflict, obstruction, line/grade deviation, ground loss/settlement, casing damage, pit issue, permit/closure loss or equipment failure. Capture stop-work decision, incident/near miss, location, readings, owner/engineer direction, repair and as-built deviation. CRM checklists cannot certify engineering acceptability or replace competent-person assessments.

Site data and utility information may be sensitive; portal permissions should follow contract. ## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Possible equipment includes auger boring machine, cutting head, casing, jacks, pit support, crane, welding equipment, excavator, pumps, survey/monitoring devices and spoil handling. Materials include casing, pipe, tooling/teeth, lubricant and restoration materials. Rig and tooling maintenance, supplier lead time, wear and mobilization cost matter; inventory policy varies by contractor. Roles may include superintendent, rig operator, laborer, pit/shaft crew, welder, surveyor, safety/competent person and subcontracted engineer. Credential, operator training, union and licensing needs vary by state, contract and equipment.
