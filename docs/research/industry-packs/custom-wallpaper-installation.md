## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Wallcovering installation is a measured, surface-preparation and pattern-alignment craft. Wallpaper type changes paste, booking, trimming, overlap, removal, and cure instructions. Wall condition, old-paper removal, primer, pattern repeat/match, lot/run, wall height, room contents, specialty materials, and customer-supplied product affect materials, labor and quote. Installers emphasize written scope, itemized materials/services, warranty and change orders; a specific manufacturer's instructions vary by product.

**Inference:** CRM intake should capture room-by-room wall dimensions/photos, substrate and existing covering, material and lot, pattern repeat, client/designer proof, prep/removal scope, site readiness, and the proposed install sequence. A lead should move to site review when walls, pattern, material or room quantity make remote pricing unreliable. Existing estimates, project scheduling, customer updates, job checklists, photos and invoices should support this workflow; no core gap is established.

**Uncertain:** “Custom” may mean applying a custom-printed mural, installing premium/specialty paper, or bespoke wallcovering design plus install. Research focuses on installation, not wallpaper manufacturing or interior design. Ask what is in scope before configuring services.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include homeowners, interior designers, decorators, builders, hotels, restaurants, offices and property managers. Jobs range from one accent wall to full rooms, murals, corridors or multi-unit commercial work. Lead sources include designer/showroom referral, repeat contractor, search, and direct homeowner inquiry. This is mainly project work; repeat jobs come from trade referrals, property portfolios and remodel pipelines rather than routine service plans.

Triggers include room renovation, new construction, feature wall, hospitality refresh, branded commercial interiors, or repair/replacement. Product selection may be by homeowner/designer; material purchase may be installer-supplied or client-supplied. Specialized, fragile, grasscloth, metallic, mural or high-repeat designs differ from basic peel-and-stick. Self-booking should not guarantee price/date when wall prep, material quantity or pattern match is unknown.

## Terminology and durable records

- **Site:** customer property and room/wall; dimensions, ceiling height, substrate/texture, condition, moisture, existing paper/adhesive, furniture, fixtures, power/outlet access, ventilation, parking/loading and occupancy window.
- **Persistent asset:** installed wallcovering by room, brand/design/SKU/lot, install date, adhesive/primer, seams/repairs and care/warranty notes. This is useful for follow-on work but is not needed for every one-time residential job.
- **Job inputs:** wall measurements and elevations, pattern repeat/match, roll width/length/lot, proof approval, quantity/waste estimate, surface test, prep/removal/skim/prime scope, material readiness, room protection, installer and completion images.
- Customer access details may be sensitive. Contract, color/pattern approvals and photos are customer-visible; internal condition/prep notes can be technician-facing.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Possible services: consultation/measure; wallpaper installation; mural/feature wall; removal; wall preparation, sizing/priming or lining paper; custom panel layout; repair/reseam; commercial rollout; and sometimes material procurement. Separate design/print/fabrication, wall repair, painting, mold remediation, furniture moving, and electrical fixture removal unless included by contract.

Intake asks address, room count/use, photos of each wall/corners, approximate dimensions/height, substrate, old covering/removal, selected product/SKU/lot, pattern repeat, access/furniture, desired dates and who supplies material. On-site measurement and surface inspection establish quantity/prep before a firm quote. Bids should specify preparation, installation, cleanup, products, warranty, payment/cancellation terms and change-order process. Wallcovering Association guidance specifically emphasizes comprehensive bids, material/brand/quantity details and written changes. Quote by room/wall/area, material complexity, height/access, removal/prep, pattern/waste, furniture protection and travel; exact models are operator-dependent.

Schedule around material arrival, room readiness, prep/dry time, other trades and multi-room sequence. Reserve enough rolls from a matching lot where color uniformity matters. Reschedule if product is damaged/short, walls are wet/uncured/unsound, or another trade blocks access. A request form can gather photos and selected pattern, then hand off to a human quote.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Mostly one-off project appointments with stages for survey, material procurement, prep, install and completion; commercial designers/builders can create repeat assignments. Capacity depends on skill, room size, material, wall condition, drying time and schedule. A small owner-operator may schedule one room at a time; a team may coordinate prep and hanging crews across multi-room sites. Route planning is secondary. Track start delays due to missing material, incompatible lot, unfinished paint, wet wall, blocked room or change in pattern/design.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Review contract, material SKU/lot, approved proof/pattern, room photos and access.
2. Verify wall state, dimensions, moisture, surface readiness, pattern repeat, room protection and product quantity.
3. Remove old covering or prepare/prime only as contracted; record hidden damage and obtain change approval.
4. Cut/activate/book/hang using that product’s instructions; align pattern and seams, trim and clean as appropriate.
5. Inspect seams, alignment, finish and visible defects; photograph accepted rooms and note remaining cure/temperature/care instructions.
6. Obtain customer/designer acceptance, close out changes and invoice.

York instructions explain that product-specific techniques differ, recommend clean/dry/smooth surfaces, and describe pattern match and booking; WIA documents professional training and trade support. Do not encode one prep or paste procedure for every wallcovering. Common complaint evidence is bubbles, seams, wall defects, poor prep, mismatch and disputes about quote cost; anonymous user reports do not establish frequency or fault. Noncompletion reasons include unsuitable wall, wet/uncured paint, insufficient/damaged paper, pattern/lot discrepancy, scope change, no access, or another trade incomplete.

## Customer communication and self-service

Provide photo/measurement request, material/order confirmation, proof approval, prep instructions, room-clearance reminder, appointment window, delay/change-order notice, completion photos, care/drying guidance and invoice. A portal can hold the approved pattern and proposal so substitutions are traceable. Avoid automatically assuring a seamless result or fixed date before confirming material/lot and wall readiness. Customer-facing photos and internal defect notes should be distinguished.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Tools/materials include laser/level, measuring tools, pasting table, sharp blades, smoother/brush, seam roller, paste, primer/sealer, drop cloths and ladders. Specialty tools and adhesive vary by product. Track roll SKU, lot, quantity, extra/waste, supplier and arrival when material is purchased for a job; small operators may buy to order. Installers need product knowledge and careful finish work. Helpers may support room prep, furniture protection and tall walls. Crew assignment should account for height/access and specialty material; payroll/time tracking need not be default for owner-only operators.

Potential measures: quote-to-booking, survey-to-start time, labor/material variance, work completed per wall/room, first-pass acceptance, callback/rework by cause, material waste/shortage, delay reason, and gross margin by product or service type.
