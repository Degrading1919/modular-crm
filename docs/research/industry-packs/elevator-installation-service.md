## Executive summary

The candidate combines new elevator/escalator installation, modernization, repair, preventive maintenance, inspection support and emergency callback/entrapment work. These have distinct commercial loops: installation/modernization are long projects with equipment, drawings, construction coordination and turnover; maintenance attaches recurring service and inspection records to each lift; emergency calls require rapid response and access/escalation. ASME A17.1/CSA B44 is the North American safety-code framework for design, construction, installation, operation, inspection, testing, maintenance, alteration and repair, adopted variably by authorities. NAEC offers technician certification programs; credentials and jurisdictional licensing vary.

Incumbent elevator software pages emphasize unit-level assets, maintenance agreements, statutory schedules, dispatch, callbacks and modernization; public packages range from per-user pricing to equipment-count tiers. That substantiates a vertical product category, but much of the evidence is vendor marketing and this pass found no direct elevator contractor operator account describing actual stack/WTP. Mark partial and validate with independent elevator contractors, mechanics and building owners. A pack should distinguish asset/service history from installation project records and avoid claiming code compliance or inspection authority.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include building owners, property managers, GCs, developers, hospitals, schools, municipalities and facilities teams. Projects include new passenger/freight elevators, platform/residential lifts, escalators, modernization/controls, repairs, service contracts and emergency callbacks. Elevator contractors may be manufacturers/authorized dealers, independent service firms, new installation subcontractors or specialized modernization shops. Installation projects depend on shaft readiness, power, pit/overhead, structural support, equipment delivery, GC schedule, inspections and commissioning. Maintenance plans vary by unit, service frequency, contract coverage, response times, parts exclusions and jurisdiction.

Large firms run dispatch centers, mechanics, inspectors, construction/modernization PMs and parts depots; regional companies may have a few mechanics and a tight maintenance territory. The same firm may manage hundreds of elevator assets and a few concurrent installations, requiring separate recurring and project workflow.

## Terminology and durable records

Track customer/building/site, elevator/escalator/lift unit, equipment ID, manufacturer/model/serial, controller, rated capacity/speed, floor/stop count, installation/modernization project, maintenance control program, contract, visit/test, callback, outage, entrapment, inspection/certificate, deficiency, parts, technician, route and work order. Unit-level asset identity and installed component history are durable. Construction projects also need drawings, submittals, shaft/installation phase, equipment delivery, tests, acceptance and warranty. Access to keys, building controls and emergency contacts is sensitive.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

New-install intake requires building and shaft details, plans/specs, unit quantity/type, capacity, floors/stops, finishes, equipment vendor, construction schedule, access and bidding terms. Maintenance intake requires each unit ID, owner, current service contract/MCP, last service, inspection dates, known deficiencies and access. Callback intake captures symptom, outage/entrapment, safety/access status and emergency contact; it must route according to provider policy and local code rather than quote automatically.

Install quotes are bid/project estimates covering equipment, labor, freight, hoisting, controls, shaft coordination, testing, permits/inspection, bonds, schedule and exclusions. Maintenance contracts price units, included visits, response/callback and parts terms; repair quote depends on diagnosis/part availability. Preserve equipment, proposals, contract, change orders, inspection and warranty. Schedule commissioning/tests and unit-specific recurring visits separately.

## Pricing and commercial model

Revenue may include equipment/install contract, modernization project, maintenance agreement, repair/time-and-material and emergency work. Price depends on unit count/type, building height, controller/equipment, modernization scope, access, labor/union, contract coverage, parts, response guarantee, testing and jurisdiction. Vendor software pages in 2026 advertise $7/user/month (EyeOnTask), $50/user/month (Joblogic) and equipment/seat-tier plans ($149–$799 monthly on vertOS); these are list claims, not verified purchasing data. No independent contractor WTP was found. Larger firms may buy specialized software; small independents may rely on legacy platforms, spreadsheets or shared calendars. Validate before recommending any price point.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Maintenance is recurring per unit/contract and inspection cycle; callbacks are priority events and may occur outside normal hours. Dispatch mechanics by certification/skill, geography, contract SLA, parts, building access and emergency status. Installation/modernization is a multi-month project with engineering, equipment, construction and acceptance dependencies; do not model as ordinary service visits. Weather matters less than site readiness, trade coordination, equipment and permit/inspection windows. Deferred work and failed inspections require unit/site status, owner notice and rescheduling.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Install: shop drawing/equipment approval → shaft readiness and delivery → install rails/doors/machine/controls → coordination with electrical/fire/GC → tests and commissioning → authority inspection → acceptance/turnover and warranty. Maintenance: identify unit and contract → isolate/access → perform scheduled maintenance/checks per code/contract → report deficiencies → owner/inspector action → close work record. Callback: confirm people safe and dispatcher/escalation → qualified mechanic response → secure equipment/release only under procedure → diagnose, repair or leave out of service → report owner and follow-up. These are high-level, not procedure steps.

ASME code and local authority requirements govern installations/maintenance/inspection. NAEC certification programs include CET/CAT/QEI pathways; they do not replace local licensing. OSHA construction and electrical/lockout/fall hazards apply by task. Record unit, date, mechanic, scope, test/deficiency, parts, photos, customer notice, inspection and return plan. Never represent CRM fields as an inspection certificate or safety determination. Rework/outage reasons include parts delay, failed test, access, unsafe equipment, construction incomplete, repeat callback or authority rejection.

## Customer communication and self-service

Building owners need maintenance status per unit, outages, callback ETA, inspection deficiencies, contract coverage, quotes, access needs, downtime and closeout documents. Public-facing building outage messaging may require fast owner approval. Work order portals and service dashboards are useful, while emergency safety and inspection communications remain human controlled. Elevators are high-consequence assets; customer self-service cannot bypass technician judgment.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Parts include doors/operators, controllers, relays, sensors, cables, rails, motors, safety components and consumables; compatibility, model and obsolescence matter. Track parts by unit, truck/warehouse, supplier and lead time. Installation needs hoisting/rigging and specialized tools. Workforce includes mechanics, apprentices/helpers, constructors, troubleshooters, inspectors, PMs and dispatchers; qualification, union contracts, service territories and on-call rotation vary. Time tracking and payroll need trade rules and premium/on-call time.

## Reporting and operating measures

Candidate measures supported by incumbent framing include maintenance visit completion by unit, callback response/first-time fix, outage duration, inspection deficiencies/closure, contract profitability, part delay, mechanic utilization, installation milestone and project margin. Definitions should align with contract, AHJ and company policy. Do not report statutory compliance based solely on CRM task closure.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Vertical offerings such as FIELDBOSS, vertOS, EyeOnTask and Joblogic market asset/unit history, preventive maintenance, contracts, callbacks, compliance tracking, parts and dispatch. Their pages indicate specialized incumbents and packaging, but not market share or user satisfaction. A contractor may also use accounting/ERP, CAD/equipment vendor systems and customer portals. No independent elevator contractor complaint or actual WTP statement was found here; public prices are vendor list prices with adoption claims unverified. Research status partial reflects this gap.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask installation vs service revenue, active units, per-unit contracts, callback/on-call policy, parts management, local inspection data, modernization work and customer/authority portals. Prototype separate project installation and asset-based recurring service paths. Avoid compliance certification claims and validate maintenance-control/inspection record requirements with operators and authorities before implementing.
