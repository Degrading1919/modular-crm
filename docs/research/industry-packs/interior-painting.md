## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Interior painters sell room, whole-home and finish projects to occupied or vacant residences, rental-property owners/managers and remodelers. The distinctive operating problem is coordinating room-level scope, protection, access, occupants and possessions while allowing preparation and coats to dry/recoat. Contractor preparation guidance divides customer responsibilities from crew protection, while EPA indoor-air guidance advises checking/assuming lead risk before preparation in pre-1978 homes and selecting interior-suitable paint. Jobber markets estimates with prep/coats, colors, approvals, crew details, photos and project history.

**Inference:** Interior work merits its own workflow even when one company also paints exteriors: room access, furniture/valuable protection, ventilation/odor preferences, pets/children, occupied-home sequencing, finish lighting and color approval are central. The customer’s readiness checklist and the contractor’s protection/prep commitments should be recorded separately so they do not create disputed assumptions. There is no evidence for a universal moving, ventilation, VOC, payment or warranty policy.

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners buy one-room refreshes, whole-home repaints, trim/doors, cabinets or specialty finish work; landlords/property managers buy turnover refreshes; remodelers/builders may subcontract new or renovated interiors. The owner may be present or absent; actual color approval authority can be a homeowner, tenant, designer, property manager or GC. Work is typically quoted per project and may be staged room by room; recurring work is usually future repaint or property turnover rather than routine visits. Move-in, renovation, sale, damage, color change and tenant turnover are plausible triggers, but source prevalence data is absent. Solo painters and teams differ in how much furniture moving, setup and daily reset they can perform.

## Terminology and durable records

Terms include property, room/area, wall/ceiling/trim/door/cabinet, substrate, patch/repair, masking/protection, primer, finish coat, color, sheen, sample/mock-up, punch list and walkthrough. Property record: address, contact/decision maker, occupancy, access/parking, alarm/pets/children sensitivities, available utilities, preferred work hours, room use, ventilation constraints and prior finish/product/color history. Quote/job scope stores room and surface quantities, wall heights/condition, included repairs, colors/sheens, coating system, protection standard, furniture movement, exclusions and customer prep tasks. Prior color formula/product and dated touch-up history can persist; every wall/defect should not become a permanent asset. Avoid storing health claims; customer preference/constraint notes can be limited, permissioned and customer-visible only as appropriate.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Common scope: walls, ceilings, trim/baseboards, doors, cabinets, closets, stairwells, drywall patching, wallpaper removal and decorative finishes. Add-ons vary and may require separate skills/quotes. Intake should ask rooms and exact surfaces, dimensions/ceiling height, current finish/condition, repairs or stains, color and sheen decisions, number of colors, product preference, access/furniture/fragile items, occupancy and pets/children, work hours, ventilation or odor sensitivity, desired completion/deadline, photos, construction year and any pre-1978 lead-safe-work trigger. Quote line items need explicit surface coverage, prep level, coats/primer, products, furniture moving, protection, clean-up and exclusions. PCA guidance says setup, prep and cleanup should be separately accounted for from non-prep production rates. Walkthrough is useful when room condition, furniture, repairs or boundaries are unclear. Approve colors/sample panels and scope before mobilization; customer site request should hand unusual damage, suspected lead, large repair or sensitive occupancy to a person.

## Pricing and commercial model

Evidence-backed drivers: painted surface area and height; room count; trim/door/cabinet detail; repairs, stain blocking, sanding and patching; masking/furniture moving; occupied-home logistics; number of colors and transitions; coating grade/finish, primer and coats; labor access and schedule; travel and material cost. Jobber describes scope line items and optional upgrades; PCA calls out prep/setup/cleanup and company-specific production tracking. Per-room, measured, labor/material, or fixed quote patterns are possible; this evidence does not establish prevalence. Deposits, staged billing, cancellation, extra repair authorization, color change and punch/touch-up warranty are tenant-defined.

**Software WTP:** No operator statement of actual software payment or verified plan purchase was found. PaintScout lists painting-sales software at $119/month monthly or $99/month annual, and Operations add-on $99/$79, with $20/user monthly seats; accessed 2026-09-24. These are advertised USD list prices and show a category price point only, not interior painters’ WTP. Jobber promotes painting functionality but the consulted feature page does not provide a fixed price.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

The project is a one-time multi-room sequence more often than a recurring route, with rebooking through future repaint/turnover. Schedule by room access, occupied/vacant status, furniture clearing and reset, crew size, product drying/recoat times, ventilation and customer household needs. A job may permit painting isolated rooms and maintaining safe pathways, or require vacant-home access; do not assume either. Exact arrival windows, multiple days, sample/signoff hold points, daily secure-up, reschedule and return/punch visit should be supported. Neighbor-based routing is usually secondary to project/crew capacity.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Suggested visit: confirm room/surfaces/colors/decision maker and readiness; photograph existing damage if relevant; agree on furniture/fragile-item boundaries; move or cover as quoted; protect floors, fixtures, belongings and adjacent rooms; remove/cover plates as agreed; clean/scrape/sand/fill/caulk; isolate stains/prime; confirm interior-rated product, tint, sheen and lot; ventilate consistent with label/SDS and site conditions; apply coats in agreed sequence and observe product drying/recoat; restore room safely; inspect in representative lighting and record touch-ups/exclusions; customer walkthrough and acceptance; remove masking/debris; record leftover material/formula and invoice. Rework may involve missed edges, holidays, lap marks, visible patch/flash, color mismatch, overspray/spill, pre-existing substrate defects or damage after completion; log responsibility and approved corrective scope. EPA suggests checking a surface is lead-free or assuming existing paint may contain lead before prep; for US pre-1978 homes it recommends lead-safe certified renovators. RRP applicability depends on paid activity and exceptions; state/tribal programs may differ. Product labels/SDS guide ventilation, drying, cleanup and disposal; no one CRM checklist replaces them.

## Customer communication and self-service

Provide room/scope consultation, color/sample and finish approvals, written protection/furniture responsibility, arrival/reminder, daily access plan, delay/recoat/room availability updates, change authorization, walkthrough, drying/care guidance, invoice and callback process. Intake portal can gather room photos and preferred colors and offer quote appointment; it should not imply the customer must move heavy furniture or that the contractor will do so unless quote specifies it. Automated customer notifications should avoid entering private spaces/accessing alarms or making an unapproved color, damage, VOC/health, completion or warranty claim.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Typical supplies include interior-rated paints, primers/stain blockers, color samples, brushes, rollers, trays, sprayers, ladders, sanders, scrapers, fillers/caulk, masking film/tape, drop cloths, vacuums, PPE and ventilation equipment as needed. Product/primer/finish/lot and color formula tied to job can aid touch-ups. Inventory defaults should be optional because many firms procure job-by-job and product selection varies. Crew can include estimator, foreperson and painters; furniture handling, finish skill, language and customer presence change staffing. Capture crew/time only if a company tracks labor or job costs; do not assume payroll method or crew certifications.

## Reporting and operating measures

Vendor features support job costs, estimates, photos, crew/checklist and color history. Inferred measures: estimate conversion by room/job type/lead; prep labor and actual vs estimated labor/material; planned vs completed rooms/day; rework and punch rate; time to color approval; schedule extension by cause; job margin; customer rating/referral; open callbacks and aged invoice. There is no evidence these are industry-standard KPI definitions.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Millman Painting’s homeowner checklist describes small-item/customer preparation versus contractor handling of heavy furniture/protection and who supplies leftover paint; this is one operator’s policy. Artisticrat Painting’s checklist likewise demonstrates company-specific room preparation, alarm and furniture expectations. Reddit homeowner/contractor threads discuss moving furniture and chargeable prep, but are anonymous and mixed-quality. Jobber promotes scope lines for prep/coats, color swatches, estimate approvals, team scheduling, job notes, photos, checklist/time, payment and color history. Benjamin Moore provides product-specific documentation rather than business management. No direct evidence from this source set compares software complaints among interior specialists or reveals actual software plan purchases. Advertised plan price is not equivalent to operator WTP.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources support protection, customer readiness, explicit scope, manufacturer-specific product handling and lead-aware prep. They differ on who moves furniture, whether it is included, and how customers prepare. A company checklist describes its own contract; it does not define trade-wide practice. US EPA advice and RRP requirements have a defined jurisdiction and scope. Confidence is **high** for the need to coordinate room access/protection/approval, **medium** for typical quote inputs and feature fit, and **low** for service prevalence, pricing method shares, common warranties, and software WTP. Open questions: what proportion of firms paint occupied versus vacant homes, what specific color/product history operators retain, how local rules affect ventilation/disposal, and whether there is a widely shared touch-up SLA.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype room/surface quoting with a clear protection/prep matrix; capture occupancy, customer-ready actions, access, color/finish approval, products and multi-day room progress; preserve before/after photos and a customer walkthrough/punch record. Ask which surfaces/services, who moves furniture, occupied/vacant mix, household/access constraints, color approval owner, lead-sensitive work, crew count and job-cost needs. Keep customer/crew responsibility, prep threshold, ventilation plan, color, coating system, room sequence, payment/cancellation and callback terms editable. Route exterior jobs into the separate exterior workflow. Treat demographics of demand, software adoption and WTP as unanswered research questions.
