## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Exterior repainting is a quoted property-maintenance project involving exterior walls/siding, trim, doors, decks or other substrates. Scope and price depend on condition/prep, material and height/access, not just building size. Job work is sequenced around cleaning/drying, scraping/sanding/repair, masking/landscape protection, primer/coats, cleanup and inspection; weather and substrate temperature can delay application or curing. Manufacturer guidance says use the selected product’s TDS, account for surface temperature/humidity/dew point and avoid rain; EPA RRP can govern paid disturbance of paint on pre-1978 homes/child-occupied facilities and has exterior-containment requirements.

**Inference:** Exterior painting has a distinct schedule and compliance branch from interior painting: elevation/access, landscaping/neighbor protection, moisture, wind, sunlight, rain/dew, seasonal work windows and lead-safe debris containment must be represented per job. Weather forecasts can inform planning but cannot guarantee substrate conditions or coating cure. The pack should record applicable method and thresholds from the selected coating label/TDS and regulated-work determination, not set generic temperatures, wind limits, or a one-size-fits-all guarantee.

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners buy full-house or selective elevation/trim repaint, doors/shutters, porch/deck/fence staining and other exposed-surface finishes. Landlords/property managers commission portfolio upkeep; remodelers/builders may request exterior finishing. The purchase trigger may be fading/peeling, moisture/wood deterioration, sale/move, renovation or scheduled upkeep; these are common-sense candidates, not market incidence data. Project demand and workable days are seasonal in many climates, with local variation (mild/wet/hot climates differ). Owner-operator and crew firms differ in access equipment, production, lead-safe capability, travel and weather rescheduling. Contracts can be homeowner fixed-scope work, maintenance cycles or builder bid; source evidence does not establish share by segment.

Job-specific record: measured elevations/linear trim, included surfaces/repairs, access method, surface prep, selected coating/system/color, weather measurements/forecast notes, lead determination/containment, crew/qualification, photos, exclusions and recoat/cure hold. Store product/color and previous project history for future maintenance; surface measurements and observed damage remain dated project observations, not permanent certified property facts. Lead, fall-protection and environmental records should be access-controlled and jurisdiction-aware.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Scope types include siding/body, trim/fascia/soffit, doors/windows/shutters, porch/deck/fence stain, masonry, specialty coatings and prep/repair. Do not assume a painter provides carpentry, rot replacement, pressure washing, lead abatement or roof work; itemize or refer where relevant. Intake: address and construction age; building height/stories, substrate and square/linear measurements; peeling/chalking/mildew/moisture/failed coating; photos and site visit; surfaces included/excluded; existing coating/lead history; access/trees/grade/utilities; landscaping and adjacent-property protection; finish/color/product, number of coats and repairs; desired date/season; customer/HOA/permit/neighbor constraints; and local lead-safe capability. Price by measured areas/items and condition/prep, labor, materials, access/equipment, travel, protection and weather schedule risk. An in-person walk-around is appropriate when height, substrate, hidden damage, access, or containment cannot be safely assessed remotely. Quote should name prep and repair boundaries, coatings and assumptions, access method, protection, wash/dry requirements, weather hold, sequence, payment, and change approval. Self-service requests can collect address, photos and preferences; staff must review safety, lead and access uncertainties.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

The normal unit is a multi-day project, with maintenance/repaint reminders possible over years but no standard interval established. Plan work by workable weather window and coating-specific application/dry/recoat limits, substrate dryness, sunlight/shade, crew/equipment, surface access, property protection and homeowner availability. Hold points may be needed for wash/dry, repairs, primer and recoat. Weather event can halt application, move crews, change a committed start or cause inspection/rework. Make rescheduling and customer notice explicit, retain a manual override, and avoid promising that a weather app is sufficient to release a hold. Dispatch is usually project/site/crew and route efficiency is secondary unless many smaller jobs or maintenance accounts run concurrently.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Typical sequence: confirm written scope, access, colors/materials and customer protection plan; assess weather/substrate and site hazards; inspect/photo existing damage and adjacent property; determine applicable lead-safe obligations before disturbing old coating; establish required containment/protection and access equipment; wash/clean or prepare per substrate/coating instructions and allow dry; scrape/sand/repair/prime within scope; confirm air/surface/material temperatures, humidity, dew point and product limits; apply coating on eligible surfaces and at specified thickness/coverage; protect from rain/dew/wind as directed; respect dry/recoat/cure periods; remove containment/clean chips and debris safely; inspect each elevation and log incomplete/weather-held areas; customer walkthrough; invoice and preserve product/color/touch-up details. Record partial/no-work causes: rain/dew/temperature/humidity, wet substrate, wind/containment unsafe, access/equipment, lead review, hidden damage, customer/neighbor access, material delay or scope change. Common rework risk includes peeling, blistering, poor film formation, wrinkling, missed surfaces, overspray, coating failure or weather damage; manufacturer notes cold can extend dry time or impair film formation, heat can cause flow/leveling issues, and premature rain can bubble paint. Do not normalize a speculative warranty response: inspect, document, and agree corrective action. In the US, EPA RRP generally applies to paid work disturbing paint on pre-1978 housing/child-occupied facilities; rule defines exceptions and requires lead-safe methods/containment for covered work. State/tribal rules may differ. OSHA and state fall protection, ladder/scaffold rules, chemical exposure, waste and runoff requirements depend on method/site/jurisdiction. CRM record prompts can support evidence but cannot replace law, TDS or safety plan.

## Customer communication and self-service

Explain surfaces, prep, exclusions, access equipment, landscaping/driveway protection, lead-safe plan when applicable, weather holds, likely schedule range, color/product decisions, change pricing and inspection/callback process in writing. Notify about arrival, prep/wash drying, rain/temperature reschedule, partial work, next coat/return day and completion. A customer portal can provide photos, quote approval, color signoff, schedule status, documents, invoice and callback request. Never auto-release a weather hold from forecast alone, declare lead status from age alone, promise that paint is cured before product direction, or send unsupported guarantee language. Neighbor notifications, HOA/color approval and water use are policy/local-context dependent.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Coating/primer/stain, caulk/filler, scrapers/sanders, brushes/rollers/sprayers, ladders/scaffolds/lifts, drop cloths/masking, wash equipment, containment sheeting, ground covers, PPE, moisture/temperature instruments and vehicles may be needed by scope. Height and lead-safe work change qualifications/equipment materially. Track product/color/lot and relevant supplies per job; inventory control is conditional on crews/volume. Keep access method, equipment inspection/training/certification and crew role where business/regulation requires it; do not infer all house painters do high work or have lift/lead equipment. Company employee/subcontractor model, payroll and license requirements vary.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

PCA estimating guidance and manufacturer documentation agree that condition/prep, labor estimates and product directions matter. Manufacturer notes address weather application limits; owner/operator discussions illustrate context-specific calls but do not override label/TDS. EPA RRP coverage is tied to jurisdiction, work and building use/age and provides defined exceptions; state/tribal rules can add or administer requirements. Industry practice varies with region, season, building/surface, customer expectations and crew capability. Confidence is **high** for project scheduling around substrate/product/weather and for conditional US lead-safe workflows, **medium** for field data and software fit, and **low** for common price units, season length, complaint prevalence, WTP or market size. Open questions include local licensing, the share of painters doing high-rise/lead work, common evidence retention requirements, whether weather hold automation helps more than it harms, and which warranty/callback terms buyers expect.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype exterior quote scope by elevation/substrate and measurable items; include existing-condition photos, access/height, prep boundary, protection/containment, coating system, crew/equipment, and weather hold/release fields; record partial work, reschedule cause and callback. Ask whether the company does old homes, decks/staining, high access, lead-safe work, multiple crews, and which customer segments/regions it serves. Keep temperature, dew point, recoat/cure, containment and safety limits tied to selected product, jurisdiction and site; keep duration, guarantees, change, billing and weather-cancellation policy tenant-defined. Test with hot/humid, cold/wet and lead-sensitive examples before selecting defaults. Market demand and actual software WTP remain unvalidated.
