## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Epoxy floor coating is a measured, substrate-dependent project requiring slab evaluation/preparation, product/system selection, controlled application and cure. Countertop epoxy is a separate bench/top resurfacing or finishing workflow with different substrate geometry, masking, edge work, application environment and cure/customer-use instructions. Product documents specify different handling and temperature constraints.

**Uncertain:** The candidate joins both services with a slash but does not establish whether one company offers both or whether “counter tops” is a misspelling/nearby business concept. Existing separate profiles cover the other inventory labels; this profile deliberately considers only the combined Trades/construction entry. It is partial because the scope and seller/operator overlap cannot be resolved from independent evidence. Do not unify into one service template merely because both use epoxy resin.

## Business model and customer segments

- Floor coating buyers include homeowners (garages/basements), commercial/industrial facility owners, property managers and GCs. Countertop epoxy buyers may be homeowners, remodelers, cabinet/countertop businesses or hospitality operators; evidence for the latter market mix is limited.
- Both are usually project/quote purchases rather than recurring service. Triggers include worn/damaged concrete or desire for cleanable/decorative flooring; countertop triggers may include remodel or surface refresh (**inference**).
- A coating specialist may offer floors only; a decorative resurfacing operator may serve counters. Company size, material sourcing, licensing and whether products are installed on site or prefabricated are uncertain.

## Terminology and durable records

- Floor terms: substrate/slab, moisture vapor, concrete surface profile (CSP), diamond grinding, crack/patch repair, primer, broadcast flake/quartz, base/topcoat, pot life, recoat window, mil thickness, cure, cove base. Counter terms: substrate/top, edge, coating/resin, pigment, pour, bubble removal, masking and cure.
- **Service-location data:** floor area/zones, slab age/condition, moisture/temperature, use/traffic/chemical exposure and access; for counters, site/top dimensions, edges, sink/cooktop interfaces, substrate and occupied-room conditions.
- **Persistent customer assets:** coated floor zones/countertops, chosen product/system/color, prep and condition records, warranty/maintenance notes. Measurements and batch/application conditions are job-specific evidence.
- Safety: chemical SDS/PPE/ventilation, silica exposure during concrete prep, slip/cure-time handoff; avoid documenting an unverified health or code certification.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Floor services include inspection, prep/repair, moisture mitigation, coating system installation and recoating; counter epoxy can include surface preparation, decorative coating, refinishing and edge work. Candidate may imply one or both.
- Intake should identify surface type/use, dimensions, existing coating/contamination/cracks, moisture and environmental conditions, desired appearance/performance, occupancy/access, and photos/site visit. Counter scope needs sink/edge/fixture and removal details.
- Quote should separate measured area/surface, preparation, repairs/mitigation, material system/layers, edges/base, access, removal and optional finish. TDS-specific quantities, costs and cure windows mean no universal price formula.
- Booking needs site preparation, material availability, ambient conditions, sequenced visits/recoat windows and customer return-to-use instructions. Human review for unexpected moisture, contamination or substrate damage.

## Pricing and commercial model

- **Observed evidence:** Epoxy estimator pages model area, system layers, coverage/waste, slab prep condition and material costs; countertop product instructions likewise specify product and environment. This supports measured, system- and condition-based proposals, not a standard rate.
- Floors may have different residential/commercial systems, prep, traffic/chemical demands and job minimums. Countertops are generally measured by surface/edges and finish details (**inference; sparse independent commercial evidence**).
- Deposits, payment stages, warranty and callbacks are seller/contract specific. Public tool prices (e.g., CoatQuote promotional $9.99/month) are software list prices and do not evidence operator WTP. No direct operator WTP evidence found.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Installation is one-time project work; maintenance/recoat reminders are optional and system-specific, not assumed recurrence.
- Floor work depends on cure/recoat timing, humidity/moisture, temperature, ventilation and access restriction; countertop cure and return-to-use may constrain occupied spaces. Crew size and number of visits vary with system and prep.
- Scheduling is project sequencing, not route density. Do not apply epoxy floor curing assumptions to counters or vice versa.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Identify surface/use/system; survey and measure; inspect/document substrate and environmental conditions.
2. Confirm specification, site readiness, ventilation/PPE, protection and customer downtime expectations.
3. Prepare substrate; test/mitigate moisture as product/system requires; capture any added repairs for approval.
4. Apply materials in prescribed mix, thickness, sequence and environmental/recoat windows; record product/batch and conditions as relevant.
5. Inspect finish, document completion, advise cure/return-to-use and care, then track callback/warranty.
- **Observed evidence:** Manufacturer specs emphasize substrate preparation, ambient conditions and cure; floor contractor materials identify moisture and prep as failure risks. Advice differs by product; no generic cure duration is safe.
- Failures include delamination, bubbles, surface defects, wrong appearance, missed repairs, contamination and premature use. Customer approval of a different finish and scope changes needs human confirmation.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Before work: photos/measurements, use and appearance choices, estimate/options, surface prep and room/garage clearance instructions. During work: environmental delay and newly discovered substrate issue/change approval. After: cure/re-entry instructions, care, product/warranty documentation, photos and callback path.
- Self-service can request quotes and appearance preference but cannot diagnose moisture, condition or final suitability without evaluation. Automated “ready to use” notices must rely on actual system-specific cure guidance and recorded install conditions.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Floor work can require grinders, dust collection, moisture testing, repair materials, resin/hardener, broadcast media and PPE. Counter work uses different finishing tools, forms/masking and resin/pigments; exact equipment differs by method.
- Inventory lots, coverage, waste and shelf life matter for firms that stock products. Applicator skill and safety training vary by material and jurisdiction; do not impose a universal certification.
- Material data sheets and batch/condition records may support warranty and rework diagnosis.

## Reporting and operating measures

- **Inference:** estimate conversion, prep/repair share, square footage or top area, estimated/actual material use, crew hours, cure delay, change-order value, callback/delamination rate and margin by system can help owners. Sources do not establish a standard KPI vocabulary.
- Floor and countertop service, substrate and system should be distinct report cuts if the business actually performs both.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- Trade-specific floor estimating products expose project dimensions, system/material recipes, prep matrices, branded estimates, follow-up and payment/pipeline. This demonstrates products address those workflows but not adoption or unmet need at industry scale.
- Product documents from countertop epoxy suppliers specify temperature/application and cure constraints. Operator discussion in epoxy contractor forums repeatedly emphasizes prep/moisture, but is anecdotal and often responder expertise is not verifiable.
- Contractor sources reviewed do not establish whether floor coating firms commonly install epoxy counters, preferred CRM stack, representative service prices or complaints. Treat the paired candidate wording as unresolved.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree on measured area, coating-system/material choice, preparation and environmental conditions for floors. Countertop evidence is product-manual oriented and does not resolve installation business structure. Vendor estimating products emphasize different price formulas/features; these are product claims, not neutral benchmarks. Major gap: whether combined candidate means a single trade business, plus lack of independent countertop contractor/operator voice and prices. Profile remains partial. Floor-specific evidence should not be generalized to countertop work.
