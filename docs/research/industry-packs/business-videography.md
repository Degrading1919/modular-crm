## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Business videographers sell commissioned video to companies, agencies, nonprofits, public buyers and internal communications/HR. Work may include brand/product films, testimonials, recruiting, training, explainers, social campaigns and commercials. AICP publishes commercial production bid forms/guidelines and a sample agreement addressing budgets, approved scripts/storyboards, change costs and delivery. A project commonly moves through discovery/brief, concept/script/storyboard approval, talent/location/rights, production, post, client review and licensed delivery. Retainers occur but are not universal.

**Inference:** The product opportunity is a workspace linking brief, buyer/approvers, estimate/budget, approvals, crew/vendors, locations, rights/releases, deliverable variants, revisions, actual costs and acceptance. This project approval and usage model distinguishes business video from wedding/event coverage.

**Uncertain:** Candidate scope ranges from solo capture-only contractors to full production studios and recurring content retainers. Ask what is produced, who buys/approves, whether creative/post is included, and how output will be used.

## Business model and customer segments

Companies may buy directly or through agencies; buyers also include HR/L&D, communications, product, nonprofit, government and enterprise procurement. Marketing may approve creative while legal/brand, procurement and finance separately approve claims, releases, budget and PO. Revenue may be project fee, itemized bid, day-rate capture, post scope, retainer or pass-through expenses. Leads come from referrals, agencies, RFPs, web and existing accounts. Small shops may be solo producer-shooters using freelancers; larger teams coordinate producers/directors, camera/audio/lighting, talent, editors, animation, locations and suppliers. Campaign and quarterly/annual content can repeat; each deliverable still has its own approvals and usage.

## Terminology and durable records

Terms include **client/agency/brand**, **brief**, **creative treatment**, **script**, **storyboard**, **bid/estimate**, **production fee**, **crew/vendor**, **call sheet**, **location/talent release**, **usage/license**, **rough/fine cut**, **revision/change order**, **master/cutdown**, **acceptance** and **wrap**. Store client organization/approver roles, shoot and post sites, access/loading, power, permits, safety limits, brand references, confidential materials, delivery platforms and time zone. Staff, locations, gear, vendors, rate card and account may persist; claims, script, shot list, budget, talent/release status and assets are project snapshots. Protect confidential strategy, unreleased products, employee footage, PII, trade secrets and licensed media. Record source/license, territory, duration, channel, exclusivity, releases and who cleared supplied assets.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services include concept/script, interviews/testimonials, product/brand footage, training/internal communications, animation, campaigns/ads, capture-only, editing/color/audio, captions, aspect-ratio cutdowns and retainers. Intake should capture goal/audience, buyer/approvers, claims, intended channel and usage, deliverables/specs, deadline, budget, locations, talent, source assets, approval chain, procurement/PO and confidentiality. A discovery call, site survey or RFP may be needed; custom production is rarely safe as unconditional instant checkout. Quote may itemize prep, creative, crew, equipment, location, talent, travel, production, post, licensing and contingency/markup. AICP describes commercial cost groups and bidding guidance, but does not establish freelancer norms. Contract/PO should define deliverables, acceptance, usage, payment milestones, revisions, overages/change approval, cancellation/postponement, supplied assets and raw/archive terms.

## Pricing and commercial model

Inputs include creative/prep hours, shoot days, crew, locations, kit, talent/casting, travel, post/animation, deliverable formats/count, revisions, usage duration/territory/media/exclusivity, licensing, rush, captions/localization, insurance and procurement overhead. AICP’s commercial bid groups cost items and production fee; cost/markup/terms remain negotiated. One US producer guide publishes $3,750 capture-only to $25,000 for multi-day event work; that is a single provider's service price, not a market quote for all business video. Retainer cadence and unused-capacity terms vary. **Software WTP evidence:** HoneyBook lists Starter $29/month annual or $36 monthly, Essentials $49/$59, Premium $109/$129 (annual/monthly); StudioBinder's opened support page describes a limited free plan and paid production features but not paid list prices. These are advertised software prices, not evidence of purchase. No representative operator WTP evidence found.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Project dates depend on client approvals, talent/site availability, legal/brand review, shoot days and delivery. Content retainers create repeat planning cycles. Schedule brief, estimate/PO, script/storyboard, releases, casting, location, crew/gear, call sheet, shoot, edit review, final master and channel variants. Match crew skills/kit to production and location; reserve rentals/talent. Corporate stakeholders may delay approval or source assets; communicate impact and rescope if necessary. Routing is secondary except multi-site crews. Date changes, weather, talent issues, client delays and approved-claim changes trigger schedule/cost review.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Qualify brief/buyer; scope estimate/usage; contract/PO and payment; get approved script, claims, storyboard and assets; clear locations/talent/releases/permits; plan crew/gear/call sheet; verify access/weather/power; capture and log deviations; ingest/backup; edit and collect consolidated notes; validate captions/music/legal/brand; obtain authorized acceptance; deliver masters/variants with license metadata; reconcile costs and archive per contract. Evidence: bid, call sheet, test, production report, release, version approvals, license receipts and delivery receipt. Worksite safety, rigging/power, filming permissions, privacy, labor/union, permits and FAA drone rules are conditional/local. Changes to approved script, claims or usage can require new costs. No-show, weather, gear, access, client delay or release issue can block completion. Escalate unsafe/uncleared material, scope changes, unlicensed media, missed client deadlines and overages for human review.

## Customer communication and self-service

Communicate discovery, bid, scope/usage, agreement/PO, asset collection, script/storyboard signoff, call sheet, shoot updates, edit-review invitations, consolidated feedback, acceptance and delivery/license. A portal can expose milestones, estimate, files, approvals, PO/invoice, versions and authorized users. Named approvers/due dates reduce conflicting feedback. Automate reminders, not claims/legal clearance, scope changes, additional rounds, rights or publication decisions. Delivery should specify format, use/license, retention and raw/source conditions.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Camera/lenses, lighting/grip, audio, media/storage, monitors, teleprompter, vehicles, studio/location, rented kit, talent, makeup, art, animation and post vendors create project dependencies and costs. Track inventory/resource reservations when an operator owns or coordinates recurring kit; distinguish rental vendors and client AV from owned assets. Crew may include producer, director, DP, camera/audio, lighting/grip, PA, editor, animator, colorist, captioner, talent and location manager. Capture role/skill, call time, rate, vendor commitment, travel and actual costs. A solo interview operator may not need production-level resource tracking.

## Reporting and operating measures

AICP bid/project accounting suggests estimate vs actual costs. Candidate measures: pipeline/win rate, gross margin and variance, utilization, crew/rental commitments, approval-cycle and client-delay days, overtime, revision rounds, on-time acceptance/delivery, rights-expiration inventory, rework and repeat/retainer revenue. Enterprise work may need PO/campaign views. These are suggested, not a uniform KPI standard.

## Existing software and operator evidence

AICP templates support commercial production business practice. StudioBinder specializes in preproduction contacts, locations, shot lists, storyboards and call sheets; horizontal HoneyBook/Dubsado cover leads, contracts and finance; separate editing/review tools handle media. Small studios may combine CRM, calendar, spreadsheets, accounting, review/file transfer, editing and storage. Videographers discuss payment disputes after clients react to final cuts, recommending deposits, written scope, approvals and revision limits; another operator cautions rigid approval gates can burden actual client relationships. A 2022 small production-shop thread reports that one buyer requested an AICP form unknown to that shop's prior clients, showing buyer/scale variation. These posts illustrate risk, not its frequency. Do not infer software WTP from production spend.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

AICP supports formal bids, itemized estimates and written approvals in US commercial advertising; independent operators say their buyers do not always use these norms. Corporate stakeholders add brand/legal/procurement complexity; solo capture/edit firms may need simple CRM/calendar/invoice. **High confidence:** scope, budget, approvers, production/post stages and usage belong on project. **Medium:** crew/cost tracking for companies and revision-friction prevalence. **Low:** industry plan prices, typical markup, payment schedule and WTP. Open questions: solo/full-service mix, licensing metadata norms, PO use by small buyers and demand for review portals.
