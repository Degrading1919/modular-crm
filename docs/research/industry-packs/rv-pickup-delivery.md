## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** RV transport operators move towable RVs and motorhomes for private owners, manufacturers, retailers, dealers, fleets and campgrounds; the unit may be delivered to an RV park, storage yard or buyer. Operator intake needs origin/destination, timing, RV type/dimensions/access, tow configuration and customer coordination. An owner-operator source emphasizes one driver, direct updates and handling from pickup to delivery; a carrier network source describes independent transport drivers. FMCSA distinguishes motor carrier, broker and related roles and defines CMV thresholds for interstate operations.

**Uncertain:** The inventory label “RV pickup/delivery” may instead mean campsite delivery of a rented/customer-owned RV, or dealer/service-center pickup and return. These have different products, custody, setup and liability; current sources do not resolve which exact business was intended. **Inference:** onboarding must ask whether the business transports RVs as cargo, delivers/rents units to sites, or collects customer RVs for another service. This profile prioritizes transport while treating the other models as alternatives requiring a separate configuration.

## Business model and customer segments

Core transport customers: RV owners moving between home/storage/campground; manufacturers/dealers relocating inventory; sellers/buyers; rental or fleet operators; campgrounds/storage yards receiving a unit. Work is local, regional or long-haul; season and purchase/move dates drive demand. Operators may be a solo driver, carrier with employed/contract drivers, or broker/dispatch company. A campsite rental delivery model instead sells delivery, leveling/setup and removal, often tied to a rental reservation; this should be a distinct profile/configuration. Dealer or repair-shop pickup/return is yet another included-in-service logistics workflow, not equivalent to for-hire long-distance transport.

## Terminology and durable records

Persistent asset: RV class (motorhome, travel trailer, fifth wheel), year/make/model, VIN/serial, length/height/width/weight and tow/hitch/brake/condition details. Job records: owner/authorized contact, origin/destination/site contact, access and site restrictions, pickup/delivery windows, route constraints, carrier/driver, power unit/trailer, insurance/authority checks, keys/documents, fuel/odometer, exterior/interior pre/post condition, accessories and personal property, photos, bill of lading/condition report, signatures and delivery acceptance. A customer/site address and RV asset are separate; customer belongings and route conditions are job-specific. Record high-value asset custody and disclosed limitations carefully.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Primary service: quote, dispatch and transport RV from defined pickup to delivery, potentially door-to-door or dealer/site-yard. Intake: type/model/VIN; dimensions/weight; towability and hitch/brake system; operable or special handling; pickup/drop-off access, surface, height/width limits, keys/authorization; distance and dates; condition/photo survey; owner/receiver; insurance/claim needs. Operator may use a dedicated tow vehicle or RV’s own motorized chassis; fifth-wheel/travel trailer towaway requires compatible equipment and qualified driver. Quote is route/distance, RV type/size, dates/urgency, deadhead/repositioning, permit/escort/route constraints, toll/fuel and service level; site survey or dispatch confirmation for access/oversize. Customer signature/condition acceptance and payment/deposit terms vary.

Alternate models to disambiguate: campsite rental delivery generally requires reservation/customer dates, campground confirmation, site fit, leveling/setup and pickup window; dealer/service pickup-return needs repair order and keys/custody. Do not configure one quote form to book all three without a business-type answer.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service pricing evidence:** The reviewed transport operators request a quote using pickup/delivery/RV details rather than publishing a universal tariff. Spot to Spot advertises free no-obligation quote, nationwide service, owner-operated delivery and direct communication ([operator](https://spottospotrvtransport.com/), accessed 2026-09-24); another dispatcher describes RV dimensions, access, equipment fit, timing and communication as quote/dispatch inputs ([operator/broker](https://dispatchautotransport.com/rv-transport/), accessed 2026-09-24). No representative published quote amount was found. Model likely depends on loaded miles, type/size, tow/service method, access, urgency, positioning, permit/escort and route; these are quote inputs, not a claimed market formula. For campsite delivery, rental duration/setup/removal fees may apply; for shop pickup, service-center mileage/area fees may apply—separate pricebooks.

Software pricing/WTP: no direct transport operator subscription purchase or WTP quote located. Fleet/logistics management and RV-specific shop software exist, but neither list prices nor enterprise fleet subscriptions establish a small RV hauler’s willingness to pay. Avoid extrapolating from freight ticket size. Solo drivers may work by phone/text/calendar/accounting, while brokers/fleets need dispatch, carrier onboarding and track/communication; validate actual buying evidence.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Schedule by pickup/delivery windows, destination acceptance and route/driver/equipment availability. Long-haul routing is constrained by driver location, deadhead, legal/vehicle dimensions, weather/road limits, customer date flexibility and backhaul. Local campsite delivery clusters reservations/sites and requires arrival/setup windows. Record route deviation, delays, safe staging, contact escalation and proof-of-delivery. No exact-time guarantee should be automated when route, weather or legal restrictions can change. Customer may cancel or destination access may fail; record wait, redelivery and storage charges/approval according to contract.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Verify carrier role, authority/insurance and driver/equipment eligibility for jurisdiction and transport type; confirm owner/receiver consent, RV identity and dimensions, route/access and delivery acceptance. Document pre-existing condition, mileage/fuel, hitch/connection or motorhome handoff, accessories, keys and contents per custody contract; take timestamped photos at pickup and delivery; send status and obtain proof of delivery/signature; close invoice and incident report if needed. Cargo claim and damage disputes require condition evidence. For interstate transport, FMCSA registration guidance identifies self-classification roles; CMV applicability depends on interstate vehicle/combination weight and other criteria, not merely the word “RV transport.” State intrastate, towing, oversize permits, insurance and consumer contract laws also vary. Do not give automated legal eligibility determinations; require operator setup by counsel/agency as needed. Roadside failure, weather, unsafe access, overwidth/height, inoperable unit, customer absent, failed hitch/brake light or facility denial are noncompletion/incident reasons.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs quote details/terms, pickup checklist (disconnect utilities, secure loose items, remove valuables, fuel/keys/documents as required), arrival window, driver/carrier identity, delay/status, destination readiness, delivery photos/condition report, signed acceptance and receipt. Transport status notifications matter more than appointment reminders for long routes. For rental/campsite model, customer additionally needs reservation confirmation, site-fit/setup instructions and removal booking. Do not notify “delivered” until driver proof and recipient acceptance are recorded.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Assets assigned include tow vehicle, hitch/fifth wheel, brake/lighting connections, tires, safety chains, permits, straps/chocks and route/communication devices. Driver skill and licensing depends on transport vehicle and operating jurisdiction; commercial carrier compliance is model specific. Broker must vet carrier/insurance and track assignments; owner-operator directly controls equipment. No parts inventory core for the transport-only model; safety/recovery items and vehicle maintenance are fleet records. Campsite delivery carries leveling blocks, power/water/sewer setup consumables; repair pickup has separate technician/shop tooling and should not imply hauler mechanic capability.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful inferred measures: quote response/acceptance, loaded and deadhead miles, trip margin, pickup/delivery on-time rate, waiting time, route interruption, claims/damage incidents, failed access/redelivery, carrier/driver completion and customer status-update gaps. Track by transport type, lane/region, RV class and broker-vs-carrier model. Sources provide no standard benchmark.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Observed operator positioning emphasizes direct phone/text contact, owner-driver accountability, quote requests and status updates. Broker/dispatch sources match available carriers to RV dimensions, access and timing. A transport-only operator may stitch calls/SMS, calendar, maps, spreadsheets, accounting, photo storage, invoicing and payment; this is an inference, not measured usage. RVDesk/ServiceNomad concern RV service/repair, not proof of transport operator adoption. No credible transport-specific software pricing/WTP or recurring complaints was established. The “pickup/delivery” label itself remains ambiguous, so software fit depends materially on whether this is carrier dispatch, campsite rental delivery or repair pickup.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Owner-operator carrier and dispatch/broker models coexist; transportation role and regulatory obligations vary. FMCSA thresholds refer to interstate operation and combination weights, not a universal RV transporter license. **Inference:** asset condition and updates are key because custody spans pickup-to-delivery. **Uncertain:** candidate might describe an RV rental site-delivery business or dealer pickup/return instead of a for-hire carrier. Public pricing and software WTP are scarce. Confidence medium for transport workflow, low for intended meaning and product-market generalization.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

First setup question should distinguish carrier transport, broker/dispatch, campsite delivery/rental, and service-center pickup. For a carrier, prototype quote inputs, asset dimensions, route review, driver/equipment assignment, custody photos, status and signed delivery proof. Keep state/federal compliance and permit requirements outside automatic legal conclusions. Confirm commercial insurance, claims, site-fit, and actual software purchases/WTP through operator interviews before deciding default integrations or a dedicated transport core capability.
