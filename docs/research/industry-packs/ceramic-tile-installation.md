## Executive summary

Ceramic tile installers sell room-based or commercial installation of floors, walls, showers and other tiled surfaces. Work requires substrate and waterproofing review, layout, cuts, setting material, grout, movement joints, cure and finish details. A small remodeler may measure/quote bathrooms directly; a commercial tile subcontractor prices plans, assemblies, alternates, submittals and phased areas. Substrate readiness and correct installation method are central to quality and warranty disputes.

TCNA maintains ANSI A108 tile installation standards and a technical reference manual; NTCA provides contractor education and accreditation. These sources show that tile work is governed by method/material details beyond generic labor scheduling. They do not establish one universal CRM workflow or local licensing rule. A pack can offer room/surface records, estimate inputs, substrate and waterproofing checklists, photo evidence, change and punch workflows; exact method, standards edition, product and inspection conditions stay tenant/project-specific.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential customers include homeowners, remodelers and builders buying backsplashes, floors, showers, tub surrounds, fireplaces and repairs. Commercial clients include GCs, architects, developers, property managers and institutions; scopes can include lobbies, kitchens, restrooms, pools or large floor areas. Firms may supply tile and setting materials or install owner-provided products; some specialize in shower/waterproofing, stone, mosaics, restoration or commercial production. This profile excludes masonry brick/block installation and broad carpet/flooring operations except where a company also installs tile as a distinct scope.

Jobs range from single-room renovation to phased multi-floor contract. Small firms may be owner plus installer/helper; commercial outfits use estimators, foremen, crews, warehouse and submittal/quality roles. Customer selection changes, long lead decorative tile, substrate condition, waterproofing trade coordination, cure time and occupied-site constraints shape delivery.

## Terminology and durable records

Track customer/site, room/surface, substrate, assembly, tile product/size/color/lot, layout, setting material, waterproofing, movement joint, grout, transition, estimate, submittal, work phase, inspection, punch, warranty and change order. Location data includes room, floor, wet/dry area, dimensions, substrate type/condition, drains/penetrations, waterproofing tie-ins, access and occupancy. Room and surface may be durable location history; product batch, moisture/flatness measurement and installation are project records. Preserve drawings, tile layout, manufacturer instructions, technical submittals, substrate photos, waterproofing evidence, product/lot data, change approval and final walkthrough.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Ask service type, room/surface dimensions, plans/photos, tile size/material, pattern, edge/trim, substrate and moisture condition, demolition, waterproofing, drain/penetration details, access, occupied status, target date and who supplies product. A site inspection is important for remodels and wet areas; online estimates are preliminary. Quotes may be area/assembly based with additional lines for prep, leveling, demolition, waterproofing, niche/bench, trim, mosaics, stairs, cuts, large-format handling, material and grout, protection, disposal and minimum. Preserve tile layout, waste allowance, exclusions, alternates and change orders.

Workflow includes measure and design/layout review → product and method submittal → substrate readiness/flatness checks → waterproofing and inspection hold → layout/set → cure → grout/caulk/seal as specified → protection/cleanup → final inspection/punch. Coordinate plumbing fixtures, cabinets, glass, countertop, waterproofing and GC sequence. Missing tile/lot, damaged cartons, late selections or substrate remediation can move schedule. Do not promise access/cure/usage date without method/product-specific basis.

## Pricing and commercial model

Price inputs include tile type/size/finish, layout complexity, area, cuts/openings, substrate preparation, waterproofing, corners/edges, grout, setting materials, demolition, disposal, access, pattern/waste, labor productivity, travel and warranty. Direct remodels may use deposit and milestones; commercial contracts may use unit/area bids and progress billing/retainage. Changes often arise from customer selection, concealed conditions, out-of-tolerance substrate, scope revisions or material availability.

NTCA accreditation program materials refer to company quality-control measures and professional installation, but provide no current software pricing. No reliable operator WTP statement specific to ceramic tile software was found. Estimator/operator discussions in adjacent flooring trades show takeoff and custom assembly needs, but should not be represented as tile-specific adoption. Pricing/WTP remains uncertain; larger commercial contractors and small bathroom installers likely differ substantially.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most work is project-based, with repairs, callbacks and repeated commercial property renovations. Scheduling must coordinate material lot/order, substrate and waterproofing inspection, adjacent trades, room access, crew skill and cure time. Multi-day and phased work is common for larger jobs; routing is secondary to readiness/phase management. Noncompletion reasons include substrate failure, moisture, waterproofing hold, tile shortage/breakage, customer selection delay, access, plumbing/fixture delay or unsafe site. Document condition, notice, responsibility and revised sequence.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Preflight confirms tile lot, drawings/layout, substrate and method; protect adjacent finishes; prepare/flatten substrate; complete and document waterproofing where specified; pass inspection/hold; set tile; grout/finish; observe cure/protection; inspect coverage, lippage, joints, layout and punch; obtain acceptance. Evidence can include substrate flatness/moisture, membrane/flash details, product lots, photos before cover-up, setting/grout batches, movement joints, daily area completed and inspection records. ANSI/TCNA method selection and manufacturer instructions govern application when adopted by contract/specification.

Silica exposure can arise from cutting/grinding tile and substrate; OSHA's construction silica standard is relevant, with controls depending on task. Other hazards include lifting, wet saws, chemical/epoxy grout and knee/ergonomic strain. Credential and licensing requirements vary; NTCA/CTEF credentials are voluntary industry programs unless specified. Rework may include hollow/loose tile, lippage, cracks, water intrusion, failed bond, wrong layout/color, grout defect or missed movement joint. Trace affected room/assembly/material and preserve evidence before demolition.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need product/lot and layout signoff, room readiness and access instructions, schedule/cure restrictions, waterproofing/substrate findings, change approval, progress photos, inspection/punch and care/warranty directions. A portal can capture selections, photos and approvals, but technical diagnosis and change scope require human review. Commercial users may require submittal and daily-report channels.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Materials include tile by lot, thinset, grout, membranes, backer systems, leveling products, trims, spacers and sealants. Warehouses track stock and dye lot; installers may buy to project. Equipment includes wet saws, cutters, mixers, leveling tools and dust controls. Roles include estimator/designer, tile setter/helper, waterproofing lead, foreman and PM; subcontractors may handle plumbing/glass. Time/material tracking depends on company size and commercial job costing.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred measures include quote conversion, room/square-foot production by assembly, material waste/shortage, substrate remediation, schedule/cure adherence, inspection pass, callback/rework by method/installer, margin and punch aging. Segment by wet vs dry, room type, material, substrate, residential/commercial, crew and GC. Do not turn an industry-standard method into a simplistic binary compliance KPI.

## Existing software and operator evidence

NTCA/TCNA technical resources and manufacturer documents support method/spec work; estimating and drawing tools are often separate from customer/job administration. Public tile-specific software pricing evidence is limited. General contractor systems may serve commercial coordination, while small businesses use manual estimates and accounting tools. Because direct operator statements were sparse, software complaint themes and WTP are low confidence; do not infer that no purpose-built tools exist from this search.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

ANSI A108/TCNA materials are technical standards and reference resources; requirements bind through adoption, law or contract, not automatically to every job. NTCA's accreditation is not universal licensure. Direct operator evidence and price are limited, so actual software stacks and WTP remain open questions (low-to-medium confidence). Distinguish tile-specific water-management and setting workflow from general carpet/flooring installation.
