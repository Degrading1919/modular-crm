## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Training video production can turn an approved learning need or source material into learning objectives, script/storyboard, screen or camera capture, edit, subject-matter-expert review, accessible outputs, and delivery to an LMS or video host. CDC Quality Training Standards (benchmarks for CDC-developed/funded trainings) call for needs assessment, measurable objectives, accurate content, learner engagement, usability/accessibility, evaluation, assessment and follow-up. W3C WCAG guidance includes captions for prerecorded synchronized media at criterion 1.2.2. TechSmith Camtasia, a screen-recording/editing tool, sells annual subscriptions and advertises captions and SCORM export.

**Inference:** The product opportunity is project coordination and versioned approval: tie objectives/source materials, stakeholders, script/storyboard, scheduled recording, licensed assets/releases, review feedback, captions/SCORM/video files and signoff to one project. Training producers must coordinate instructional review and audience/hosting needs in addition to typical video production.

**Uncertain:** “Training video creation” could describe a freelancer making simple narrated screen capture, a corporate video crew producing live-action lessons, a course developer producing interactive e-learning, or AI/avatar content. This profile treats these as different configurable models and does not assume video alone constitutes effective training or that every client needs SCORM/LMS packaging.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include HR/L&D, compliance, safety, operations, customer education, healthcare/public-sector training, software vendors, nonprofits and consultants. Typical project is fixed-scope video or lesson series, not only a shoot day: discovery/content review, learning design/script, SME and legal/compliance approval, recording, post-production, accessibility, file/host/LMS handoff and updates. Clients may supply SMEs/locations/source files; producer may supply instructional designer, producer, scriptwriter, camera/audio crew, editor, animator, captioner, translator and LMS specialist. Repeat work may be a series, annual compliance refresh or version updates when policies/products change. Simple screen-recording and enterprise multi-module courses differ in review chain, learner tracking and documentation.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Schedule discovery and approvals before reserving production days; track version of source content and client approver deadlines. Production windows depend on SME availability, locations, talent, room noise, access/security, equipment and internal review chain. Edit, review, caption/translation, LMS testing and approval follow capture; plan time for substantive factual changes separately from included stylistic revisions. Recurrence is series/module work and maintenance after policy/product changes, not service-route recurrence. Late SME feedback, source changes, new stakeholder, script drift, location cancellation, reshoot, asset licensing delay or LMS specifications can move delivery. Reserve retake windows and content-validity owner for high-risk training.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm learning need/audience/objectives and source owner; agree script/storyboard and reviewers; clear location, on-screen data, participants, music/graphics, releases and rights; schedule SME/presenter/crew; test location/screen software, sound/light, accessibility needs and backups; record/capture; edit and version; conduct factual/SME, client, accessibility and technical review as contracted; correct feedback in versioned passes; generate captions/transcript/alternate outputs and interactions if in scope; test LMS/video encoding, tracking/SCORM package and links; obtain final approval; deliver master, requested source files, transcript/captions, license summary and archive/deletion record. A training needs assessment is not necessarily included in a narrow filming contract. CDC QTS is an authoritative standard for CDC work, not a universal legal mandate; W3C WCAG technical guidance may inform accessibility but applicability depends on the client's obligations. Noncompletion/rework may arise from changed/incorrect source policy, unapproved subject, missing release, inaccessible media, wrong LMS export, inaccurate captions, screen-data leak, requested extra revision or rejected content. Keep quality and legal/compliance signoff with client/qualified reviewers; automated transcripts or AI-generated material require factual/human review.

## Customer communication and self-service

The client needs clear scope, review responsibilities, script approval, capture date, version and feedback deadline, accessibility/delivery formats, LMS test handoff, usage rights, renewal/update trigger and secure access. A review portal can collect time-coded notes and approval by named SME/legal/brand owners; feedback must identify the exact version and stage. Notifications should distinguish factual approval from creative signoff and final LMS acceptance. Don't mark content valid indefinitely: the client needs a content owner and review date. Avoid publishing unapproved employee/customer likeness, confidential screen capture, or training materials into public links.

Crew roles vary from solo screen-recording editor to instructional designer, producer, SME, presenter, director, camera/audio, editor, animator, voice talent, captioner/translator and LMS tech. Music/stock, actor/talent, studio, translation and hosting vendors may require separate agreements.

## Reporting and operating measures

Candidate measures: discovery-to-book time, script approval cycle, approval latency by stakeholder, first-pass factual acceptance, revision rounds, reshoot/rework hours, production/edit hours per finished minute/module, caption/translation corrections, on-time delivery, LMS package QA failures, content expiry/refresh age, hosting usage and project margin. Learning completion, assessment or behavior impact belongs to the client/LMS unless explicitly instrumented and contracted; view counts alone do not prove learning. CDC guidance supports needs assessment and evaluation, but does not prescribe producer CRM KPIs.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Typical stack may combine CRM/proposals, document/script collaboration, calendar, camera/screen recorder, NLE/editor, audio cleanup, review/versioning, captions, cloud storage, e-signature, accounting/payment, LMS and analytics. ## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

CDC provides a quality framework for its own funded/developed trainings, while video vendors expose particular authoring/output features; neither establishes every training-production engagement needs an instructional designer or SCORM. W3C accessibility guidance gives technical criteria, but legal duties depend on use/jurisdiction/client. Videographers in Reddit threads disagree about whether raw footage should be included and whether the client or producer retains rights; these require negotiated scope rather than a pack default. **High confidence:** source accuracy, reviewer/approval roles, versioning, intended format and ownership/use should be clarified. **Medium:** review workflow, captions and LMS technical QA matter for many, but not all, training deliverables. **Low:** standard pricing, software stack and willingness to pay. Confirm content validity owner and update cadence in discovery.
