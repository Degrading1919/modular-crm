## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** This label can represent at least two workflows: delivery/assembly/secure anchoring of a purchased wall bed, or a made-to-measure Murphy bed integrated with storage/closets/office cabinetry. NYC apartments add tight measurements, wall/substrate variation, elevator/loading rules, co-op/condo scheduling and limited workspace. Custom firms consult, measure, design/render, select finishes, manufacture/order, schedule and install; independent installers may offer a short appointment to assemble a catalog model. These paths should remain selectable and the NYC limitation retained.

The pack opportunity is accurate room/wall/access records, product/drawing and anchoring details, delivery coordination, safe operation check and punch/warranty. CRM can organize evidence but cannot certify wall structure or product safety. Operator software stacks and verified WTP are not documented in the sources; general contractor SaaS pricing is not treated as niche purchasing evidence.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include apartment homeowners/renters with landlord approval, designers, developers/furnishers, custom millwork shops and closet/space-planning companies. Uses include spare-room/guest beds, studio apartments, home offices, and wall beds with storage, desk, sofa, lighting or shelving. A custom shop sells design/fabrication/installation; a retailer/dealer sells an engineered kit and installation; a furniture assembler installs a customer-supplied unit. B2B multifamily fit-out may involve repeated units and building coordination. NYC specificity suggests dense apartments but does not prove all candidates operate only in NYC.

## Terminology and durable records

Track property/building/unit, room width/height/depth, ceiling/trim/baseboard, wall substrate/stud or masonry conditions, floor level, bed orientation/size, mattress and mechanism, cabinetry, finish, plan revision, hardware/anchor kit, access/elevator/COI/building rules, delivery, installer, functional test and warranty. The bed mechanism/cabinet is a persistent asset; measurements and hidden wall-anchor photos are job-specific and vital if future service/reconfiguration occurs. Avoid collecting unnecessary household or access information.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks customer-supplied vs custom system, vertical/horizontal, size/mattress, room photos/dimensions, desired cabinets/desk/lighting, wall type, building access/elevator/loading windows, finish, product model, apartment/landlord approval and existing trim/outlets. Site measure is needed for custom work and may be needed for masonry/uncertain anchors. Quote may cover consultation/design, bed mechanism, cabinet fabrication, finish, delivery, assembly/anchoring, disposal, travel, stairs/parking and building requirements. Custom quotes should approve drawings and finish before fabrication; product-only installers should confirm model and manual before booking.

Schedule: consult/measure → design approval/order → production/lead time → building access and delivery reservation → installation/anchor/test → walkthrough and warranty. Booking pages can collect photos and product specs; suitability and safe anchoring need installer review.

## Pricing and commercial model

Inputs include catalog vs bespoke, bed size/orientation, wood/material/finish, cabinetry/desk/storage, hardware, lighting, wall/substrate/reinforcement, delivery/access, removal and installation. A NYC custom provider says price depends on materials, footage, accessories and details and installation turnaround is 6+ weeks; it publishes no quote. Public business pages offer consultation but not a reliable benchmark. Software WTP: no operator purchase testimony or observed plan purchase found; do not infer spend from furniture price.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most work is one-time. Repeat revenue can come from rooms in developments, additional storage, repair or move/reinstall. NYC building access/COI, elevator reservations, street parking, delivery window, stairs and occupant availability can dominate route scheduling. Custom manufacturing lead time is distinct from installer calendar. Noncompletion includes wrong measurement/model, missing parts, unsuitable wall/anchors, blocked elevator/loading, access denial, damaged finish and customer design change. Record who owns readiness and confirm date only after access/delivery.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Review product instructions and approved layout → confirm room/wall/floor/access → protect occupied unit → unload and check parts/finish → assemble/cabinet install → anchor to appropriate structural substrate per system/manual → verify plumb/level, mechanism balance/locking, clearances and smooth operation → demonstrate safe operation → remove packaging, photos, customer sign-off and warranty. NYC independent installer advertises mounting on drywall, concrete, brick and reinforced walls; this is a vendor claim and actual anchoring must follow site/product engineering. Heavy panels, pinch/crush points, lifting and drilling hazards apply. Building permits/contractor licensing applicability depends on work value and scope; confirm NYC rules rather than presuming furniture install is regulated construction. Rework includes wrong width/height, unlevel floor, damaged shipment, missing hardware, poor anchor placement, mechanism misalignment, adjacent door conflicts or wall damage.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs design/finish options, measurement visit, model confirmation, building insurance/access prep, delivery window, room clearance, appointment, change approval, operating demonstration, care/warranty and callback scheduling. Portal can collect photos/model and approve drawings; installer still verifies dimensions, anchoring and product safety. Automated arrival notices and building access confirmations are useful; do not auto-mark unit safe without field test.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Bed mechanisms, cabinet panels, hinges/springs/pistons, anchors/fasteners, trim, lighting and finish; dollies, levels, drills and lifting aids. Custom shops need manufacturing/order status and finish samples; independent installers may bring common anchors/tools while customer or retailer supplies unit. Team ranges solo installer to designer, fabricator, delivery and installation crew. Store job-specific supplier/model and missing/damaged component record.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred: consultation-to-design approval, order lead time, access failure, first-visit install, anchor/rework callback, damage/missing-part, hours/install, punch and margin by custom/catalog and building type. No validated benchmark or common vendor KPI evidence found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Consumer forum queries show buyers struggle to find a specialist and weigh DIY kit vs contractor. These sources demonstrate distinct service channels but not CRM tools or software complaints. Treat any structural or electrical work as separately scoped and locally reviewed; validate actual software/WTP with NYC operators.
