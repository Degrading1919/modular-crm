## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Mobile/event DJs sell date-specific performance packages, sometimes adding ceremony audio, MC, lighting, photo booths, uplighting, or multiple rooms. A booking joins lead/client/hosts, venue logistics, event schedule and music preferences, contract/payment terms, assigned DJ(s), and gear availability. DJ Event Planner markets lead-to-booking CRM, contracts, planning forms, calendar/availability, staffing, equipment/package tracking, and payroll; Vibo specializes in client-facing music and timeline planning. Operator discussion distinguishes an end-to-end business system from a polished mobile planning app.

**Inference:** A compact event-service job workflow with detailed performance/run-of-show planning, structured music restrictions/must-plays, venue/load-in/power/sound-limit notes, gear assignment, backup plan, and contractor scheduling could be materially more useful than a generic event project. Solo operators have simple date capacity; multi-op firms need technician availability, role assignment, equipment/package conflicts, and payroll inputs.

**Uncertain:** Candidate scope may mean mobile wedding/private-event DJ, corporate AV/entertainment, club/residency or touring artists. Defaults should target date-booked mobile events while onboarding asks the business model. Do not assume wedding-specific ceremonies, guest requests, or MC responsibilities for every booking.

## Business model and customer segments

Clients include couples/families, schools, nonprofits, corporate organizers, event planners, and venues. Lead sources include referrals, venue/vendor relationships, marketplaces, web forms, repeat clients, and corporate procurement. Purchase triggers include a fixed date, venue, desired music/announcements, and confidence in reliable sound/lighting. Services may be DJ performance, MC/announcements, ceremony/cocktail audio, lighting, extra sound zone, photo booth, karaoke, or multi-room/multi-DJ coverage. Solo operators work a limited number of date-bound events; multi-op businesses sell more than one event at a time through hired/contract DJs and shared equipment. Club DJs, festival/touring acts, and audio production teams may have artist riders, promoters, settlement, travel, and stage plots instead of client planning apps.

## Terminology and durable records

Use **client/host**, **event/gig**, **venue/room**, **DJ/MC/operator**, **package/add-on**, **timeline/run of show**, **music requests/do-not-play**, **load-in/setup/sound check**, **performance window**, **rider**, and **strike**. Retain client/decision-maker and event contacts, exact venue address/room, date/time zone, access/loading dock/elevator hours, parking, setup location, power, noise limits/sound limiter, outdoor/weather cover, venue restrictions, planner/co-vendor contacts, performance hours, and event milestones. Persist DJ/staff qualifications, gear/vehicle inventory, and venue-specific repeat notes as appropriate. Client song requests, set list, wedding-party names, scripts, and event-specific timeline are job inputs, not assets. Music taste/preferences and contact info are personal; safety/access details and client-only notes need appropriate visibility. Track who can approve songs, schedule changes, overtime, and final event plan.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake: event type, date/date flexibility, venue/room, audience/attendance, performance hours, set-up/strike window, DJ/MC expectations, ceremony/other zones, music and announcement preferences, sound/lighting needs, indoor/outdoor, power/load-in restrictions, planner contact, and budget. Corporate/school events may need insurance, W-9/PO, sound limits, or procurement. Packages can be standardized but vary by performance duration, event type, geography, travel, gear/lighting, extra rooms, ceremony audio, photo booth, and staffing. Quote approval and signed contract plus retainer/deposit typically hold a date in the sample workflows; balance due and cancellation/reschedule terms vary by operator. Overtime should be priced and approved; do not promise or auto-extend performance. Booking may trigger client planning portal access and assignment of a particular operator/gear.

## Pricing and commercial model

Observed price inputs: date/season/day, hours and time window, distance/travel, service/package, rooms/zones, sound/lighting and equipment, setup complexity, staffing/MC, ceremony, photo booth, and client customization. Fixed packages, custom quotes, hourly overtime, and add-ons coexist. A DJ contract checklist product advises specifying performance hours, overtime, deposit, cancellation, gear, power, insurance, backup, weather, and load-in; this is template guidance, not a uniform legal rule. **Software WTP evidence:** a multi-op Reddit poster said DJ Event Planner was **$50/month** and described using it as the backbone for 47 employees and 2,000+ events/year (anecdotal, March 2024). Vibo currently lists $179 monthly or $149/month billed yearly and $20/month per additional team member; this is a vendor price, not proof of realized operator adoption. DJ Event Planner’s own pricing page is dynamic and does not expose readable static tiers in the page capture.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Private events generally book a specific date and performance window, not recurring service. Wedding bookings may be 12–18 months ahead but no reliable universal lead time found; venue/corporate contracts can recur. Calendar must prevent double-booking the business, assigned DJ, booth, speaker/lighting package, or vehicle; hold/prospect status and deposit deadline affect availability. Multi-op dispatch matches staff by location, style, availability, requested language/MC skills, credentials, and equipment. Event timeline dependencies include ceremony audio, cocktail hour, introductions, speeches, dances, dinner cues, set, and venue end time if applicable. Exact load-in, sound check, performance, overtime approval, and strike times are operational. Late client/venue changes, DJ illness, weather, travel conflicts, and venue noise restrictions need escalation and substitutions; routing is useful only for multiple same-day locations/team travel.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Review event order, client music forms, venue rules, gear manifest, and access instructions; load in; safely cable and position equipment; test power/audio/mics and sound limiter; confirm key people and cue order; perform and adapt to approved requests; seek consent before taking/posting photos; pack/inspect equipment; document start/end/overtime, damage, incident, and completion; send closeout/invoice and feedback request. Checklists should support setup tests, backups, weather protection, cable management, and venue sign-off. No single federal license applies to general mobile DJ work, but venue rules, local noise ordinances, liability coverage, worker safety, and alcohol rules (if bundled) are local/contract-dependent. Noncompletion can be date cancellation, venue/access issue, unsafe power/weather, equipment failure, client change, operator illness, or partial coverage. Recovery may require substitute DJ, replacement gear, refund/credit under contract, reschedule, or post-event complaint resolution.

## Customer communication and self-service

Automated flow can acknowledge inquiry, check date, send quote/contract/payment reminders, collect planning forms/music requests, show due dates, confirm final timeline, remind about venue details, and send invoice/review request. Vibo’s model lets clients/hosts collaborate on music and timeline by phone; DJEP has web client planning. A client can edit preferences, but changes to date, hours, gear, fees, venue, or safety constraints need human approval. Avoid promising a requested song/artist availability, accepting explicit/do-not-play conflicts, publishing client playlists, or disclosing private event info without review.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Speakers, subs, mixer/controller, laptop/media backup, microphones, stands, cables, power strips, lighting/uplights, ceremony/battery system, booth/screen, cases, vehicle/trailer, and spares may be business-owned or rented. Assign availability by event package and prevent double-booking or track repairs. Operators may use minimal mobile setup, elaborate production, or venue-provided house system. Multi-op firms assign employee/contract DJs, assistants, booth attendants, and MC duties. Store availability, event role, travel, load-in hours, time worked, and event-specific compensation; payroll/tips/commission are business-model dependent. A written gear and backup checklist is more broadly useful than detailed inventory tracking for a solo DJ.

## Reporting and operating measures

DJEP documents event counts, leads, contracts, calendar/staffing, equipment availability, billing, and payroll use. Candidate reports: leads/close rate by source, booked revenue/date, deposit/balance aging, utilization by DJ and gear, events/hour/overtime, add-on attach rate, travel, cancellation/refund, on-time setup, gear failures, ratings/referrals, and profit by package. Differentiate operator utilization from equipment utilization; no common standard KPI set was found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

DJ Event Planner is an all-in-one specialist system for DJ lead/booking, contracts, forms, client planning, calendar, staffing, equipment, billing, payroll and marketing. Vibo focuses on music/timeline client planning, playlists, collaboration, mobile UX, not a full CRM/scheduling/finance replacement according to a DJ operator in r/mobileDJ. HoneyBook, DJ Intelligence, Gigbuilder, Cue DJ, Check Cherry, Vibo, shared Google Sheets, calendars, and accounting/payment tools appear in operator discussions. A multi-op reports DJEP supporting 47 staff and 2,000+ yearly events for $50/month; a second discussion reports a mobile-friendliness gap in DJEP while praising Vibo's client app; another DJ says shared sheets are replaced by client song/timeline tools. These are individual reports and may be dated. Friction is fragmentation between business CRM and attractive mobile client planning; client completion requires a kickoff/reminder; operator fears around price or dated UX conflict with high value reports. Software WTP signals are unusually explicit but small-sample and segment-sensitive. Vibo's high current list price and DJEP's reported historic spend illustrate different scopes, not a market average.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Evidence agrees that a booked date, customer plan, service hours, contract/payment, and equipment/operator availability matter. DJEP aims to cover full operations; Vibo is intentionally a client-friendly planning layer and some DJs combine both. Solo vs multi-op scale materially changes staffing, capacity, payroll, and gear tracking. The contract examples share deposit, schedule, overtime, cancellation, setup, and equipment topics but do not set universal legal terms. Confidence is **high** for date availability, event details, performance window, gear and client planning; **medium** for prevalence of client apps and software budget; **low** for common service prices, industrywide cancellation rules, and non-wedding mobile event workflows. Unanswered: proportion of candidate businesses that are solo vs multi-op; modern DJEP static prices; whether users want one tool or intentionally keep music planning separate.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Start with a date-booked event service: ask mobile event vs other DJ work, solo/team size, services and add-ons, staff/equipment ownership, client planning needs, and advance timing. Configure inquiry, quote/package, contract/deposit, music/timeline planning, venue/access notes, staff/gear assignment, setup/test/performance/strike checklist, overtime approval, and closeout. Keep cancellation, deposit, overtime, music policy, weather, gear, contractor pay, and content-sharing settings tenant-defined. Prototype with one solo wedding DJ and one multi-op company; keep this profile distinct from event planners and caterers.
