# Laundry services

## Profile metadata

- **Industry key:** `laundry-services`
- **Source name:** Laundry services
- **Source category:** Home services
- **Candidate source:** [Businesses I Love](https://www.sweatystartup.com/blog/businesses-i-love), Home services, position 20 (after “Maid services”; before “Carpet cleaning / steaming”).
- **Other exact/near duplicate labels:** Dry cleaning, laundromat self-service and linen rental are related but distinct offerings. This profile centers wash/dry/fold, garment care, and optional customer pickup/delivery; dry-clean-only and linen rental have separate production/inventory workflows.

## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Laundry service can be retail drop-off wash/dry/fold, route-based household pickup/delivery, specialty garment/dry cleaning, or recurring commercial linen/towel service. Residential delivery pricing commonly uses dry weight, minimum order, service zone and turnaround; vendors offer scheduled weekly/biweekly subscriptions and one-time orders. Cents’ list price differentiates order/POS, outsourced gig delivery, and own-fleet routing plans. Separate bag/order custody, customer preferences, stain/damage notes and tracking are important because handling errors involve people’s property.

**Inference:** The pack should ask which fulfillment and customer model applies, then track the bag/order from intake through sorted processing and pickup/delivery. Route delivery and laundromat POS need distinct workflows. **Uncertain:** actual use of any vendor by the population is not established by vendor marketing; customer segment, pricing, pickup geography and garment acceptance vary locally.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include households/apartment residents, busy professionals, students, hotels, salons/spas, gyms, restaurants, medical practices and other linen/towel accounts. Models: drop-off counter service, full-service pickup/delivery, platform/gig processing, dry-cleaning counter/network, commercial recurring accounts, self-service laundromat (not the same service business). Revenue may be per pound, per item, recurring membership/account contract, rush charge, specialty care and delivery. Residential is repeat weekly/biweekly but often opt-in; commercial has fixed collection/delivery days, volume/terms and service-level expectations. Operator may own plant/equipment or outsource processing; site capacity materially changes workflows.

## Terminology and durable records

Record customer, household/commercial account, addresses/access/gate code, bag/order ID, service type, received weight, item counts/specialty items, stated care instructions/preferences (scent-free/detergent, separate loads), stain/damage notes and photos, intake/delivery timestamps, promised turnaround, plant/workstation status, driver/route, invoice and dispute/claim. Commercial account additionally requires location, linen category/count, recurring pickup and delivery, contract rate, PO and net terms. Bag/item custody is persistent during job; customer, preference, allergy/sensitivity notes should be minimal/consensual. Do not represent weight as item count or imply routine WDF covers dry cleaning, alterations or hazardous contamination.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Service categories include wash/dry/fold by dry weight, per-item specialty/comforter, dry cleaning via own/partner plant, ironing, stain treatment, subscription pickup, express/rush, commercial towels/linens, and self-service machine cycle. Intake asks service/eligible items, bag count, address/zone, access instructions, pickup window, preferences/detergent, stain/valuable/special-care disclosure, turnaround and commercial account details. Order weight is often measured after pickup at plant; notify customer of final price and any minimum/special item before processing where possible. Separate/categorize items by customer and instructions; intake tag/order ID must follow bag through process. Quote model varies by pound, item, weight tier, minimum, rush, zone/delivery fee, subscription rate, specialty treatment and commercial volume/terms. Explicitly disclose what is excluded and claim process for lost/damaged items.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service prices:** Drop & Dash Chicago lists $1.95/lb, 25 lb minimum, $5 delivery and 24-hour standard turnaround; comforters/pillows priced separately ([operator prices](https://www.dropanddash.com/pricing/), accessed 2026-09-24). Nashville Laundry Delivery lists $2.50/lb, $40 minimum, 24-hour delivery ([operator prices](https://nashvillelaundry.com/pricing/), accessed 2026-09-24). Fresh Drop CT lists $1.99/lb one-time with $35 minimum, $1.79/lb recurring weekly/biweekly with $30 minimum, 24–48-hour turnaround ([operator prices](https://www.freshdropct.com/pricing), accessed 2026-09-24). These are local list prices; not national averages. Commercial routes may use negotiated per-pound/per-item, minimums, recurring windows, net terms, account minimum and contamination handling; no example tariff found in the opened commercial source.

Software commercial evidence: Cents publishes Starter $89/month, Growth $249/month (gig network/online order) and Ultimate $399/month for in-house pickup/delivery fleet, route windows, driver app and customer tracking ([vendor plan page](https://www.trycents.com/pricing), accessed 2026-09-24). These are advertised subscription prices, not proof that laundry operators actually purchase or are willing to pay those prices. No direct operator WTP quote/purchase record was found. Customer laundry service prices are separate from SaaS prices.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Recurring pickup windows, route zone, service day, capacity, plant turnaround, dry weight, commercial account service levels, machine capacity and driver routes all constrain schedule. At pickup record no-show/porch/conciege location; scan/mark custody, route stop and delivery proof. Route operator clusters by ZIP/zone and household recurring cadence; same-day service only when plant/route capacity allows. A missed pickup can disrupt same-day plant queue; delivery reschedule needs customer contact. Commercial routes have contracted time windows and location-level bags/counts. Track rush only after human capacity confirmation. Route planning is less relevant for drop-off only.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Receive and identify order/bags; weigh dry laundry or count priced items; record special instructions, stains/existing damage and exceptions; create customer-specific batch; process wash/dry/fold or partner service by care label; quality check/sort/package; reconcile weight/items/bag count; notify ready; deliver to verified contact/site with proof; invoice and record complaint. Laundry is custody-sensitive: lost garment, mix-up, shrinkage, stain setting, fragrance/detergent reaction, late route or incomplete bag should be logged against bag/order and resolved. Caution customer with known contamination, needles/chemical/biological materials; refuse or route to qualified handling based on policy. OSHA workplace controls for machinery, chemicals, heat, wet floors, repetitive work and vehicle operations are employer/site-specific; the research did not identify one laundry-specific national service license or standard. Follow washer/dryer/fire and chemical manufacturer instructions, SDS, PPE and applicable OSHA standards. Use consent before storing sensitive preferences; avoid sharing order contents beyond staff who need it.

## Customer communication and self-service

Self-service should show available area/window, item exclusions, weight/minimum/rush pricing, detergent/temperature/separation preferences, pickup instructions, order status, final weighed price/approval, ready/delivery updates, payment and claim channel. Send driver assigned/approaching/pickup/delivery and no-show notification; preserve email/SMS consent. Commercial manager needs route changes, missing-bag exceptions, recurring quantity reconciliation, invoice/PO and account-level service report. Do not auto-approve a high final weight, stain treatment surcharge or special garment service without the configured customer permission.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Plant resources: washers/dryers, ironing/press, detergent/chemical inventory, scales, bags/tags/barcodes, folding tables, carts/racks, garment packaging, route vehicle, drivers and point-of-sale devices. Equipment capacity and plant outsource status govern slots. Inventory includes supplies, reusable bags, tags, customer-owned specialty items and commercial linen stock where applicable; do not equate WDF custody with linen ownership. Staff include counter/intake, wash/fold/quality, route driver, plant manager and commercial account service. Driver route and plant shifts require separate schedules; staffing/time exports and payroll connectors are conditional by scale.

## Reporting and operating measures

Track orders by service and channel, dry pounds/items, order-to-ready and promised turnaround, lost/damaged/mix-up complaint rate, rewash, utilization by machine/shift, cost/lb, route stops/miles/on-time, missed pickup, repeat/subscription retention, minimum/order average and commercial account margin/shortage. Clarify denominator (per order, item, pound, route stop). Cents markets order status, customer messaging, delivery routes and business reporting; no independent benchmark found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Laundry operations have purpose-built POS/order/tags, customer portals, SMS, online ordering, delivery-driver/route apps and commercial account handling. Cents markets its dispatch plan and CleanCloud sells laundry POS/pickup delivery software. Operators also use scale, tag/barcode, accounting, phone, website, texts and spreadsheets; actual prevalence beyond vendor claims is unknown. Reviews/complaints not systematically sampled. One direct vendor list price shows SaaS costs from $89 to $399/mo by delivery complexity; this is an actual offer, not operator WTP. A one-person processor may use app-marketplace/gig delivery rather than own routes, whereas commercial accounts need contracts, route service and invoices.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Per-pound minimums, recurring discounts and turnarounds differ among operators; software vendors package delivery differently. Customers pay for convenience and dependable custody, but no source quantifies software WTP. **Inference:** order/bag lifecycle and final-weighed-price communication are critical. **Uncertain:** commercial linen segmentation, dry-clean partners and claims practice vary; no representative failure/complaint statistics found. Confidence high for service and price model, medium for common software stack, low for business-wide adoption.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask fulfillment (drop-off/route/gig), household vs commercial, plant ownership, pricing unit/minimum, dry clean/specialty, repeat schedules, own driver fleet and bag/garment tracking. Prototype bag/order ID, customer instructions, weight/final approval, production stages, pickup/delivery route and proof. Confirm whether existing jobs can track item/bag custody and commercial account recurrence before claiming a core gap.
