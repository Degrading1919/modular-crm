## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A GC sells or manages a building/remodel project and coordinates owner/client, design team, specialty subcontractors, suppliers, permits/inspections, schedule, budget and closeout. Some self-perform trades; others manage most work. The durable record is the project/site plus contract scope, plans, selections, budget/cost codes, permit status, sub/vendor documents and warranties. The distinctive loop is preconstruction/estimate → bids/contract → schedule/mobilize → sequence trades/materials/inspections → logs and approved changes → progress billing/cost control → punch/closeout/warranty. This is broad coordination, distinct from roofing’s tear-off/install safety loop and a carpenter’s bounded craft job.

A pack can configure project lifecycle, owner/sub roles, bid/permit/change/punch steps, and recommend estimates, scheduling, documents, job costing, billing and accounting. Current pack contract can suggest workflows but cannot itself represent a CPM dependency graph, subcontract, cost commitment or retainage rule. These are cross-industry validation questions, not accepted product decisions; no single GC workflow or core gap is proven.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential custom builders, remodelers, additions, kitchen/bath firms and small repair GCs serve homeowners, landlords and property managers. Commercial GCs serve developers, owners, institutions, public agencies or tenant-improvement clients. Work may be negotiated, hard bid, design-build, construction management, cost-plus or fixed price. A GC can be prime, construction manager or subcontractor, changing contract and documentation responsibilities. Triggers include purchase/remodel, damage, expansion, tenant turnover and capital projects. Projects are episodic and referral/reputation driven; warranty work recurs.

Scale ranges from an owner estimating and supervising subs to estimators, PMs, supers, accounting/admin, procurement and many concurrent jobs. Residential remodel selection/client workflows differ from commercial RFI/submittal/site meeting/pay-application workflows. Public or large commercial firms may need systems beyond a lightweight CRM. Intake must learn project segment, self-perform/sub mix, concurrency, contract type and financial controls.

## Terminology and durable records

Terms include owner/client, lead, project/site, plans/specifications, scope, bid/takeoff, estimate, contract, allowance/selection, schedule/milestone, cost code/budget, subcontractor/vendor, PO, RFI, submittal, daily log, change order, pay application/progress draw, retainage, punch, closeout and warranty. Site data may include owner/occupant/billing contacts, address/access, occupancy, existing conditions, utility/neighbor constraints, permit authority/number and logistics. A site/project is not always a persistent customer asset; store installed system/equipment history only where useful. Plans, contracts, budget, bids, waivers, certificates and internal notes can be sensitive. Owner, architect, GC, subcontractor, vendor and finance users need separate visibility; external parties should not see margin, competing bids or private notes.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Work includes new home, remodel/addition, tenant fit-out, kitchen/bath, construction management, repair/restoration coordination and small project. Intake needs site, scope/outcome, plans/photos/inspection documents, budget/funding readiness, decision makers, desired dates, occupancy constraints, hazards, access, design/permit status and bid documents. A site walk or paid preconstruction may precede committed pricing.

Estimate can be conceptual range, fixed, cost-plus, T&M, unit-price, GMP or competitive bid. It may require quantity takeoff, labor/material/equipment, subcontractor bids, selections/allowances, contingency, fee, exclusions, permit, schedule and payment terms. Changes need written scope/price/time and owner approval before execution except contract-defined emergency work. Progress billing may be milestone, schedule-of-values, percent-complete, draw or statutory application; retainage and waivers depend on contract and law. Website forms should collect a lead and route to discovery, not promise a construction date or final price.

## Pricing and commercial model

Revenue may use lump sum, cost-plus, T&M, unit price, management fee, negotiated GMP or hybrid. Inputs include scope maturity, quantities, labor burden, subcontract bids, material/equipment, site condition/access, permits, schedule/lead time, bonding/insurance, contingency, overhead, fee and risk. Approved change must update contract sum, budget and job cost. Billing schedules and tax/lien rules are tenant/jurisdiction-specific.

**Observed software price/WTP boundary, accessed 2026-09-24:** JobTread publishes $199/month for its first internal user and $20 for additional internal users, with customer/sub/vendor external portals described as free. Procore prices annual contracts by product and Annual Construction Volume; the reviewed page gives no universal dollar price. Buildertrend says its price is tailored to job volume, team and scope. These are advertised packaging signals, not verified purchases. An anonymous contractor-management thread calls Procore's quote high for that user's volume and recommends spreadsheets for buyers unwilling to spend hundreds monthly. No representative WTP study or paid transaction evidence found.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Projects often span days/months and depend on trades, inspections, owner selections, long-lead materials, access/occupancy, weather and shared PM/crew capacity. Scheduling spans preconstruction, permits, procurement, trade sequence, inspection windows, walkthrough and closeout. One small project may use tasks/calendar; larger concurrent jobs use look-ahead and critical-path planning. Route planning is secondary except for repair/service firms. Record blocker, responsible party, notice, cost/date effect and recovery plan when delayed.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Lead/RFP → plans/site review → estimate/bids → contract/budget → permits/vendor setup → baseline schedule/procurement/selections → preconstruction and safety/site controls → mobilize → coordinate trades, inspections, daily logs, photos/RFIs/submittals → price and approve changes → cost/progress billing → punch → final inspection/acceptance, manuals/waivers/as-builts/warranty and final invoice. Buildertrend documents sales, preconstruction, construction, finance and post-construction workflows; commercial jobs may add formal RFI/submittal/pay application.

OSHA duties depend on employer and site roles; a CRM checklist is not a safety program. Permits, licensed design, inspections, insurance, bond, waiver and accessibility needs depend on place/project. Track responsible party, document and outcome without claiming that a saved document establishes compliance. Blockers include permit/plan/selection, sub no-show, material delay, failed inspection, hidden conditions, unsafe site, weather, funding, change approval or dispute. Rework can reflect design/scope coordination, defective work, inspection failure, damage or warranty; record cause/trade, cost/time impact, approval and acceptance.

## Customer communication and self-service

Owners need proposal assumptions, budget/allowance, selection deadlines, schedule/status, decisions, changes, progress photos, billing, delay impact and warranty files. Subs/vendors need only their scope, schedule, access, approved plans/changes, PO and inspection information. A role-limited portal may handle files, selections, approvals, messages and invoices. Schedule/cost changes, incidents, failed inspection and scope interpretation need human review before automated messaging.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

The GC coordinates job-specific materials, finish selections, POs, deliveries, rentals, storage and returns; warehouse stock is not universal. Supplier price/lead time and substitutions affect schedule. Workforce includes owner/PM/estimator/superintendent/admin, self-perform crews and subs. Company/contact, trade, bid/contract, job scope, schedule, insurance/license/W-9/waiver and emergency details may matter by policy or contract. Expiration reminders do not establish legal compliance. Time/equipment/payroll/job cost become more important with employees, self-perform or concurrency.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures include pipeline/backlog, contract value and approved changes, budget vs committed/actual cost, cash flow, invoice/AR, schedule variance, trade commitments, open RFI/submittal, punch/warranty and profit by project/client/PM/trade/cost code. Percent complete and earned revenue require an accounting policy and reliable inputs; do not present a simple progress percentage as audited WIP. Metrics/definitions vary by public, residential, commercial and bonding needs.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Buildertrend markets lead, estimate, schedule, selection, owner/sub portal, daily log, budget, PO, change, invoice and warranty features with tailored pricing. JobTread publishes per-internal-user pricing, free external portal access and estimating/job-cost/change/PO/pay application tools. Procore markets broad construction collaboration with annual-construction-volume pricing. A small GC may stitch spreadsheets, email/text, photos/cloud folders, QuickBooks and estimating tools; larger commercial teams may add ERP/document control.

An operator thread describes sub certificates, invoices and schedules split across spreadsheets/email and expresses reluctance to pay hundreds monthly. A carpenter/contractor thread says Jobber worked for quotes/invoices but not detailed accounting and QuickBooks was too complex for a solo shop. This illustrates scale variation, not GC-wide prevalence. Mature vendor workflows document role permissions, change approvals, POs, cost impact and warranties. Vendor and anonymous posts do not establish market WTP.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Vendors document workflows but market their own solution. JobTread's public seat price, Procore's annual-volume pricing and Buildertrend's tailored pricing show different packaging, not common WTP. Operator posts reveal lighter stacks and price concerns but are few, anonymous and self-selected. Residential remodel, homebuilding, commercial, public, GC-as-sub and construction-management firms differ. Confidence is high on coordination/change/project records, medium on reporting priorities, low on population WTP and core-versus-optional architecture.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a light residential project path with estimate, permits/docs, owner-approved changes, sub/vendor work, procurement dates, progress billing and closeout. Ask about project type, concurrent count/value/duration, self-perform/sub mix, billing, cost-code, portal and WIP needs. Keep contract, permit, safety, insurance, retainage, accounting and permissions tenant-configurable. Keep repair/service separate from multi-month projects. Resolve which project primitives exist in core and whether shared scheduling/budget can support dependencies across construction profiles.
