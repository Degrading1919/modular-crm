## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Portable sanitation businesses place units at construction, agriculture, roadwork, disaster-recovery, industrial, film and temporary commercial sites. A customer/site contract may run weeks or months with one or more recurring pump/clean visits, unit swaps, supplies, relocations and off-rent/pickup. PSAI provides separate workplace and public-use standards; OSHA's construction rule sets employee sanitation requirements in that context. Specialist systems model assigned units, recurring service intervals, routes, mobile service completion and billing cycles.

**Inference:** The key workflow is site onboarding -> unit type/count/placement -> recurring service interval and contract billing -> dispatch/driver service proof -> issue/relocation or swap -> off-rent/pickup -> cleaning/repair/disposal closeout. Unit/site/service history and route scheduling are more central here than event guest experience.

**Uncertain:** Candidate label alone doesn't say construction vs agriculture vs commercial renovation or whether it includes only rentals or service/pumping. Ask predominant customer, placement duration, service cycle, disposal footprint and whether septic or other waste services share routes.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include general contractors, construction firms, farms, municipalities, road crews, industrial sites, disaster response, film/TV productions and businesses under renovation. Revenue includes weekly/monthly/28-day rental, delivery/pickup, recurring service, extra pump-out, relocation, supplies and damage/cleaning charges. Long-term jobsite accounts may persist through project phases; service cadence can vary with workers/use/weather and contract. Leads come from contractor/site networks, procurement, web and repeat accounts. Small operators may run a few units/truck; scaled companies track hundreds/thousands of units and multiple service vehicles, drivers, route planners, sales and billing staff. Some operators combine portable restroom, septic, dumpster or temporary-fence service, but workflows should remain selectable.

## Terminology and durable records

Terms include **customer/account**, **jobsite/site**, **unit/toilet**, **unit ID**, **placement**, **service interval**, **pump-out**, **service ticket**, **swap/relocation**, **off-rent**, **28-day billing cycle**, **disposal facility**. Persistent records: customer, jobsite/geofence, contacts, gate/access hours, PO/project number, worker count/use profile, unit type/ID/capacity, placement point, delivery dates, recurring service, route, disposal site and service history. Site plans, placement photos, utility/access notes, service proof, waste/incident record, permits and billing authorizations change over time; preserve version/date. Unit GPS/location, tank/service state, cleanliness, repair and supplies affect availability. Waste, exposure, spill and disposal data are safety/environment records; customer and worker details need role-limited access.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services include unit rental, standard/accessible/handwash facilities, delivery/placement, scheduled service/pump-out, restocking, emergency/extra service, relocation, swaps, repair and final pickup. Intake should capture business/site owner, project ID/PO, location/contact, start and estimated end, workers/users, shifts, unit count/type, placement, route access/gate/terrain, service cadence, utility/handwash needs, disposal jurisdiction, billing terms and off-rent notice. Site review may be needed for truck access, slope, placement or environmental limits. Quote may combine per-unit weekly/monthly/28-day rental, delivery, planned visits, distance, supplies, extra callout, relocation and damage/cleaning. Service agreement clarifies access, restocking, customer-caused blockage/damage, unsafe conditions, payment cycle, volume change, off-rent/notice, tank servicing and cancellation. Procurement may require W-9, insurance, PO and site safety documents.

## Pricing and commercial model

Drivers include unit type/count, contract duration, service cycle, truck mileage, route density, site access, user count, handwash/accessible models, supplies, emergency response, relocation and disposal/trucking costs. Billing cycles may differ from calendar month: software vendors explicitly model 28-day billing and four weekly service visits per cycle, but this is a product-supported method, not an industry mandate. No universal service price is supported. **Software WTP evidence:** PortaPro advertises US$149/month Starter with unlimited users/no per-unit fee; PumpDocket advertises $99/month for 1-3 trucks, $230 for 4-10, $454 for 11+; Tank Track lists $180/month for the first truck and $150 for each additional, with 10% annual discount. These are vendor list prices, distinct from service rental prices. Tank Track hosts named customer testimonials from a Washington owner who chose it for cost/ease and a Wisconsin owner describing replacement of a 20+ year old system; this is first-person purchase/use evidence but vendor-selected, so it cannot establish market willingness to pay.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Recurring service route is core: create account/site service frequency and unit locations, sequence stops by service due date/geography, assign truck/driver, capture completion/proof, update next due, then bill. Route work must account for disposal facility hours, travel, site gates, unit count, truck tank/capacity, service skill, access/weather and emergency stops. Customers may request additional service or move/close a site; preserve authorization and next service/billing adjustments. A missed stop, inaccessible gate, unit relocated by contractor, blocked unit, full tank or spill requires exception task and customer notice. Event-only rental is a separate dispatch pattern with a return date and exact event window.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm service order, unit ID/location, access, site safety and route; driver receives stop list, service interval and site notes; service unit using procedures/PPE; replenish supplies, inspect cleanliness/damage and tank level; record timestamp, GPS/photo/signature, completion or reason blocked; route waste to authorized facility per applicable requirements; report spill, damage, relocation or additional service; next due date recalculates; office reviews exceptions and invoice/cycle; off-rent triggers retrieval, pump/clean, inspection and inventory release. Evidence may include service ticket, unit/site photo, GPS/arrival, driver completion, disposal record, customer signature and incident report. OSHA 29 CFR 1926.51 applies to construction sanitation; PSAI Z4.3 addresses non-sewered sanitation and has separate public-use guidance. These do not collapse into one universal state/city permit rule. Follow applicable environmental, health, transport and local disposal requirements; escalate spills/exposure immediately. Noncompletion includes locked site, unsafe access, blocked/missing unit, truck failure, disposal facility unavailable, route/weather delay or unpaid/closed account. Revisit, swap, extra service, credit or off-rent adjustment follows contract and local process.

## Customer communication and self-service

Businesses need quote/contract, delivery notice, routine service completion or missed-stop notice, unit/location confirmation, extra-service request, service issue escalation, cycle invoice, PO/cost-center reporting and off-rent confirmation. Portal can list sites/units, planned service, invoice and request changes. Automated service-completion SMS is useful only if opted-in/authorized and location/service records are current; missed access or unsafe service needs human explanation. Do not auto-close a site or stop billing without authenticated customer authorization and retrieved unit status.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Track serialized units by type/capacity, tank/service status, cleanliness, damage, repair, supplies, location/site/customer, delivery/off-rent and next due date. Assets also include pump/vacuum trucks, water/handwash units, hoses, PPE, chemicals, tools, fuel and replacement parts. Fleet and unit condition determine daily capacity; maintenance/set-aside must block new assignments. Supplier/disposal facilities need hours, accepted materials, manifests/records and capacity. Roles include dispatch, route driver/technician, mechanic, sales, billing and customer service. Drivers need mobile stop instructions, emergency procedure, PPE/safety and route changes; record time, service completion, mileage and exceptions. Do not assume payroll/dispatch complexity for one-truck operators.

## Reporting and operating measures

Specialist systems expose route/service status, units/assets, billing cycle, stop completion, customer/site and reports. Candidate KPIs: units on rent and utilization, service stops due/completed/missed, route stops and miles per shift, service cost/unit, access-failure rate, extra callout/overflow, disposal cost, unit repair/turn time, aged receivable, 28-day invoice accuracy, off-rent retrieval delay, customer retention and margin per site/route. Include service interval and contract cycle in definitions; monthly calendar reporting may obscure 28-day cycles.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Portable-specific software includes ServiceCore, PortaPro, PumpDocket, Tank Track, Toitrack, EZTrakR and AMCS; offerings cover units/sites/customers, route/dispatch/mobile tickets, recurring invoices and reporting. Some allow mixed septic and portable routes. Operators report legacy paper/faxed route dispatch, large fleets/drivers and maintenance/cleaning; one named customer testimonial describes replacing a 20-year system and choosing current software for cost/ease. A Reddit thread reports high startup capital, truck/asset scale, fuel and service labor; another asks how to move from faxed routes, underscoring older workflows but not industry prevalence. Complaints/workarounds include late/missed service, hard-to-find unit/site placements, outdated dispatch records and lost revenue when site/off-rent/service data disagree. Vendors' claims of faster routes or savings are not independent proof. List pricing varies by truck, user, module or custom quote; do not extrapolate a single software budget.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

PSAI standards, OSHA construction rule and software vendors agree on sanitation access, unit/service/site management and recurring route workflows. Exact service interval and toilet count depend on use, workplace/public context and authority; jobsite recurrence must not be generalized to one-day events. Operator accounts describe both old/manual routing and modern mobile systems, with wide asset/fleet scale. **High confidence:** site/unit/service due date/driver record are durable operational needs for recurring service. **Medium:** 28-day billing prevalence and dispatch/mobile adoption. **Low:** standard local service fee, saturation, CRM price tolerance and universal disposal documentation. Open questions: proportion of event-only vs jobsite business within this label; local manifest requirements; whether combined septic businesses need one operational pack.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype account/site plus assigned units, contract term, service cadence, route stop, driver proof, exception handling, recurring billing, off-rent retrieval and unit turn.
