## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Boat cleaning is site- and vessel-based. A topside wash/detailer may clean decks, hull topsides, cockpit, cabin, windows, canvas and brightwork at a marina slip, private dock, boatyard or trailer; a diver may clean the wetted hull and inspect running gear from the slip. Work is often sold per linear foot, by condition/scope or on weekly/monthly recurring service. Water access, slip number, marina vendor authorization, tides/shore power, access, boat length, hull paint and cleaning method shape the job. Environmental rules can govern both above-water washwater and release of underwater fouling/antifouling material; some jobs need haul-out or containment rather than in-water cleaning.

**Inference:** Store the vessel identity and dock/slip/location as durable records, but schedule individual wash visits as recurring jobs that can move with the boat. Completion should record the areas cleaned, condition, method, date/location, photos or dive log, and any coating damage/anode or running-gear observation without silently turning it into a repair. Keep boat cleaning separate from mechanical maintenance and boat repair.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include recreational boat owners, yacht owners, sailing clubs, marinas, boat dealers, charter/rental fleets and sometimes commercial vessel operators. Independent operators may work aboard at a slip or private dock; a marina may bundle wash/detailing with dockage and service; underwater hull cleaning requires trained, insured dive staff and marina approval. Some detail businesses offer one-off scrub/wax/correction; diver routes may recur every few weeks/monthly in warm saltwater markets. Seasonal freshwater boats may receive cleanup during launch/haul-out and be stored between seasons, but winterization is a different listed candidate and is not a default cleaning service.

Scope distinction matters: topside washing (above waterline), interior cleaning, polishing/wax/gelcoat correction and underwater hull maintenance use different equipment, access, skill, environmental controls and pricing. Underwater hull cleaning is not a cosmetic extension that any topside cleaner can promise; recent antifouling, ablative paint, heavy growth or restricted site may require a different approach or no in-water work.

## Terminology and durable records

Use owner/customer, vessel/boat, hull, waterline, topside/deck, cabin, slip, marina/yard, diver/detailer, washdown/detail, recurring hull clean and service report. Asset record: vessel name, HIN (sensitive identifier), make/model/year, length/beam/draft, vessel type, hull material and antifouling coating (type/application date if known), engine not necessary for a cleaning-only pack, canvas/teak/glass/upholstery finishes, photo/condition history, last clean, owner and authorized contact. Slip/dock location may change; keep marina name, basin/slip or private dock, access gate/ladder, shore access, vessel orientation, shore-power and water limits, marina vendor paperwork, work-hour restrictions and emergency contact at the location with effective dates.

For recurring underwater visits, retain date, waterbody, water visibility/conditions if operationally relevant, diver and method, hull fouling, areas cleaned, running gear/anode observation (not repair), coating/plume concern, before/after imagery, and recommendation/escalation. Do not capture marina credentials in public asset notes. Job-level inputs include weather/tide, hull condition and customer-requested areas.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Service menu may include fresh/saltwater rinse, exterior wash/dry, nonskid/deck, cabin/interior, canvas/vinyl/windows, brightwork/teak, one-time detail, wax/polish/gelcoat restoration, underwater bottom/hull scrub, prop/running-gear polish, visual dive report, and cleaning after launch/haul-out. Do not include painting, repair, anode replacement, engine maintenance or winterization unless the operator separately offers and is qualified/authorized for them.

Intake: vessel name/HIN or other identifier, type and length, dock/slip/private-dock/trailer location, marina/site contact and access, owner authorization, last clean and planned cadence, hull paint/coating type and application date if known, amount/type of growth, requested areas, topside vs underwater scope, photos, water/shore access, divers/vessel/staff count, marina vendor approval, and environmental/site restrictions. Identify site-specific wastewater method, current hull coating and whether hard growth needs haul-out. Self-service should request a quote; route uncertain coating, new paint, heavy fouling, protected waterbody or no marina permission to a qualified human. A service agreement should document areas/condition, interval, access, excluded repair work, price, pause/cancel, damage notification and after-hours/emergency terms.

## Pricing and commercial model

Cleaning prices commonly use boat length and scope, adjusted for hull/gelcoat condition, growth, coating, access, additional cabin or brightwork, location, frequency and emergency/rush. Marco Boat Works (Southwest Florida) lists $15/ft for a detail wash, $35/ft wash-and-wax and $45/ft interior+exterior, with weekly/biweekly/monthly wash rates $24/$14/$10 per foot (accessed 2026-09-24). The figures are operator prices, not market averages; scope is not directly comparable to a small-boat flat rate. Jayhawk Marina’s 2023 cleaning/detailing agreement uses boat type and length and says price assumes average dirt/grime. South Florida diver market guide reports monthly rates around $4–$6.50/ft on selected price pages and owner forum reports often lower ($2–$4/ft); market coverage and service inclusions vary. California operator CaliCoast publicly lists $2/ft monthly, $2.75 bi-monthly, $3.50 quarterly and $4 one-off for underwater hull cleaning; this is a particular operator’s service, not cleaning/detaling market standard.

**Software willingness-to-pay:** Marine-specific tools such as DockMaster and HullDive address diver logs, dispatch, billing and marina workflows; DockMaster’s service software is demo/contact-sales priced. Public operator conversion or software subscription purchase evidence for independent cleaners is sparse. A diver who also does boat cleaning is not evidence of broad detailer CRM adoption. No honest numerical WTP for a boat cleaner can be inferred from the per-foot customer cleaning rate. Generic FSM, calendar, accounting and text may be used, but a prevalent stack was not established.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Topside wash intervals depend on salt spray, storage, usage and owner expectations. Underwater hull service may recur more frequently in warm water and with particular hull materials/coatings; frequency must be set by the local operator and manufacturer/marina rules. California Clean Marina education says wait 90 days after antifouling application before underwater cleaning and avoid underwater cleaning of soft/sloughing ablative paint; it recommends gentle practices and haul-out for aggressive growth. Treat that guidance as a California/regional source, not a universal interval.

For diver routes, associate jobs with current marina/slip, vessel location, diver qualification, weather/visibility, site authorization, boat length, interval and estimated underwater time; route by basin/waterbody and marina access window. Above-water crews need hose/wastewater access, parking, shore-power/water allowance, ladder/pier access and weather buffers. A boat may move; confirm location shortly before each visit. Route optimization is conditional on multiple boats/stops. Reschedule for weather, poor visibility, boat absent/moved, dock access, recent bottom paint, discharge restriction, marina vendor rules, unsafe hull/access or unworkable condition.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Check work order, owner and site authorization, marina rules, vessel/slip, access and exact work boundary. Record pre-work condition and coating/paint concerns. Above water: contain/collect washwater as local rule requires, use the agreed product/method, protect adjacent boats and water, complete purchased zones, collect/secure waste and inspect. Underwater: confirm diver and buddy/boat procedures, water conditions and clearance, hull/paint eligibility, then document cleaned surfaces, observed fouling, running gear/anodes only as inspection, and any concerning plume/coating loss. UC Cooperative Extension/San Diego County says no colored plume should be produced, and aggressive cleaning should be hauled out; its guidance says remove anodes to shore for appropriate recycling/disposal. Do not proceed where method/regulation/site authorization is unclear.

Michigan’s mobile boat-wash guidance (AIS decontamination washing) says wastewater discharges to surface water or connected drains are prohibited without permit; its no-permit alternatives include site-specific upland discharge under constraints or containment/collection for approved wastewater treatment, and it recommends logging date/location/items washed for three years when discharging at various locations. This guidance is for its stated mobile AIS boat wash context, not a national rule for every rinse. EPA boatyard stormwater BMPs also constrain repair-yard blasting/sanding but are not a generic rule for a hand wash. Capture service proof: date/time, vessel/slip, topside/bottom areas, method, divergence from scope, weather/visibility, photos/video/dive log, wastewater disposition/permission where required, customer notice and any damage/repair referral. Do not claim a clean hull guarantees speed/fuel savings.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need recurring service confirmation, location/boat-present check, access/arrival status, weather cancellation, service report/photo, condition finding, access or bottom-paint warning, invoice and next due date. Self-service can maintain boat/slip and request wash/detail, approve an add-on or pause schedule. Do not present diver observation as a repair diagnosis; route suspected coating loss, prop damage, loose gear, anodes, leaks or electrical/structural defects to a qualified repair provider. Avoid access code disclosures in SMS.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Topside crews use hoses/DI water, low-pressure/pressure washer according to surface, brushes, poles, ladders, buffers, polishers, pads, towels, canvas/upholstery and biodegradable/approved cleaning products; mobile wastewater capture/recovery and spill kit may be needed. Divers carry dive equipment, communications/umbilical/air, underwater lights/camera, scrapers/soft pads, anode tools and surface support vessel as their operation requires. Replenishable chemical/towel inventory may be optional for solo cleaning but useful for teams; diver-specific consumables, PPE and gas/inspection records are safety-critical. Marina access permits, diver certification/insurance and employee qualifications are business/site dependent; don't invent a universal “boat cleaner license.” Staff must not perform repair/electrical/mechanical work outside training/authorization.

## Reporting and operating measures

Reports may show recurring vessels due/overdue, service interval, revenue per boat/foot, route/marina, canceled boat-not-present jobs, work time by crew, washwater exceptions, diver logs, reported coating damage, callbacks/rework, plan retention and add-on uptake. Underwater vendors expose dive report/photos and zinc/gear observations; detailed KPI use remains vendor-specific. Distinguish clean completion from mechanic repair, and distinguish per-vessel recurring cleaning from maintenance for marina infrastructure.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Marine platforms DockMaster and HullDive focus on vessel/service scheduling, diver/technician dispatch, logs, photos, customer billing and marina access. DockMaster advertises flat rate billing by boat length, maintenance history, offline work, image documentation, deposits, parts and warranty handling, indicating a broader dealership/yard customer than many independent cleaners. Marine repair software and generic FSM may be excessive for a solo wash route. Boat operators online discuss per-foot prices, wax/gelcoat hours and recurrent wash cadence; a Reddit owner/operator pricing thread reports local rates vary widely and contrasts $3–4/ft high-volume washes with $10/ft detail-quality claims. These are anonymous/local. No representative cleaner-specific software stack, complaint frequency or paid WTP evidence was found. Service agreements and route lists may be paper/calendar/text for small operations, but prevalence is not known.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Topside and underwater operations have different equipment, risks and schedules. Recurring per-foot rates depend on location and hull condition. California guidance warns about antifouling paint/plume and promotes haul-out for aggressive cleaning; Michigan AIS boat-wash guidance specifies discharge and record conditions in its regulatory scope. Marina contracts and rules may further limit work. **Inference:** keeping current slip history separate from vessel identity prevents stale dispatch and makes environmental evidence locatable. **Uncertain:** exact applicability depends on vessel/coating, method, waterbody, marina and locality. WTP/software adoption is not well evidenced for independent cleaners. Confidence high for operational distinctions; low for nationwide environmental generalization.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype vessel plus movable service location, recurring wash/dive visit, permission/environment checklist, per-foot scope/quote and field report. Ask whether the business does topside, interior, in-water hull, or haul-out work; which marinas/waterbodies it serves; how wastewater is handled; what boat-length price and cadence rules apply; and whether it employs divers. Keep local discharge permits, coating restrictions, slip data, diving qualifications and service area configurable. Do not merge this workflow with boat repair or winterizing.
