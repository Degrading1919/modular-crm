## Executive summary

**Observed evidence.** Mold remediation sells investigation-driven removal/cleaning and source-control coordination to owners, landlords, property managers, schools and commercial building operators. Work is often triggered by visible growth, odor, occupant concern, water damage, or an inspection finding. Unlike generic cleaning, the central loop is assess extent and moisture cause, plan controls, contain affected areas, remove or clean materials, correct/dry the moisture source, then document completion and any verification. EPA emphasizes resolving moisture, containment/PPE according to scope, cleaning rather than merely killing mold, and reassessment when hidden mold appears. IICRC S520 explicitly covers residential, commercial and institutional structures/contents, safety, documentation, HVAC and post-remediation verification (PRV).

**Regional distinction.** In Texas, licensed assessment and remediation roles are regulated and generally cannot be combined on the same project, subject to a school-district exception; the remediation contractor follows a work plan prepared by a licensed assessor. This is not a nationwide rule. A pack should therefore capture assessment/protocol/clearance documents and role ownership without hard-coding Texas law as universal. No meaningful evidence supports a mold-specific new core platform gap.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential homeowners and landlords buy single-property work; property managers, schools and commercial/institutional owners may require access coordination, occupant communications, vendor qualification and formal closeout. Referrals can come from plumbers, inspectors, indoor environmental professionals (IEPs), property managers, insurers, or direct web/phone leads. Triggers include leaks, flooding, HVAC/condensation, chronic humidity, visible growth and odor. A site assessment is usually needed before a reliable estimate because hidden cavities, material condition, affected area, access, containment and source repair change scope. Work can be multi-day and may follow water mitigation; reconstruction or leak repair may be a separate contractor. Ongoing prevention/inspection contracts are plausible for institutional property but not established as the default for small operators.

## Terminology and durable records

Terms include **assessment**, **remediation work plan/protocol**, **containment**, **negative pressure**, **HEPA filtration/vacuuming**, **affected materials**, **source correction**, **clearance/PRV**, and **IEP**. Store customer-reported symptoms/conditions separately from professional findings. Persistent record: property/building, unit, affected room/assembly, HVAC zone, and moisture-source history. One-time job inputs: visible extent, sample/lab report, moisture readings, containment layout, PPE/equipment, removed materials, waste disposition, and verification result. Records can include health-sensitive occupant statements, lab results and access/security information; restrict internal safety notes and distinguish independent assessor/IEP reports from contractor work records. The evidence does not support storing a biological “mold asset” as durable customer equipment.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services include inspection/remediation, containment setup, selective demolition, HEPA cleaning, treatment where appropriate, drying/source-control coordination, contents handling, HVAC-related work, and post-work verification through an independent professional where applicable. EPA says cleanup is removal, not simply killing; routine biocide use is not recommended, and wet materials/source problems matter. Intake: location, observed growth/odor, known leak/moisture history, affected rooms/materials, occupancy/special sensitivities if volunteered, prior testing/protocol, access, photos, landlord/owner authority, and urgency. Do not provide an automated health diagnosis. Quote from site findings or a qualified written protocol; square footage alone is insufficient in complex/hidden cases. Website can collect a concern and schedule assessment, then hand off for scope review. Distinguish assessment, remediation, testing, repairs and reconstruction in the estimate.

## Pricing and commercial model

Observed service-price signals: a 2026 consumer cost guide reports a broad **US$10–25 per affected sq ft** and typical project range **US$1,200–3,750**; Florida operator Mold Testing FL advertises inspections from **US$250**. The first is a secondary editorial estimate, the second a starting price from one provider, not comparable nationwide quotes. Better-supported price inputs are affected area/depth, material removal versus clean-in-place, access, containment/air filtration duration, labor, disposal, testing/lab costs, source repair and reconstruction. Work can be cash/direct-to-owner or insurance-connected; coverage and claim workflow vary. No current mold-specific SaaS list price or operator willingness-to-pay statement was established. Cinderblock markets job software to this segment but does not publicly price it on the reviewed page. Do not infer CRM budget from project prices.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

This is assessment- and project-based rather than route-dense. Schedule an inspection, prerequisite document/lab results when needed, containment/removal crew, drying or source repair, and verification/clearance. Revisit if hidden mold is found, moisture returns, or verification fails. Crew/equipment matching may depend on containment scale, respirator/PPE qualifications, occupied-site controls, HVAC involvement and hazardous materials. Emergency response is possible after water events but is not evidenced as universal mold-company positioning. Multi-day work, building access windows and occupant coordination matter more than optimized driving routes.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

EPA describes selecting a remediation manager; assessing size/materials and moisture cause; planning; selecting PPE/containment; remediating; fixing moisture; cleaning/drying or discarding unsalvageable materials; and checking for recurrence, with reassessment if hidden mold is found. Larger/heavier work can require sealed containment, negative pressure, HEPA equipment and decontamination procedures. OSHA respiratory protection rules apply where respirators are used; training, medical clearance and fit testing can be required. In Texas, TDLR says the remediation company must follow a work plan from a licensed mold assessment consultant, and assessment/remediation roles generally may not be held by the same project actor. Other states have differing requirements; verify AHJ rules and pesticide/chemical licensing when applicable. Closeout may include photos, removed-material records, moisture-source disposition, cleaning checklist, independent PRV or lab documents, customer acknowledgment and warranty terms. A failed/changed condition triggers reevaluation, not automated “pass” advice.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need prompt acknowledgment, inspection window, preparation/access guidance, explanation of who assesses versus performs work, approval of scope/change orders, occupant notices, daily updates on multi-day work, verification handoff and final records. Portals can expose appointment status, approved reports, estimate, invoice and remediation records. Automated messages must avoid claiming mold species, health risk, safe occupancy or successful clearance without qualified review. Consent and occupant/privacy policies vary, particularly in rentals, schools and healthcare settings.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Job-specific inventory includes PPE/respirators, containment sheeting/tape, HEPA air scrubbers/vacuums, moisture meters, dehumidifiers, cleaning materials, bags and disposal supplies. Track equipment deployment and inspection where required by company policy. Crew roles may include remediation technician/worker, supervisor, licensed contractor, assessor/consultant and independent IEP/lab partner; these roles are not interchangeable in every jurisdiction. Inventory, time/payroll and procurement are optional needs whose importance depends on company size and project type.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful reports include leads by referral/source, days from inquiry to assessment, estimate approval rate, jobs by area/material, containment/equipment days, labor/disposal/lab cost per job, repeat visits, PRV outcome, callback/reappearance, and margin by crew/property manager. Only PRV and documentation topics are directly grounded in the standard overview; KPI definitions are **inference** and should be validated with operators. Do not use a “mold-free” binary claim absent an agreed independent protocol.

## Existing software and operator evidence

IICRC S520 and EPA guidance provide the clearest workflow evidence. Texas regulator materials and Florida operator/regulator-facing pages show role separation and document prerequisites in multiple jurisdictions, though rules differ. Panther Restorations describes receiving an independent protocol, containment/source removal and separate verification; Mold Testing FL advertises inspection, remediation only where it did not assess, and independent post-remediation testing. These are operator descriptions, not independent audits. Vendor pages aimed at mold contractors emphasize job records, scheduling, files, estimates, invoices and offline mobile use; they show positioning, not adoption. Reddit homeowner discussions are anecdotes. No credible evidence established a dominant mold-only CRM, common stitched stack, or repeated software complaints.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

EPA school/commercial guidance dates to a 2008 reprint and is not a universal law or a substitute for the current standard. IICRC S520 is a paid consensus standard, so this review uses the public overview only. Texas regulator rules are unusually specific and demonstrate why jurisdiction must remain configurable. Consumer guides suggest wide service-price dispersion; assessments and scope definitions differ, so per-square-foot comparisons may not be comparable. **Uncertain:** actual operator software usage, common direct-vs-insurance mix, frequency of independent PRV, exact licensing requirements outside Texas and software willingness to pay. Confidence high for the remediation sequence; medium for scheduling/pricing drivers; low for market software patterns.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype site-specific intake, uploaded assessment/protocol, room/material scope, controlled multi-day checklist, equipment/containment records, approval/change handling, and closeout documents. Ask jurisdiction, whether the business assesses/tests as well as remediates, who supplies protocol/PRV, occupied/commercial mix, HVAC work and insurance share. Keep regulatory role checks, technical acceptance criteria, warranty, pricing and source repairs tenant- and jurisdiction-controlled; route health, safety, and clearance decisions to qualified people.
