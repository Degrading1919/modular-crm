## Executive summary

Asbestos abatement contractors survey, contain, remove or encapsulate asbestos-containing material (ACM) in buildings/industrial settings, maintain regulated work zones, monitor exposure/air quality, package and transport waste, decontaminate, clear the site and provide closeout documentation. Some firms conduct surveys/testing as a separate licensed service; others only bid removal after an environmental consultant's survey. EPA's Asbestos NESHAP covers certain demolition/renovation operations and requires thorough inspection; OSHA construction standard addresses employee exposure, competent-person supervision, regulated areas, controls, monitoring, training and records. State licensing, notification and disposal requirements add variation.

Specialty software exists: FieldFlō markets work orders, waste/materials/equipment tracking, change orders, time, safety and daily field reports; Formitize's asbestos app emphasizes dockets, workforce, vehicles/equipment, waste, signatures and offline capture. Their feature statements and prices are vendor evidence, not proof of operator adoption or WTP. No independent direct operator/software complaint source was found in this pass; profile remains partial while core process evidence is strong. CRM must organize work and records without deciding regulatory applicability, clearance or safety compliance.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include building owners, GCs, facility managers, industrial owners, public agencies, insurers and restoration firms. Projects include survey/inspection, removal/encapsulation, pipe/boiler insulation, surfacing material, floor tile/mastic, roofing/siding, demolition-related abatement, emergency contamination and maintenance. Scope/class of work and friability/material condition determine methods and controls. One contractor may specialize in small occupied-building work; another handles large industrial/municipal projects and multiple regulated crews.

Contracts can be public bids, GC subcontract, owner direct, unit-price schedule or time-and-material remediation. Work requires preconstruction planning, material survey, notifications, approved work plan, containment, air monitoring, waste transport/disposal and independent clearance where specified. Emergency response and non-friable removal differ from full containment projects.

## Terminology and durable records

Store owner/site/building/area, ACM/PACM material, survey/inspection report, quantity/condition, work class, competent person, regulated area, containment, negative pressure/HEPA, air samples, worker/credential, work plan, daily log, waste bag/load/manifest, disposal facility, clearance, change, incident and closeout. Location and material inventory are durable building history; work-zone boundaries, controls, exposure measurements, daily quantities and manifests are per-project. Sensitive health/medical records have stricter access and retention needs than CRM notes. Keep surveyor/lab/industrial hygienist and removal contractor roles distinct.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks building use/occupancy, age, material survey results, ACM type/location/quantity, intended renovation/demolition, project area, friability/condition, public/occupied spaces, plans/specification, notifications, permits, clearance/air monitoring, access and schedule. Contractors should not diagnose ACM from a CRM intake form; sampling and surveys belong to qualified parties as required locally. Estimate includes labor, containment/decon, negative-air equipment, PPE, monitoring, waste bags/transport/disposal, permits/notification, lab/consultant, access, occupied-site controls, mobilization and restoration. Preserve bid basis, exclusions, addenda, change approval and schedule notice.

Workflow: survey/records review → applicability and scope assessment by qualified staff → notification/permits → work plan and owner coordination → isolate work area → regulated removal/encapsulation → monitoring and daily records → waste packaging/transport/disposal → decon/cleanup → independent clearance if required → release and closeout. EPA notifications can be due before covered projects; exact applicability/threshold and state delegation must be checked. No generic automatic “approval” should replace regulatory review.

## Pricing and commercial model

Pricing drivers include material type/condition/quantity, work class, containment size, access, occupancy, height, PPE/respirator, labor qualification, monitoring, lab clearance, waste classification/volume, transport/disposal, permits, shift/night work, schedule and restoration. Public/commercial jobs may use bid items/progress claims; residential jobs use fixed scopes with exclusions and change orders for concealed materials. No reliable independent operator WTP was found. Formitize lists $29.99/user/month for standard plan and variable monthly price for dedicated industry solution; advertised list pricing is not a specific asbestos contractor's spend. FieldFlō pricing was not public on the reviewed page.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Work is project-based, with repeat owner/building maintenance and emergency response possible. Coordinate survey, notification waiting periods, access, work-area controls, negative pressure, monitor/lab availability, waste pickup, transport and clearance. Multi-crew scheduling must respect separation/credential needs. Delays can arise from untested material, agency notice, occupancy, containment failure, air sample, disposal capacity, weather/access, GC changes or failed clearance. Track stop-work, affected area, responsible party, notice and revised authorization.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

OSHA construction asbestos standard categorizes work and governs exposure assessment, regulated areas, competent-person oversight, engineering controls/work practices, communication and training. EPA NESHAP rules apply to certain regulated demolitions/renovations, with inspection, notification and emission control requirements; small residential building exceptions and state/local laws matter. Field record should include scope/material, boundary/containment inspection, pressure/air monitoring, crew, PPE/training, daily work, waste quantity, bag/manifest, transport, cleanup, clearance and release. Independent consultants/labs may perform monitoring/clearance; don't represent contractor-completed checklist as third-party clearance.

Hazards include fibers, exposure, contamination migration, respiratory equipment, slips/trips, heat, waste handling and occupant/public exposure. A failed containment or air result can require halt, repair, reclean, repeat sampling and owner notification. Waste shipment/records are regulated; forms and retention vary. The CRM cannot direct abatement procedure or determine safe reoccupancy.

## Customer communication and self-service

Owner/GC needs scope, notice/permit state, containment setup, access restrictions, progress, air sample/clearance status, stop-work/incident notices, waste documents, release and closeout. Sensitive health data and secure-site details should be restricted. Safety/containment failure notices require human review and prescribed channels; no automatic “safe” message based solely on workflow status.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Equipment includes containment materials, negative-air/HEPA units, decontamination stations, respirators, PPE, monitoring tools, waste containers, vehicles and access equipment. Track certification/inspection, equipment assignment, consumable replenishment, waste loads and disposal vendor. Roles include project manager, competent person, supervisor, abatement worker, safety officer, air monitor, inspector, transporter and clearance consultant. State training, licenses, medical surveillance and respirator-fit records apply with special privacy controls. Subcontractor and lab credentials may be required by owner.

## Reporting and operating measures

Candidate reports include notice deadlines, jobs by building/material, containment and monitoring completion, waste volume/manifests, air/clearance result, corrective action, worker/crew record, safety event, job duration, change recovery and project margin. Regulatory definitions and source-of-truth records must remain authority/owner-specific. Don't imply a generic compliance dashboard guarantees legal compliance.

## Existing software and operator evidence

FieldFlō specifically targets abatement subcontractors and describes waste/material/equipment tracking, change orders, time, safety and daily field reports. Formitize asbestos solutions include digital dockets, workforce/equipment/waste, approvals and offline mode. These support market-specific workflow fit but are product claims; no operator interview or independent review verified adoption, complaints or WTP. Horizontal project/accounting apps may coexist with regulators, labs and owner portals. Research remains partial until direct contractors describe common stacks, pricing, migration and actual record obligations.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Do not build a compliance authority. Ask jurisdiction, service type, work class, who performs survey/monitoring/clearance, notice and disposal needs, retention rules, privacy controls, offline requirements and owner portals. Prototype configurable project, work-zone, daily field, waste and closeout records. Validate actual operator stacks and sensitive record handling before recommending advanced defaults.
