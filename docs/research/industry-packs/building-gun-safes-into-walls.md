## Executive summary

This is a narrow carpentry/security installation: modify or build a wall cavity/recess, install a wall-mounted safe intended to store firearms, anchor it to suitable framing/masonry, restore the finish and hand over operation information. Scope may be safe installation into existing framing, custom niche/cabinet build-out, wall reinforcement, or coordination with a remodel. It is not simply a safe delivery or standalone freestanding gun-safe anchoring business.

Manufacturer instructions are product-specific: wall thickness, dimensions, anchoring, hidden obstructions, wall type and operation check must follow the selected model. Available U.S. operator evidence is sparse; internet sources include non-U.S. firearm-storage discussions and do not establish U.S. licensing or liability norms. The profile is partial because independent trade/operator workflow and software pricing evidence are missing. The CRM opportunity, if validated, is a careful site-survey, product/manual record, authorization, installation photo and customer handoff—not a universal safety/legal rules engine.

## Business model and customer segments

Potential buyers include homeowners, gun owners, safe dealers, remodelers, builders and property managers (subject to authorization and local law). Project types include in-wall safe installation in a wood-framed wall, recessed wall safe in masonry, custom trim/cabinet concealment, and associated finish repair. Safe-only businesses may subcontract a carpenter; carpenters may coordinate an authorized owner-supplied safe. Do not infer that every hidden safe is a gun safe or that installation inherently provides a tested fire/security rating.

Jobs are one-off and highly site/product-specific. A solo carpenter may perform a small opening/finish job; heavy, rated or masonry safes may require specialized equipment or safe/masonry professionals. Wall framing, load, utilities, plumbing, fire separation, exterior wall condition and access can make a proposed location unsuitable. Scope might require electrician, drywall/paint or structural professional and local permit review.

## Terminology and durable records

Capture customer/authorized contact, site/room/wall, safe manufacturer/model/serial where appropriate, size/weight, manual/version, wall type/thickness, framing/stud layout, rough opening, anchorage plan, utility/obstruction survey, permit/approval, install work order, anchoring/evidence, combination/key handoff status, finish repair and acceptance. Safe asset is persistent; wall opening dimensions, existing utilities and installation conditions are job records. Access to safe model, location and key/combination information is sensitive; store only necessary data, restrict access and never record a combination/key code in ordinary CRM notes.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks whether safe already purchased, manufacturer/model/manual, dimensions/weight, wall construction/location, visible access, desired appearance, existing electrical/plumbing/HVAC, rental/HOA/owner authorization, finish repair and scope of any safe handling. Field assessment is required; online quote should only qualify/request site inspection. Quote may include site survey, opening/framing, safe handling/installation, anchoring, blocking/reinforcement subject to approved method, debris removal, drywall/trim/paint, specialty trade and contingencies. Make exclusions explicit around structural changes, fire ratings, concealment/security certification and permits.

Flow: confirm authorization and product → review product manual/site → locate utilities/structure → assess whether wall and access suit work → confirm scope/permit/trade responsibilities → quote and approval → safe procurement/delivery → controlled opening and installation → operation/anchor inspection per manual → finish/cleanup → handoff and acceptance. Changes to wall condition or discovered services require stop and review, not an automated substitute decision.

## Pricing and commercial model

Price depends on safe model/weight, wall material, access, framing/reinforcement, masonry cutting, required patch/finish, equipment/two-person handling, electrical/plumbing relocation, permits and travel. This is a project-price inference; no representative service price or WTP was found. Manufacturers provide product pricing but it is not installation labor price. Software subscription prices for this niche were not found. A one-off owner-operator may use estimate/invoice tools, while a safe dealer could need inventory/order and service systems; both are hypotheses.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

The work is one-time, with follow-up for lock malfunction, adjustment, finish repair or new safe. Appointment length depends on wall/material and safe handling. Schedule safe delivery, building access, power/tool restrictions, other trades, finish cure and property authorization. Delay reasons include wrong safe dimensions, unsuitable wall, utility conflict, missing manual, access/weight handling, permit issue or owner not authorized. Return visits should be linked to installation record but sensitive lock data excluded.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Review selected manufacturer's current instructions and approved location; verify room/wall and obstructions by appropriate means; confirm framing/masonry and services before cutting; protect occupants/contents; open and reinforce only within authorized, competent scope; install/anchor as product specifies; check operation and clearances; repair finish; remove debris; photograph installation without exposing sensitive security details; provide manual/warranty and sign-off. A wall-safe manual warns that wall type and fit matter; another manufacturer's example says a load-bearing wall requires professional installation, but that is product-specific and not universal code advice.

Hazards include hidden electrical/plumbing/gas, load-bearing structure, dust/silica in masonry, lifting, power tools, finish dust and firearm access during work. Local firearm-storage rules and building regulations should be checked by the customer/provider; exact legal requirements were not established in this review. Rework could involve damage to utilities, unsafe or inadequate attachment, jammed door, wrong orientation/clearance, finish damage or unsuitable wall. Capture unresolved condition and referral; CRM checklist cannot certify theft/fire resistance or firearm-storage compliance.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs scope and authorization, assessment appointment, site preparation, approved manual/product, schedule, discovery/change notice, completion and warranty. Avoid showing exact safe location or sensitive security details in general customer portal exports, texts or team-wide job lists. Online intake should not solicit weapon inventory or lock combinations. Keep access limited to authorized staff and customer.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Equipment/materials may include framing lumber, anchors, safe-handling cart/lift, saw/drill, masonry tools, dust collection, drywall/trim/paint and wall patch materials. Safe units are normally ordered to a job, not general inventory, unless a dealer sells stocked products. Personnel may include carpenter, safe technician, helper, drywall finisher, electrician or structural professional. Required qualifications and insurance vary. Protect safe serial/location and customer data; a privacy-aware role/access policy is important even at low job volume.

## Reporting and operating measures

Candidate measures (inference only): assessment-to-approval, installation first-time completion, change due to wall condition, finish punch, safe handling incident, return visit, installation warranty and time/material variance. Low volume likely makes aggregate KPI value uncertain. No operator source confirmed common reporting practice.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Manufacturer installation manuals are the primary technical records; generic estimate/schedule/invoice systems may handle a small carpenter's administrative workflow. Search did not identify a U.S. wall-safe installation trade association, mature niche software or direct safe-installer operator WTP. Non-U.S. security installer regulations and gun-owner forums cannot establish U.S. market practice. Absence from this search is not evidence there is no software or established operator process. Mark software stack, complaints, packaging and willingness to pay unresolved.
