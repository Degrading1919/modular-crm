## Executive summary

Bridge painting is a specialist infrastructure coating contractor workflow involving surface assessment, containment, hazardous coating removal where required, surface preparation, coating application, environmental controls, inspection and acceptance. Work is usually awarded through public or infrastructure bids and may be performed as prime or specialty subcontractor. It is distinct from residential painting: access, traffic/rail closure, containment, waste handling, lead exposure, surface-preparation grade, coating-system specification and inspection evidence shape the project.

AMPP's QP-1/QP-2 accreditation materials describe contractor qualification for industrial/marine field coating and hazardous coating removal on structures including bridges; its Bridge Coating Inspector course covers preparation, application, containment and safety. OSHA's construction lead standard requires task-specific assessment and controls when lead exposure may occur. A profile can configure compliance-oriented project stages and evidence records, but cannot replace the employer's safety program, contract specifications or inspector authority. Public software WTP evidence is weak; a vendor case study provides seat-price math but is promotional.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include state/local DOTs, toll authorities, municipalities, rail/port authorities, bridge owners and prime contractors. Scope may cover full repaint, spot repair, coating maintenance, lead-containing coating removal, steel preservation, containment, access/scaffolding, inspection support, waste packaging and disposal. Bids are often contract/spec based, with prequalification and safety/quality requirements. Projects may be seasonal, multi-phase and constrained by lane closures, navigable waterways, rail possessions, weather and coating cure windows.

Firm scale ranges from small specialty crews to multi-project industrial contractors with estimators, safety managers, quality managers, project managers, competent persons, equipment and subcontracted inspection/access. Contract owner may supply design and coating specification; specialist contractor prices means/methods, labor, equipment, containment, waste and schedule risk. Local direct industrial maintenance may differ from large DOT bid administration.

## Terminology and durable records

Store owner/prime/subcontract, bridge/site/structure ID, contract and bid item, work zone/span/member, coating system, surface condition, existing-coating hazard/characterization, containment zone, access method, work plan, submittal, daily report, weather, surface profile/cleanliness, environmental reading, coating batch/lot, wet/dry film thickness, inspector hold point, test/deficiency, waste manifest and acceptance/closeout. Bridge and member identifiers can be long-lived assets; work zones, containment and coating lots are per-project records. Drawings/spec revisions and inspector direction should be versioned.

Sensitive records include worker exposure/medical/safety documentation, incident details, regulated waste records and security-sensitive infrastructure details. Restrict access according to law and contract. Store training/credential validity and company prequalification separately from a CRM checkbox that might imply compliance.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Lead intake requires structure/owner, solicitation, plans/specifications, site visit, existing coating information, access/traffic limits, work windows, containment class, environmental requirements, coating system, inspection/acceptance criteria, wage/union requirements and subcontract scope. Estimates are usually bid/project estimates or unit/lump-sum pay items. Include mobilization, containment, blasting/hand prep, coating materials, equipment/access, labor, testing, environmental monitoring, waste transport/disposal, traffic control, bonds/insurance, subcontracts, weather and schedule contingency. Tender addenda and written questions should create versioned estimate changes; scope directives need formal change documentation.

Plan mobilization, staging, containment erection, surface prep, inspector release, coating application, cure/inspection, containment removal and closeout by bridge zone and work window. Dependencies include certified/qualified crews, access/scaffold/containment, abrasive/coating supply, environmental conditions, owner closures, inspector availability, waste hauler and rail/traffic permit. Booking an ordinary appointment does not capture the project schedule.

## Pricing and commercial model

Cost drivers include surface area/condition, coating thickness/system, number of coats, access height/geometry, containment complexity, existing hazardous coating, removal method, abrasive recovery, waste classification/volume, traffic management, duration, weather window, inspection/testing and required credentials/bonds. Contracts may pay lump sum, area/quantity unit price or milestone/progress applications, often with retainage and formal change orders. Bid price is not software WTP.

TruQC's Thomas Industrial Coatings bridge case study includes vendor-reported savings and a $149/user licensing assumption in its comparison. Treat it as marketing illustration, not current verified list price or independent willingness-to-pay evidence. No independent operator report established price paid for a bridge-painting-specific CRM; industrial contractors may combine accounting/ERP, safety/quality tools and owner-required platforms, with cost controlled by project scale and contract mandate.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

The work is project-based, with recurring maintenance based on owner inspection/condition plans. Schedule by structure zone, crew qualifications, equipment and approved work windows. Weather, dew point/surface condition, temperature, humidity, wind, cure, traffic/rail closure, inspection signoff and containment boundaries constrain daily production. If work cannot proceed, record affected zone, actual condition, readings, reason, notification, idle labor/equipment and revised plan; contract notice and time-extension rules are not generic automated messages.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Preconstruction review and approved submittals → permits/closures and access → containment inspection → hazard controls and surface prep → inspector release → coating mix/application → environmental and film-thickness checks → cure/recoat verification → deficiency correction → accepted final inspection → waste disposition and closeout. Evidence includes daily site log, crew/credential, weather and surface readings, containment inspection/photos, exposure controls, surface prep profile/cleanliness, material batch/mix/pot life, application time, thickness/readings, inspector approval, waste records and closeout. Required measurements and sampling depend on contract specification.

OSHA 29 CFR 1926.62 governs construction work where employees may be occupationally exposed to lead; abrasive blasting, coating removal and containment may trigger controls based on conditions. AMPP QP-2 addresses hazardous coating removal contractor capability; QP-1 and inspector certifications are qualifications, not universal licenses. Fall, confined-space, respiratory, environmental and traffic hazards may also apply. Failed prep, coating defects, contamination, thickness/cure failure or breached containment may require containment repair, re-prep, recoat, retest, change claim or warranty work. Completion should require owner/inspector release, not simply field status.

## Customer communication and self-service

Owners and primes need submittal/qualification status, closure and mobilization plans, daily progress, inspection holds, deficiencies, change notices, schedule impact, environmental/waste documentation, pay application and closeout package. Secure document exchange may help, but regulatory and contractual submissions must follow specified channels. Human safety/quality review is essential; do not automate compliance certification or acceptance.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Equipment includes access systems, containment, blast/abrasive recovery, compressors, surface preparation tools, spray systems, ventilation, monitoring and waste containers. Materials include abrasives, coating components, PPE/respirators and containment supplies. Track batch/lot, shelf life, storage, mix ratios, supplier lead time, equipment maintenance and waste manifests by job when required. Roles may include superintendent/PM, foreman, blaster/painter, competent person, safety/quality staff, AMPP-qualified inspector, industrial hygienist, scaffold/access and hazardous-waste contractors. Credentials and union/state rules vary; time capture may need shift, zone and premium allocation.

## Reporting and operating measures

Contract-driven measures can include area prepared/coated per shift, acceptance/rework, inspection hold duration, coating thickness compliance, containment/environmental incidents, safety observations, waste quantity/manifest closure, closure-window utilization, change-order recovery, schedule/cost variance and gross margin by pay item. Several are inferred from project specs and quality tasks rather than standardized across contracts; allow project-defined metrics.

## Existing software and operator evidence

AMPP credentials and owner qualification systems, agency portals, drawing/document platforms, safety/quality apps, accounting and project controls may coexist. A vendor case study depicts one contractor using field forms for quality/safety/accounting documentation, but its outcomes are promotional. A bridge contractor's own services page confirms specialist bridge painting, hazardous coating removal and QP accreditations, but does not document its internal stack. In this niche, customer/agency systems may dictate submittals and reporting; a CRM should reduce duplicate entry and organize work evidence without becoming the official safety or engineering system.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

AMPP accreditation is a contractor qualification program and course—not a universal legal requirement. OSHA lead controls depend on exposure and tasks; local environmental and owner specifications add conditions. Vendor case-study productivity and price assumptions are marketing claims. Direct owner/operator discussions specific to bridge painters were sparse, so stack prevalence and actual subscription WTP remain low-confidence. Interview both DOT primes and small regional coating subs before defining defaults.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype project qualification, structure/work-zone records, submittals, daily field logs, inspection hold points, change tracking and closeout evidence. Onboarding must ask about owner/prime workflows, lead removal, containment, accreditation, closures, inspection specs, contract billing and crew roles. Make checklists and fields configurable; do not represent the product as a compliance or safety authority. Validate current document/audit capabilities with bridge contractors and DOT subcontractors before asserting a core gap.
