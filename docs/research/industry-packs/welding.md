## Executive summary

Welding contractors join, repair or fabricate metal items for construction, industrial maintenance, structural steel, pipe, trailers/equipment, agricultural and residential customers. Scope ranges from mobile repair to shop fabrication and contract production. Bids may require drawings, material/grade, weld process, procedure qualification, welder qualifications, inspection/NDE, coating, fit-up, delivery and installation. AWS certification and code-specific qualification are not interchangeable with a generic “welder certified” field; owner and applicable code determine requirements.

AWS describes performance qualification and procedure-specific WPS testing; OSHA details hot-work fire prevention and fume/gas hazards. Welding shop software vendors describe quote, job, operator schedule, hours, parts and WPS/quality records; public pricing and operator WTP are weak. A metalwork shop operator in a job-shop scheduling discussion reports needing production orders, operators, phases and dynamic rescheduling, with ERP and custom development as current stack; this is adjacent shop evidence rather than specifically a welding contractor. Profile remains partial until direct U.S. welding contractor operator evidence is gathered.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include GCs, steel fabricators, equipment owners, farms, manufacturers, utilities, oil/gas/pipeline firms, marine, transportation and residential clients. Services include mobile field repair, structural erection/welding, pipe welding, shop fabrication, railings/gates, machinery repair and production parts. Shop fabricators quote drawings/parts and manage production; mobile welders quote service call/time/material and travel; structural/pipeline work is governed by customer/spec qualification and inspections. Many businesses combine work but capacity, tools, records and risk vary.

Company size spans a solo mobile welder and small multi-process shop to certified structural/pipeline fabricators with estimators, shop planner, QC/inspection, production, shipping and field crews. Common dependencies are steel/material availability, drawings revision, welding procedure, qualified person, equipment, fit-up, inspection/NDE and downstream coating/erection.

## Terminology and durable records

Record customer/site, work order/project, part/assembly, material/grade/heat/lot, drawing revision, weld joint/location, WPS, welder qualification/process/position, fit-up, filler, consumable, equipment, inspection/NDE result, nonconformance, repair, hours, delivery and acceptance. Structural/pressure components need traceability by contract/code; small repairs may not. Site data includes access, equipment condition, hazard/hot-work authorization, dimensions, shutdown window and customer contact. Part/asset history may persist; job materials, weld maps, readings and inspection are per-project.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks repair vs fabrication, onsite vs shop, material/grade/thickness, drawings/photos, dimensions, load/pressure/structural function, environment, customer code/spec, required WPS/qualifications/inspection, access, shutdown and delivery. A field site visit or drawing review is needed for many jobs. Quote may be time/material, minimum callout, per part, labor hours, material, setup, consumables, fit-up, machining, inspection/NDE, coating, travel, mobilization and after-hours. Maintain drawing/estimate version, assumptions, qualified scope and customer-approved change.

Flow: RFQ/site inspection → material/design/spec review → estimate and schedule → procurement/fit-up → weld under applicable procedure → inspect/test and repair if needed → coat/deliver/install → acceptance/invoice. For field hot work, permit and hazard review precede welding. A production job needs operator/machine assignment and sequence; mobile repair needs dispatch, site access and safety coordination.

## Pricing and commercial model

Cost drivers include process/material, weld length/complexity, thickness, position/access, fit-up/prep, WPS/inspection/NDE, qualified labor, shop vs field, travel, consumables, tooling, machining, coating, urgency, shutdown, certs and documentation. Structural/industrial contracts may use bid schedule, milestones and retainage; repairs may use service call plus labor/material. AWS standard documentation is sold at $306 member/$408 nonmember for B2.1/B2.1M:2026; this is a standards purchase price, not welding software or contractor WTP. No independent operator reported CRM spend. Software WTP remains unclear across mobile repair and certified shop work.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Shop fabrication can create repeat customer orders with production stages; field repair is episodic and urgent. Schedule welders by process/qualification, equipment, material, WPS, shop capacity, inspection availability and customer shutdown window. Custom part times range widely; shop planners may need multi-phase dynamic replanning. Delays include wrong drawing/material, failed fit-up, consumable/equipment, worker qualification, QC hold, weather/access, permit or inspection/NDE. For mobile jobs, route planning and customer ETA may matter; for shops, production capacity is more central.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm job scope/material, drawing revision, process and applicable WPS/qualification; inspect tools/cables/gas; conduct hazard review and hot-work permit/fire watch as needed; control ignition/fumes/ventilation; prepare/fit-up; weld; inspect/testing per contract; repair nonconformance; coat/clean; document and hand over. AWS certification illustrates performance qualification in relation to a WPS. OSHA construction fire prevention requires moving/protecting combustibles, fire extinguishing readiness and fire watch when needed; fume, gases, UV, shock, burns and noise vary by process/material. Do not auto-qualify welding work based on CRM records.

Evidence includes drawing/WPS revision, material/heat, welder identity/qualification, weld/joint location, consumables, inspection/NDE, hot-work permit, fire watch, ventilation, equipment check, repair history, photos, delivery and acceptance. Rework may be crack/porosity, incomplete fusion, fit-up mismatch, distortion, drawing change, inspection rejection or field access. Preserve hold/repair decisions and inspector acceptance, not just technician completion.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs quote status, drawing/spec questions, material lead time, mobilization, outage/shutdown and arrival, inspection hold, change approval, repair/delivery and certification package. A portal can exchange drawings and approved quote; weld procedure and safety decisions require authorized staff. Customer specs, engineering documents and proprietary part drawings may be confidential.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Material includes plate, shapes, pipe, rod/filler, wire, gas, electrodes, consumables, coatings and PPE. Track heat/lot, receiving certs, stock, job reservation and remnants when code/customer requires. Equipment includes welding machines, generators, torches, positioners, cranes, grinders, fume extraction, test gauges and mobile trucks. Workers include welder/fitter, helper, shop planner, supervisor, QC inspector, CWI/NDE contractor, engineer and project manager. Qualification records are process/code-specific and expire/renew by program; union and pay practices vary.

## Reporting and operating measures

Potential measures (inferred) include quote response/win rate, production hours vs estimate, utilization by process/machine, material yield, first-pass acceptance, repair/rework, NDE hold, delivery on-time, field call response and margin. Track by process, work type, welder, customer, material and code/spec. Small mobile business may need only quote/dispatch/hours; a certified shop may need traceability and production scheduling.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Welding/shop packages market quoting, WPS, job tracking, production schedule and quality records. A shop management product page targets owner-operators and small shops, while broad ERP scales to more formal manufacturing. One metalwork SME discussion describes lack of convincing shop scheduling software and requirements for production orders, operator assignment, phases and dynamic replanning; the original thread author is a new software vendor, so treat concern as lead rather than independent operator evidence. AWS and OSHA establish technical/safety framework, not CRM adoption. Public prices for targeted welding software and actual WTP remain unresolved; mark research partial.

Prototype separate field-call and shop/project flows with configurable customer specs.
