## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Mirror installers sell measured, fabricated, transported, and mounted glass for vanities, bathrooms, gyms/dance studios, closets, commercial interiors, and decorative applications. Large panels usually require site measurement before fabrication: wall plumb/flatness, substrate, penetrations, panel size, access route, edge treatment, backing, mounting support, and moisture/impact exposure affect scope. Industry guidance calls for ventilation behind mirror backing; commercial specifications use manufacturer-recommended mastic plus mechanical support and require protection/inspection.

**Inference:** A CRM workflow should preserve room/wall measurements and photos, exact approved drawing/material/edge/cutout, measure-to-order and fabrication status, access/lift/crew requirements, install method, and customer acceptance. This is a project-install business more than recurring maintenance. Existing estimate, project scheduling, job checklist, communications, invoicing, and file storage capabilities appear sufficient; no core gap is established.

**Uncertain:** The candidate does not specify frameless custom mirrors, stock mirror hanging, shower/architectural glazing, or commercial glazing scope. Local code, safety backing, electrical work near illuminated mirrors, and who fabricates must be confirmed by operator and site.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include homeowners, remodelers, interior designers, gyms/dance studios, hospitality operators, builders, and commercial property managers. A small job may hang one stock mirror; custom wall mirrors usually include measure, fabrication coordination, delivery, mounting, finish inspection, and possibly removal/disposal of existing glass. Designers and general contractors may supply specifications and schedule coordination. Repeat work can arise from builder/designer referrals, multi-unit construction, gym chains, or commercial remodels; household work is generally one-time.

Common triggers are a remodel, new vanity, gym buildout, dance studio, damaged mirror, or design feature. Large fragile panels restrict travel/handling and may need two-person crews, glass racks, suction cups, protective packaging, and careful access planning. Public site forms can capture room, approximate dimensions, photos, address, and requested date, but should route unusual dimensions and large panels to a site survey rather than promise a firm quote from rough measurements.

## Terminology and durable records

- **Customer/site:** homeowner, designer, GC, property manager; service address, room/area, parking/loading and elevator/stair/access instructions, occupied-site restrictions, work hours, and finish-protection needs.
- **Persistent asset:** installed mirror assembly by room/location, with panel dimensions, glass/backing type, edge, mounting approach, fabrication source, install date, warranty and prior repair. A single small mirror may be only a job item, not a separately managed asset.
- **Job inputs:** field template, dimensions at multiple points, wall condition/plumb, substrate, outlet/sconce/cabinet locations and cutouts, ceiling/baseboard conditions, moisture/impact exposure, access path, photos, approved drawing, hardware/adhesive, and lead time.
- **Sensitive/safety data:** access instructions and occupancy notes may be restricted to assigned crew. Mark fragile-glass handling, overhead work, and site-specific hazards. Avoid storing unnecessary household or access details.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Typical work: stock mirror hanging; custom vanity/wall mirror measure-and-install; gym/dance wall panels; framed/decorative mirrors; mirror removal and replacement; cutouts/bevel/polished edges; safety-backed options; repair or resecure existing panels. Include a scope boundary for wall repair, electrical changes, fabrication, delivery, demolition, and disposal.

Intake requests address, room/use, photos of the whole wall and obstructions, approximate dimensions, substrate/finish, number of panels, desired edge/frame, existing mirror removal, building access, and timing. A site measure confirms fit before order. The written quote should state assumptions and what happens if site dimensions, substrate, or wall preparation differ. Operator sources show measured quotes and quote forms, not universal fixed pricing.

Appointments depend on site survey, fabrication delivery, crew availability, and access windows. Some jobs need two visits (measure then install); larger/commercial projects may coordinate with other trades. Deposit, change-order, cancellation, and warranty terms are operator-specific and should be recorded from the signed proposal.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Mostly project scheduling, with survey, fabrication/lead-time, and install milestones. Repeat work is driven by designers, builders, property portfolios, or multi-unit sites rather than household subscriptions. Reserve the required trained crew and glass-handling equipment; travel route optimization is secondary for low-volume work and may matter for multiple crews/site visits. Rescheduling a measure or fabrication delay should not silently preserve the original install promise. Track return trip causes such as wrong dimensions, damaged shipment, incomplete substrate, or access failure.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Confirm address, room, approved drawing, access and site readiness.
2. Survey and record dimensions, level/plumb, substrate, penetrations, and clearances; photograph condition.
3. Confirm glass type, cutouts, finish, backing, mounting system, and fabrication approval before ordering.
4. Protect floors/fixtures, transport and handle glass with appropriate crew/equipment, inspect delivery for damage.
5. Prepare wall only within contracted scope; install using specified support/adhesive and manufacturer directions.
6. Verify alignment, support, edge/finish, clearances, clean-up, and customer acceptance; capture completion images and care/warranty instructions.

National Glass Association's GANA manual discusses air space/ventilation behind mirror backing; Ohio public-project specifications give one example of mastic plus mechanical fastening and manufacturer directions. These are useful technical evidence, not a universal code statement. Noncompletion reasons include measurement/material mismatch, unsafe handling conditions, unsuitable wall, damage, blocked access, missing fabrication, or out-of-scope electrical/wall repair. Rework should record the failure and responsibility before another visit is booked.

## Customer communication and self-service

Send survey confirmation, quote and assumptions, approval/deposit request, fabrication/lead-time update, install window, access/preparation instructions, delay notice, completion evidence, invoice, and care/warranty material. Customer self-service can collect photos, approve a quote, select a survey window, and retrieve documents. Do not automate a final date until custom fabrication and site readiness are confirmed. Customer notification preferences and consent should be honored.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Glass racks, suction cups, gloves, edge protection, levels/laser, measuring/template tools, drills, approved mastic, clips/J-channel/standoffs, fasteners, drop cloths, and cleanup supplies are typical candidates; the actual kit depends on panel and mounting. Mirror/fabrication orders should retain supplier, product/batch, size, finish, backing and delivery condition. Large panels require trained handling and possibly two installers; electrician, wall repair, and fabrication may be subcontracted. Inventory may be optional for a small installer and important to multi-crew operations carrying standard mounting materials.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures to validate with an operator: quote-to-approval time; site-survey-to-install lead time; estimate variance; fabrication damage/redo rate; first-visit completion; return trips by cause; material and labor margin by job; on-time arrival; and customer acceptance. These are proposed measures, not evidence that operators currently track them.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Installer pages agree on site measurement, exact cutouts, surface condition and mounting selection; industry/public specifications add backing ventilation and support requirements. Published per-area service ranges are local budgeting examples only. **High confidence:** large custom mirrors need measured intake and delivery/install coordination. **Medium:** crews will benefit from an asset record when serving commercial or repeat sites. **Low:** common software stack and subscription WTP. Confirm whether the target is stock-hanging, custom mirror walls, or broader glazing; check code and licensing locally.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype survey-to-install records around address/room, measured wall, obstruction photos, approved material/cutout, fabrication status, access, crew and acceptance. Ask whether work is stock, custom, commercial or gym/studio; who fabricates; who handles electrical/wall repair; and what local safety/code rules apply. Keep material, edge, mounting, backing, warranty, quote amounts and permit/licensing requirements tenant-configurable.
