## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Tree care spans pruning, removals, plant health care (PHC), cabling/bracing, transplanting, consulting, and storm response. A typical loop is inquiry and site assessment, tree/worksite risk review, measured or scoped estimate, crew/equipment assignment, controlled worksite execution, debris handling, closeout, and sometimes treatment or inspection follow-up. Large removals and storm work are project/urgent work; PHC and scheduled pruning can recur. OSHA documents fall, struck-by, chainsaw/chipper, electrical, and traffic hazards. Arborist-specific incumbents market estimating, proposals, scheduling, job costing, and resource management, while operator reports identify cost, setup complexity, offline access, glitches, and support as tradeoffs.

**Inference:** The highest-value pack defaults are a property plus individually serviceable tree records, estimate-first intake, tree/worksite hazard review, crew and equipment dispatch, job photos, and optional PHC/treatment follow-up. A configurable shared job workflow can represent the need; evidence does not establish that tree-specific database tables or automated safety decisions are required. Keep climbing methods, credentials, permits, and safety procedures under company and jurisdictional control.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential owners request pruning, hazardous limb removal, removals/stump work, diagnosis, and storm cleanup. Commercial, HOA, institutional, utility, and municipal buyers may commission recurring inventories/pruning cycles, risk assessments, or larger bids; procurement, insurance, and formal scopes grow in importance. Leads arrive via referrals, search, property managers, and storm events. Urgency ranges from routine pruning to imminent hazards. Seasonality varies with climate and species; storms create unpredictable surges. Service radius is bounded by crew/equipment travel, disposal access, and route economics. Solo arborists may consult or climb with a helper; larger firms divide sales/arborist assessment, climber, ground crew, bucket/crane/chipper operators, PHC staff, and office dispatch.

## Terminology and durable records

Use customer/property, tree, work order/job, estimate/proposal, crew, arborist, pruning/removal, PHC, stump grinding, rigging, drop zone, chipper, and closeout. Store each tree as an optional persistent customer asset: species/common name, location/map pin, approximate size/condition, defects/targets, prior work, photos, treatment/cabling history, and relevant owner/municipal constraints. Property records need access, slope/terrain, structures/landscape to protect, overhead/underground utilities where known, parking/traffic constraints, disposal/staging area, and customer instructions. Tree condition and hazards must be reassessed per visit; do not treat old notes as a current safety clearance. Crew-only notes may include hazards/access and should be permission controlled. Estimates, permissions, permits, and PHC records may be customer-visible; private safety notes and credentials should not be.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Potential menu: pruning, removal, stump grinding, storm cleanup, cabling/bracing, PHC diagnosis/treatment, planting/transplanting, risk assessment, and consulting. These vary by qualifications and equipment. Intake asks address, tree count/type/size and photos, requested outcome, proximity to structures/lines/roads, access and parking, urgency, prior work, and referral source. Site visits are common when scope, condition, rigging, cleanup, or risk affects price. Quotes may be fixed by scoped work, time/material, equipment, disposal, or bid; allow options and exclusions. Approval/deposit, permits, utility coordination, and change orders depend on scope/locality. Routine PHC can be scheduled windows/routes; removal dates depend on crew, equipment, weather, access, and permits. Web forms should collect photos and triage urgency, then send uncertain/high-risk work to arborist review instead of instant booking.

## Pricing and commercial model

Inputs include tree size/condition/species, access and rigging difficulty, proximity to targets/utilities, crew/equipment and mobilization, disposal/log hauling, stump work, treatment/materials, urgency, permits, and cleanup. Commercial/municipal bids may include inventory, service cycles, insurance and specifications. No universal price formula is supported. **Software willingness-to-pay:** Arborgold’s current US-facing site says plans start at US$119/month (accessed 2026-09-24); this is vendor list positioning, not an operator’s actual spend or willingness to pay. Reddit/operator reports describe arborist software as expensive and a two-person business rejecting SingleOps after a 12-month commitment and setup/offline concerns; anonymous and not prevalence evidence. Service-job anecdotes are not used as CRM budget evidence.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Routine pruning/PHC follow-up can recur on arborist-defined cycles, while removals are one-time and storm response is priority work. Assign by qualifications, crew composition, climbing/bucket/crane/chipper/stump equipment, estimated duration, disposal capacity, access, and area. Multi-day jobs and coordination with utility owners or property access may apply. Weather, wind, lightning, heat, saturated ground, and storm volume can force changes; avoid auto-promising precise times for storm work. Return visits may reflect treatment, cleanup, repair, or customer concerns. Route optimization is useful for small PHC/pruning visits but cannot replace dispatch judgment for equipment-heavy jobs.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Suggested sequence: arrive and confirm scope/property; conduct a current tree and worksite hazard assessment; brief crew and establish work/drop zones and traffic control; verify equipment/PPE and utility coordination; execute approved scope; remove debris and protect property; record work, materials and before/after photos; note changed conditions, additional work, and customer acceptance; close out/invoice or schedule follow-up. OSHA sources describe falls, falling objects, electrocution, chainsaw/chipper and vehicle hazards, and recommend pre-work site/tree assessment. U.S. federal requirements apply alongside relevant state plans and other standards; local permit/line-clearance rules vary. App checklists can prompt documentation but must not be represented as safety training, risk assessment, or legal compliance. Noncompletion includes unsafe weather/site, inaccessible property, utility conflict, scope change, equipment failure, customer deferral, or permit delay; billability is policy dependent. Document damage, complaint, callback, and warranty review.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need estimate options, access/parking preparation, work date/window, weather or utility delay, debris/wood disposition, treatment instructions, completion photos, invoice, and follow-up recommendations. Forms and portal can submit photos, approve quotes/change orders, share documents, and pay. Do not send treatment or safety conclusions without qualified review; do not announce a confirmed storm arrival until dispatched. Record contact preferences and applicable messaging consent in shared controls.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Relevant equipment may include chainsaws, climbing/rigging systems, aerial lifts/bucket trucks, cranes, chippers, stump grinders, trucks/trailers, PPE, and PHC sprayers. Assign high-value equipment and inspection/maintenance status where the shared product supports it. Consumables include fuel, chain, rope, rigging, PPE, and treatment materials; PHC inventory and application records are conditional on those services. Crew skills/credentials and role assignment matter; payroll, time, travel/mileage, subcontractors, and production pay practices vary. OSHA/TCIA evidence supports serious safety responsibilities, not one universal staffing model or pay arrangement.

## Reporting and operating measures

Observed incumbent feature claims include job costing, estimates, schedule/resource management and service history. Candidate measures: estimate conversion/value, planned vs actual crew/equipment hours, job margin, revenue by service/tree/property/territory/source, route/travel time, equipment utilization, callbacks/damage claims, PHC follow-up, and safety events/near misses. Treat safety metrics as controlled internal records, not leaderboard targets. These are proposed reporting cuts; public operator evidence does not establish standard KPI definitions.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Arborgold targets tree/lawn/landscape firms and advertises customer records, estimates, scheduling, job management, invoicing, resources, and plans from $119/month. SingleOps is mentioned by arborist operators for tree-specific proposals/mapping but also cost/contract and setup burden. Jobber is a simpler horizontal alternative; an OPE forum participant said the tree company had used Jobber and Arborgold and switched after a glitchy app. Another Reddit thread describes a two-person operator rejecting SingleOps over setup, offline access, and a locked 12-month term. These first-person but anonymous reports are small, potentially dated samples; they conflict with positive comments praising specialty features. The likely stack is field CRM + accounting (often QuickBooks) + maps/measurement + forms/photos + phone/text, but those combinations are not proven universal. No replicated evidence establishes willingness to pay for a new CRM.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree that tree work spans both skilled hazard-managed field work and office estimating/dispatch. Vertical applications add value for proposals, job cost, inventory, and resource management; operators also report specialty platforms can be expensive, complicated, unreliable, or inaccessible offline. Evidence is limited by anonymous forums, vendor-authored claims, and differing company scale. **Uncertain (medium):** PHC frequency/revenue share and municipal/commercial work share. **Uncertain (high):** local permit, licensing, and energized-line requirements; this profile makes no national claim. **Inference:** online quote intake should hand off for review when tree risk/scope is unclear.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a tree asset linked to a property, quote-first service request, assessment/estimate/approval flow, equipment-aware scheduling, configurable worksite review, job photos, completion/invoice, and optional PHC follow-up. Keep tree fields, services, recurrence, deposit/change-order policy, permits, credentials, equipment, safety checklist, and customer-facing proof tenant-configurable. Ask which services and equipment are offered, whether work is recurring or emergency-driven, crew count/roles, PHC/treatment, whether estimates require a site visit, and whether field staff need offline capture. Preserve the separation between tree work and lawn/landscape work; do not turn checklists into a safety guarantee.
