## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Excavation can range from short residential grading, trenching, drainage or foundation work to multi-crew commercial sitework and utility projects. Scope and cost depend on plans/quantities, soil and groundwater, site access, equipment, haul/export/disposal, utility conflicts, weather and safety controls. OSHA requires protective systems/competent-person practices under defined conditions; the utility locate process is jurisdictionally administered.

**Inference:** The high-value operation is a project workflow linking estimate assumptions and quantities to active site conditions, machine/crew hours, locates/permits, safety checks, trucking/materials, changes and final quantity/cost reconciliation. This is materially different from septic installation (system-specific permitting/service records) and from general contractors coordinating all trades.

## Business model and customer segments

- Customers include homeowners/property owners, builders/GCs, developers, civil/utility contractors, municipalities and commercial/industrial facilities. Work may be subcontracted, bid from plans, time-and-materials, unit-price or equipment/operator hire.
- Services can include grading, site prep, trenching, drainage, foundations, demolition, earth moving, utility excavations, hauling/backfill and finish grade. Septic excavation is only a subset and requires septic-system workflow.
- Operators range from one-machine owner-operators doing residential jobs to heavy-civil firms with estimators, project managers, multiple crews/trucks, surveyors and equipment fleets. Contract form, revenue timing and compliance burden change with scale.
- Triggers include new construction, utility repair/install, drainage/landscape changes, site development and emergency repair. Weather, freeze/thaw and ground conditions create regional seasonality.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms include cut/fill, grade, elevation, benchmark, trench, spoil, haul-off/export, backfill, compaction, mobilization, equipment hours, locate ticket/811, competent person, protective system, dewatering, change order and as-built.
- **Service-location data:** property/site boundary, access/haul route, grade/survey plans, soil/groundwater assumptions, utilities/markings, adjacent structures, traffic/environmental restrictions and jurisdiction.
- **Persistent customer assets:** project/site and infrastructure installed/modified (if assigned ongoing service); machines/fleet are contractor assets. One-time job records include marked utilities, soil observations, daily quantities, tickets, photos and test/compaction reports.
- Safety-critical: current utility marks, trench depth/soil/water conditions, competent-person inspection, protective system, access/egress, overhead/underground hazards and spoil/equipment setbacks. Retain evidence and human authorization; software cannot determine trench safety by itself.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Qualify drawings/scope, location/quantities, access, work type, utilities/locate status, soil/geotech assumptions, disposal destination, equipment needs, schedule window, permits and required inspections.
- Quotes can use takeoff quantities/cut-fill estimates, machine/operator hourly rates, unit prices, time/material, mobilization, trucking, material, disposal and allowances. Unknown rock, groundwater, buried obstructions and utility conflicts require assumptions/change order language.
- **Observed evidence:** excavation software articles distinguish small residential work from commercial sitework requiring earthwork takeoff and machine-control solutions; equipment/rental contracts can list machine/operator rates and minimum hours. No one price basis is universal.
- Booking is a multi-day site/resource commitment with crew, equipment, trucks, locates and predecessor trade dependencies. Emergency work and locate/permit restrictions change dispatch priority.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Cost drivers include equipment ownership/rental and utilization, operator/labor hours, mobilization, fuel, truck/haul cycles, material, disposal, quantity and productivity assumptions, compaction/testing, permits, site conditions and weather risk.
- Residential jobs may use hourly/day-rate or scoped fixed bids; public/commercial jobs may use unit-price schedules, plans/specs and progress billing. Distinguish equipment rental dry-hire from machine+operator service.
- Example direct service pricing: Woods Excavating in Maine publishes equipment rates with operator/fuel and a four-hour minimum, and separately notes daily/weekly/monthly rates without operator or fuel. These are one firm's prices, accessed 2026-09-24, not market benchmarks.
- No operator statements about software willingness-to-pay found. Advertised software subscriptions do not establish CRM budgets.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Work is project/dispatch based; no common recurrence. Repeat commercial maintenance and utility emergency agreements are conditional.
- Match job to machine attachments/capacity, trained operator, truck/hauler, survey/control equipment, safety support and work sequence. Track mobilization, standby, fuel, mechanical downtime, locates, weather, inspection and haul/disposal windows.
- Route optimization applies to fleet mobilization/haul cycles more than service-call routes. An equipment breakdown or unsuitable soil can reshape multiple crews and project dates.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Review plans/quantities, scope, access and job hazard/environmental constraints; estimate and document assumptions.
2. Confirm permits, utility locates (including private utility checks where needed), site marks, equipment/operator and traffic/erosion controls before digging.
3. Mobilize; conduct competent-person inspection and protective-system decisions where required; log changing conditions, quantities, hours, haul tickets and photos.
4. Excavate/grade to plan; stop/escalate utility conflict, unsafe conditions, unexpected rock/water/soil or scope changes; obtain approval before extra work where feasible.
5. Test/inspect/backfill/compact/restore as contract requires; reconcile quantities, tickets, as-builts and customer/GC acceptance.
- **Observed evidence:** OSHA identifies cave-in, utility and atmospheric hazards and competent-person inspections; 811 is the U.S. call-before-dig system but state rules/time differ.
- Noncompletion causes include expired/missing marks, weather, access, unsafe conditions, utility conflicts, failed inspection, equipment failure, permit delay, unexpected conditions and customer/GC hold. Stop-work, emergency, safety, and billing decisions require authorized humans.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Before: scope/plan review, locates, access, utility shutoff/traffic, schedule, weather assumptions and site prep. During: arrival, daily progress/quantities, discoveries, delays, safety hold and change orders. After: quantities, tickets, as-builts, test/compaction/inspection records, invoice and restoration.
- Public quote intake can gather site/address/plans/photos but should hand off to site survey and utility/safety review. Automatic start confirmations cannot imply mark/permit readiness.
- Communication may span owner, GC, utility, inspector, trucking and environmental stakeholders. No evidence establishes one channel/consent default.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Equipment includes excavators, dozers, loaders, skid steers, compactors, trench boxes/shoring, pumps, trucks, attachments, survey/grade tools and PPE. Fleet size and rentals vary.
- Fuel, parts, rental/vendor bills, disposal, aggregate/pipe and equipment maintenance can materially affect job costing. Machine hours and idle/broken time should be distinguishable from labor time.
- Workforce includes operators, laborers, competent persons, truckers, surveyors and subcontractors; certifications, CDL and safety requirements vary by equipment/job/jurisdiction.

## Reporting and operating measures

- **Observed evidence:** construction software exposes equipment on-site/in-use/idle hours, deliveries, installed quantities, timesheets and cost codes; excavation providers stress machine hours, job costs and quantities.
- Candidate measures: estimate vs actual cost/quantity, cubic yards moved/exported, machine productive/idle/down hours, labor/haul cycles, fuel, disposal, mobilization, change recovery, schedule variance, safety/locate completion, inspection and job margin. Definitions must align with contract.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- Heavy civil workflows often need dedicated takeoff/bid, field tracking, equipment maintenance/telematics and construction accounting tools; smaller residential work may fit general job/estimate/invoice products. Vendor comparison articles describe a clear size/scope split but are not neutral adoption surveys.
- OSHA/811 sources establish safety/locate processes. Woods Excavating's published rate sheet is direct contractor/operator evidence of pricing boundaries (operator/fuel included vs dry rental excluded); it is one regional provider.
- No independent owner interview, representative software stack, common complaint frequency or direct software WTP evidence was found. Product marketing often overgeneralizes “excavation” across residential and heavy-civil work.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

OSHA evidence is authoritative for U.S. worker protections but does not define all contractor business practices. The utility locate lead time in OSHA training material is not a universal operational promise; 811 center/state rules vary. Software pages disagree by implicitly targeting small service contractors versus heavy-civil firms, reflecting real size/scope differences. Contractor pricing example is one Maine firm. No direct operator software-pain interviews or representative pricing/WTP survey found. Confidence high for safety distinction, medium for broad workflow, low for stack/WTP.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask if the operator does small residential excavation, utility trenching, commercial earthwork, equipment-with-operator hire, or combinations. Prototype a project/site with estimates and change orders, locate/permit status, daily reports, quantities, machines/operators/hours, tickets and closeout documents. Keep estimates, safety checks, forms, reporting units and equipment fleet configurable. Use a specialist integration when the tenant needs GPS machine control or telematics; do not imply native support. Safety/stop-work status requires qualified human input.
