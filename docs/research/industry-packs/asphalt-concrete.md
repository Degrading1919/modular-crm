## Executive summary

The inventory label combines two related but distinct scopes: asphalt paving/surfacing and cast-in-place concrete work. Both sell measured, crew-and-equipment-intensive projects, but the work plans and production units differ. Asphalt work depends on haul/plant coordination, surface preparation, mix temperature, paver/roller capacity, weather and traffic control. Concrete work depends on formwork, reinforcement, placement/finishing, curing and inspection. The shared operating loop is lead or bid → site/plan takeoff → estimate and scope → material/equipment/crew coordination → field production and quality evidence → measured acceptance/progress billing → closeout and warranty.

**Observed evidence:** FHWA paving inspection guidance tracks mix and surface temperature, tack coat, weather, plant-to-field handoff, quality assurance and quantities/payment records. A concrete owner/operator account describes takeoffs, labor pricing, production, crews, equipment, materials, GC communication, change orders and pay applications. **Inference:** A modular contractor pack can configure estimates, project stages, checklists, job costing and reports; paving production, concrete placement and public contract requirements should remain separate templates. Small residential flatwork firms may quote and schedule directly, while road contractors work through agency specifications, primes and subcontractors.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential customers buy driveways, patios, sidewalks, aprons, resurfacing or repairs. Commercial buyers include property managers, developers, general contractors and facility operators; public work is bought by municipalities, counties, states and DOTs through bid packages. Concrete firms may specialize in foundations, slabs, curb/gutter, sidewalks, paving, retaining structures or decorative work. Asphalt firms may pave lots/roads, patch, mill-and-overlay, sealcoat or stripe; these should not be assumed to be one service.

Work may be a one-day driveway, multi-day commercial lot, phased roadway contract or recurring maintenance agreement. Climate and plant/aggregate distance affect season and service radius. Crew scale varies from owner plus a few finishers to multi-crew contractors with estimators, project managers, dispatch, equipment and safety staff. Public and larger GC work entails submittals, schedule-of-values/pay applications, change orders, certified documentation and acceptance requirements; small direct jobs more often use a signed proposal, deposit/final invoice and warranty terms.

## Terminology and durable records

Store customer/buyer, service site, project/contract, bid package, plan/specification revision, estimate and alternates, change order, work area/phase, crew/equipment assignment, material ticket, daily report, inspection/test result, quantity installed, pay item, invoice/pay application and closeout/warranty. Site fields can include address/GPS, access and traffic-control constraints, surface dimensions/area, existing substrate and condition, grades/drainage, utility conflicts, weather exposure, permits, traffic windows and owner/GC contacts. These project conditions are not persistent customer assets; pavement segments or facilities become assets only when the customer asks for maintenance history.

Keep drawings, takeoff measurements, bid assumptions, mix/product specifications, delivery tickets, batch/plant records, temperature observations, test results, before/during/after photos, daily crew/equipment logs and signed change approvals tied to a specific revision. Do not imply that generic CRM fields replace engineering plans, contract records, regulated safety records or owner quality systems.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake needs scope type, buyer and billing contact, site address/access, plans or photos, dimensions/quantity, desired schedule, existing surface/subgrade condition, drainage/grade, traffic/use constraints, specification/finish, utilities and tender deadlines. A site visit or plan takeoff is common for anything beyond simple small residential work. Quotes may be lump sum, unit-price/pay-item, measured quantity, time-and-materials or cost-plus; estimates account for labor, equipment, mix/concrete, aggregate/rebar/forms, trucking, disposal, traffic control, mobilization, subcontracting, weather risk, overhead and contingency. Maintain estimate revisions, alternates and exclusions; only approved scope changes should update contract value and quantities.

Asphalt scheduling must coordinate plant production, truck cycle, paver/rollers, crew, temperature/weather and traffic closures. Concrete requires form/rebar readiness, concrete supply, pump/placing/finishing capacity, inspection and curing/protection windows. Suppliers and subs may include asphalt plants, ready-mix plants, quarries, trucking, saw-cut/milling, striping, traffic control, testing labs and excavation. A calendar entry alone cannot represent these dependencies; multi-day phases, quantities and hold points are useful candidate capabilities.

## Pricing and commercial model

Prices vary by installed area/volume/tonnage, thickness/lift, mix/design, surface prep, demolition/haul-off, base/subgrade, reinforcing/formwork, access, mobilization, distance to plant, traffic management, testing, time restrictions, material escalation and prevailing requirements. A bid schedule may use unit prices and estimated quantities, with payment based on accepted installed quantities. Change orders often capture concealed conditions, scope changes, quantity growth, extra mobilization or schedule impact; progress billing is common on larger work while residential jobs may use deposit/milestones/final payment.

Bitumio publicly lists $149/month per admin, estimator, office manager or foreman; employees using only mobile schedule/job details are listed free. This is an advertised vendor price, not evidence that contractors pay that amount or that it covers a firm's entire software budget. A concrete owner thread shows a small business using an Excel estimate plus Outlook calendar and PlanSwift; the user called PlanSwift useful but expensive and recalled paying about $1,500 (anecdotal, date/context in source). These observations suggest different WTP constraints by scale and bid volume; do not infer willingness to pay from project value.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most paving and placement jobs are project-based; patching, sealcoating and facility maintenance can recur annually or by condition. Weather, temperature, cure, plant supply, delivery cycles, traffic permits, inspections and downstream trades determine the feasible work window. Crew/equipment matching matters: paver/rollers/trucks differ from concrete forms, pumps, finishers and saws. Rain, cold, hot mix delay, failed inspection, plant outage, access or late predecessor work may stop a project. Record impact, quantity placed, reason, affected resources, buyer notice and remobilization plan.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Typical flow is preconstruction/spec review → layout and utility/traffic confirmation → site prep → material/delivery verification → placement → compaction/finish → inspection/testing and quantity capture → cleanup/open-to-traffic or cure protection → acceptance and closeout. FHWA's guidance identifies weather restrictions, tack/surface condition, mixture temperature, paving sequence, quality records and payment quantities as roadway inspection concerns. Field evidence can include location/area, delivery tickets, mix/lot, surface and mix temperatures, lift/thickness, compaction/finish, test reports, weather, daily quantities, inspection holds, photos and signed owner/GC acceptance.

Hazards can include moving equipment/traffic, hot asphalt, silica from cutting/concrete work, lifting, noise and excavation. OSHA's construction silica standard is relevant to concrete/stone cutting and grinding; controls depend on task and jurisdiction. Failed density/finish/grade, cold joint, drainage issue, unsuitable base, cracking or out-of-spec mix can require repair/removal, retest, claim or warranty callback. Do not auto-close a job solely on crew status where owner/agency inspection controls acceptance.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need site-visit scheduling, bid status, exclusions/alternates, start window, weather/traffic-impact notices, change authorization, delivery/placement progress, inspection/acceptance documents, invoice/pay-app status and warranty information. Quote approval and document exchange can be self-service; plan/spec interpretation, scope changes and schedule commitments need human review. SMS is useful for residential timing updates but formal contract notices and public-project records may require specified channels.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Asphalt resources include pavers, rollers, trucks, milling/compaction, tack equipment and traffic-control devices; concrete resources may include forms, rebar, pumps, screeds, saws and finishing tools. Material inputs include mix design, asphalt tonnage, aggregate, cement/concrete mix, reinforcing, forms and curing materials. Track procurement, delivery slots, quantities and job allocation where the operator needs them, not a universal warehouse ledger. Crews include operators, laborers, finishers, foremen, estimators, project managers and safety/quality staff; specialized testing, striping and traffic control may be subcontracted. Time and equipment utilization are valuable but payroll rules and certifications vary.

## Reporting and operating measures

Observed FHWA quality workflow supports reports for bid-to-actual installed quantities, daily production, acceptance/test outcomes, weather delays, mix tickets and payment quantities. Likely useful measures (inference) include estimate hit rate, gross margin by job/pay item, tons or square yards per crew shift, truck/paver utilization, rework/callbacks, schedule variance, change-order recovery, days-to-acceptance and receivables aging. Public contract reporting dimensions must be tenant- and contract-configurable.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Bitumio targets asphalt estimating, proposals, scheduling and job costing, and publishes the price above. Horizontal stacks shown in operator evidence include spreadsheets, Outlook, PlanSwift, QuickBooks and GC-provided project tools; commercial projects may require the prime's system. FHWA's inspection materials show that agency QC/acceptance/payment data may sit outside a contractor CRM. Small shops can prefer spreadsheets because they are fast and familiar; digitizing should avoid duplicate estimate-to-job entry and keep field use simple. Public vendor price is the clearest software price evidence found; direct willingness-to-pay beyond an anecdotal one-time takeoff purchase is uncertain.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Roadway QA evidence concerns formal agency contracts, whereas residential flatwork is usually simpler. Asphalt and concrete share project administration but not production templates, equipment or quality measures. Anonymous operator evidence is self-selected and cannot estimate market share. Pricing evidence is vendor list price and one user's recalled takeoff-software cost, not representative WTP. More interviews are needed to establish willingness to pay among one-crew residential firms versus public works subcontractors (medium confidence).

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype separate asphalt and concrete service templates under one candidate pack only if both are retained in scope; ask trade mix, buyer segment, bid/pay-app needs, crew/equipment count and material handling. Keep direct quote-to-schedule flow easy for small residential firms and contract/spec/inspection workflow configurable for public and GC work. Validate whether job quantities and pay items can be represented with shared capabilities before proposing a core gap.
