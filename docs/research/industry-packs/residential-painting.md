## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Residential painting is a project business selling assessed, scoped surface work to homeowners, landlords, property managers, and sometimes builders. A job moves from inquiry and walkthrough/measurement through a written scope and customer approval, scheduling/material preparation, room or elevation sequencing, protected preparation, coating, inspection, cleanup, invoice, and possible punch-list/callback. The source startup guide describes one-on-one homeowner sales, job-based pricing, photos, QuickBooks/payroll tools, hiring and insurance; PCA estimating guidance emphasizes scope, separate setup/prep/cleanup time, and measuring company production rates. Jobber’s painting product page describes estimate-to-job-to-invoice records including colors, notes, photos, crew schedule, time and cost.

**Inference:** A useful general painting profile needs durable property/customer history, but quote inputs should hold the surfaces, quantities, condition, included preparation, coating system, color/sheens, access, protection, and customer-supplied decisions for a particular project. Interior occupancy and exterior weather/lead conditions are meaningful workflow branches; they are expanded in the two dedicated profiles. Nothing in the candidate list or this limited research establishes market viability, willingness to pay, or a universal workflow.

## Business model and customer segments

- Homeowners commission room/whole-home repaints, trim/door work, cabinet refinishing or exterior maintenance; landlords/property managers may coordinate turnovers; builders/remodelers may subcontract work. Buyer and scope authority can differ (resident, owner, manager, designer, general contractor).
- Triggers include move-in/sale, remodeling, worn/damaged finish, color change, turnover, weather exposure and periodic maintenance. Interiors can be scheduled year-round; exterior work is climate- and season-dependent. The cited startup guide suggests new-home purchases as a lead trigger, but it is promotional advice, not demand data.
- Solo owner-painters, employee crews and subcontractor-heavy firms have different estimating, quality-control, payroll, and communication needs. A Jobber community discussion gives one Texas owner’s employee-versus-subcontractor experience; it does not establish a dominant labor model.
- Contract type ranges from fixed project quote with defined allowances/exclusions to unit/production-rate bids and negotiated commercial/residential maintenance scope. Change work should be recorded and approved before it alters price or schedule.

## Terminology and durable records

Use customer/owner, property/site, project/job, room/area/elevation, substrate/surface, prep, primer, coat, finish/color/sheens, quote/estimate, change order, crew/foreperson, punch list, completion and callback. Record property address, access/parking, resident/manager contacts, occupancy/working-hour constraints, pets/security instructions, previous project/color/product history, and photos when useful. Treat measured surfaces, quantities, condition, selected products and colors as project scope snapshots; do not presume a full per-wall asset register is needed. Paint color/product and touch-up records can be durable customer-visible history. Sensitive access details should be restricted. Lead status, containment, training/certification and regulated-work documents are safety/compliance records where applicable, not generic marketing fields.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Common project types include interior repaint; exterior repaint; trim, door and window work; deck/fence staining; cabinet/furniture finishes; drywall repair or wallpaper removal as separately scoped work. Do not bundle specialty coating, restoration or lead abatement into a default house-painter pack without evidence. Intake: service address and buyer/decision maker; residential/property type and construction age where relevant; rooms/surfaces and approximate dimensions/count; present condition and prep requested; access/occupancy and furniture/property protection; colors, sheen and coating preference; photos/walkthrough; timing; prior coating issues; and whether testing or a licensed/certified specialist is needed. Scope should state included/excluded surfaces, preparation level, products/coats, finish expectations, protection/cleanup, assumptions, price, schedule, customer responsibilities, and how changes are approved. Walkthrough or site visit is needed when photos/measurements do not resolve condition/access/scope. Quote approval may include deposit, color confirmation, and scheduled start; terms are tenant- and jurisdiction-specific. Website self-service can collect a request and photos but unusual repair, lead risk, high access, or ambiguous scope needs human review.

## Pricing and commercial model

Observed quote inputs include surface area/count, substrate/condition, preparation and repair extent, number/type of coats, color transitions, product/finish, masking/protection and furniture moving, height/access, labor production rates, crew travel, materials, schedule constraints and local labor/overhead. PCA cautions that non-prep production rates omit prep, setup and cleanup and recommends recording the company’s own rates. Quote unit can be fixed price, measured/production-based, or negotiated; no universal formula is evidenced. Deposits, progress invoices, change orders, cancellation, warranty and touch-up terms need tenant configuration. Sales price examples in the candidate startup guide are anecdotal dated advice and not used as present market benchmarks.

**Software WTP:** No representative operator statement of actual CRM spend or verified customer purchase was found. The current PaintScout pricing page (accessed 2026-09-24) advertises Sales at $119/month monthly or $99/month annual; Operations is an add-on listed at $99 monthly or $79 annual, with team seats $20/user/month. ## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most repainting is one-time project work; repeat work can follow move/turnover, property portfolio maintenance or future repaint cycles, but no uniform cadence is established. Schedule by agreed start window and estimated crew-days, scope dependencies, occupied-room access, drying/recoat instructions, material/color readiness, and available crew/skills. Interior work can sequence by room while keeping pathways/essential rooms usable; exterior work requires weather windows and dry/cure constraints. Multi-day status, affected areas, customer rescheduling, rain/temperature delay, partial completion and return/punch work matter more than route optimization. Larger companies may schedule several teams across jobs; small firms may use a calendar and manual updates.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Typical sequence: review approved scope/colors/access; protect floors, furniture, fixtures, landscaping and adjacent finishes as applicable; inspect substrate and note pre-existing defects; perform included cleaning/scraping/sanding/filling/caulking/priming; confirm material/product/lot and environmental conditions; apply specified coats using suitable tools; respect dry/recoat instructions; inspect against agreed finish and lighting; remove masking, clean, record completed/excluded areas and remaining work; customer walkthrough/acceptance; invoice and retain color/product/touch-up notes. Capture before/during/after photos and signed scope/change approval where helpful. Defects can stem from substrate movement/moisture, poor preparation, unsuitable coating, application conditions, scope mismatch or damage after work; record cause, responsibility, billability and return visit rather than promise free repaint by default. US EPA RRP may apply when paid work disturbs paint in pre-1978 housing/child-occupied facilities; federal coverage includes painting preparation subject to rule exceptions, and state/tribal programs can differ. OSHA, state safety rules, licensing, insurance and disposal requirements depend on work and jurisdiction. No universal checklist should claim legal compliance.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need prompt inquiry response, site-visit confirmation, written scope and exclusions, color/product approval, start reminder, daily access/protection plan, schedule/delay changes, change-order approval, completion/punch notice, invoice, warranty/touch-up policy, and care/dry-time guidance consistent with the selected coating manufacturer. A portal can collect photos and preferences, approve estimates/changes, display schedule and documents, and request a walkthrough/callback. Avoid auto-confirming color, hidden damage repairs, completion date, safety/risk determination, or warranty coverage without human review. Occupied-home contact preferences and commercial quiet hours should be honored.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Brushes, rollers, sprayers, ladders/scaffolds, sanders, scrapers, fillers/caulk, masking, drop cloths, ventilation/PPE and cleaning materials vary by surface and method. Inventory relevance increases with multiple crews and standardized products; keep coating, primer, color formula, sheen, quantity and supplier as job material history. Crew models range from owner-operator to employees/subcontractors; capture assigned foreperson/crew, time and materials when costing matters. Benefits, workers’ compensation, payroll, certifications, language, skill and subcontractor records vary by jurisdiction and company; the community thread is a single operator account, not a default.

## Reporting and operating measures

Observed vendor features include quote conversion/follow-up, schedule, customer history, job-cost inputs (labor, paint and materials), time, payments and reviews. Candidate measures inferred from those workflows: lead response and quote conversion by source/service; estimate-versus-actual labor/material; gross margin/project; days from approval to start and completion; crew utilization; schedule slippage by reason; change-order frequency/value; punch/callback rate and resolution time; repeat/referral rate; and project aging/receivables. No common KPI definitions were established in sources; calibrate by business model.

## Existing software and operator evidence

The startup guide recommends web/Google presence, QuickBooks, payroll services and later Jobber, and says Jobber supports online billing and job photos. Jobber pitches painting estimates, optional upgrades/color swatches, approvals, crew schedule, job notes/photos/checklists/time, QuickBooks sync and color history. PaintScout markets production-rate estimating, proposals, payments, follow-up and optional operations/scheduling. These are vendor/product claims. A Jobber-hosted contractor discussion shows one Texas owner moving from employees to subcontractors and another operator describing growth and systems/automation, but it is platform-affiliated and anecdotal. One 2026 contractor thread mentions PaintScout, spreadsheets, CompanyCam and vendor calculators alongside Jobber from an operator describing residential software experience; anonymous and not representative. Switching/migration concerns, feature gaps, and willingness to pay are not quantified by this sample. Published software plan prices below are vendor list prices, not customer-reported purchases.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree that scope, prep, setup/cleanup, quality expectations, records and labor/material costs materially shape a quote. A project job is distinct from a recurring route service. The startup guide promotes speed, direct homeowner sales and job-based pricing; a contractor’s actual segment, lead-time promise and employee/subcontractor balance may differ. EPA RRP is US federal guidance with jurisdictional variation; age is an intake prompt, not a substitute for applicable rule/test determination. Confidence is **high** for the project lifecycle, quoting inputs and variable staffing; **medium** for customer segments and software workflow; **low** for typical pricing, repeat cadence, KPI benchmarks and software budget. Unknowns include prevalence of commercial work among this candidate, how often painters preserve detailed colors/surface histories, and the most useful conversion/quality benchmarks.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype project-oriented intake, walkthrough/quote with explicitly included prep and surfaces, line-item changes, material/color history, crew assignment, phase/checklist progress, photos, customer approval, invoice and callback. Ask which interior/exterior/specialty work is offered, customer mix, occupancy, construction-age/regulated-work exposure, staffing model, crew count, quoting units, materials/job-cost needs, and acceptance/warranty policy. Keep production rates, product catalog, lead determination, staff qualifications, labor model, access, deposits and callback terms tenant-configurable. Use the interior and exterior profiles for distinct checklist/schedule branches. Validate market viability and actual software willingness-to-pay separately; neither follows from candidate-list inclusion or vendor list prices.
