## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Steam-shower work combines a watertight shower enclosure with a steam generator, controls/sensor, steam head, drain, plumbing and dedicated electrical supply. The bathroom remodel often includes tile, glass, bench/ceiling slope, waterproof membrane, generator access, power and drain. It may be sold and installed by a bathroom remodeler, plumber, tile/waterproofing contractor or specialist dealer with qualified electrical subcontractor. This is operationally distinct from sauna kit installation and from ordinary shower replacement.

The CRM opportunity is a project record connecting enclosure details and selected product model to hidden waterproofing evidence, rough-in readiness, inspection, testing and warranty handoff. Errors can cause concealed moisture damage or scald risk; checklists need model/jurisdiction human review. Direct operator software adoption and WTP evidence is sparse, so product-fit beyond general project tools remains provisional.

## Business model and customer segments

Primary buyers are homeowners renovating or building bathrooms; remodelers, designers/builders, plumbing showrooms and specialty contractors influence selection. A simple system can retrofit a suitable shower, while full conversion requires a steam-rated enclosed shower. Scope can include demolition, framing, sloped ceiling/bench, vapor-retarding waterproof system, tile, glass/door, drain, generator and controls. Product-seller, tile contractor, plumber and electrician responsibilities vary by project. Work is one-time with possible generator repair/service and warranty callback. Residential evidence dominates; commercial spa/hotel work has distinct occupancy and maintenance demands.

## Terminology and durable records

Capture room/site, shower dimensions/volume, ceiling/bench, wall/floor construction, waterproof membrane/system, drain/slope, steam generator model/serial/kW, control/sensor/head position, water supply, dedicated electrical circuit, generator location/access, ventilation/cooling plan, installer/trades, inspection, test and warranty. A generator/control is a persistent asset; waterproofing layers, concealed rough-ins, photos and measurements are project records that matter later. Store manufacturer manuals and approved model-specific drawings.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake should distinguish steam retrofit vs new bathroom, enclosure dimensions/material, current shower/base, tile/waterproofing system, ceiling/bench, generator model/size, panel/circuit feasibility, generator location, drain and plumbing access, access and existing plans. Kohler documentation gives exact model-specific placement and electrical information (e.g. generator sizing, steam head/control positions); it must not be generalized to other models. Site inspection is needed before fixed pricing. Estimate can break out demolition, enclosure, waterproofing/tile, generator/control, plumbing, electrical, glass, finish and commissioning. Design and model selection precede rough-in; changes after membrane/tile should be explicit change orders.

Milestones: consult → measure/system sizing → quote/design/product approval → rough-in/site readiness → waterproof enclosure and inspection → tile/glass → generator/plumbing/electrical installation → test and owner instruction → documentation/invoice. Online lead forms should request dimensions/photos and route to site review.

## Pricing and commercial model

Price inputs include shower size and enclosure condition, demolition, substrate repair, waterproofing system, tile/stone, glass, bench/ceiling, generator size, controls, water/drain, electrical panel and dedicated circuit, access, finish and permit/inspection. Product and labor may be sold together or by different trades. Deposits/progress/final payment and warranty scope vary by contract. No representative operator price or verified software purchase/WTP source was found. Public CRM prices represent available list packages, not proof a steam-shower installer buys them.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Coordinate demolition, rough plumbing/electrical, waterproofing, inspection, cure, tile, glass fabrication, equipment delivery, final connections and commissioning. A bathroom may be unavailable to occupants; protect alternate-use expectations. Delays include product lead time, failed waterproof inspection, unsuitable generator location/circuit, substrate discovery, glass measurement after tile or unready subcontractor. Schedule around cure/inspection holds instead of promising completion from the initial estimate alone.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm signed product/design and room dimensions → protect home and isolate utilities → document demolition/substrate → rough plumbing/electrical and generator access → install sloped, sealed enclosure per approved system → photograph concealed membrane/seams/penetrations before cover → inspection/hold point where required → tile/glass → install generator/control/head to product manual → leak/drain/electrical/control and steam-operation checks by qualified trades → handover manuals, cleaning and warranty. Kohler's primary guide sets product-specific head height, door setback, control location, generator distance and electrical supply. Scald, electrical, wet-area, lifting and silica hazards apply as tasks dictate. Licensure/permits and inspection rules are local. Noncompletion includes failed waterproofing, leak, poor drain, wrong generator/circuit, missing part, glass misfit and customer selections; callbacks may reveal concealed damage, which should be tied to installation stage and evidence.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need selections, outage/alternate bath planning, access/dust, rough-in readiness, inspection holds, cure windows, test, cleaning/care, warranty and response path. A portal could approve fixture/model choices and show non-sensitive photos/manuals. Automated “ready” or completion notices must not imply membrane inspection or electrical approval when unresolved.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Materials include membrane/sealants, tile/stone, glass, generator, control/sensor, steam head, plumbing fittings, drain and wiring. Product model compatibility and serial/warranty matter. Crew roles may include remodeler/PM, plumber, electrician, waterproof/tile installer, glazier and generator technician. Record which trade owns each concealed or regulated scope; workforce credentials depend on location and applicable work.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred metrics: quote conversion, product lead time, readiness/inspection delay, waterproofing rework, schedule slip, callback/leak rate, completion and margin by remodel vs retrofit. No common vendor KPI standard or operator dashboards found.
