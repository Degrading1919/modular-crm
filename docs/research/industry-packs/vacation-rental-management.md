## Executive summary

**Observed evidence.** A vacation-rental manager operates furnished homes for short stays on an owner's behalf. The operating loop is listing/pricing → reservation → guest communication/access → departure and turnover cleaning/inspection → maintenance or incident escalation → owner reporting. Airbnb's co-host documentation separates listing/calendar/pricing/reservation permissions from cleaning, welcoming and guest care; owners remain responsible for co-hosts. The Sweaty Startup article frames the offer around owner income projections, risk, insurance, local rules and a proposed profit share.

This is a lodging operation with high-frequency reservations and compressed turnovers, distinct from long-term tenant management. A property is a durable asset; each reservation, guest stay and turnover is a separate record. **Inference:** a CRM can coordinate owners, properties, vendors and exceptions, but channel synchronization, booking accounting, lodging tax and payment handling need specialist integrations or verified external systems. Neither the list nor this research establishes that all property managers operate STRs.

## Business model and customer segments

- Buyers are homeowners/investors; guests are separate temporary customers. Operators range from individual co-hosts and cleaners to local portfolio firms and larger managers.
- Scope can include listing setup, channel calendar/rate handling, guest support, access, turnover cleaning/laundry/restocking, inspections, repairs, incident handling and owner statements. Some co-hosts do only a subset.
- Compensation can be percentage of booking revenue, fixed/property fee, co-host share, per-turnover charge, setup fee, and reimbursed owner expenses. Sweaty Startup's proposed 50/50 profit share is one author's suggestion, not an industry benchmark.
- Demand varies with destination seasonality, weekends/events, owner-use blocks and local regulation. Whole-home stays, urban furnished apartments, resort condos, mid-term stays and home-share have different rules and workloads.
- Acquisition may be referrals, owner outreach, real-estate networks or direct marketing. The source article recommends comparing STR and monthly rental economics; it is entrepreneurial advice, not market research.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms: owner/client, property/listing, unit, reservation/booking, guest, stay, turnover, co-host/manager, cleaner/vendor, owner statement.
- Property facts: address/unit/time zone, bedrooms/baths/capacity, amenities, parking, access device reference, owner-use blocks, house rules, supplies, emergency contact, permit/registration status and vendor instructions. Restrict door/alarm codes and other credentials.
- Durable property assets include appliances/systems, furnishings, linens and access devices with service history. Inventory tracking is most relevant when manager owns linens or consumables.
- Reservation is a changeable date-bound commitment (channel, guests, dates, rates/fees, status, cancellation, payout reference and requests). Turnover is a distinct job linked to checkout and next arrival, with cleaner, deadline and condition proof.
- Sensitive data includes guest contacts/stay dates, access credentials, incident/damage records and owner financials. Separate owner, guest and vendor visibility.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Services commonly include listing setup/photography/copy, calendar/pricing administration, inquiry handling, booking support, guest messaging, check-in/access, turnover, linen/restock, inspection, maintenance dispatch and owner reports.
- Owner intake: management authority and agreement; property/listings/accounts; available calendar and owner use; minimum stay/rules/pricing; vendors/access/supplies; insurance; emergency/spend approvals; permit/tax obligations; reporting and payout preferences.
- Quotes are portfolio/property specific: percentage/fixed fee plus possible turnover, setup, pass-through costs and minimum. Agreement should define fee basis and expense treatment.
- Guests book through OTAs or direct site; channel synchronization prevents double booking. Exceptions such as long stays, large groups, suspected parties, local limits and unusual requests need review. A website alone does not replace availability sync.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Pricing levers include revenue-share or fixed fee, property size/complexity, booking/turn count, season, stay length, on-call coverage, setup/photography, laundry/restock and pass-through repair. Contracts need authority, owner approval threshold, reserve, cancellation/refund responsibility, damage, insurance, pending reservations and termination rules.
- Rental income, OTA payout, lodging taxes, cleaning fee, manager fee, refund and damage reimbursement are different flows. IRS Publication 527 discusses rental income/expenses and notes that substantial tenant-convenience services can change reporting; software should retain itemized source records rather than decide tax treatment.
- **Software WTP evidence:** Hostfully advertises Growth from **$15/property/month plus platform fee** for 1–50 listings and Pro from **$25/property/month plus platform fee** (1–199), Enterprise custom; accessed 2026-09-24. This is list pricing and product packaging, not proof of purchases or general CRM budget.
- No reliable market-wide service-fee range was established; the source's 50/50 suggestion is not a benchmark.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Turnovers are reservation-triggered, often between checkout and the next arrival. Same-day turn, late checkout, early entry, laundry cycle, inspection/re-clean and urgent repair are key exceptions.
- Schedule by property, departure/arrival, duration, vendors/crew, geography, linen availability and dependencies. Calendar authority must account for OTAs, direct bookings, owner blocks, repairs and cancellations.
- Dispatch may include cleaners, laundry, maintenance trades, photographers and guest support. Route planning becomes useful with many same-day turns; remote managers may use independent vendors.
- Late cleans, guest extensions, damage, access failure, cancellations and emergencies need human triage. Do not automatically promise a guest a new arrival time or refund.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Turnover: booking dates arrive → create task/deadline → cleaner enters → clean/laundry/restock → checklist and photos → report damage/repair/missing stock → manager handles exceptions → release unit.
- Proof may include room checklist, timestamped photos, supplies/linen count, access issue, damage notes and cleaner confirmation. Photo retention/privacy policy is tenant-specific.
- Safety checks may cover smoke/CO alarms, pool barriers, hot tubs, stairs, fire exits and local occupancy rules. A CRM checklist is not legal compliance.
- Rework reasons: missed/poor clean, missing item, damage, maintenance, access failure or booking conflict. Track responsibility, billability, customer notice, return visit and owner decision.
- Offline cleaner use is plausible but not established by sources; validate with operators.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Owners need onboarding, occupancy/revenue summaries, expense approvals, incident alerts, statements and calendar blocks. Guests need confirmation, access, house rules, support, checkout, and change/cancel guidance.
- Airbnb supports shared host/co-host messages, filters, quick replies, scheduled messages and permission tiers. Full access can message guests, update calendar/pricing, manage reservations/cancellations and resolution requests. Owners set permissions and remain responsible.
- Channel-specific email/SMS/in-platform rules and consent matter. Avoid moving guest data outside approved channels. Keep owner books and access notes invisible to guests/vendors.
- Useful self-service: owner availability/spend requests, guest arrival information, and maintenance report with photos. Escalate safety, damage, suspected fraud/party, accessibility, refund demands and booking conflicts to a person.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Properties contain durable equipment/furnishings; linens, toiletries, batteries and cleaning stock may need replenishment. Inventory defaults should be optional.
- Housekeepers, laundry, maintenance trades, photographers and guest-support staff are common roles. Airbnb permission tiers illustrate that booking authority and guest messaging can differ by worker.
- Vendor arrival windows, checklist/photo proof, access limits and payment approval matter. Employee versus contractor, payroll and certifications vary by business.

## Reporting and operating measures

- Useful measures include occupancy, average daily rate, revenue per available night, lead time, source/channel, cancellations, response time, on-time turnover, re-clean/incident rate, maintenance spend, owner payout and property contribution.
- Hostfully features owner reports; a vendor story claims 90% less report-prep time for one operator, which is not a general baseline.
- Reconcile by owner/property; distinguish gross booking revenue from manager revenue. Dimensions include channel, season, vendor, issue, and unit.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- Hostfully provides channel/reservation management, direct booking, mobile tools and partner integrations for dynamic pricing, cleaning/turnover, accounting, insurance and guest screening. Airbnb handles its own listing, calendar, guest messages, permissions and reservations. Pricing optimization, locks, accounting and cleaning may be separate tools, although bundles exist.
- Hostfully's ecosystem guide is older (2023) and vendor-authored; the current page describes 100+ partners. Integration categories imply a stitched ecosystem, but no prevalence estimate is established.
- A first-person manager of about 20 mixed mid-term/short-term rentals asked for calendar connections, cleaner scheduling and direct-booking capability, explicitly noting a constrained software budget. Replies were mixed and included product promotion; this is one small-operator case, not a prevalence or willingness-to-pay estimate. Other workflow needs remain inferred from incumbent scope, not evidence that managers broadly complain.
- Hostfully live list pricing: Growth $15/listing/month + platform fee; Pro $25/listing/month + platform fee; Enterprise custom. Add-ons/fees vary; distinguish this from management-service fees.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree on owner/guest distinction, co-host permissions, reservation/calendar/pricing authority and turnover responsibilities. They do not establish a universal fee, SLA, channel mix, complaint rate, or record-retention period. The 50/50 share in Sweaty Startup is one author's proposal. Hostfully is vendor-authored, Airbnb is platform-specific, and IRS guidance is US-only. Local STR licensing, lodging tax, zoning and safety must be checked by location.

Confidence is high for reservation-to-turnover operations; medium for pack fit; low for fee norms and whether to build booking sync vs integrate. First-person STR manager interviews remain a gap.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype separate owner/guest roles, property/listing asset, and reservation-triggered turnover/repair work with deadline, access, checklist, proof, vendor and escalation. Ask if operator is co-host/full service, multi-channel, direct-booking, in-house cleaning and how owner accounting is handled. Keep fee basis, owner approval, access secrets, permits, channel and stock configurable. Validate channel sync, owner statements and turnover SLAs with local operators before promoting a core gap.
