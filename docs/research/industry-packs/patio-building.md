## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Patio projects range from concrete slabs to interlocking concrete pavers, natural stone and mixed outdoor living scopes with steps, retaining edges, drainage or pergolas. A paver installation is measurement/layout, excavation and haul-off, subgrade/base compaction, drainage/slope, bedding, placement, restraint, jointing and cleanup. Soil strength, freezing, traffic and water routing alter construction and rework risk. Small owner-operator installers may sell a bounded patio; larger landscape/hardscape companies coordinate demolition, walls, lighting, irrigation and subcontractors.

A pack should capture the surface and boundaries, area/grade/drainage/site access, material/pattern, work scope, weather and sequence, then retain base/grade photos, change approval and completion evidence. **Inference:** most jobs are project-based rather than recurring, and customer updates around schedule/slippage and scope changes matter. Operator sources are anecdotal and do not establish software WTP.

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners are primary buyers; landscape designers, GCs, pool contractors, property managers and small commercial venues may refer work. Scope can be pavers, slab, stone, demolition/replacement, steps, edging, drainage and adjacent outdoor features. Keep patio distinct from fence and deck builds: it is a surface and grading job, sometimes part of wider landscaping. Seasonality/weather and local freeze/thaw create regional differences. Solo crews may do layout, excavation and placement; larger firms use excavation/equipment operators and sub out concrete, walls or utilities.

## Terminology and durable records

Records: property/yard, footprint, elevation/slope, drainage route, substrate/soil, existing slab/patio, utilities/irrigation, material/pattern, base assembly, joint product, edge restraint, access/equipment, permit/HOA, crew, change order, warranty and punch. Location should store gate width, haul route, grade, adjacent foundation/door thresholds, downspout/irrigation and runoff destination. The patio is a persistent installed structure, but job-specific soil tests, measurements, layout strings, quantities, photos and material lot belong to project history. Avoid storing unneeded neighbor/property information.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Collect desired area/use, material/pattern, approximate dimensions/photos, existing surface/demo, slope/water pooling, drainage, access, utilities, stairs/retaining edge, traffic load, furniture/pergola/pool adjacency, location and timing. Site visit and measure are needed for fixed quote. Estimate separates square footage, base depth/material, pavers/slab/stone, cuts/curves, edge, excavation/haul, drainage, steps, access/equipment and optional features. Written scope must identify surface, base and drainage assumptions; changes for buried conditions require approval. Quote models include measured fixed price, unit area, material/labor split or larger bid.

Schedule locates underground utilities, demolition, excavation, haul, base delivery/compaction, drainage, setting materials, cure/jointing, inspection and cleanup. Web intake can qualify; final grade and price should be reviewed on site.

## Pricing and commercial model

Drivers include area, material, pattern/cuts, excavation depth and disposal, soil condition, access, haul distance, base/compaction, grade/drainage, steps/retaining, lighting and subcontracting. CMHA notes pedestrian patio base depth varies with soil, drainage and freezing; that is technical guidance, not a quote formula. Deposits/material payment and completion payment vary. No reliable representative patio price/WTP data was identified. **Software WTP:** owner discussion may reveal delay/communication problems but not purchase of software. Generic FSM vendor prices are package prices, not evidence of a hardscape firm's spend.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Jobs are usually one-time, with repair/settlement callback possible. Schedule delivery and excavation equipment, crew and weather; concrete pour/cure or paver stages can prevent concurrent trades. Rain can soften subgrade/base and interrupt work; customer access/yard use and neighborhood restrictions matter. Noncompletion can stem from rain, buried utility, unsuitable soil, access, material shortage, design change or inspector/HOA hold. Record reason, next action, billability and revised date. For a compact crew route between jobs may matter, but project milestones outweigh appointment dispatch.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Locate utilities/confirm scope → protect home/yard → mark layout/elevations → remove existing work/excavate → verify subgrade → install and compact base by lifts → form drainage/slope → bedding/paver or concrete/stone installation → restraint/joints/finish/cure → verify transitions, drainage and surface → cleanup/photo/customer walk-through. Use manufacturer and applicable CMHA/engineered guidance; base choices are not universal. Equipment, excavation, compaction, silica/dust, lifting and traffic hazards require task controls. Permits, stormwater and utility locating are local. Rework includes settling, ponding against house, low/high transitions, broken edges, poor jointing, staining or damage to irrigation. Before/after photos and base-depth/grade hold-point evidence help resolve quality issues.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need design/material confirmation, utility locate, yard/access preparation, weather/delay changes, expected usable/cure date, daily progress, changed soil conditions, final drainage inspection, maintenance/sealer and warranty. Share options and quote approval digitally; do not promise a firm date before materials/weather and site readiness are verified.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Equipment can include excavator/skid steer, dump trailer, compactor, saw, screed/laser, wheelbarrows and truck. Materials include aggregate, bedding, pavers/concrete/stone, restraint, joint sand, geotextile and drainage. Order quantities and delivery access are job-specific; stock tracking matters more for supply/installation firms. Roles include estimator/owner, crew lead, equipment operator, mason/paver installer, laborer and optional drainage/electrical/landscape subs.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred measures: quote conversion, schedule slip/weather days, base/grade rework, material waste, productivity per area, first-pass drainage acceptance, callbacks, margin by material/job and change-order recovery. Track separately pavers, poured slabs, natural stone and integrated landscape jobs; no validated common KPI set found.
