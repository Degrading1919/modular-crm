## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Golf-cart repair ranges from dealer shop service to mobile work and pickup/return. Persistent asset fields matter: make/model/year, serial/VIN, electric/gas powertrain, battery chemistry/voltage/age, charger, accessories and prior repairs. Common work includes safety inspection, batteries/charging, tires, brakes, steering/suspension, motor/controller, belts/engine, wiring and accessories. Manufacturer manuals provide schedule/technician cautions; cart-specific dealer software markets work orders, parts catalogs, customer/cart history, service scheduling and communications. Mobile operator forms ask cart make/model and service location.

**Inference:** CRM/service workflow should tie each job and parts history to the specific cart, distinguish customer/site from cart asset, and support both shop queue and mobile/pickup dispatch. **Uncertain:** operators vary greatly between dealer, neighborhood mobile specialist, golf-course fleet vendor and repair shop; the last may need fleet PM rather than residential online booking. No reliable industry-wide software WTP or prevalence survey was found.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include households/HOAs in golf-cart communities, golf courses, resorts/campgrounds, campuses, commercial facilities, rental fleets, dealers and maintenance contractors. Jobs arise from breakdowns, seasonal/preventive service, battery replacement, upgrades and fleet inspections. A mobile technician can fix at home/site; heavy or complex repairs may require pickup and shop. A dealership may combine sales, rentals, parts, warranty and service, but this candidate must not assume sales/rental is included. Fleet contracts have scheduled inspections and downtime requirements; individual owners are episodic.

## Terminology and durable records

Record customer/organization/site; cart make/model/year, serial/VIN, electric/gas, voltage, battery chemistry/count/serial/date, charger, motor/controller, tire size, brake/steering/suspension, accessories/lift/aftermarket modifications, odometer/hour meter, street-legal equipment if relevant, purchase/dealer/warranty and service history. Fleet carts need asset ID, department/route/assigned operator, usage hours, location and maintenance intervals. Job-only fields include symptom, current condition, battery readings, pickup key, site access and requested date. A golf cart may be road-registered LSV in some jurisdictions; do not presume legal status from appearance.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Typical: inspection/tune-up; battery test, replacement and charger; tires; brakes; steering/suspension; motor/controller/solenoid/wiring; gas engine/belt/fuel; accessories/lift/windshield/lights; pickup/return; fleet PM. Intake asks asset ID, make/model/year/serial, power type and battery/charger, symptom, photos/video, location/access, warranty/dealer status, requested urgency and whether mobile or pickup/shop service. Quote may use inspection/diagnostic or service-call fee plus flat operation/hourly labor, parts, travel/pickup, disposal/core charge and tax. Batteries and aftermarket changes can require technician compatibility review. Online booking should be request/arrival window; diagnostic outcome and parts estimate require approval. Fleet service intake needs cart batch/location and downtime window.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service price evidence:** A mobile shop’s 2026 article lists diagnostic/service call $75–$150 and lead-acid set replacement $800–$1,500, while noting large motor/body work may need shop service; the page is one operator’s local guide and not a national benchmark ([Canyon Lake Mobile](https://canyonlakemobile.com/blogs/news/how-golf-cart-repair-cost-2026-price-guide), accessed 2026-09-24). A customer forum post describes a local $150 pickup, battery replacement labor and general service package; anonymous anecdote only ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/golfcarts/comments/17amsfm/), accessed 2026-09-24). Operator rates should separate diagnostic/service call, labor, parts/battery, disposal/core, travel/pickup, shop supplies and tax. Fleet contracts may use scheduled labor, per-cart inspection and parts terms; no representative fleet rate source found.

Software pricing/WTP: golf-cart-specific DMS/service vendors Powerdash, Shift Industry and Lightspeed promote service scheduling, cart/customer history, work orders, inventory and communications, but the reviewed pages required trial/demo/quote and did not publish list prices. No direct operator statement or verified invoice shows willingness to pay. Generic auto-shop products publish prices but do not establish demand among golf-cart repair businesses. Do not treat cart repair ticket sizes, vendor positioning, or service volume as software budget evidence.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Shop service uses bays, parts/technician availability and estimate approval. Mobile service schedules by travel zone, site rules, cart readiness and required tools/parts; pickup/return requires tow trailer, load/unload and custody window. Golf courses, resorts, HOAs and rental fleets may need batch inspections or off-peak work to reduce cart downtime; intervals should be tenant/model/fleet configured. Weather/season and golf-community usage patterns can create peaks, but no universal season is supported. Delay may be caused by special-order controller/battery, diagnosis, missing authorization, site access or unsafe cart condition. Route planning becomes useful for several mobile calls; it is not core for a single shop appointment.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Identify cart and authorization, document pre-existing condition, disable/secure vehicle, inspect per make/model manual, test powertrain and safety components, record readings/diagnosis, prepare estimate, obtain approval, complete authorized repair, test operation/braking/steering/charging, document installed parts and postcondition, obtain customer acceptance, close invoice and schedule advised maintenance. Club Car says only trained technicians should perform repair/service and directs periodic schedules; severe use can require more frequent checks. Battery handling risk depends on chemistry: lead-acid includes acid/electrical/charging hazards; lithium has distinct OEM precautions. OSHA 1926.441 sets requirements for battery charging installations in construction contexts; it should not be treated as the sole universal rule for every repair shop. Apply relevant OSHA general-industry rules, manufacturer instructions and local fire/electrical policies after jurisdictional review. Street use can invoke state LSV equipment/registration rules; do not certify legality without jurisdiction-specific inspection. Stop if damaged battery, unsafe lift/support, unknown conversion, improper charging area, unsafe test route, customer declines necessary safety repair, parts incompatibility or cart inaccessible. Record unsafe-to-operate warnings and callbacks.

## Customer communication and self-service

Confirm cart identity/location, estimate and diagnostic fee, parts ETA, service/pickup window, delay, approval/change order, test/repair result, battery warranty/core return, invoice and next maintenance reminder. Owner portal can show individual cart history, parts, photos and warranty. Fleet manager may need batch status and downtime report. Avoid automatic “street legal” or “safe” claims; technician records test scope and jurisdiction-specific limitation.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Technicians use model-specific service information, multimeter/battery tester/charger analyzer, hand tools, jack/lift stands, tire and brake tools, diagnostic controller tools, PPE and service vehicle/trailer. Stock/ordered items include battery sets/individual batteries, chargers, tires, solenoids, controllers, motors, belts, cables, switches, lights and accessories. Parts matching depends on model/year/serial and aftermarket modification; record vendor, core, lot/warranty and return. Gas and electric specialists may need different skills; dealer warranty authorization and fleet-contract scope vary. Battery charging/storage setup and damaged-battery isolation require site/shop procedure. Do not assume technicians are interchangeable across OEMs or custom builds.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures: repair orders by stage/age, first diagnosis/approval time, parts-wait days, bay/mobile utilization, trip and pickup cost, first-time fix/callback, estimate approval/decline, labor/parts margin, battery/core returns, warranty recovery, maintenance due by asset/hours, fleet downtime and repeat failures. Golf-cart DMS feature claims support service schedule, parts and history use cases; operational KPI definitions differ by shop. No target benchmarks evidenced.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Cart dealers have specialized dealer management systems (Shift Industry, Lightspeed, Powerdash) for sales, parts, service schedules, customer/cart history, work orders and communications; larger/mobile fleet operations may layer dispatch/work-order tools onto back-office systems. Mobile shop web forms ask cart make/model, phone and service location. A plausible small-operator stack is calls/text, calendar, estimates/invoices, accounting, parts vendor catalogs, photos and spreadsheets; this is inference, not measured prevalence. Reddit discussion of dealer management systems mentions current dealer use and comparison of Ideal/Lightspeed, but is a few owners’ reports, not market share. Public software list prices and actual buyer WTP were not located; the specialization of vendors demonstrates supply, not purchase or fit for solo technicians.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Manufacturer manuals define maintenance, trained-technician expectations and model-specific steps. Dealer software targets cart serial/inventory/service integration; mobile specialists ask for cart model and location. Repair and mobile pickup prices vary; online evidence is sparse. **Inference:** asset history, parts matching, diagnostic approval, location and mobile/pickup workflow are core configurable defaults. **Uncertain:** no independent prevalence study, complete state LSV survey, or direct software WTP was found; software vendors’ features and marketing do not demonstrate customer adoption. Confidence high for workflow/asset needs, medium for feature fit, low for market/software penetration.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask shop/mobile/pickup model, customer vs fleet mix, OEMs, electric/gas/battery chemistry, warranty and inventory needs, and whether any units are road-registered LSVs. Prototype cart history, diagnostic fee/estimate approval, battery/parts tracking, shop/mobile scheduling, photos/test/signoff and fleet service due dates. Keep pricing, intervals, batteries, OEM procedures, local road rules and charging safety tenant/jurisdiction-specific. Confirm meter-based recurrence, asset-component linking and any warranty integration before identifying a core-platform gap.
