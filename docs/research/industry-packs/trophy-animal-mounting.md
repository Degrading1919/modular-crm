## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Trophy taxidermy is a long-cycle, shop-based custom production and custody workflow. At intake, a shop identifies the customer, specimen/species, harvest origin/date and license/tag or wildlife-transfer documents, mount type/pose, measurements, condition, requested habitat/base, quote, deposit, and a unique physical tag. Production may pass through freezer, fleshing, outside tannery, mounting, drying, finishing, pickup/shipping; delays often span many months and depend on season, outsourced tanning, and shop capacity. State and federal rules can require authorization, tagged specimens and traceable records; migratory birds receive specific federal treatment.

**Inference:** The strongest software fit is a specimen-first job pipeline that ties durable physical ID, legal provenance documents, customer scope/pose/photo, payment and each production stage together, then drives transparent status and pickup communication. Treat a trophy as regulated customer property requiring custody controls, not simply a generic job. Do not accept or promise work for a species or origin without jurisdiction-specific review. No evidence supports assuming all taxidermists use software; current vertical vendors and a forum thread both suggest binders, spreadsheets, whiteboards and offline programs remain part of the landscape.

## Business model and customer segments

Primary customer is a hunter or angler commissioning a shoulder, life-size, pedestal, rug, European/skull, bird, fish, replica or other mount from legally obtained material. Some operators also offer tanning, dip-and-pack, hide work, repair/restoration, custom habitats, pickup/drop-off or outbound shipping. Commercial accounts may include outfitters, hunting ranches and overseas trophy coordinators, but personal local hunters dominate the ordinary shop examples. A shop may be solo/part-time, seasonal, multi-artist or high-volume with outsourced tanning. A single taxidermist can fill several roles: intake, production scheduling, craft, client service, accounting and shipping.

Seasonal influx after hunting seasons leads to intake peaks and long work-in-progress queues. The customer pays for a custom result with long expected lead time; a particular specimen is not readily replaceable. The operator must preserve and identify the right cape/skull/skin with the right order. Therefore a trophy mount job is not scheduled like an appointment or repeated maintenance call; it is a staged project with custody, inventory and pickup obligations.

## Terminology and durable records

Use customer/hunter, specimen/trophy, harvest/take, species, mount, pose, cape/hide, antlers/horns, base/habitat, work order/job ID, tannery, freezer, stage, ready for pickup and release. The durable job record should identify owner, scientific/common species where needed, specimen parts/quantity, date/place/state/country of harvest, hunting/fishing license and tag/permit/seal numbers, proof-of-origin documents, wildlife-resource transfer document when required, intake photos, condition/field-care, measurements, mount type, form/pose, side/turn, mouth/eyes/antlers, base/habitat, customer approvals, quote/deposit/balance, subcontract tannery and shipment, physical storage location, due estimate, status history, return/release signatures, and relevant permits/receipts. Preserve a unique, weather-resistant tag on each specimen/part, linked to the job and physical storage position.

Sensitive records include government ID/license numbers, home address, hunting location, endangered/migratory status, import/export paperwork and confidential customer photos. Restrict access and keep legally required retention separately identifiable. Status labels must distinguish physically received specimen from customer-provided data and stage-complete from legally cleared. Do not interpret “customer says it is legal” as a verified permit.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Service menu can include shoulder/head mount, full/half life-size, pedestal, rug, European/skull mount, bird, fish skin/replica, antler/horn mount, tanning-only, dip-and-pack, repair, custom habitat/base, rush/expedite, pickup and shipping. Shops have different expertise/species acceptance, preservation standards, outsourced versus in-house tanning and capacity. Intake requires positive species/part ID, harvest jurisdiction/date and supporting tags/licenses, condition and storage state, desired mount, pose/measurements/photos, any special materials/base, deadline, contact, and owner/authority. The shop needs refusal/escalation flow for species with protected status, missing/inconsistent documents, out-of-state/international origin, prohibited part, questionable take, poor condition or capacity/skill mismatch.

## Pricing and commercial model

Quote can be a price-list base by species and mount plus form/pose, open-mouth, pedestal, custom habitat/plaque, antler size, tanning, shipping, rush, special handling and tax. Physical specimen condition assessment may revise quote/time with written approval. Peak Taxidermy (Maine) lists its 2026 deer shoulder mount at $1,300, requires 50% deposit and notes custom habitats/plaque add cost; Treasure Valley Taxidermy lists deer shoulder at $850 and a 30% deposit, with a no-pickup clause after 90 days. Leyba Creations describes 12–24+ month turnarounds for some species/services; such expected timelines vary by shop, species and backlog. These are individual shop terms, not a national rate card. Deposits, rush charges, storage/abandonment, cancellation and pickup policies are contract-specific.

**Software willingness-to-pay:** Taxidermy Systems lists a Professional plan at $99/month; MountMonitor lists $49.99/month for its Lite plan and higher tiers; Taxidermy Pro lists $40/month or $400/year (all current public prices accessed 2026-09-24). These are advertised list prices, not observed purchases. MountMonitor’s site quotes testimonials about binders and job-status calls; an older Taxidermy Talk forum thread discusses offline programs and some taxidermists being price-sensitive. The signals contrast with multiple purpose-built paid products, but do not establish adoption or typical WTP. No independently verified subscription receipt/operator purchase was located. Mount prices are customer service revenue and do not establish software budget.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

New work may be booked at the shop, through a scheduled drop-off, or accepted at a hunting event/processor. Work scheduling is a capacity pipeline: preserve specimen on arrival; group prep/fleshing/tanning work by species/step; await tannery/parts; allocate bench/artist and drying/finishing space; notify customer for approvals; and track ready items and unpaid balances. Stages may take months and external tannery lead times can change. Rush work is a priced exception and must reflect actual shop/tannery capacity; do not provide an automated guaranteed delivery date from nominal stage durations.

Customer follow-up has due-date estimates, milestone updates, quote/deposit/payment, pickup readiness, storage/overdue pickup and shipping. A completed item waiting for collection still occupies space and custody. Recurring service is normally unnecessary for mount work; repeat customers may return seasonally, but that is a new project/job rather than a set interval. Routing is optional when the operator does event intake, pickup/delivery or multi-location service.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

At drop-off: confirm identity and legal owner/agent, inspect/document specimen and condition with dated photos, identify parts/species, record harvest origin/date and all required tag/license/permit numbers, attach shop job ID to each part, receive/issue applicable receipt, quote price/deposit, get written scope/pose and storage/field-care acknowledgment, then move to documented freezer/storage location. Before processing a restricted species or international job, review applicable state/federal paperwork and ask regulator or qualified counsel where unclear. During work, log prep, tannery shipment/return and tracking, mount/form/pose, drying, finishing, custom additions, customer approval/change order and storage moves. At handoff, photograph finished work, document invoice/balance, obtain pickup/shipping release acknowledgment and verify the right person is receiving the correct trophy.

Texas guidance illustrates requirements rather than a national rule: game parts given for mounting may require a Wildlife Resource Document, some species require a prescribed proof-of-sex receipt, and the current Outdoor Annual says the taxidermist retains WRD/tag for deer or wild turkey for two years after return/sale. Florida says no FWC permit is needed to mount non-protected wildlife for commercial use there, but migratory bird work requires federal authorization and Florida record/tag practices apply. USFWS requires a federal migratory-bird taxidermy permit for covered work on another person’s bird/parts, subject to detailed conditions; international export of CITES trophies may require permits and harvest/license/tag evidence plus an import/export license. These examples must not be generalized to other states/species.

Keep chemical/biological safety, freezer handling, ventilation, PPE, sharps, hide preservative/tanning substances and hazardous waste under shop-specific worker safety procedures; no universal taxidermy-specific worker standard was verified in this pass. Exception categories: species/document uncertainty, spoiled/poor-condition specimen, lost/mis-tagged part, tannery delay/damage, form mismatch, workmanship correction, customer change, payment dispute, completed/not collected, shipping loss, and shop capacity. Remake/refund/billability are contract/policy choices; protect the custody record if there is any dispute.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need intake receipt, written price/deposit/turnaround range, rules for field care and tag/document drop-off, occasional production milestone and tannery delay updates, change/pose approval, balance-due and ready-for-pickup alerts, storage/late-pickup policy, shipping status and final care instructions. A customer portal may show job ID, species, public-facing stage, photos and payments without exposing sensitive harvest coordinates or other customers’ work. Online booking can collect a preliminary request but should not reserve production capacity or assert legality/acceptance before human specimen/document review. Automated “ready” or completed messages must follow a staff-confirmed stage.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Shop inputs include freezers/coolers, tags/barcodes, fleshing tools, forms/mannequins, eyes, wire, adhesives, hide prep chemicals, tanning services, paint/airbrush, bases/habitat materials, PPE and shipping/packing. The shop’s physical layout and capacity (freezer volume, benches, drying/finishing and ready storage) can constrain acceptance more than calendar slots. Many operations outsource tanning; represent vendor, outbound/return date, shipment tracking, invoice and received condition. Staff/contract taxidermists may specialize by species or stage; record who owns the work and handoff. Inventory tracking is optional for solo craft operations but useful when teams hold many active specimens, forms/parts or retail stock. Specific license/permit evidence should be stored with the job and expiration alerts where relevant, not treated as a generic worker credential requirement.

## Reporting and operating measures

Vertical shop-management vendors advertise active work lists, job-stage tracking, customer/deposit/balance history, turn time, revenue, due dates, and species/mount-type reports. Useful reports include active jobs by stage/species/artist/tannery, freezer/storage count/location, seasonal intake and deposit liability, age since receipt by stage, tannery days, promised vs actual completion, overdue pickup/space occupied, unclaimed jobs, rework/damage, margin by mount type, and document/paperwork exceptions. Define turnaround start/end (receipt, deposit, tannery receipt, or completed), and distinguish estimated from completed jobs. Reports support safety/custody and capacity, not just sales.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Purpose-built products include Taxidermy Systems (specimen form collection, work list, invoices, email/SMS, staff and reports), MountMonitor (active-job pipeline, client portal, payments, tag printing/track-and-trace at upper tier), Taxidermy Pro (species/mount/stage templates, job status, customer updates, deposits and analytics), and Mount Manager (operator testimonial of prior sticky notes/memory and improved tracking). Vendor content specifically describes paper intake, whiteboards, binders and customer status calls, but those are sales narratives; the Taxidermy Talk 2026 workflow article details solo-shop job intake, durable ID, photos and downstream physical stages. A 2016 Taxidermy Talk software discussion includes an operator preference for offline/simple/low-cost software, while newer vendors price paid plans from $40 to $99/month and above. Forum claims are anecdotal and do not demonstrate aggregate adoption or purchase.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Texas and Florida show both species-specific records and differing permit obligations; federal migratory bird and CITES requirements add another layer when applicable. Shop pricing/deposit/turnaround vary, with published examples from 30% to 50% down and completion times from weeks for certain products to 12–24+ months for large-game mounts. Current vertical software clearly sells job, payment, status, paperwork and customer communication features, while forum material includes price sensitivity/offline preference. **Inference:** Physical specimen/job custody and paperwork are the defining CRM distinction. **Uncertain:** A single nationwide license/record policy cannot be specified. Taxidermists may only mount common legal game or may handle birds, fish, exotic, imported/exported specimens, each requiring separate screening. Regulator pages and current rules should be rechecked for the operator’s state, species and transaction. WTP is evidenced only by vendor ask prices; paid adoption was not independently verified. Confidence high for long-cycle workflow/custody need, medium for common-shop software gaps, low for prevalence/adoption.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype intake with document/photo capture, physical specimen ID, ownership and custody, accepted scope/deposit, stage tracking including tannery subcontracting, status updates, payment balance and pickup release. Ask which states/species and cross-border work the shop accepts, how specimens are tagged/stored, whether tanning is in-house, active-job/freezer capacity, species/mount catalog, timeline and deposit policy. Keep legal requirements, retention, permit screening, storage location, pricing and pickup rules jurisdiction/shop-configurable; unresolved or protected species need human review before acceptance. Verify generic record and audit primitives before declaring a core gap. Treat vertical prices as testable market signals, not proven WTP.
