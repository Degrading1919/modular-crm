## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A wine-cellar project is an engineered room/system plus storage and finish, not simply racking carpentry. Buyer needs (collection size/growth, room, aesthetic) interact with enclosure insulation, vapor protection, glass/door, cooling load, electrical circuit, condensate drainage, racking, lighting and service access. Operators vary from specialist turnkey design-build firms to rack vendors/designers coordinating with the owner's general contractor and HVAC/electrical trades.

CRM opportunity: preserve technical design revisions, responsibility boundaries, site readiness, product serial/warranty records and commissioning evidence across design, construction and service. A cellar may be a persistent customer asset with temperature/humidity and cooling history. **Inference:** Service follow-up, monitoring alerts and warranty records may support recurring revenue, but sources do not establish typical paid software or actual CRM WTP.

## Business model and customer segments

Segments include private collectors/homeowners, luxury residential builders, architects/interior designers, wine retailers/restaurants/hospitality and property managers. Projects range from a small conditioned closet or glass wine wall to a large cellar with tasting area, custom racking, glazing, lighting and redundant cooling. Some firms supply racks and drawings while contractors build the envelope; others coordinate turnkey installation. New construction/remodel projects must coordinate with design, framing, drywall, electrical, HVAC/refrigeration, glass and finish trades. Existing-cellar upgrades may replace cooling, improve envelope or add storage.

The work is one-off, with possible equipment maintenance, repair, warranty and expansion. Luxury residential firms are often regional; product suppliers may sell through dealers nationwide. Scope, climate, equipment and collection scale drive workflow more than a universal size band.

## Terminology and durable records

Records: property/room, design brief, bottle capacity/format mix, layout and revision, envelope specification, racking/material/finish, cooling unit/serial, thermostat, circuit, condensate route, door/glass, lighting, contractor responsibility matrix, commissioning, warranty and maintenance. Location data includes room dimensions, adjacent conditioned/unconditioned spaces, glazing/orientation, access, ceiling/structure and drain/power options. The cellar/cooling system is persistent; bottle inventory is optional and may be handled by collection software outside the contractor CRM. Store temperature/humidity readings and photos where useful; avoid treating bottle-level inventory as required.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks collection now/future, bottle/case formats, room or plans, desired display/tasting use, style/material, site location/climate, glass exposure, existing HVAC/electrical/drain, builder/designer contacts, access and budget range if volunteered. Operators describe site verification, design/CAD, approval and technical coordination before fabrication. Quotes may separate design, enclosure, racking, glass/door, cooling, electrical, lighting, finish, freight, installation and commissioning. Design and cooling selections require approval before order; field dimensions and utility conditions need verification.

Bookings follow consultation/site visit → design and investment alignment → engineering/specification → permit/trade coordination → enclosure/rough-ins → racking/glass/equipment → commissioning → handover/care. Web forms can capture plans/photos but should route to consultation, not promise a fixed quote.

## Pricing and commercial model

Drivers include room size/shape, bottle capacity and formats, wood/metal/acrylic racking, glass and door, insulation/vapor-control, cooling type/capacity, heat load, drainage/electrical, lighting, tasting area, site access, finish and subcontract scope. Customer projects may be high-value, but no service-price example is used as software budget. **Software WTP:** no direct operator purchase evidence located. Vendors show workflow/products and public support terms; these do not establish cellar builder CRM spend. Equipment warranty may exclude labor or damage from poor installation, making scope/serial/photos valuable, but coverage varies by brand and contract.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Schedule around approved drawings, permit/GC schedule, enclosure readiness, HVAC/electrician, freight, glass fabrication, room humidity/temperature conditions and final cooling start-up. Parts may ship LTL; access, elevators, staging and site protection matter. One design-build project manager differs from a rack dealer serving a contractor. Delays include changed bottle count/design, incomplete insulation, wrong circuit/drain, equipment backorder, glass dimension error or other-trade slip. Temperature excursion after handover may become service call, not original installation closeout.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Consult/measure → capacity/design → heat/load and equipment specification → approvals → coordinate enclosure and utility work → verify insulation/vapor barrier, opening, circuit, drain and access → install racking/glass/cooling/controls → start-up/commission → record operating values and serials → train owner → warranty/care handoff. Evidence includes approved plan/revision, hidden enclosure photos, product manuals/serials, clearances, electrical and drain details, commissioning readings, alarms, acceptance and warranty holder. Refrigerant handling, electrical work and construction permits require properly qualified/authorized trades as local rules demand. Manufacturer warranty conditions can exclude damage from poor insulation, drainage or unauthorized service. Noncompletion and callback reasons include unsuitable room, site not ready, leak/condensation, temperature instability, noise, unit fault and design change.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need design options, realistic cost/lead time, builder/installer responsibility, site-prep/rough-in details, delivery scheduling, equipment operation, temperature alarm/contact path, maintenance and warranty. A portal could expose drawings, approvals, manuals, serials and commissioning report. Temperature alerts should distinguish equipment alarm from verified wine damage; require human review.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Materials include racking, glass, doors, insulation/vapor barrier, cooling units/controls, line sets/ducts, drains, lighting and finish. Supplier/dealer lead time and specified models matter; project procurement is common, while a distributor may hold inventory. Team can include cellar designer, carpenter/rack installer, HVAC/R technician, electrician, glazier and GC. Keep licensed trade assignment, manufacturer authorized status and warranty responsibility explicit.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred metrics: consultation/design approval cycle, estimated-to-actual lead time, revisions, install schedule variance, commissioning pass/return, temperature/humidity excursions, warranty/service calls, change value and project margin. Slice by active/passive cellar, size/complexity, cooling unit and turnkey/dealer model. These are inferred from workflow, not incumbent-standard KPIs.

Capture approved revision, equipment serial, warranty holder, readiness evidence and commissioning values. Keep temperature/humidity requirements tenant/product specific; do not equate customer CRM with bottle-management software.
