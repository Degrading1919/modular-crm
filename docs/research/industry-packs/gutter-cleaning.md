## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Gutter cleaners remove leaves/debris and may clear downspouts, document condition, and refer or quote repairs/guards. Operators describe solo side work, subcontracting, ladder reach/equipment limits, minimum charges, and route density; some companies blend cleaning with window or exterior maintenance. Work is seasonal in many markets, but tree cover and local weather affect timing. OSHA ladder guidance and nearby power lines create safety-relevant site/crew constraints.

**Inference:** The durable record is a property with roofline/gutter sections, height/access, material/guard details, and prior visit condition. The job needs debris/flow observations, before/after proof where useful, and a repair referral/estimate handoff. Spring/fall recurrence is a candidate default only where local debris patterns support it. Current evidence does not establish a new core gap.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential owners buy seasonal or as-needed clearing, often after leaf fall or visible overflow. Additional buyers include landlords, property managers, HOAs, small commercial sites, and roofing/window/exterior-cleaning firms seeking a subcontractor or add-on. An operator reported batching jobs to achieve route density, a minimum charge, ladder capacity limits, and subcontracting taller homes; this is a single Reddit account. Another thread describes 1–3 homes/day for a two-person installation crew, not cleaning, illustrating that cleaning/install businesses should not be conflated. Common triggers include leaf/debris buildup, overflowing gutters, storms, property sale/maintenance, and preventive work. Repeat cadence may be one or more visits annually; evergreen/needle-heavy areas or guards can require different schedules. Solo operators and small crews may serve dense neighborhoods; larger property portfolios bring access coordination and multi-site routes.

## Terminology and durable records

Use **customer**, **property/site**, **roofline**, **gutter section/run**, **downspout**, **guard/cover**, **cleaning visit**, **debris**, **crew/technician**, **estimate**, **repair referral/callback**. Site data may include stories/eave height, roof pitch/access restrictions, ladder setup locations, overhead lines, landscaping/ground conditions, parking/gates, water access, property manager/contact, gutter material, guards, downspout locations, and prior overflow/clog history. Gutter sections can be persistent site features if used to retain length/material/condition history, but a CRM can begin with configurable fields rather than create a specialized entity. Debris quantity, current blockage, water-flow test, damage, and photos are per-visit findings. Ladder/pole/vacuum equipment, safe setup zone, nearby electrical hazards, and crew training are technician-only safety details. Do not store customer access codes in broad customer-visible notes.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Core service is debris removal from gutters and downspouts. Optional work can include bagging/removing debris, flushing/testing flow, roof-valley debris clearing, guard cleaning, minor reseating/sealing, repairs, guard installation, or referrals; repair/install work is a distinct scope and may require trade competence. Intake should capture address/service area, number of stories, roofline length/complexity if known, gutter guards, tree/debris load, access/ladder constraints, photos, water availability if flushing, and requested timing. A product vendor promotes map-measured roofline footage and single-/two-story quote options; this is a vendor claim, not universal pricing practice. Operator anecdotes show flat minimums and price differences by height/job size. Fixed package quotes can work for straightforward properties; tall/complex, guarded, damaged, or multi-building sites need review. Booking usually uses an appointment window and route day, with weather-related reschedules. Online quote forms should support photos and flag second-story/high-risk or repair requests for staff review.

## Pricing and commercial model

Quote inputs can include story/height, linear footage/roofline complexity, gutter guards, debris volume/type, downspout clogs, access/ladder setup, travel/minimum, disposal, multi-building count, repair/add-on scope, season demand, and recurring plan. Operator evidence includes one owner's $89 minimum and observed local variation, but the comments are anecdotal and should not become defaults. Software vendor pages cite spring/fall plans and sample ticket sizes; these are marketing claims. Pricing may be per home, height tier, footage, labor, or a fixed plan. Repeat visits may be prepaid, per-visit, or invoiced after work. Deposits, card-on-file, cancellation, guards/repairs warranty, and payment terms vary. **Software willingness-to-pay:** current vendor list prices are visible on gutter-targeted product pages: Opervo advertises $24.99/month, and Menutize $29/month for one seat plus additional seats. These are vendor list prices, not operator-reported purchases or evidence of willingness to pay. Menutize's comparison claims about competitor costs are not treated as independently verified.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Seasonal spring/fall cleaning is commonly marketed by gutter software vendors and is reflected in operator discussions; it is not universal. Tree species, guard type, storms, customer preference, and local climate determine visit count. Fall demand can create a compressed peak and spring catch-up. Dense neighborhoods reduce ladder/setup/travel time; operators may defer low-ticket distant jobs until they can batch nearby properties. Support annual or custom repeat plans, seasonal rebooking, appointment windows, route/day grouping, job duration/access flags, weather delay and safe-condition rescheduling, and customer schedule changes. A two-story property may require a different crew/equipment assignment. Do not automatically promise dates during storm/leaf surges; dispatch should retain manual route control.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Suggested sequence: review property/height/overhead hazards and prior notes; confirm customer access and safe weather/ground conditions; choose ladder, pole/vacuum, or other approved method; document pre-existing damage and clogged/guard condition; remove debris and clear downspouts within agreed scope; optionally test flow; photograph completed sections and findings; bag/remove debris per agreement; identify damage or repair needs; share closeout and invoice or schedule approved follow-up. OSHA guidance identifies ladder fall-prevention practices; Oregon OSHA has described an employee fatality risk when a metal gutter contacted a power line. The platform should prompt hazard review and make work stoppage available, not prescribe ladder technique. Candidate noncompletion reasons: unsafe weather/ground, power line or ladder access hazard, locked gate, roofline inaccessible, guard/repair scope excluded, heavy clog needing another service, water unavailable, equipment issue, customer cancellation, or partial completion. Billability and return policy remain tenant rules. Before/after photos help show hidden debris removal but are not established as universal operator practice.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need quote/height clarification, access instructions, appointment reminder, weather/delay notice, arrival message, completion proof (especially debris/downspout flow or damage findings), repair estimate/referral, invoice/payment, and next-season reminder. Self-service should allow address/photos, building/story count, guard information, schedule preference, plan acceptance, payment, and repair follow-up request. Human review is appropriate for high/unsafe access, damage, guards or repair scope, and complaints. Avoid automatic claims that every downspout is clear or repair is included unless the checklist and estimate say so.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Ladders, stabilizers, harness/fall-protection gear where applicable, gutter vacuums/poles, scoops, blowers, hoses, buckets/bags, PPE, and vehicles vary by operator. The operator example explicitly describes a car-sized ladder and subcontracting higher jobs; the vendor pages emphasize route and quote tools. Inventory can be off by default, with optional equipment assignment/inspection and consumable/debris disposal tracking for teams. Skills and safe access capabilities should be documented where crew assignment depends on them. Time tracking and payroll inputs are conditional on employees/subcontractors. No universal commission, piece-rate, or certification requirement established from these sources.

## Reporting and operating measures

Potential measures: scheduled/completed visits by season/property, jobs per route day, travel/setup time, quote acceptance, average ticket by height/guard/size, recurring plan renewal, missed/unsafe access, callbacks, repair referrals/conversion, and revenue by channel. These are inferences; vendors market routing, photos, and repeat plans, but sources do not establish common KPI definitions. Separate safety stops from ordinary cancellations.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Gutter-targeted tools market recurring seasonal plans, route optimization, roofline measurement, photos, invoices, and payments. Opervo advertises $24.99/month; Menutize advertises $29/month for one user plus seats. These are vendor claims, and competitor comparisons and typical job values are not independently verified. General field-service platforms can support estimates, scheduling, routes, invoicing, and communication. Operator evidence from Reddit and cleaning forums shows that some shops are solo/part-time or subcontracted, bundle gutter cleaning with window work, face seasonal lead timing, and choose different methods. A 2019 cleaning forum discussion contrasts gutter work's busy fall overlap with window cleaning and a contributor's decision to stop offering it. These posts are anecdotal and geographically bounded. No independently verified report of software purchase or common complaint prevalence was found.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources converge on height/access affecting workflow, route density and seasonality affecting economics, and cleaning being distinct from installation/repair. They do not establish one universal cadence, tool, cleanup method, quote basis, or prevalence of photos and software. The vendor pages offer precise prices and typical-ticket/route claims, but those are marketing and not independent market research. Confidence is **medium-high** for height/access and route/season effects, **medium** for recurring plans and quote inputs, and **low** for common software pain, prices, and exact service rates. Open questions: regional differences in gutter guard use, the share of operators using vacuum systems, and what safety competency data small firms record.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype property height/access and gutter/guard details, simple height/length quote inputs, seasonal repeat reminders, route-day planning, hazard and weather exceptions, and concise photo-supported closeout. Ask region/tree/debris pattern, equipment method, story-height mix, repair/install scope, service cadence, route density, crew structure, and whether photos or flow tests are standard. Keep recurrence, minimums, disposal, repair inclusion, and service method tenant-configurable. Test both solo side operators and multi-crew property managers before setting defaults.
