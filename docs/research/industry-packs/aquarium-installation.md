## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Aquarium installation can include consultation/design, tank and stand selection or fabrication, delivery and placement, plumbing/equipment connections, aquascaping, cycling, water balancing, livestock selection/acclimation, and optional recurring maintenance. Operator pages show a wide range from small freshwater packages to custom multi-thousand-dollar systems; tank volume, saltwater/reef complexity, access, design, equipment, stock, and follow-on care alter both scope and risk. Installation is a project with a durable customer tank asset; maintenance is a separate recurring service attached to that asset.

**Inference:** The strongest product opportunity is a project intake and quote that becomes a structured tank record and commissioning log, then can seed optional recurring care. Record site access, tank/system configuration, measurements, water-quality readings, equipment, livestock and customer-approved scope. Avoid treating every aquarium installer as a recurring maintenance company or every aquarium as a regulated public aquatic venue. No direct evidence established a universal aquarium-installer license or a standard software budget.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential hobbyists, interior designers/home builders, offices, restaurants, hotels, healthcare/waiting areas, and public or educational displays may buy a new tank, replacement, custom in-wall system, relocation, or upgrade. The buyer might be a homeowner, facilities manager, designer, contractor, or aquarium enthusiast. Small shops may sell the tank, livestock, setup, and monthly service together; design-build firms may coordinate architect, cabinetmaker, plumber, electrician, and specialist aquarist. Commercial work adds stakeholder approval, business-hours constraints, and a higher need for service logs and continuity.

Typical project path: discovery/site review → system/specification and written estimate → deposit/order → site readiness and delivery → placement/assembly/plumbing/equipment → leak/equipment testing → substrate/aquascape/water preparation → cycling and livestock plan → handover/training → warranty and optional maintenance. Some installations are completed in one visit, while complex systems may require multiple trade/site visits and delayed stocking while biological filtration stabilizes. These are not equivalent to routine tank cleaning.

## Terminology and durable records

Use client, aquarium/tank, display, service address/site, system, equipment, livestock, installation project, commissioning, and service visit. The durable asset is the tank/system: freshwater, planted, marine or reef; dimensions and rated volume; tank/stand/cabinet and placement; sump/filter/skimmer/pump/heater/lighting/CO₂/dosing/RODI/controller; plumbing and electrical dependencies; warranty/serial/model details; commissioned date; livestock/plant inventory; water parameter history and target ranges. Aquarium credentials or controller access are sensitive secrets and must not be stored as ordinary notes.

Location intake should capture floor/room and access path, stairs/elevator/door dimensions, parking/loading, tank location and substrate/floor support concerns, water/drain access, electrical outlet and GFCI status where relevant, shutoff/access, noise/light constraints, pets/children, building approval, and site contact. Large, in-wall, or unusually heavy systems call for qualified structural/trade review; this profile does not prescribe engineering thresholds. Keep one-time measurements, current readiness photos, and delivery instructions on the project, while retaining final tank specs and equipment in the asset record. Limit access to residence entry, alarm, and network/controller credentials.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Possible services: consultation/design; tank, stand, cabinet and lighting selection; custom fabrication coordination; delivery/placement; freshwater or saltwater equipment setup; plumbing; aquascaping/hardscape; substrate and planting; water preparation/cycling; livestock sourcing/acclimation; leak and equipment test; customer orientation; relocation; upgrade/repair; and optional recurring maintenance. Clearly identify work that requires a licensed electrician, plumber, structural professional, building approval, or another contractor rather than presenting it as included by default.

Lead intake should request desired system type and use, approximate volume/dimensions, freshwater/planted/reef, desired livestock, budget range, new/replace/move, room and building type, photos/video, access/loading path, intended placement, available water/drain/power, target deadline, designer/contractor involvement, and ongoing-care interest. A site visit or drawing review is appropriate when access, flooring, custom millwork, large volume, plumbing/electrical, or livestock needs are uncertain. Species compatibility and living-animal availability require human confirmation. Website intake should be a consultation/quote request rather than instant booking for complex installations.

## Pricing and commercial model

Pricing varies with tank volume/dimensions, freshwater versus saltwater/reef, custom design/fabrication, equipment and livestock included, access, travel, labor/overage, and recurring-care scope. E’s Aquascaping (Spokane County, WA) lists install base prices of $130 (<20 gal), $200 (20–50 gal), and $330 (50–100 gal), plus hourly overages and a $50 drive charge beyond 30 miles; a $250 date deposit has a cancellation window. Space City Shrimp lists setup ranges of $300–$600 nano, $500–$900 medium, and $1,000+ custom. Diamond Aquatics lists custom installations starting at $30,000 and includes one year of biweekly maintenance. These examples are not comparable market averages because scope, equipment, livestock, geography and business model differ. Recurring care is separately priced by tank size, visit frequency and service content. Deposits, cancellations, warranty and livestock survival terms are provider-specific.

**Software willingness-to-pay:** TankOps, TankHub, and TankBud Pro market aquarium-specific operations software; pages promote free plans/trials or demo/early access rather than documenting independent operator purchases. No operator statement establishing actual CRM WTP was found. Vendor list prices or the price of a custom aquarium are not evidence of installer software budget.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Installation is usually a one-time, quoted, multi-stage project with dependencies on fabrication, delivery, site readiness, trade work, cycling, and livestock. Schedule consultation, site survey, delivery crew, install window, return commissioning/stocking and handover separately; block appropriate vehicle/crew and equipment capacity. Track customer, vendor, contractor and building access dependencies so a missed delivery does not appear as a normal service no-show. Weather, building delivery restrictions, material lead times, water leaks, unsuitable floor/access, and delayed cycle readiness can force rescheduling.

Monthly, biweekly, or weekly maintenance is common as a vendor-offered follow-on, but should be optional and configured by service/operator. Each recurring visit attaches to one or more client tanks and may require different duration, skill, consumables, route and water-test checklist. Larger commercial routes make routing useful; a one-off residential installer may need only a calendar. Emergency aquarium calls and livestock/equipment recovery are time-sensitive and need manual triage, not automatic promise of an SLA.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Before work, confirm placement, access, floor/site readiness, power/water/drain, scope, tank/system inventory, and who approves changes. Photograph existing surfaces/access and tank/stand condition. Protect floors; receive and inspect tank; place and level according to manufacturer/installer practice; connect only within verified trade scope; fill/test for leaks; verify circulation, temperature control, lighting and other specified equipment; record water source/treatment, salinity/temperature/chemistry where applicable; document cycle status; add substrate/hardscape/plants/livestock only when agreed and safe; give owner operating and escalation instructions; collect sign-off and next steps. A result photo cannot prove water chemistry, leak test, or biological readiness, so use readings and equipment-test results as relevant evidence.

Livestock sourcing, transport, acclimation and compatibility can cause loss; animal guarantees are explicitly operator-specific (some sellers give guarantees and others exclude livestock during moves). Never imply the installer can guarantee survival regardless of system maturity or customer care. Record deaths, disease suspicion, leak, equipment malfunction, out-of-range reading, building/trade dependency, customer change, access failure, and no-show as distinct exceptions. Escalate unsafe electrical/wet-area conditions and structural concerns to appropriate licensed professionals. Do not autonomously dose chemicals or change life-support equipment based only on automated recommendations.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need pre-site checklist and quote, delivery/access reminder, status and delay updates, change approval, commissioning/cycle guidance, equipment manuals/warranty, reading/service report, and optional maintenance reminder. Portal history can expose tank configuration, completed work, allowed test values and invoices; hide credentials, access codes, technician-only risk notes and private staff comments. For system design, livestock substitution, structural/electrical issues, adverse readings or mortality, route communications to a qualified human rather than automating a reassurance or a livestock order.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Transport equipment: tank cart/dolly, lift help, blankets, protective flooring, hose/pump, buckets, water containers, spill control and vehicle load restraint. Installation inventory may include tanks/stands, filters, pumps, lights, plumbing fittings, substrate, rock/wood, plants, salt/RODI water, test reagents, livestock and controller sensors. Product and livestock availability/lead time matters; stock tracking is useful for retailers or multi-crew businesses, but not proved universal for installers. Some jobs require two-person lifts or a specialist subcontractor. Record staff competencies for freshwater/reef/live-animal handling and trade authorization; never infer credential validity from a free-form profile. Dispose of wastewater and acclimation water in accordance with local guidance; do not release aquarium organisms outdoors.

## Reporting and operating measures

Incumbent aquarium software advertises recurring service compliance, per-tank water parameter history and alerts, service logs, route/visit management, invoices, inventory/commissions and customer reporting. Useful measures include quote-to-install conversion, estimate versus actual labor/material, project schedule variance, commissioning return rate, leak/equipment callbacks, time to cycle/stock, maintenance attachment and retention, service completion by route, and water-test values outside tenant-defined target range. Most are proposed metrics, not proven universal industry KPIs. Report tank/test values with units, dates, test method and target provenance; do not aggregate incomparable test methods.

## Existing software and operator evidence

TankOps advertises recurring routes, tank-level checklists, water parameter history, alerts, client reporting, invoices and controller credentials; TankHub positions scheduling, route planning, service logs, invoicing and technician assignment; TankBud Pro has client tank records, service reports, recurring reminders and invoicing. General FSM software can cover leads, projects, calendar, quote, route, payment, and communications; specialist aquarium records may otherwise live in sheets/forms. Vendor statements that customers “duct tape” tools are marketing claims, not an independent prevalence study. Reddit aquarium-service discussions include new-operator pricing questions and one customer reporting a $100 minimum maintenance call; these anecdotes demonstrate price uncertainty, not typical rates or software pain.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Public install pricing spans hundreds of dollars for basic setups to $30,000+ for custom systems; care intervals and included livestock/maintenance differ by provider. Software vendors consistently treat tank-specific test histories and recurring care as specialized records. **Inference:** The tank is the useful anchor for follow-on CRM work, while installation should remain a project lifecycle. **Uncertain:** No national installation credential was established; applicable electrical, plumbing, structural, animal-sale, wildlife, and building rules vary by scope, locality and species. Fish transport permits may distinguish wild stock from aquarium fish; Washington explicitly exempts aquarium fish from its fish-transport permit, but that is not a nationwide rule. Aquarium load/electrical requirements need local professional review and cannot be encoded as a universal checklist from this research. Confidence high in project/maintenance distinction, medium in suggested fields and low in national regulatory generalization.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype the quote-to-project-to-commissioning lifecycle, tank asset history and optional maintenance plan. Ask whether the firm sells/installs livestock, handles water/electrical/plumbing, works residential/commercial, does moves/custom builds, and provides repeat care. Keep tank volume, system type, component list, water readings, target ranges, checklist and travel pricing tenant-configurable. Require site review for heavy/custom placements, human review for species/livestock and trade boundaries, and avoid committing to cycling, survival or safety outcomes based only on automated intake. Confirm with operators whether they will pay for tank-specific test records and whether shared measurement history exists before framing it as a core gap.
