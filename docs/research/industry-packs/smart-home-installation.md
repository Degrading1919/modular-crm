## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Smart-home installation ranges from retrofit devices to multi-system residential integration: lighting/shading, distributed audio/video, networking, control processors, climate, intercom and sometimes security. CEDIA credentials cover defined technician competencies; Lutron distinguishes room-scale lighting-control pros from qualified custom integrators. Complex projects move through discovery/design, proposal, prewire, install, programming, commissioning, owner training and support. D-Tools lists product libraries, proposals, engineering documentation, procurement, labor/project management and service. One current published SI plan starts at $1,800 per user annually with a five-user minimum, targeted at larger $75K+ projects; this is software list pricing, not evidence of an individual integrator purchase.

**Inference:** This should be a project-centric pack with discovery answers for system scope, new-build/retrofit, design/procurement responsibility, programming, trade coordination and support. Room/device schedules, drawings, BOM, change approvals, phase dependencies, commissioning tests, credentials, end-user training and service history matter. Existing CRM project, estimate, schedule, inventory, files and service capabilities appear suitable; no core-platform gap is established.

**Uncertain:** The candidate is broad and may cover a solo installer of consumer devices or a design-build integrator managing a multi-year new build. Research focuses on professional residential system integration. The business type and WTP cannot be inferred from consumer smart-device sales.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include homeowners, builders, architects, designers, remodelers, luxury property managers and sometimes boutique commercial clients. Engagements may be advisory design/engineering, retrofit installations, new-construction prewire through trim-out, commissioning/programming, upgrades, repair and recurring support. Revenue may combine equipment, design, labor, programming, project management and service plan. Long-cycle projects have dependencies on architect/GC/electrician/network and may be paid through deposit, progress and closeout milestones.

Intake should capture building stage, address, rooms/zones, systems desired, existing ecosystem, network condition, drawings, construction schedule, budget range, other trades and maintenance expectations. Do not treat “smart home” as a list of compatible gadgets; identify which party owns design, install, configuration, app/account and ongoing support. Online form should request a consultation or scope review for complex systems, while simple retrofit work may offer a service booking.

## Terminology and durable records

- **Site:** property/building, room, rack, network closet, low-voltage pathway, electrical location, access, construction phase, Wi-Fi/network, ISP, other trades and owner move-in date.
- **Assets:** system, subsystem, room/zone, device/controller, rack, network endpoint, model/serial, firmware, IP/network configuration reference, warranty, install/commission date, as-built drawing, vendor/account owner and service history. Maintain secure configuration backup and credential reference; do not put passwords in general notes.
- **Project inputs:** discovery goals, approved scope/design, room/device schedule, wire/cable path, BOM, product costs/vendor, labor/programming estimates, dependencies, change orders, commissioning test, scene/automation behavior, client training and acceptance.
- System network details, admin accounts and household routines can be sensitive. Limit access to authorized technical staff and hand off credentials through secure processes.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services can include consulting/design, network infrastructure, structured wiring/prewire, whole-home lighting/shades, AV/media rooms, audio, theater, climate interfaces, smart locks/intercom, camera/alarm integration, system controller/programming, commissioning, training, repair and ongoing support. Distinguish electronic systems integration from electrical branch wiring, alarm monitoring, security guarding and standalone lighting installation. Name exclusions and responsible trade.

Proposal may be systemized equipment plus labor, phases, programming, engineering, commissioning, allowances and support. Quote needs room/device/product choices, compatible platforms, drawings/site state, client goals, labor, construction dependency, integration risk, vendor pricing, travel and contingencies. D-Tools' product library/pricing and system design capability demonstrate vertical product-library/quote workflows; no common pricing convention is implied. Change orders are material when owner adds zones, devices or features after proposal.

Booking follows discovery → design/proposal approval → prewire/rough-in → equipment procurement → install/trim-out → program and test → client training → support. Long lead and other-trade readiness can span months. Maintain dependencies and approved design versions. For a consumer retrofit, condense phases without dropping compatibility, network and handoff checks.

## Pricing and commercial model

Service prices are often scope-driven and equipment-heavy; no current operator public price list sufficiently comparable to these candidate labels was verified. Do not equate a homeowner's device price or project budget with installer service price. D-Tools' SI list price is explicitly software: starting $1,800/user/year, five-user minimum, with professional services at $200/hour; the page targets larger companies/projects ($75K+) and should not be applied to small one-person installers. No independent operator statement of CRM WTP was found. Vendor testimonials are marketing evidence that named firms use a vertical product, but not transparent purchase details or representative willingness-to-pay. Small teams may use simpler tools; no size-specific adoption survey was found.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Primary schedule is project milestones and trade/site dependencies, with service calls and support agreements recurring after handoff. Match designers/engineers, field technicians, programmers and subcontractors to stage. Route planning matters for service visits, less for large build projects. Capacity depends on programming and commissioning expertise and supplier availability. Reschedule based on construction readiness and long-lead product; don't report full completion at physical install when programming, test, training or documentation remains.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Discover goals, user needs, current systems, budget, site/build stage and who owns each trade.
2. Create versioned design, device/room schedule, BOM, drawings, quote and milestones; obtain approvals.
3. Coordinate prewire pathways, rack/closet, power/network, labeling, photos and other-trade readiness.
4. Procure and install equipment per approved design; track substitutions, serials, location and as-built changes.
5. Configure/program integrated systems, test each subsystem and expected scenes/behaviors, verify network and update documentation.
6. Train occupants/admin, provide manuals/credentials securely, obtain signoff and define support/warranty boundary.

CEDIA wiring guidance identifies service definition, home planning, installation/commissioning, and testing/documentation/certification as a project flow; manufacturer commissioning materials reinforce functional tests and user training. Electrical work follows jurisdictionally adopted codes and qualified personnel. Rework can follow unapproved changes, poor prewire documentation, network outage, incompatible product, late construction, programming or user-expectation mismatch. “Device powers on” is not equivalent to integrated-system commissioning.

## Customer communication and self-service

Use discovery questions, design review, proposal/BOM approval, phase readiness, schedule and delay updates, change orders, commissioning summary, training, secure credentials, warranty/service instructions and support intake. A client portal may host drawings, selections, approvals and closeout documents; different family members may need varying permission. Human review is required for design changes and privacy/security access. Keep a plain-language client view of scope rather than requiring customers to understand device-level jargon.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Equipment includes cable and test tools, rack hardware, network gear, AV, sensors, lighting/shade controllers, security interfaces and programming tools. D-Tools product library and vendor partner pricing show project BOM/supplier workflows; actual catalogs and costs vary. Inventory tracking can be valuable to integrators with staging/warehouse, optional for a solo retrofit specialist. Workforce can include designer, engineer, field technician, programmer, project manager, electrician and subcontractors; phase/time capture helps large projects. Certifications are optional/role-specific unless local contract/law requires.

## Reporting and operating measures

Potential measures: project gross margin and change-order recovery; estimate vs actual labor/material; procurement status; phase schedule variance; first-pass commissioning; callbacks by subsystem; service response; open support backlog; and revenue by new build/retrofit/service. Vertical tools advertise project, labor, schedule and financial reporting; exact KPI definitions are product/operator-specific.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree that system scope and project stage change substantially from a retrofit to a new-build integrator job. Vertical software prices scale from a cloud entry product to a high minimum/seat price for larger firms, but neither implies universal WTP. Some vendor workflows claim unified project records; BRCKS targets field documentation alongside rather than replacing estimation systems. **High confidence:** project, device, commissioning and secure handoff records are important to professional integrators. **Medium:** separate light retrofit and design-build pathways. **Low:** size distribution and independent subscription adoption. Confirm whether the candidate serves consumer installs or CEDIA-like integration and whether recurring support is sold.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Onboarding should ask whether the company does simple retrofit, wiring/prewire, full design-build, AV, lighting, security, network, programming, commissioning and support. Prototype approved design/BOM versions, phase dependencies, field photos, device register, test/training signoff, and secure credential handoff. Keep system compatibility, vendor catalog, credentials, licenses, electrical responsibility, project size and price tenant-configurable.
