## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A secure delivery box installation is a product/site-fit job: select parcel capacity and opening/locking method, confirm delivery address and carrier access, choose a location that fits route and property layout, anchor/mount the box, and hand over operating instructions. USPS states that customer purchase, installation and maintenance of private receptacles are generally the customer's responsibility, and that equipment must meet applicable postal specifications where required. USPS product guidance recommends checking location and mailbox requirements and consulting the postmaster for custom mailboxes. A parcel-box manufacturer publishes an installation guide; an installation operator sells USPS-approved cluster units to communities/developers.

**Inference:** A CRM should capture carrier/mail scope, product model/capacity, site photos and measurements, substrate/anchor method, access/clearance, utility/sidewalk/HOA constraints, installation proof and customer handoff. The work is usually one-time, with repair/replacement as possible follow-up. Existing estimates, schedule, checklist, invoice and customer communications are sufficient; no core-platform gap is established.

**Uncertain:** The label could mean one residential porch/curb box, a locking wall/ground parcel box, or large community cluster-box installation. Those have different engineering, procurement, postal approval, excavation and property-manager workflows. No reliable data establishes software use or WTP among this niche; these are explicitly unsupported.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential homeowners, rural property owners, small businesses, multifamily owners/HOAs, builders and developers may buy installation. Services may be standalone labor, product-plus-installation, mailbox replacement, ground anchoring, or larger cluster-box sales/installation. A residential parcel box is generally a single transaction. Community mail stations can require developer/owner coordination, postal review, approved equipment, concrete/foundation, installation and later maintenance.

Purchase triggers include package theft, weather exposure, delivery volume, a new home, inaccessible porch, shared delivery point or damaged mailbox. Intake should ask whether the customer expects USPS mail or only private parcels; box size/model; installation location; delivery-carrier practice; curb/door/driveway access; existing mailbox; substrate; HOA/landlord approval; and whether excavation or post work is needed. Do not promise delivery use simply because a box is marketed as secure.

## Terminology and durable records

- **Site:** address, curb/porch/entry, delivery approach, carrier path/clearance, sidewalk/road edge, grade, drainage, visibility, mounting substrate, buried-utility risk, property lines, HOA/building requirements and safe loading/unloading.
- **Persistent asset:** parcel receptacle by location, manufacturer/model, dimensions/capacity, lock/access type, mounting/anchor, carrier scope, install date, keys/admin handoff, warranty and maintenance. Do not store a lock combination in open text.
- **Job inputs:** selected model, intended mail/parcel types, approval/standards evidence, delivery dimensions, installation instructions, site photos, base/post/wall condition, anchor hardware, utility clearance, access and signoff.
- **Sensitive data:** keys, lock codes and access arrangements require controlled handling. Customer-facing handoff can include secure delivery method, maintenance instructions and photos without publishing credentials.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Possible work includes parcel box assembly, wall/post/concrete-pad mounting, removal/replacement, delivery locker or cluster unit installation, anchoring, lock setup and product orientation. Clarify product sourcing, disposal, trenching/concrete, postal approval, paving/landscaping repair, smart access, custom fabrication and any HOA/city coordination.

Intake asks product link/model and dimensions, photos of proposed location, intended carriers, property type, mounting surface, access, distance from road/door, package size/frequency and approvals. Quote inputs include unit/capacity, shipping/handling, mounting/anchoring, surface, excavation/concrete, removal, travel, crew and multi-unit count. A site check is necessary for large, heavy, community or unusual installations. Schedule only after model, shipment, access and approvals are confirmed.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most home installations are one-off appointments. Community developments may coordinate many boxes and require site access, foundation cure, delivery windows, postal inspection/approval or phased occupancy. A small installer may handle one visit; multiple boxes require crew, lifting and delivery logistics. Route optimization is conditional on several daily jobs. Return trips can follow missing hardware, unsuitable substrate, damaged shipment, incorrect dimensions, inaccessible location or HOA/postal rejection. A general CRM should distinguish install completion from confirmed customer/carrier usability.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Confirm unit/model, address, intended carrier types and property/owner approvals.
2. Verify location, delivery/vehicle clearance, level/grade, mounting surface, buried utilities and applicable postal requirements before drilling/excavating.
3. Inspect shipment and parts, assemble per manufacturer guide, mount/anchor with the approved method and weather protection.
4. Test door/drop/lock operation, verify unit is stable and accessible, and confirm signage/placement instructions.
5. Capture before/after photos, exact model/anchor location, customer acceptance, key/credential handoff and maintenance/warranty.

USPS guidance makes a distinction between USPS-owned/authorized delivery equipment and private mail receptacles; an installer must not claim postal approval for an unapproved product. Delivery drivers may still leave a package outside a customer-provided box or use a different access route; community anecdotes show use is not guaranteed. This is a carrier operational uncertainty, not an installation defect by itself. Noncompletion reasons include postal/HOA approval, unsafe placement, utility conflict, unsuitable anchor surface, shipment damage, missing parts or customer unavailable. Local utility-location requirements and accessibility/fire egress should be checked where relevant.

## Customer communication and self-service

Before service, confirm model, location approval, carrier intent, access and site clearances. After install, provide usage, lock/key handoff, capacity and package placement guidance, plus warranty/maintenance. Self-service can collect photos/model and offer a survey/quote request but should not assert a carrier will use the box. Avoid sharing access codes or keys by ordinary unprotected messages.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Boxes, posts/pedestals, anchors/bolts, concrete, sealant, level, drill, digging tools, locating equipment and lifting/handling gear vary by installation. Product model and manufacturer instructions are job-specific; avoid generic default inventory. Community cluster units may require more than one installer and equipment for heavy components. Credentials, postal standards and product installation competency depend on product and project.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

USPS consumer guidance and its Domestic Mail Manual distinguish private customer equipment from USPS-owned equipment and point customers to approval/placement rules; parcel-product makers provide model-specific instructions. Operators show both single-unit residential and large community box installation scope. Community posts describe carriers bypassing resident boxes; the prevalence is unknown. **High confidence:** model/site/anchor/placement and use-case must be captured. **Medium:** multi-unit work warrants separate quote and project path. **Low:** ordinary software stack and WTP. Clarify residential parcel box vs centralized approved CBU work; verify carrier requirements and local utility/HOA approvals before promises.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask whether the job is a home parcel box or community mailbox installation; whether USPS mail is expected; who supplies the unit; and whether permits/HOA/postal approval are needed. Use model-specific checklists, location and anchor photos, safe credential handoff and explicit carrier-use uncertainty. Do not promise that all delivery companies will use a customer-selected box.
