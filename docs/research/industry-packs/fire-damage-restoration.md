## Executive summary

**Observed evidence.** Fire restoration responds to an event that can affect a building, HVAC systems, contents and nearby structures through flame, smoke, soot, heat and odor. It may also include water damage from firefighting. IICRC's 2025 S700 standard distinguishes the work through assessment of residue/odor presence, intensity and boundaries, then restoration cleaning and odor management for structures and contents; it calls for work plans, event documentation, qualifications and coordination with Authorities Having Jurisdiction. A contractor's documented workflow shows stabilization/security, inspection, pack-out inventory, structural cleaning, deodorization, repairs and return of contents.

The customer is often an owner/insured who is simultaneously displaced and coordinating an adjuster, emergency services/building officials, restoration crews and contents vendors. The strongest pack opportunity is project coordination that links room-level damage, source/residue findings, contents inventory and custody, approved scope, multi-trade work, customer decisions and claim documents. Fire has substantially more contents custody and odor evaluation than a basic water-drying profile. Wildfire, industrial/chemical fire, healthcare and other special-hazard work need separate qualification; the current S700 overview excludes some of these contexts.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential owners dominate the visible consumer path; multifamily managers, commercial owners and institutions add business interruption, occupant/security and authority coordination. Referral/lead paths include emergency calls, insurers/adjusters, property managers, fire restoration networks and direct search. Fire severity ranges from localized smoke/soot cleaning to large structural loss. Urgency starts with securing the property and mitigating ongoing damage, while full restoration may become a multi-week, multi-vendor project. Companies vary from mitigation-only teams to firms with contents cleaning, storage, reconstruction and specialty subcontractors. That variation changes recordkeeping, inventory and billing, so not every fire pack tenant should be forced into a full contents or reconstruction workflow.

## Terminology and durable records

Useful terms: **loss/property**, **fire/smoke/soot residue**, **odor management**, **source removal**, **scope/restoration work plan**, **contents inventory**, **pack-out/pack-back**, **salvageable/non-restorable**, **adjuster**, **mitigation**, **reconstruction**, and **AHJ**. Persistent records are property/unit, room/assembly and customer history. Contents become a temporary job inventory with item description, room, photos, pre-existing condition, cleaning/restoration decision, box/container, storage location, custody movements, treatment, and return reconciliation/customer acceptance. Store observed residue/odor and material condition distinctly from conclusions. Claim, security/access, sentimental/high-value property, occupant needs and photographs are sensitive. Customer and adjuster may need separate report views.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services can include emergency board-up/security, water extraction from suppression efforts, smoke/soot inspection, contents triage/pack-out, structural cleaning, HVAC assessment/cleaning, deodorization, demolition, repairs and reconstruction. Intake should capture fire address/status, fire department release and access, active hazards, affected rooms/levels, suppression water, smoke spread, occupants and temporary housing/access needs, adjuster/claim contacts, utility status, and urgent securing needs. Site inspection and item/material testing determine scope; a blanket fixed-price booking is unsuitable for larger losses. Customer approvals about contents disposal, specialty cleaning and replacement may be necessary. Schedule urgent stabilization first, followed by separate structural, contents, vendor, and repair calendars.

## Pricing and commercial model

Pricing is scope-dependent: response/secure work, room/material area, residue severity, cleaning method, labor, contents item count and handling, storage, specialty vendors, equipment, deodorization, demolition/disposal, repairs, and project duration. Claim payment and customer responsibility vary with policy, coverage, deductible and carrier review; no single commercial model is supported. No representative fire-service price was verified in this research. **Software pricing signal:** Encircle publicly advertised restoration documentation at $270/month for up to 200 jobs with unlimited users plus separately priced floor plans, accessed 2026-09-24; applicable as documentation tool signal, not a fire-only product or CRM budget. Cotality DASH pricing is quote-based on the reviewed page.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Fire response is event-driven, urgent and project-based. Dispatch needs availability, service area, safe access, crews, board-up materials, extraction/cleaning equipment and sometimes contents warehouse capacity. Projects require dependencies: official clearance/access, assessment, approvals, pack-out before structural cleaning, specialty vendors, repair readiness and contents pack-back. Routes can help multiple smaller losses or commercial portfolio work, but are secondary to response and project dependencies. Reassessment and change orders follow newly discovered damage, hidden odor/residue or contents decisions. Delays may stem from AHJ release, carrier authorization, customer decisions, subcontractors or reconstruction; these should be tracked as distinct blockers rather than generic “late.”

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

IICRC S700 describes assessment of residue/odor location and intensity/boundaries, work-plan development, cleaning and odor management, project event documentation and AHJ compliance. An Oregon operator's customer-facing process describes stabilization and inspection, contents evaluation/photography/inventory, off-site cleaning/storage where needed, structural work, and contents return; its process is operational evidence but also marketing. Pack-out records should capture room, item description, condition before handling, photo, item disposition, tagged container/box, custody/storage, treatment, and pack-back reconciliation/customer acceptance. Soot residues and chemicals, structural instability, electrical hazards, asbestos/lead, suppression water and occupant-specific conditions can require regulated/specialist handling. S700 says wildfire, chemical/industrial fires, some special facilities and occupant exposure are not comprehensively addressed by its first edition; AHJ and other regulations govern. Completion may require cleaning/odor review, records, contents reconciliation, customer acceptance, invoice/claim support and repair signoff. Re-cleaning, persistent odor and damaged/missing contents drive callbacks and disputes.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need prompt contact, safety/access updates, clear sequence, contents decisions, item inventory access, temporary schedule changes, scope/claim documents, progress, pack-back appointments and final signoff. Automated statements about safety, restoration value, health exposure or claim coverage should be reviewed by accountable staff. Customer portal can share approved inventory, documents, estimates, invoices, photos and schedule. High-value/sentimental items and disposal decisions require explicit owner approval; delay or denial notifications should retain a human contact.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Common job resources include PPE, air filtration, cleaning chemistry, meters, extraction/drying equipment, board-up materials, packaging and labels, storage space, vehicles, and specialty cleaning/reconstruction vendors. Contents work makes barcode/box, warehouse location, custody, storage fees and return reconciliation more central than in the water-only loop. Staff may include mitigation techs, contents packers/inventory staff, project managers, estimators, specialty vendors and reconstruction trades. A small company may outsource several roles; defaults must be optional. Track assigned equipment, materials and job cost without assuming an owned warehouse or reconstruction crew.

## Reporting and operating measures

Recommended observed-artifact reports: response time, open losses by stage/blocker, room/residue findings, contents inventory by disposition/custody, storage duration, pack-back reconciliation, estimates/supplements/approvals, equipment deployment, callbacks/persistent odor, and job margin by service line. Contents inventory and claim reports are evidenced in restoration software; other operational KPIs are useful inference and should be validated with operators. Segment reports by residential/commercial, fire severity, mitigation/contents/reconstruction, referral source, adjuster/carrier and crew.

## Existing software and operator evidence

IICRC standards establish fire-specific field scope; Encircle sells field documentation, contents inventory, pack-out, condition photos and reports for restoration businesses, and Cotality DASH sells job management across field and office. These are incumbent sources, but vendor claims do not establish share or satisfaction. Oregon Restoration gives a first-person operator workflow including adjuster/common-facts documentation, pack-out and salvage triage. A homeowner Reddit post reports problems with missing/duplicate inventory and poor records during a pack-out; it illustrates risk rather than market frequency. The reviewed sources do not establish the typical accounting/CRM stack, how often fire firms offer contents services, or specific software complaints by operator size.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

S700 first edition (2025) is new; IICRC began revising it in 2025 to add wildfire coverage, so wildfire claims need distinct current rules and expertise. Its public summary does not supply full procedural detail. Fire firms differ in contents, reconstruction, catastrophe and commercial capabilities. The operator process source and Encircle are interested parties; homeowner complaints are anecdotal. **Uncertain:** extent of fire-only vs diversified restoration firms, use of pack-out tools across firm sizes, typical claim payment timing, service prices, and software adoption/willingness to pay. Confidence high for residue/contents workflow distinction; medium for commercial and incumbent capabilities; low for market prevalence/prices.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype urgent loss intake, room/residue inspection, contents triage and inventory, before-handling condition photos, pack-out custody/box tracking, multi-stage approvals, repair dependencies, customer-visible documents and pack-back reconciliation. Ask whether the firm offers contents, storage, reconstruction, 24/7 response, commercial/catastrophe jobs, and which claim workflow it supports. Keep hazard acceptance, AHJ release, item disposition, storage policy, claim documents and pricing configurable; do not treat wildfire or hazardous fires as the standard fire workflow.
