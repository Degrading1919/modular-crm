## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Holiday lighting/decor providers sell consultation/design, custom material, installation, in-season service calls, takedown and optional off-season storage. Public and operator examples commonly use roofline/per-foot or home complexity and package first-year vs repeat-year; one local operator lists a package with consult/design, install, removal and storage, while industry/operator discussions flag storage and warranty expectations. The workload is unusually seasonal, weather/roof-access constrained and dependent on storing customer-owned or provider-owned decorations accurately.

**Inference:** Product fit is seasonal quote/design approval, property roofline/site records, material ownership/tagging, crew/route scheduling, safe installation checklists, service calls, takedown and storage inventory linking each decoration set to customer/property. **Uncertain:** “decorations” may include indoor trees, garlands, wreaths and holiday staging, not only exterior lights; evidence is strongest for Christmas lights. Enable decor types by operator scope, and do not assume all services occur at height.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential homeowners, HOAs, retailers, restaurants, offices, hotels, commercial plazas and municipal properties buy holiday atmosphere without self-installation. Some providers lease commercial-grade lights/decor, others sell customer-owned materials with installation, and some work only on customer-supplied commercial-grade product. Revenue may bundle design/material/install/takedown/storage, annual re-install service, warranty/service calls, or interior decor. Customers may purchase during fall; season ends after holiday with January takedown. Capacity, ladder/roof skill, storage space and inventory finance distinguish solo seasonal crews from full-time landscape/lighting firms.

## Terminology and durable records

Property record: roofline/linear footage, stories, roof pitch/material, gutters/eaves/peaks, trees/landscape, power/outlets/circuits, access/parking, HOA/site permission, indoor spaces and client-approved design/photo. Inventory record: provider-owned vs customer-owned item, make/type/color/length, custom-cut segment, plug/connection, condition, serial/tag/bin/storage location, quantity and seasonal assignment. Job inputs: install map, timer/circuit location, seasonal dates, weather, crew, ladder/lift, customer decor box and fragile items. Keep each set linked to location and owner; property image/design and prior repair history persist while install condition changes each season.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services may include design consultation, roofline lighting, trees/yard, wreaths/garlands, indoor tree/decor, install, timer, in-season bulb/line repair, takedown, organization/storage and annual re-install. Intake requests property photos/measurements, decor types/color/design, height/roof/power/access, provider or customer material, storage needs, HOA restrictions, desired date/removal deadline, and service area. Quote based on length/complexity/stories/roof pitch, lights and accessory materials, timer/power, labor/install and takedown, design, lift/access, storage and service/warranty. Confirm whether first-year materials are purchase or lease and who owns them next year; approve mockup/design before custom cuts. Online booking should start a survey/quote, not assign an exact install date before route capacity and roof/access assessment.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service prices:** Axios’ 2019 Charlotte reporting describes local full-service packages beginning at $999 for a 2,000-square-foot home with straight rooflines, including consultation, custom design, setup, takedown and storage, and another provider averaging $2,000+ ([local operator reporting](https://www.axios.com/local/charlotte/2019/12/11/6-ways-to-outsource-the-stress-of-the-holidays-including-light-installation-and-gift-wrapping-188796), accessed 2026-09-24). These are dated regional examples, not current national rates. A first-person installer forum reports $8–$10/ft with takedown, but another customer/operator thread notes storage complaints and significant regional variation ([operator thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChristmasLights/comments/182sk5i/), accessed 2026-09-24). A contractor pricing article decomposes price into material, installation labor, takedown and storage; source is industry-supplier guidance, not independent price survey ([guide](https://thunderlightingsupply.com/christmas-light-installation-pricing/), accessed 2026-09-24).

Inputs: measured roofline/trees/decor area, stories/pitch, fixture/material ownership, custom cuts, timer/accessories, install/takedown, season service/callback, storage bin/space, route/crew/lift, commercial site requirements and warranty. Deposit/material payment, seasonal bundle and renewal are common product choices but terms are provider-specific. Software pricing/WTP: no operator-specific software purchase or stated willingness to pay found. General field-service software list prices are not evidence of seasonal decorator demand; small operator may use quote forms, calendar, payment, inventory tags and spreadsheets.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Install demand clusters in weeks before holidays; takedown follows in January under customer/municipal deadlines. Schedule by weather, daylight, roof dryness, crew/lift, neighborhood route, material prep and commercial opening hours. Prebook returning customers from prior install map, inventory and dates, with a fresh property/site check after roof/gutter changes. Weather reschedule and roof access can cascade across several routes. On-season repair visits need priority rules; permanent fixtures/long-term architectural lighting is a separate install business. Storage pickup and return require labeled set/bins by customer/property; do not mix sets across seasons.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Approve design/scope, ownership/price and install map; verify weather, roof/access, circuits and permission; inspect materials/cords/fixtures; assign trained crew and ladder/lift; install with outdoor-rated product and secure fasteners, check load/circuit/timer and follow manufacturer limits; document photos/circuit/timer and customer walkthrough; record in-season repair requests; schedule takedown, test/label/coil/storage; mark inventory returned and set condition. CPSC says secure outdoor lights, limit standard sets per extension cord and use products intended for conditions. OSHA ladder/fall protection requirements apply to covered work; job hazard/site-specific analysis needed for roof pitch, height, weather and power lines. Do not promise code compliance based solely on CRM checklist; follow local electrical/fire/building rules and product listing. Never use damaged cords or overloaded circuits. Noncompletion: unsafe roof/wet/ice/wind, faulty wiring/outlet, power capacity, damaged material, inaccessible elevation, HOA/customer design change or blocked access. Rework includes failed bulbs/line, wind damage, timer failure, gutter detachment, property damage and missed takedown/storage set.

## Customer communication and self-service

Consultation/design choices, exact package/ownership, prep/power/access, install window/weather delay, completion photos, in-season service response, takedown window, storage location and renewal options should be explicit. Portal should show design and itemized scope/material ownership. Obtain customer approval for substitutions, extra line, roof access and repair charge. Commercial clients need insurance/site access windows and maintenance response. Send renewal before fall booking but do not auto-renew or charge until contract permits.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Commercial LED strings/bulbs, clips/fasteners, extension cords/timers, wreaths/garlands, transformers, testers, ladders/lifts, PPE, harness/fall gear as required, service vehicle/trailer, storage bins, labels and photo maps. Material may be cut to property-specific roofline and is hard to redeploy; track owned/leased, color, length, customer assignment and storage condition. Crew needs ladder/roof/electrical safety and decor installation skills; spotter/ground crew, lift operator and design consultant may be distinct. Off-season storage capacity and labeling are real operational costs. Indoor staging has separate fragile-item handling and less height/electrical scope.

## Reporting and operating measures

Track lead-to-survey, quote approval, material cost/ownership, roofline/crew-hour, install on-time, season revenue per route, service callback, takedown completion, inventory return/missing/damaged, storage bin accuracy, renewal/retention and injury/property damage. Separate install, service, removal and storage margins. These measures are inferred from business model; no industry KPI benchmark located.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Specialized providers use design/measure forms, customer-owned/provider-owned light inventory, seasonal scheduling, crew dispatch, service calls and storage. General CRM/job management and accounting can handle quotes, schedules and payments; specialized light design/measurement software may exist but its adoption was not established in the sources. First-person installer discussions show seasonal pricing/packaging and customer frustration around storage; anecdotal only. No credible software plan or operator WTP source was found. Evaluate whether a generic field-service setup fits a seasonal crew before assuming vertical software demand.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Full-service bundles include design, install, removal and storage; per-foot service pricing appears in first-person forums but varies by roof/material; CPSC/OSHA provide safety limits. **Inference:** repeat service depends on stored decor sets and route capacity; storage is a material cost and complaint source. **Uncertain:** inventory may be provider lease or customer-owned; indoor decor market is thin in sources; local electric/fire/fall requirements vary. Confidence high for exterior light workflow, medium for broad holiday decor scope and price, low for software WTP.
