## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Graffiti abatement is a property- and surface-specific field service sold to storefronts, property managers, municipalities, transit and institutional property owners. Intake and method depend on graffiti medium/age, substrate and coating, area, access, sensitivity, and whether removal, repainting, or an anti-graffiti coating is in scope. National Park Service preservation guidance stresses documentation, discreet test patches, least-aggressive methods, appropriate PPE, and reassessment because one technique can damage porous historic masonry.

**Inference:** The strongest product opportunity is a rapid-response site-and-incident record with surface history, photos, method/product, SLA, access, disposal notes and before/after proof. Municipal or multi-site accounts may need recurring patrols and work orders; single-storefront work may be one estimate and visit. **Uncertain:** small independent operators' software stacks and software willingness to pay are not established by the available sources.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include commercial property managers, retailers, owners of multi-site portfolios, municipalities, transit agencies, schools and historic-property stewards. Work ranges from one-off tag removal to contract response across a named set of sites. Municipal requests may be triaged from public submissions, while private work comes from property managers, direct calls and pressure-washing/conservation referrals. Re-tagging can create repeat work or coating/maintenance contracts, but there is no universal recurrence interval. Response priority, authorization to enter/clean, and public right-of-way access can differ by buyer.

## Terminology and durable records

Keep customer/account, property/site, incident/tag, surface and work order distinct. Durable site fields: address/GPS, owner/authorization, asset type (wall, storefront, sign, fence, transit shelter), substrate (brick, stone, concrete, painted metal/wood/glass), finish/coating history, access/height, water/drainage constraints, sensitive/historic status and preferred response SLA. Incident fields: discovered date, graffiti medium/color/age if known, area/height, photos, prior attempts, removal method/test patch, cleaner/SDS/product batch, coating applied, result/ghosting and follow-up. Exact affected area, weather, access and one incident's disposition are job data rather than permanent asset attributes. Avoid storing speculative offender identity.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services can include chemical removal, low-pressure wash/steam, controlled micro-abrasion for suitable substrates, glass scraping, repaint/cover-up, test patches and sacrificial or permanent anti-graffiti coating. Historic masonry may require conservation expertise; carved or deeply penetrated graffiti may not be removable without surface loss. A city request intake asks for exact location, details and a photo; an operator describes photo, surface and size intake and a written quote with method, price and timeline. Collect site authorization, substrate/finish, graffiti medium/area/height, age, nearby occupants/vehicles, water and drainage, access restrictions, historic status, urgency, prior coating and photo. Site assessment or test patch is appropriate where method compatibility is uncertain. Quote may use minimum/callout plus area, method, height/access, repeat applications, coating, containment, traffic control and urgency. Online flow should request assessment rather than promise removal from a single photo.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service pricing:** GraffitiRemoval.ca (Canadian contractor/marketplace) lists method ranges including $2–$6/sq ft for pressure washing and $3–$8/sq ft for chemical removal; its broad Canadian guide states $1–$20/sq ft depending on method, substrate and location. Currency is not clearly specified on the page, so do not assume CAD. Treat these as that vendor's current advertised ranges, not a US benchmark. It advertises anti-graffiti coating as a separate square-foot service. A Seattle operator has transparent pricing but directs users to quote, while an Ohio pressure-washing contractor advertises anti-graffiti coating at $1.50–$4/sq ft and recurring patrol as custom pricing. These examples support area/method/site/contract inputs, not a universal tariff.

**Software pricing/WTP:** No operator statement or verified subscription purchase for graffiti-removal software was found. Software WTP is unconfirmed; quoted service revenue must not be used as a proxy. Public-works contract scale is not evidence of a small operator's software budget.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Urgent response can be valuable because prompt removal may discourage additional tagging; municipal case evidence reports sub-24-hour response for priority/offensive graffiti under a specific borough contract. Multi-site contracts need location priority, access window, response clock, assignment and escalation; incidents can be clustered into route days. Each site may have opt-out/owner refusal or inaccessible surfaces. Weather, surface drying, dwell time, public traffic and water access may postpone or extend work. Do not assume recurring service for a one-off customer; patrol frequency is a site-contract decision.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Verify authorization and location; capture before photos and surface condition; isolate pedestrians/property and set traffic/access controls; identify substrate and chemical/product instructions; test a small discreet patch when appropriate; select gentlest effective method; apply/allow dwell/rinse or cover; capture after photos and remaining ghosting/damage; review result, neutralize/rinse/collect waste as required by product and local rules; note non-removal, customer decision, next treatment and acceptance. NPS warns of permanent staining/substrate damage, solvents/VOCs, alkaline burns, steam burns and pressure damage; it advises test patches and trained crews for historic masonry. Follow SDS, PPE, ventilation, fall protection and equipment controls for the actual method. No universal discharge authorization can be inferred; confirm local stormwater/sewer and chemical-disposal requirements. Noncompletion includes denied access, unsafe height/traffic, unknown sensitive substrate, test patch failure, severe staining, weather or owner refusal. Rework includes ghosting, incomplete removal, surface discoloration/erosion, overspray and rapid re-tagging.

## Customer communication and self-service

Offer a report/quote form with photo and precise location, acknowledgement and response estimate, appointment/access notes, delay notice, method/limitations and any repaint/coating decision. Send before/after evidence and invoice/closeout record; notify an account contact for each site incident. Keep human review for historic/fragile surfaces, uncertain material, hazardous access or a method likely to alter finish. Explain that complete color restoration may require painting and that “removed” does not mean no ghosting.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Depending on method: surface-compatible removers and neutralizers, brushes/scrapers, low-pressure/hot-water washer, controlled abrasive/steam equipment, containment/collection, coating, masking, ladders/lifts, water supply, PPE and traffic-control gear. Store SDS and method instructions with products. Skills include substrate recognition, test-patch interpretation, safe chemical handling, pressure/abrasive control and historic-surface escalation. Larger contracts may require crew assignment, fleet, water/containment equipment and replenishment; small operators may use only a few consumables. Do not treat a product list as a standard inventory for all surfaces.

## Reporting and operating measures

Observed public-contract measures include response time, incidents and locations; proposed operator measures include quote-to-work conversion, response SLA attainment, completion by site/material/method, repeat incident rate, re-tag interval, coating coverage, callback/damage rate, chemical/material use, travel, margin and owner acceptance. These are useful inferred measures; no common cross-industry benchmark was found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

The sources demonstrate photo forms, site logs, before/after images, response SLAs and contract reporting; they do not establish a dominant graffiti-specific CRM. Likely adjacent tools include municipal work-order systems or a pressure-washing/field-service CRM, maps, phone/text, photos, estimating, invoicing and accounting, but the small-operator stack is **unconfirmed**. No operator software purchase or WTP evidence found. Advertised service prices are not software prices. A public tender specification requires before/after photos, emergency phone availability, a site list and refusal reporting; a council contractor describes a borough-wide location/surface survey and SLA outcomes. These are institutional requirements, not proof that every contractor needs the same workflow.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** NPS preservation guidance prioritizes safe, least-aggressive treatment and test patches; commercial vendors offer photo-based quotes and area/method pricing; municipal specifications require location records, response and photo proof. **Inference:** multi-site SLAs, route dispatch and per-site incident history are more important for public/portfolio work than for one-off storefront work. **Uncertain:** the prevalence of specialist standalone firms, common CRM adoption, typical US price distributions and actual software WTP are not established. Confidence is high for surface/safety workflow, medium for business software fit, low for market size/WTP.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a location-linked incident with image intake, material/finish/access fields, test-patch/method checklist, SLA, before/after proof and closeout. Ask whether the operator does private one-offs, public contracts or portfolio patrols; keep method, coating, rates, access and local disposal policy tenant-specific. Escalate historic/fragile surfaces and uncertain substrate for human review. Validate operator software use and the required customer-facing proof with independent small businesses before choosing defaults.
