## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Residential moving sells labor and transportation to move customer possessions between origins and destinations, often with optional packing, storage, and handling. A move record links origin/destination, inventory, estimate, service order, bill of lading, delivery, and any loss/damage claim. FMCSA consumer guidance describes written estimates, shipment inventories, bills of lading, and valuation choices for interstate household goods moves. Movegistics markets moving-specific CRM, sales, survey, digital inventory, dispatch, and crew/warehouse workflows, including hourly-rate tables for local moves.

**Inference:** The two Sweaty Startup labels likely refer to the same broad household-moving candidate because their article link is identical, but “hauling” may include labor-only or small-load work outside regulated interstate shipments. A reusable pack should be moving-first with configurable local labor-only, residential, commercial, storage, and long-distance workflows. Ask about shipment type and jurisdiction before showing regulated documents; federal interstate rules are not universal.

## Business model and customer segments

Households, renters, seniors/down-sizers, real-estate agents, property managers, offices, and institutions can buy local/interstate moves, packing/unpacking, loading/unloading, furniture rearranging, storage/warehouse handling, or labor-only help. Triggers include home/office move, closing date, lease turnover, renovation, or urgent delivery. Work is often one-time, though realtors, property managers, and commercial accounts can produce repeat referrals. Booking is date- and capacity-sensitive; month-end, summer, school calendars, and closings may create peaks, but this research did not quantify seasonality.

Solo labor-only movers, local truck-and-crew movers, and interstate carriers/brokers have different capacity and paperwork needs. **Uncertain:** The source article does not establish which model its label means or that every “hauling” service is a regulated carrier.

## Terminology and durable records

Use customer/shipper, origin and destination/service locations, shipment/move, inventory, estimate, order for service, bill of lading, crew, truck, delivery, and claim; allow tenant wording. Capture addresses, stairs/elevator, parking/loading restrictions, long carry, access windows, building contacts, COI where required, and origin/destination access. Persistent records are customer and shipment history; item inventory is shipment-specific, not a long-lived customer asset. Item identifiers, condition, high-value items, packing status, and delivery status can support handoff/claims. Protect contact/payment details and insurance documents. No universal customer asset type is evidenced.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Menu candidates: local hourly move, long-distance move, packing/unpacking, loading/unloading, labor-only, specialty/heavy item, storage/warehouse, and commercial move. Intake needs both addresses, date/window, home/office size or item list, access, stairs/elevator, packing needs, special/high-value items, storage, and billing contact. Photos/video or a virtual/in-person survey can improve volume/labor estimates; survey rules depend on jurisdiction and service. FMCSA-covered interstate household goods moves have specific estimate and document requirements. Local work may use minimum hours plus crew/truck rates; long-distance jobs may use weight/volume, distance, accessorials, and shipment terms. Scope changes need documented estimate updates and required customer approval. Use request-first booking when survey or dispatch review is needed.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed:** Movegistics advertises local pricing with weekday/weekend/month-end hourly rate tables and minimum hours. FMCSA materials document binding/nonbinding estimate distinctions and charge disclosures for covered interstate jobs. Price inputs vary by geography, distance, shipment size/weight, labor, packing materials, access, specialty items, storage, timing, and service. The sources do not support a typical customer rate. Movegistics' pricing page is tiered by company size/features but does not expose a stable universal price in captured content; no software willingness-to-pay is established. Service prices do not imply software budget.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Moves are chiefly one-time, fixed-date projects rather than recurring route visits. Dispatch needs date-specific crew/truck/equipment capacity, estimated duration, travel legs, loading/delivery windows, access constraints, crew skills, and multi-day moves. Support delays, weather, elevator reservations, partial delivery/storage, changed inventory, and cancellations. Routing helps fleets but must respect shipment legs and windows more than stop density. Repeat commercial/property account work is optional, not a recurrence default.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm booking, dates, addresses, access, crew/truck, and contacts → survey/confirm inventory and scope → issue/revise estimate and required service documents → protect/pack, tag/load, record condition/inventory → transport → unload/deliver and resolve exceptions → obtain completion/signature, invoice/collect, retain documents, and open any claim. Exact documents depend on jurisdiction and service type. Capture inventory IDs, condition/damage notes, photos, signatures, exceptions, and delivery confirmation. Noncompletion reasons include access/parking/building denial, customer not ready, weather, truck/crew failure, safety concern, changed scope, or cancellation. No cross-jurisdiction license/permit matrix has been researched.

## Customer communication and self-service

Useful messages cover survey/estimate confirmation, date and arrival window, customer prep, crew/truck status, delay, scope change requiring approval, delivery, invoice, and claim support. Self-service can request an estimate, submit photos/inventory, approve, manage documents/payment, and view status. It should not silently change shipment contents, legal estimate status, carrier, delivery promise, or claims. Consent/preferences are tenant and jurisdiction dependent.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Fleet/truck capacity, dollies, pads, straps, ramps, cartons, packing materials, warehouse space, and specialty equipment matter; customer shipment inventory differs from consumable stock. Roles may include estimator, driver, lead, movers, packers, warehouse staff, and subcontractors. Track truck/crew, actual labor, time, mileage, supplies, and subcontractor cost as needed. Payroll, warehouse stock, GPS, and equipment maintenance depend on scale.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures include lead response and estimate conversion, booked capacity by date, crew/truck utilization, estimate-versus-actual hours/weight/volume, revenue/margin by move type, delays/noncompletion, damage/claim frequency, delivery timeliness, and referral source. These are suggestions; reviewed vendor pages do not establish standard KPI definitions.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Movegistics positions itself as a moving vertical suite with CRM/sales, surveys, digital inventory, dispatch, crew app, warehouse, pricing, and reports. Its public pricing page advertises tiers by company needs and local hourly rate tables, but no stable fixed subscription price was captured. General field-service tools can handle leads, schedules, quotes, payments, and basic records but need tenant forms for shipment-specific inventory/documents. Search found regulator and vendor evidence but little first-person mover software feedback; no broad complaint pattern or purchase-price evidence is claimed. FMCSA complaint resources show paperwork and estimates matter to consumer protection, not prevalence of CRM pain.

An owner/operator software discussion asks for a system to preserve repeat-customer move history, item inventory, access/driveway notes and contacts; replies name SmartMoving and Moverbase, while the poster questions whether a small crew needs paid software and wants consistent quoting. This is useful first-person workflow and affordability evidence but is one small Reddit thread, not a representative stack survey.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

FMCSA guidance applies to interstate household-goods moves for individual shippers, not every local or labor-only job. Vendors show that survey, inventory, dispatch, and warehouse features exist but do not establish adoption or pain severity. Confidence is high for covered interstate documents, medium for local price patterns/service mix, and low for software complaints and intended candidate model. Intrastate rules and source-article context remain open.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a move-first workflow with origin/destination, date, access, survey, quote approval, crew/truck dispatch, shipment-specific item/condition capture, delivery proof, and optional packing/storage. Ask about jurisdiction, interstate status, carrier/broker/labor-only role, estimate basis, survey, fleet, and storage. Keep documents, rates, item granularity and claims tenant-configurable; validate whether shared forms/files support shipment inventory before proposing a platform gap.
