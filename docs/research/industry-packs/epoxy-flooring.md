## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Epoxy floor coating is a measured, usually project-based concrete preparation and coating service. Contractor quote pages assess slab area/condition, cracks, moisture and prior coating, then select a system such as solid epoxy, flake broadcast or epoxy base with polyaspartic topcoat. Grinding and surface preparation affect adhesion, price and schedule; cure times differ by system. OSHA's construction silica standard applies to relevant concrete grinding exposures.

This Home services candidate remains separate from countertop epoxy design and from the Trades/construction “Epoxy flooring / counter tops” candidate. **Inference:** The strongest pack defaults are estimate/site inspection, slab history, system selection, crew scheduling, prep/coating checklist, customer cure instructions and callback tracking. Pricing evidence is contractor-advertised regional pricing rather than a representative national survey. Software WTP for an epoxy-flooring-specific CRM remains unknown.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential homeowners request garage, basement and utility-floor coatings; light-commercial owners may request small workshop, showroom or service-space coating. Buyers can be owners, remodelers, property managers or contractors. Operators may specialize in floor coatings or add the work to concrete/painting businesses. Jobs are quoted per project and may be one to several site days, depending on prep, layers and system. Some contractors sell fast-cure polyaspartic or hybrid systems alongside epoxy. This profile does not cover slab construction, polished concrete, decorative countertop finishing or the distinct combined construction-category candidate.

## Terminology and durable records

Durable asset/site: property, floor zone, concrete age, area/dimensions, slab use, prior coating/system, previous failure, moisture history, drains/edges/steps, access and owner. Job data: cracks/spalls, contamination/oil, moisture test readings/method, surface profile/prep, ambient/slab conditions, selected manufacturer system/product/lot, color/flake blend/broadcast, coats, cure/re-entry, photos, warranty and care. Capture exact product technical datasheet and SDS revision. Existing slab and installed coating history persist; temperature, RH, moisture condition and contamination vary by work date. A photo is not a substitute for product- and system-specific slab evaluation.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services may include site assessment/moisture testing, mechanical grinding and crack repair, primer/moisture mitigation, solid-color epoxy, decorative flake or metallic system, urethane/polyaspartic topcoat, stairs/vertical edges and repair/recoat. Intake asks for room/garage dimensions, floor photos, concrete age, oil/sealer/prior coating, cracks, moisture concerns, drain and edge details, use/traffic/chemicals, desired color/flake, access/vehicle removal and desired start date. Site inspection often precedes firm quote because prep condition matters; a quote form asks about known moisture/efflorescence, flake blend and system. Common price inputs: area and minimum, prep severity, repairs, moisture system, coating chemistry/number of layers, full flake/quartz, edge/stairs, color, travel, curing schedule and warranty. Self-service can request estimate; an instant booking should not claim fixed pricing before slab assessment.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service pricing:** An Atlanta contractor advertises $5–$10/sq ft for professional garage coatings and states that 400-sq-ft two-car garages can span $1,400–$4,800 depending largely on prep and system. A Gainesville contractor gives system starting prices per sq ft with a $2,500 minimum for a basement moisture system. These are local vendor list prices accessed 2026-09-24, not a national average. Independent contractor pricing pages also show separate prep and system inputs. Quote the exact system and scope; do not compare an epoxy-only kit with mechanically prepared professional installation.

**Software pricing/WTP:** No epoxy-flooring operator plan purchase or direct software WTP statement was identified. Contractors may use field-service, construction/project, estimate/payment tools, but prevalence is unverified. Service contract dollars and floor area are not software WTP.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most residential work is a one-time installation with prep/application and cure/re-entry interval; a second coat or moisture system may require staged work, and weather/temperature/humidity can constrain specified products. Schedule after vehicles/items are removed, slab is ready, access available and other trades complete. Customers need realistic walk-on/vehicle return time based on system, not generic duration. Route planning is optional for crews doing multiple estimates/installations per day; jobs requiring multi-day cure stages need team/equipment and site capacity. Repair/recoat is condition-driven, not assumed membership recurrence.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm dimensions/scope and selected system; inspect slab and disclose exclusions; test/document moisture and surface issues per the coating system; secure site, remove debris/oil/sealer and mechanically profile using appropriate grinder/dust controls; repair cracks/edges and prepare substrate; apply primer/moisture mitigation/base; broadcast flake/quartz or decorative material; scrape/vacuum; apply compatible topcoat(s); record products/lots, mix/ambient conditions and coats; inspect coverage, edges, cure/contamination and finish; capture photos; hand over cure, cleaning and warranty instructions. OSHA construction silica rule includes exposure controls for concrete grinding; select controls for actual equipment/task and regulatory applicability. Uncured epoxy can sensitize skin; use product SDS, PPE, ventilation and exposure controls. Other coatings may involve isocyanates or different hazards. Follow label temperature/moisture limits and local VOC/air/waste requirements where applicable. Noncompletion includes high moisture/active water, unsound concrete, unapproved crack/level correction, contamination, unsafe dust control, inaccessible site or unsuitable conditions. Rework includes delamination, bubbles, pinholes, fisheyes, uneven flake/coverage, cracks telegraphing, soft cure or damage during early use.

## Customer communication and self-service

Provide photo/site assessment, quote with named product/system, preparation scope, repairs/exclusions, color/flake approval, deposit/payment, access and vehicle-removal date. Send arrival, delay and day-by-day work updates; explain ventilation, cure/re-entry and care instructions per system. Provide before/after photos, system and warranty documents. Obtain explicit approval for moisture mitigation, crack scope, repair add-ons and changes. Human review is needed when moisture/structural issues exceed coating scope or the proposed system differs from the quote.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Diamond grinders/walk-behind grinders, dust shrouds/collectors, HEPA vacuums, hand grinders, crack repair tools, moisture testing equipment, mixing drills/paddles, squeegees/rollers, spike shoes, broadcast flakes/quartz, resin/hardener/primer/topcoat, masking, ventilation and PPE. Tool choice and respirator/dust controls depend on task and system. Track compatible product components, lot/expiry and color blends; exact quantities follow measured floor and system coverage. Teams may use a lead installer and helper; bigger commercial jobs may require more crew and phased/overnight work. Staffing/certification practices were not consistently evidenced.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures: estimate-to-job conversion, area/system mix, prep hours and material/sq ft, moisture test exceptions, jobs by substrate/condition, on-time starts, cure/re-entry adherence, callbacks by defect/system, warranty cost, margin, crew utilization and customer acceptance. These are inferred; no public common benchmark was found.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Regional floor-coating contractors tie pricing to concrete prep/condition and system and publish per-square-foot prices/minima. OSHA specifies silica exposure controls for construction concrete grinding; OSHA epoxy materials discuss sensitization. Contractors may label hybrid polyurethane/polyaspartic systems as garage-floor coatings, so the service name does not guarantee pure epoxy. **Inference:** a pack should record actual system chemistry and installation scope instead of hardcoding “epoxy.” **Uncertain:** market share of residential specialists versus concrete/painting contractors, common software and WTP. Exact chemical/worker rules depend on system and task. Confidence high for job-flow/safety prompts, medium for software fit, low for WTP.
