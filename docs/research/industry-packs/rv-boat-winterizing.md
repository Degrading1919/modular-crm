## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Winterization is a seasonal, asset-specific service sold before freezing exposure, often bundled with inspection, oil/filter service, storage, haul-out, or spring de-winterizing. RV winterization centers on model-specific freshwater, water-heater, pump, fixture, tank, appliance, and sometimes hydronic systems. Boat winterization centers on engine type and onboard systems such as freshwater, ballast, generator, and water heater. Both can be mobile or shop/marina work; the boat menu explicitly varies by propulsion type and installed systems.

**Inference:** The strongest product fit is a seasonal intake-to-checklist workflow keyed to the persistent RV or vessel plus system configuration, with explicit exception/technician sign-off and a separate spring reactivation visit. A combined “winterize” service template without asset-type and OEM/system branching would be unsafe. **Uncertain:** the candidate label combines two markets, but evidence does not show one operator commonly specializing in both. Product defaults should ask whether the business services RVs, boats, or both before configuring services.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers are private RV/boat owners, marinas, dealerships, campgrounds/storage facilities, rental/fleet operators, and sometimes property managers. Demand is weather- and storage-driven: northern operators book fall capacity against a freeze deadline; warmer areas may have little or no demand. Spring de-winterization is an adjacent but separate return job. Operators range from a one-person mobile RV tech or marine mechanic to a dealer/marina with haul-out/storage capacity and seasonal technicians.

## Terminology and durable records

RV records need owner, make/model/year/VIN, floorplan or coach type, water heater and plumbing configuration, pump/bypass/low-point locations, installed washer/ice-maker/hydronic appliances, storage location/access, and freeze exposure. The serial/label and the exact owner manual matter. Boat records need vessel/HIN, propulsion (outboard, inboard, sterndrive, jet), engine make/model/serial/hours, generator, ballast, freshwater and water-heater systems, battery, marina/slip/yard/trailer location, haul-out access, and any owner-selected storage service. These are persistent assets with work history, unlike job-only weather/date/material inputs.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Common RV jobs: drain/blow-out or approved RV antifreeze method; bypass/drain water heater; protect fixtures, traps, tanks and auxiliary systems; optional roof/seal inspection; storage prep; spring flush, sanitize, filter replacement and recommissioning. Winnebago/Forest River procedures differ by configuration; water heater bypass and component-specific instructions must be confirmed, never inferred from generic model assumptions. Boat jobs: engine winterization by propulsion/system, fuel stabilization, oil/filter/gear lube service, cooling-system protection, battery prep, freshwater/ballast/generator/washdown systems, and optional storage/haul-out. A vendor menu separates basic and full boat packages and charges extra for system options.

Intake captures asset identity, OEM/manual, installed systems and modifications, prior service, customer’s storage plan/location, available utilities/access, freeze deadline, whether customer needs winterization only or bundled storage/service, and known defects. Quote may be package/asset class plus add-ons, engine/system count, LOA, access/haul-out/travel, parts and inspection findings. Dealer or mobile technician should review unusual water heaters, hydronic units, liveaboard needs or damaged systems before booking. Do not sell shrink-wrap as synonymous with winterization.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service prices (US):** Lynden RV’s 2025–26 menu publishes model/package-specific services and a roof inspection included with a package ([menu](https://lyndenrvcenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/WINTERIZation2526.pdf), accessed 2026-09-24); Vogt RV publishes a broader service menu where hydronic heating/all-appliance winterizing changes scope ([menu](https://www.vogtrv.com/complete-service-menu), accessed 2026-09-24). Boat examples range substantially by package: Tobler Marina (Idaho) lists basic freeze protection at $281.25 outboard / $607.50 inboard plus parts, with premium winterize/oil/filter/annual service at $843.75 outboard / $956.25 inboard / $900 sterndrive plus parts ([menu](https://toblermarina.com/winterization), accessed 2026-09-24). Highland Lake Marina’s 2026 menu lists outboard $450, inboard/I-O $550, add-ons for heaters, showers and ballast, and separate haul-out/travel/storage lines ([menu](https://www.marinahighlandlake.com/winter-service), accessed 2026-09-24). These are regional operator list prices, not a national price range.

Price inputs: asset class/length, engine type/count, RV type and installed systems, method/materials, additional appliances, condition, travel, haul-out, storage, parts/oil/filters/antifreeze, inspection and tax. Seasonal deposits or signed work/storage terms may apply. Software pricing/WTP: RVDesk markets shop management to independent RV repair shops, but its public pricing page did not expose an amount in the rendered page at access; ServiceNomad markets an RV service platform and plan page, but pricing is not used here as operator WTP. Marine repair platforms such as DockMaster use quote/demo sales. No direct operator statement establishes software WTP for seasonal winterization businesses. Do not infer software budget from winterization ticket value.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Fall is a compressed seasonal surge before forecast freeze and storage closure; spring de-winterization clusters before travel/launch. A yearly reminder is useful, but timing must be customer- and region-configurable and weather-triggered rather than a universal fixed date. Capacity depends on trained technicians, model/engine competency, consumables, shop bays, mobile territory, marina access, haul-out/storage availability, and number of installed systems. A mobile route can cluster RV storage lots or marina locations. Record deadline and acceptable window, site contact/access, and whether customer may move/start/use the system before spring sign-off. Late cancellation/no-show may create a re-route charge; repeat visits can be triggered by missing access, systems not disclosed, leaks, or equipment-specific procedure.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Verify asset/owner authorization, exact system and OEM procedure; inspect for pre-existing leaks/damage; confirm storage mode and freeze risk; complete only the selected asset-specific checklist; document readings, bypass/drain state, parts/fluids, photos, exceptions and technician; attach customer acknowledgement and invoice; leave a clear no-use/de-winterize-before-use notice where relevant. Forest River and Winnebago warn against automotive antifreeze in potable systems and direct users to manufacturer-specific procedures and sanitization before use. Boat procedures also depend on propulsion/engine and OEM directions; Mercury directs owners to serial-specific manuals. Tech must stop and escalate if model/system is unknown, unsafe, damaged, inaccessible, or a required system cannot be reached. A failed freeze protection or incomplete bypass can cause costly damage; callbacks need checklist, service record and warranty attribution.

Waste management must follow local/state requirements and product label; EPA explains waste-antifreeze rules can differ by state and is not blanket permission to drain or discharge. Marina/yard rules and wastewater controls can also apply. Distinguish RV plumbing antifreeze from automotive coolant and boat engine materials. No claim of a single national technician license is supported.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs booking confirmation, prep instructions (remove belongings/open access, provide keys/shore power/water as applicable), asset-specific quote and add-on approval, weather/arrival updates, completion proof, exceptions, storage/de-winterize timing, and spring flush/sanitization instructions. Portal history should store procedures, model/manual reference, selected method, checklists, photos, invoice and spring appointment. Automate reminders and status, but do not auto-assert that an asset is freeze-safe or ready for use without technician sign-off.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

RV: air compressor/regulator (if OEM method permits), blow-out adapter, approved RV antifreeze, pump siphon, hoses, hand tools, drain pan, PPE, water-heater tools, filters and appliance-specific parts. Boat: engine/OEM tools, flush connections, antifreeze, stabilizer, oils/filters/gear lube, battery equipment, spill containment and potentially haul-out equipment. Technicians need RV plumbing/appliance or marine engine/system skill matched to the job; marina/dealer may add service writer, yard/haul-out crew and storage coordinator. Inventory depends on package and seasonal stock; record lot/quantity and installed materials where useful. Cross-training across RV and marine work should not be assumed.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures: scheduled vs completed by asset/system, seasonal backlog against freeze/launch date, first-pass checklist completion, add-on/inspection findings and approval, material use/cost, travel/haul-out time, tech capacity by competency, reschedule/noncompletion cause, callback/leak/freeze-damage claim, spring de-winterization conversion and margin by package. These are inferred from operator menus/workflow; sources do not establish universal industry benchmarks.

## Existing software and operator evidence

RV dealers and independent mobile technicians may use RV-focused shop/work-order systems, service calendars, OEM manuals, phone/text, payment/accounting tools, and paper checklists. Marine yards may use DockMaster for service work orders and vessel history, plus engine-vendor systems and marina storage software. RVDesk and ServiceNomad describe RV-specific workflows; their marketing is evidence of product positioning, not market penetration or user satisfaction. Consumer/operator forum posts show some owners see basic winterizing as DIY, while service menus show packages, inspection and expensive system options; these examples signal heterogeneous outsourcing intent. They do not establish a representative software stack. No verified direct WTP or broad complaint study was found for winterizing operators; current vertical vendor list price is missing/quote based.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Manufacturer procedures vary by vehicle/engine configuration; menus price system complexity and optional work separately. Some RV owners report self-service; other owners buy dealership service, storage and inspection bundles. **Inference:** onboarding must choose RV vs boat and supported equipment families; seasonal automation should be a reminder, not an automatic technical guarantee. **Uncertain:** candidate list intentionally groups distinct submarkets; no evidence shows shared operators or software WTP. Antifreeze disposal restrictions vary by state/locality. Confidence high for asset-specific workflow, medium for digital capability fit, low for cross-sell prevalence.
