## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** “Custom lighting” can mean fixture replacement/installation, architectural or landscape lighting design, lighting-control retrofits, or whole-home wired/wireless lighting controls. These jobs differ in whether they alter branch-circuit wiring, need an electrician/permit, use a designer, require device compatibility/programming, or involve multi-room commissioning. Lutron separates Preferred Pros installing wall-box controls from Dealers/custom integrators selling advanced whole-home systems; its startup service can audit installation, configure the approved sequence, function-test, and train end users.

**Inference:** Intake must establish the product and scope before presenting an instant booking: fixture only, new wiring, low-voltage/landscape, control system, or design/commissioning. Project records should connect room/circuit/device schedule, approved design, permit/inspection when applicable, compatibility, installation, configuration and owner handoff. Estimates, scheduling, job tracking, files, billing and communications appear supportable with current shared capabilities; no core gap is established.

**Uncertain:** The candidate gives no indication whether it is an electrical contractor, lighting designer, smart-control dealer, or installation-only operator. We do not generalize from one manufacturer's training program or code reference into universal license rules.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential clients, remodelers, builders, interior designers, property managers and small commercial buyers may request decorative fixture installation, recessed/architectural lighting, under-cabinet/landscape lighting, controls, or whole-home systems. Revenue can be a one-room retrofit, bid project, new-construction rough-in and finish, or recurring service/upgrade relationship. Design, material procurement and installation may be bundled or separate; smart controls can require separate electrician and integrator roles.

Purchase triggers include remodel/new build, inadequate lighting, efficiency/ambience goals, fixture replacement, lighting scenes, accessibility, or failed controls. A new circuit/panel/wiring job is materially different from swapping a compatible control device. Public intake should collect photos, address, room count, existing controls/fixtures, goals and project stage, then route electrical scope and complex control design to a qualified person. Avoid self-booking where site assessment, load/compatibility or permitting is unresolved.

## Terminology and durable records

- **Site:** property, room, ceiling/wall, switch location, circuit/panel, access, ceiling height, finish protection, outage window, Wi-Fi/network availability, and other trade dependencies.
- **Assets:** installed fixture, control, hub/bridge, panel, scene/zone, model/serial, load/device compatibility, location, commissioning status, warranty, and documentation; retain only if future service matters.
- **Job inputs:** fixture/control schedule, photos, drawings, voltage/load and existing wiring notes, fixture heights/locations, dimming/control goals, product approval, parts lead time, electrician/design roles, permits/inspection and client signoff.
- Electrical panel, wiring, alarm/security integration and network credentials are sensitive/technician-only; credentials should not be stored as unprotected notes. Record safety/isolation and manufacturer setup steps.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services may include custom lighting design, fixture procurement/installation, replacement, new wiring/circuit work, recessed/landscape/under-cabinet lighting, dimmer/sensor installation, whole-home controls, programming/scenes, commissioning, repair and upgrades. Distinguish system design and material supply from labor-only install, and note exclusions such as drywall/paint repair, structural work, panel upgrades, and network remediation.

Minimum intake: address, project type/stage, room/fixture count, photos, height/access, existing wiring and controls, desired light/scene, products selected, other contractors, timeline and whether inspection is required. A site walk may be necessary for unknown wiring, custom design, inaccessible ceiling, loads, or integrated control. Quote could be per fixture/device for simple replacement, measured/hourly for service, or fixed proposal with equipment, design, rough-in, trim-out, programming, commissioning and change-order lines. Customer must approve substitutions and scope changes.

Schedule by dependencies: design/approval → permit/material order → rough-in → inspection/close wall → finish installation → programming/commissioning → training. A one-visit switch replacement is a different booking path. Avoid promising exact completion before long-lead components, access and inspection windows are known.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most work is project or repair scheduling; planned phases and other-trade dependencies constrain slots. Existing customers may request service, expansion, control changes, or fixture replacement. Match qualified electrician/installer/programmer and equipment to the job. Route planning matters only for multiple crews or dense service work; a complex one-off project depends more on milestones and parts readiness. Track postponements due to material, inspection, incomplete construction, electrical issue, access or unavailable network.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Confirm work order, approved fixture/control schedule, room, circuit, access and permit status.
2. Inspect existing installation, load/compatibility, wall/ceiling condition, and other-trade readiness; document deviations.
3. Follow applicable electrical safety procedures and manufacturer instructions; isolate circuits where work requires it and use a locally qualified/licensed electrician when required.
4. Install devices/fixtures, document model/serial/room and changes, restore power, test expected function.
5. For control projects, configure the approved scenes/zones, test controls and functional behavior, record setup and provide owner training.
6. Complete inspection/permit paperwork where required, clean/protect surfaces, capture proof and customer acceptance.

CPSC describes NEC/NFPA 70 as a widely adopted model code aimed at electrical hazards; the applicable adopted edition, permits and licensing are determined locally. Lutron's service process is evidence for its systems only. Rework can result from incompatible equipment, undocumented wiring, design changes, failed control/network setup, missed inspections, or damaged finish. Do not record Wi-Fi or alarm passwords in a general job note.
