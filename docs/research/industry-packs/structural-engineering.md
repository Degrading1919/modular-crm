## Executive summary

Structural engineering consultants design or evaluate load-bearing systems and issue calculations, drawings, specifications, reports, and field observations for architects, owners, contractors, developers, and other engineers. Work ranges from residential additions, openings and repairs to commercial buildings, industrial structures, and large projects; existing-structure evaluation and new-build design have different unknowns and exposure. Deliverables depend on project phase and code criteria and must be checked, issued, and where required signed/sealed by appropriately licensed professionals.

**Observed evidence:** NCEES materials and state-board frameworks describe licensure as jurisdictional; structural engineer registration is not uniform across states. Structural Engineering Institute (SEI)/ASCE standards cover buildings and other structures, including existing-building evaluation. Operator accounts mention clients/referrals, late bills, software/insurance costs, and the risk carried by small and solo practices. **Inference:** A project/fee/revision/approval system can support the business loop, but cannot perform structural analysis or validate a seal. Evidence does not establish a structural-specific shared-platform core gap.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include architects, residential owners and contractors, developers, design-build firms, general contractors, building owners/managers, insurers/adjusters, attorneys, and public/institutional owners. Common work includes new-building structural design, delegated design review, renovations, additions, retaining walls, foundations, temporary or specialty structures, condition assessments, damage/forensic investigations, repair design, and construction RFIs/site observations. A residential consultant may receive homeowner/contractor calls and site photos; a commercial practice may work as an architect's subconsultant on a larger design team.

Purchasing triggers include permit requirements, remodel/opening, distress or damage, change of use, contractor means/method questions, lender/insurer requirements, and new construction. Pricing varies by scoped deliverables, urgency, size/complexity, site visit, liability, and phase; hourly, fixed fee, and time-and-materials are plausible. Referral networks matter. Solo/principal-led firms perform sales, calculations, drafting, client intake, billing, and QA; larger firms allocate discipline leads, BIM/CAD production, QA, and project administration.

## Terminology and durable records

Use client/owner, project, building/structure, site, framing/system, existing condition, load path, design criteria, calculation package, sheet/set, issue/revision, RFI, submittal, site observation, deficiency, repair, and closeout. Records may include building address, occupancy/use, construction era, number of stories, structural system/material, known modifications, observed damage, available drawings, code/criteria edition, geotechnical inputs, architectural background revision, design assumptions, excluded elements, and site-access conditions. These are project-specific facts; distinguish measured/observed from client-reported.

Keep signed/sealed calculation and drawing versions, check/approval identity, issue date/purpose, transmittal, assumptions, design criteria, incoming changes, RFI/submittal responses, photos, limitations, and correction history. A structure may be a durable subject asset for repeat inspections, but most projects remain separate commissions. Sensitive building/security or forensic/legal records should not default to broad customer visibility.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake should capture caller role/authority, project address and jurisdiction, building type/use, scope and trigger, existing drawings/photos, observed symptoms and urgency, desired permit/construction dates, architect/contractor contacts, requested calculations/drawings/site visit, known code/design criteria, and whether another engineer is involved. Distress/safety concerns warrant rapid human triage; software should not diagnose structural safety from images.

Quotes should define systems/elements covered, deliverables and issue purpose, assumptions, exclusions, number of revisions, site visit/field limits, coordination, response times, and construction-phase services. Owners may seek fixed fee for bounded residential work; unknown conditions, forensic work, or recurring RFI review may need hourly/NTE terms. Permit review and construction dates are dependencies, not guaranteed appointment slots. Public self-service may collect documents, but scope and risk review belongs to the licensed professional.

## Pricing and commercial model

Fee drivers include building size and complexity, structural material/system, available background data, existing-condition uncertainty, field access, urgency, calculations/drawing level, architect/contractor coordination, number of review rounds, and liability/insurance. Milestone, phase, hourly, fixed, and NTE structures can occur. Added openings, changed architectural plans, undocumented field conditions, and extra contractor RFIs can cause additional work; record request, impact, owner authorization, and fee/schedule amendment before proceeding when possible.

Invoices may be deposit/retainer plus milestone or periodic progress, depending on firm/contract. Track forecast and actual time, unpaid invoices, revisions, unbilled work, fee remaining, and late client inputs. Operator reports describe delayed payment and high startup overhead from analysis/CAD software, insurance, and accounting; anecdotal fees in old forum posts are not a current price benchmark. No credible direct evidence in this sample establishes WTP for a new CRM. Software should be priced against verified small-practice alternatives and value, not project-design fees.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Schedule through project milestones: intake/data review, preliminary design, coordination, calculations, internal check, issue, permit/plan review, revised issue, construction RFIs/submittals, site observations, and closeout. Dependencies include architect backgrounds, loads/use, geotechnical report, owner decisions, contractor pricing, permit comments, fabrication/shop drawings, access, and site conditions. Field visits are episodic, location-bound, and credential/experience matched; routing is secondary. Urgent damage assessment may disrupt planned work. Record who is waiting on whom and whether return visit/revision is authorized.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

For assessment/observation: confirm scope and access, identify building/areas, document visible conditions with photos/measurements, note inaccessible/concealed conditions and limitations, report observations to authorized parties, track questions/deficiencies, then issue a signed report or follow-up. For design, office calculations and drawings pass independent check/approval and issue control before construction use. Structural consultant observation is limited to contracted scope and must not be represented as continuous inspection or guarantee.

Keep reviewer/sealer, jurisdiction, license validity where needed, relevant standard/code edition, issue status, and sealed-file history. NCEES Model Law is not itself local law; PE/SE categories vary. Safety concerns should be escalated through professional judgment and emergency/site authorities. Noncompletion may be missing background, inaccessible/concealed work, changed scope, permit delay, or unsafe conditions. Rework should distinguish consultant error, changed owner/architect design, contractor deviation, and new conditions; retain correspondence and authorization. Never allow silent replacement of a previously issued document.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need a clear list of required plans/photos, accepted scope, expected response timing, requested decisions, design status, issued files, permit comments, RFI/submittal receipt, field-observation limitations, fee balance/change authorization, and invoice status. Email plus controlled storage/PDF markup are common. Customer access should be limited to authorized project files and released versions. Automations can chase missing documents and approvals; do not auto-approve engineering changes or tell owners a structure is safe.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Tools include structural analysis/calculation software, CAD/BIM, PDF review, spreadsheets, code/standard references, field measuring/photo equipment, and office accounting/time/project tools. Specific packages vary by materials and niche: RISA products are one analysis option, not universal. A principal/PE or SE, junior engineers, modelers/drafters, and admin may share one shop; commercial firms have discipline/project leads and independent checkers. Capture credentials and jurisdiction, reviewer/sealer assignments, billable time, design/check roles, and subcontracted specialty scope. Physical construction materials are generally purchased by the builder; do not default an engineering firm to contractor inventory.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures include fee and hours by project phase, planned/actual calculation and drafting time, utilization/realization, backlog, proposal conversion, change authorization lag, revisions per issue, RFI turnaround, overdue owner inputs, unbilled work, receivables aging, late milestone rate, field revisit rate, and correction/rework hours. These are suggested management measures, not standardized across the profession. Separate new-condition/owner-driven revisions from firm-controlled correction.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Stack often combines specialized calculation/analysis tools, CAD/BIM, spreadsheets, PDF markups, email/cloud files, time/billing, and bookkeeping/project accounting. RISA offers RISA-3D with subscription licensing and product pages invite trials; public packaging indicates a specialized per-seat/tool cost, not an all-in business platform. Exact quote or license depends on modules and terms. Small-practice threads mention accounting, professional liability coverage, CAD/analysis license expense, and late-payment exposure; users report varying business models, from homeowner inspections to architect subconsulting. Such forum comments are first-person but self-selected and cannot support an industry WTP number.

There is no single vertical incumbent validated here as a complete CRM for structural engineering. Generic project-management tools can track tasks, but controlled calculations/drawings and engineering QA remain in discipline systems. Product alternatives and license terms should be revalidated at procurement time.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Small residential and forensic consultants have different risk, fee, client, and field patterns from commercial project subconsultants. PE is broadly regulated, but a separate SE license/title is state-dependent; verify local boards before configuration. Standards are discipline sources, not software requirements. Operator posts consistently make costs/liability salient but do not establish average prices or CRM budget. Confidence: high for jurisdictional licensure variation and professional issue control; medium for small-firm workflow/software stack; low for common CRM WTP and exact recurring metrics. Validate service mix and document QA expectations in interviews.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype project intake with building/system context, project phases, fee balance, revision/issue ledger, assigned reviewer/sealer, field visit and follow-up records. Keep optional building asset history separate from one-off projects. Onboarding should ask practice jurisdictions and residential/commercial mix, permitted scope, fee method, existing/new work, client mix, and analysis/CAD/accounting tools. Investigate audit trail, permissioning, and sealed-document storage before specifying any core enhancement.
