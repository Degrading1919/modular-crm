## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** The candidate label says “cubical,” while U.S. operators and manufacturers use “cubicle” or “modular office furniture.” This profile interprets it as modular workstation/panel assembly and reconfiguration, not as manufacturing office cubicles or general office construction. Installer businesses provide delivery, staging/warehousing, system-specific layout, assembly, moves/adds/changes, removal, repair, project management and sometimes AutoCAD/layout design. Projects serve employers, furniture dealers, facilities teams, schools, healthcare, government and office movers; they are scheduled around occupancy, loading docks, elevators and client move-in dates.

Compared with small carpentry calls, this is a commercial, multi-station project with countable component inventory, drawing/system identity, crew labor, delivery reconciliation, electrical/data handoff and punch/sign-off. Specialist software advertises project, crew, labor, document, punch and invoice management. Vendor list pricing exists, but no verified installer purchase or actual WTP study was found. Partial status reflects the candidate-label ambiguity and lack of first-person software adoption/complaint evidence.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers are corporate facilities/workplace teams, furniture dealers, GCs, relocation managers, schools, healthcare providers and public agencies. Jobs include new cubicle/workstation installation, disassembly/reinstall during relocation, internal reconfiguration, refresh, decommission, warehousing, damage repair and service. System brands/connectors vary; a certified installer may work for dealer or directly for end customer. Small operators run regional jobs with a project manager and install crew; larger firms maintain warehouse, trucks, multiple teams and dealer relationships. Work happens nights/weekends or phased by floor/department to reduce downtime.

## Terminology and durable records

Records include client/site, floor/zone, floor plan/layout revision, furniture manufacturer/system, workstation/run, panel/work surface/storage components, SKU/finish/serial, shipment/pallet/condition, installation standard, crew, delivery/access window, power/data handoff, punch, customer acceptance and warranty. Installed layout and inventory by site can persist; receiving condition, packaging, missing/short/damaged counts and daily labor are project records. Capture loading dock/elevator/union/building access rules, hours, security, IT/electrical dependencies and waste/removal. Floor plans and workstation locations can be sensitive; share by project role.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks new/reuse, workstation count, brand/system, floor and zones, plans/CAD, inventory list, delivery/staging, existing furniture removal, power/data/IT, building hours/dock/elevator, phased occupancy, requested date, union/site rules, warranty and client sign-off. Site walkthrough and drawing freeze are common. Quote may be per workstation, crew labor/hour, project, move, storage, receiving, disposal and after-hours; clarifies furniture supply, delivery, assembly, electrical final connection, data/IT, patch/repair, permits and waste. Receive/inspect before field install; reconcile parts by run; change layout through written drawing revision.

Typical sequence: walk/site survey → freeze layout/system and scope → estimate/PO → receive/stage/inspect → schedule crews/building access → install by floor/zone → coordinate qualified electrician/data/IT → inspect alignment/leveling and reconcile exceptions → punch/sign-off/invoice. Public intake can gather plans/counts, but final scope needs site and inventory verification.

## Pricing and commercial model

Cost drivers include workstation count/complexity, new vs reused system, configuration, floor count, access, staging/warehouse, delivery, after-hours work, crew size/hours, elevator/dock, power/data handoff, removal/disposal, repair, travel and multiple mobilizations. ## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Installation is project-based; M/A/C work and warranty/service calls can recur. Sequence depends on furniture delivery and staging, elevator/dock, site readiness, desk/power/data, trade access, quiet hours, security and move-in date. Multiple floors or locations need phased crews and product-to-zone allocation. Noncompletion includes wrong/incomplete shipment, site not ready, drawing mismatch, access window missed, electrical/data handoff, damage, unavailable elevator or late design change. Flag delay early because tenant occupancy may be fixed.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm system/drawing revision → pre-stage by floor/run → receiving count and condition → protect site and confirm access → assemble panels/frames/worksurfaces/storage to current manufacturer directions → level/align and install power/data components per product documentation → leave building mains connection to qualified electrician where required → verify count, function, stability, configuration and cleanup → punch exceptions, photos and customer sign-off. Manufacturer instructions and local electrical/building requirements govern system-specific work. Hazards include heavy panels, repetitive lifting, pinch/crush, ladders, powered tools, electrical connections and occupied work areas. Noncompletion: shortage/damage, wrong revision, incompatible reused parts, floor/space conflict, unsafe room or trade hold. Rework requires a link to run/part/zone and cause.

## Customer communication and self-service

Client needs plan approval, access/COI, delivery/receiving, phasing/downtime, progress by floor/zone, shortages/damage, trade handoff, punch closure, final count, warranty and invoice. Customer portal can approve layout, confirm delivery windows and sign off zones; floor plans/employee maps should be permission-limited. A field update must identify exceptions early without disclosing building security information to unauthorized recipients.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Products include panels, posts/frames, connectors, worksurfaces, pedestals, overhead storage, seating, cable/power components and accessories. Warehouse, trucks, carts, lifts, dollies and tools support receiving and install. Staging and component-to-run tracking are important for large jobs. Team includes estimator/designer, PM, warehouse/receiving staff, crew lead, installer, mover, electrician/IT subcontractor and field supervisor. Manufacturer certification and local electrician licensing vary by task/system; track assignment and credentials only when necessary.
