## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A real-property appraisal is a scoped valuation assignment, not a real estate sales transaction or an automated estimate. The appraiser receives an engagement with intended use/user and property, checks competency and assignment conditions, gathers property/market data, may inspect subject, analyzes approaches/comparables, prepares a signed report and retains assignment records under applicable standards. Lender assignments may come directly or through an appraisal management company (AMC), with client-specific delivery, review and revision steps. Commercial, private estate/tax/divorce, review and residential mortgage assignments differ in property data, report form, intended use and schedule.

**Inference:** Product fit is a secure assignment pipeline with client/order, scope, property, credential, appointment, data/report milestones, revision requests, fee/payout and archive. It should link to specialized appraisal authoring/data systems and keep valuation judgement outside CRM automation. **Uncertain:** “Appraiser” is broader than residential lender appraisal; public primary workflow sources available here are strongest for US one-to-four-unit GSE work. Commercial, business/personal-property, eminent-domain and other jurisdictional scopes need distinct investigation; status is partial for that reason.

## Business model and customer segments

Real property appraisers may work as sole independent fee appraisers, staff at a bank/valuation firm, commercial appraisal firm, or reviewer. Buyers/clients include lenders, AMCs, banks, attorneys, private property owners, courts, tax/estate/divorce clients, government acquisition entities and investors. Residential mortgage work is often order-based and delivered under lender/AMC scope, turn time and report requirements. Commercial appraisal may involve income/cost/sales approaches, leases/rent roll, operating statements, inspections and longer negotiated scopes; fees may be quoted by fixed assignment or time/scope. Private/estate work has different client purpose and reliance restrictions. The candidate's Real estate transactions placement suggests real property, but the source label alone does not define asset class or intended use. Do not conflate an appraiser with a Realtor, assessor, home inspector or automatic valuation model.

## Terminology and durable records

Track appraiser/firm, license or certification jurisdiction/class, client/lender/AMC, assignment/order, intended use and intended users, subject property, interest valued, value premise/type, effective date, scope of work, assignment conditions, engagement letter, inspection, market area, comparable/lease/rent/expense data, analysis, report, delivery, revision/reconsideration request, invoice/payout, complaint or review. Subject record may have address/parcel, property type/use, legal/site/building attributes, access/contact, tenancy, prior report/inspection and market area. Do not mix property facts for one assignment with another report's confidential data. Preserve source records, workfile, correspondence, report versions and signed certification as required by current USPAP, client and jurisdiction; the precise retention period depends on applicable standards/rules and should not be invented in CRM. Sensitive client financial/tenant records and nonpublic transaction docs need restricted access.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Assignment types include residential lender appraisal, desktop/hybrid/limited-scope appraisal where permitted, commercial real estate valuation, private valuation for estate/tax/divorce/decision support, review/consulting and update/completion work. Intake establishes client and engagement, property/asset class, intended use/user, interest/right appraised, value premise, effective date, assignment conditions, jurisdiction, required standards/report format, access, due date, fees, independence/conflict check and competency/geographic-market fit. Lender/AMC order forms may supply specific report/UAD and delivery requirements; private client needs a scoping conversation and contract. Quoting considers property type/size/complexity, geography/travel, data scarcity, inspection scope, urgency, intended use/report form, court/witness work, client terms and revision/update scope. Avoid automated value estimates masquerading as professional appraisal or accepting a scope without appraiser review.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service pricing:** Residential lender work may be bid/assigned by client or AMC and appraiser compensation can differ from the borrower-facing appraisal charge; public first-person threads report substantial variation, fees, tech-fee deductions and revision time. These are anecdotes and not rate guidance. Commercial valuation often requires an agreed fixed fee or time/cap after defining asset, use, date and scope; Canadian commercial operator guidance describes fixed fee, hourly with cap, or retainer, but should not be generalized to US residential lender work. Quote inputs are scope/complexity, access, data and travel, deadline, report type and additional revisions/updates—not simple property price alone.

**Software pricing/WTP:** True Footage's current TrueTracts page advertises Basic at $49/month and Pro at $99/month; Pro includes Spark functions and UAD 3.6 support. The a la mode TOTAL Store lists Spark at $69/month unlimited or $28 for four reports but warns Spark stopped accepting new accounts July 31, 2026 and will not work with new UAD 3.6 forms. These are current software list prices, not evidence each appraiser pays them. Operator posts report legacy Spark/comp-data subscriptions and pay-per-report tool choices, but actual subscription purchases and product migration are not representative. Appraisal authoring software, MLS/data, form-filler and AMC portal may be separate software spend. WTP is mixed/volume-sensitive; avoid inferring CRM spend from client appraisal fees.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Assignments arrive from order panels, direct clients or recurring bank/corporate relationships. The appraiser balances acceptance/decline, market/credential fit, due date, property access and geography; site visits can be route batched across assignments. Desk/review assignments may require no inspection. Residential report timeline includes appointment, inspection if required, market analysis, report and review/revision; commercial scope may require multiple inspections and extended analysis. Rescheduling may follow tenant/owner unavailability, weather/access, incomplete records, complex subject or additional client requests. No automatic periodic recurrence should be assumed; update/appraisal cycles are client/asset specific.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Receive order/engagement → verify license/credential and competency, intended use/user, scope, jurisdiction and client independence requirements → accept/decline and confirm fee/due/access → contact property/occupant → prepare data and inspection plan → inspect subject or perform authorized desktop scope, take permitted photos/measurements/notes → analyze market/approaches and supporting data → complete report in compliant authoring tool and perform quality check → deliver securely to named client → answer factual revision/clarification or ROV routed by client while preserving independence → invoice/record payout and maintain workfile. Fannie Mae's residential lending guidance requires lender review of report/comparables and UAD migration is actively rolling out: as of this research date Nov. 2, 2026 is the published mandate date for UAD 3.6 users. That is a GSE residential program, not every appraisal format. Appraisal Foundation says assignment conditions/laws and standards affect scope. Real estate site visits require ordinary property access and field-safety planning; no universal PPE regime was sourced. A homeowner/borrower must not pressure an appraiser to reach a target value; route ROV through lender process and preserve independence. Noncompletion: access refused, uninhabitable/unsafe site, missing records, scope/fee dispute, outside competency/market, conflict, no data or client cancellation. Rework includes factual correction, additional analysis, client-compliant format, update/reinspection or a new assignment; do not make unapproved value changes.

## Customer communication and self-service

Client needs order acknowledgment, engagement/scope/fee, access appointment, missing-data request, status/ETA, secure report delivery, revision triage and invoice/payment. Borrower/property contact may schedule access but is not necessarily the client or intended user. Online portal should collect order requirements and docs for authenticated clients; general public cannot modify concluded value. Keep confidential borrower, client and property data segregated and avoid sending sensitive workfiles through ordinary email. Follow client policy for messages/ROV; human review is mandatory for scope, value, standards and conclusions.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Field gear can include camera/phone, laser measure, sketch/floor plan tools, vehicle and PPE appropriate to property; office stack includes licensed appraisal report-writing/form-filler, MLS/public-record data, cost/market tools, secure portal, e-signature, accounting and backup. One person or staff appraisers/trainees/analysts/admin may split inspection, comp research, writing, review and signing, within credential and client rules. Vendor evidence shows Spark imports MLS exports and transfers sales-grid/market data into appraisal report software; new TrueTracts packages align data tools and UAD 3.6 migration. Physical inventory is not material. Track credential expirations, software/data subscriptions and report resources; licensing and qualification are jurisdiction-specific.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures include order acceptance/decline by client/area/type, fee vs actual effort/travel, on-time delivery, report revision count/reason, data/inspection exceptions, days to collect, client concentration, occupancy/market segment and workload by credential. GSE/lender review measures report quality/completeness and comparable support; appraisal software packages provide comp and market-analysis analytics. These metrics should be appraiser/audience-specific; no cross-type benchmark or quality score is inferred. Report-level records must preserve assignment scope and evidence, not just an outcome/value field.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Residential appraisers use specialized form/report software plus MLS/public records, comp-analysis/cost tools and client/AMC order portals. True Footage documentation describes Spark's MLS export import and report data export; it states Spark is being merged/replaced by TrueTracts and warns of UAD 3.6 compatibility limits. Fannie Mae publishes UAD 3.6 technical resources for appraisers and software providers, evidencing material form/data migration. Appraiser forum posts describe frustration with repeated or poorly targeted AMC revisions, client-specific expectations and tool configuration; other posts reveal there are reasonable AMC/client relationships too. Commercial practitioners may use different valuation/database and document systems; current evidence is insufficient for a confident common stack. Firms may employ appraiser, trainee, researcher and reviewer roles; sole operators may choose per-use data plans.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** ASC oversees state appraiser/AMC regulatory programs; The Appraisal Foundation publishes USPAP application guidance; Fannie Mae specifies lender residential report/comparable review and UAD 3.6 migration. Specialized systems handle appraisal forms and market data. **Inference:** the CRM should track engagement and deadlines but not compute/decide value or become the regulated report of record. **Uncertain:** this broad candidate may include commercial real estate, personal property or business valuation; workflow and software evidence here centers real property and lender residential work. Pricing and AMC fee perceptions are contentious and self-selected. UAD dates/specifications change; verify with GSE sources at implementation. Status partial because the commercial/private coverage and cross-type common software workflow are not sufficiently researched to make confident generalized defaults.
