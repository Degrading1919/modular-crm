## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Retrofitting an egress window in a basement can involve more than replacing a window: survey and code check, permit/engineering, utility locate, exterior excavation and well/drainage, temporary support, cutting a foundation, installing lintel/frame/window, waterproofing, interior/exterior finish and inspections. A window manufacturer manual covers product installation only; an egress opening must meet the locally adopted code and site conditions. Foundation material, finished-room impact, access and drainage can change estimate and schedule materially.

This niche warrants a project workflow distinct from broad door/window installation. The useful records are the specific basement room/opening, wall structure, approved dimensions, well/drainage, permit/engineering, utility clearance, concealed-work photos, inspection and product serial/warranty. Safety and emergency egress are critical; a CRM cannot calculate structural adequacy or certify code. Direct operator workflow and primary code sources are available; software adoption and WTP are not established.

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners, basement finishers, real-estate investors, remodelers and GCs buy new or enlarged emergency escape windows, often to support a sleeping room or basement conversion. Specialist foundation/window companies may offer turnkey excavation/cutting/window-well/drainage/finish, while carpenters or window installers may take only framing/window scope under a GC. Job ranges from an above-grade opening replacement to deep well, poured-concrete cut, finish restoration and pump/drain. One-time project with possible warranty/drainage callback; service area is local due excavation/equipment logistics.

## Terminology and durable records

Capture property, basement room/use, existing opening and wall type/thickness, opening size/net-clear plan, sill height, grade, window/well, well ladder/cover, drainage/outlet, foundation structural details, excavation depth, nearby utilities, finish, plans/engineering revision, permit/inspection, product model, crew/equipment, change and warranty. Room and installed window/well persist; layout, shoring, locate response and cut photos are project evidence. Room use matters because adopted rules may require an emergency escape/rescue opening in sleeping rooms; the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) decides applicable code.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks replacement vs new/enlarged opening, basement room use, existing window, foundation type, rough dimensions, exterior grade/space, drainage, finished basement, access, known utilities, plans/survey, location, desired date and permit status. Operator sources identify wall material, excavation depth, well/drainage, structural header, utility conflict and finish as quote drivers. Site measurement and often engineering/AHJ confirmation precede a binding quote. Quote should separate design/permit, locate, shoring, cut, excavation/disposal, well, drainage, window, waterproofing, finish, equipment, inspection and restoration. Approve opening and scope before cutting; hidden utilities or unsuitable foundation require stop/change workflow.

Stages: enquiry → feasibility/site visit → layout/engineer/AHJ and permit → locate and mobilize → excavate/shoring/cut → lintel/opening/waterproofing/well/drain/window → finish and inspection → customer handoff. Website can capture photos, but should schedule review rather than promise code compliance.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Coordinate engineer/AHJ approval, utility marking, excavation equipment, concrete-cut crew, window/well product, drain/pump connection, interior finish and inspection. Rain, groundwater, frost and soil can delay excavation and create water risk. Finished basement protection/containment affects work windows. Holds include permit, unclear structure, utility, weather/groundwater, drainage design, product availability or inspection. Keep approval and field dates distinct; customer may be unable to use the room until sign-off.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm code/AHJ-approved opening and plans → obtain permit/engineering/utility clearance → protect room and isolate work zone → excavate and shore safely → cut foundation with dust/water controls → install engineered support, frame, waterproofing, well and drainage → install listed window and verify clear opening/sill/operation → restore interior/exterior and test drainage → inspection/photos/punch, manual/warranty and acceptance. OSHA notes concrete cutting/drilling can generate respirable crystalline silica; construction standard/control requirements depend on tasks and employer. Excavation, wall stability, heavy equipment, electrical, dust, water and confined access hazards need competent planning. Do not use profile fields as code calculator. Rework includes water infiltration, poor drainage, undersized clear opening, cracked wall, wrong lintel, difficult operation, damaged finishes or failed inspection.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need feasibility/permit expectations, scope and finish, utility locate, excavation/room protection, weather delays, inspection timing and restored-room availability. Permit/status updates and change approvals should be explicit; after completion provide opening operation, well-cover/ladder care, drainage maintenance, window model/warranty and photos. Never call a basement bedroom compliant solely because a window was installed.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Equipment includes excavator, concrete saw/core tools, shoring, lifting/handling, pump/drain, well materials, window, dust/water controls and hauling. Product orders may be model/size-specific. Team may involve estimator, structural engineer, utility locator, excavator, concrete cutter, carpenter/window installer, drainage/plumber, finisher and inspector. Local licensing and credential rules vary; record responsibility/credential for applicable scopes.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred measures: feasibility-to-permit, permit delay, quote variance by wall/soil, install days, utility/groundwater hold, first-pass inspection, drainage callback, finish restoration, change recovery and gross margin. Segment by foundation type, depth, finished/unfinished basement and turnkey/subcontract scope. No benchmark KPI source found.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask room use, current wall/opening, foundation, grade, drainage, utility/permit/engineering status and finish scope. Use explicit readiness gates before excavation and cutting; retain the adopted-code authority, approved plan/revision, locate, concealed-work photos, inspection and warranty. Do not automate structural or code-compliance conclusions. Validate installer software stack and WTP through interviews.
