## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Residential green/home-performance consultation can mean an energy assessment, home energy score/audit, walkthrough, diagnostic testing, utility/rebate guidance and prioritized retrofit recommendations. DOE Home Energy Score uses standard data to estimate energy use/cost and recommendations; BPI credentials distinguish field data collection from professional analysis/reporting. A regional home-performance provider sells an assessment for $495; utilities may offer subsidized/free narrower surveys, so service scopes and buyer payment vary.

**Inference:** A consultant’s durable work product is a property assessment and recommendations/report, with evidence, assumptions, assessor credentials/program, and optional referrals to contractors. This differs from thermal-imaging inspection and from doing the upgrades. **Uncertain:** the source phrase “green home” could also include materials, indoor environmental health, water, electrification or sustainability strategy beyond energy; available evidence primarily supports energy/home-performance services. Setup must allow consultant-defined scope rather than overstate a universal audit.

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners, homebuyers/sellers, landlords/property managers, builders/remodelers and affordable-housing/utility program participants seek reduced energy use, comfort, electrification readiness, rebates or documented assessment. Revenue model may be paid standalone diagnostic/report, bundled with home inspection, utility/program subsidy, builder service or referral/consulting engagement. Consultant may be independent, a BPI/RESNET certified assessor, utility trade ally or contractor selling upgrades; independence/conflict-of-interest should be disclosed. A report may lead to separate retrofit work but is not itself installation. Program availability/geography and incentive eligibility shape purchasing; some households use free utility assessments.

## Terminology and durable records

Property record: address, building type/age/area, envelope, insulation/attic, windows/doors, HVAC/water heater, fuel, ventilation, ducts, utility usage/bills, occupancy, comfort concerns and prior upgrades. Visit data: blower-door/duct readings if performed, photographs, equipment model/condition, temperature, assumptions and test calibration. Report: rating/score, energy estimates, prioritized measures, cost/benefit assumptions, rebate/program, assessor/certification, date/version and limitations. Store customer consent for utility data, owner/tenant access and photographs. Do not present report as a home inspection, engineering certification, code inspection or guaranteed savings unless credential/scope supports it.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Possible service modes: low-cost walkthrough/utility survey; comprehensive assessment with diagnostics; DOE Home Energy Score; energy modeling/consultation; rebate/project planning; post-retrofit verification. Intake asks goal, property type/age/area, address/program eligibility, recent bills/fuel, HVAC and insulation known, comfort/moisture concerns, occupant/access, desired deliverable, use for rebate/tax/transaction and whether installed upgrades are requested. Screen for suspected mold/combustion/structural issues to refer. Quote by assessment level, home size/complexity, diagnostics, travel, report/model, incentive support and follow-up. State in writing the scope, data required, report standards, whether measures are estimates or measured and any referral/contractor relationship. Booking can be online request but program eligibility and report use may need human review.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service prices:** North Country Home Performance posts a $495 prepaid professional diagnostic assessment, emphasizing it is not a free sales appointment ([operator rate](https://northcountryhomeperformance.com/assessment), accessed 2026-09-24). DOE Home Energy Score describes assessor report and may be part of an audit, inspection or standalone product but does not set a consumer price ([program](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/articles/home-energy-score), accessed 2026-09-24). Utility-supported audits can be subsidized/free and narrower; a recent local report describes a free Huntsville Utilities home survey with appliance/attic recommendations, illustrative of local program variance ([report](https://www.axios.com/local/huntsville/2026/09/04/huntsville-utilities-free-home-audit-energy), accessed 2026-09-24). These services are not comparable scope-for-scope.

Price inputs: home size/type, energy data, diagnostic equipment/time, report/score/program requirements, travel, modeling, follow-up, subsidy/utility payment, and rebate documentation. Avoid contingent upsell unless clearly disclosed. Software WTP: DOE provides Home Energy Score tools/program resources; BPI/RESNET credential platforms and modeling tools may add subscription/equipment costs, but no direct auditor subscription purchase/WTP evidence was found. The $495 assessment is customer service price, not software budget. No current independent energy-audit software plan pricing validated.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Usually one diagnostic/report engagement per property; a follow-up may occur after upgrades or when energy systems change. Schedule occupied-home walkthrough, access to attic/mechanical areas, utility bills and blower-door testing if scope includes it; weather/wind and HVAC conditions may affect tests. Utility program and assessor availability determine dates. Batch by region/program and reporting deadlines. Follow-up or verification is a separately defined visit and data set, not recurring default. Customer may reschedule if access or bill history absent; report pipeline needs time for analysis after onsite visit.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm authorized scope, program/credential/report purpose and property access; gather data and consent; visually inspect systems/envelope; conduct only selected tests using appropriate equipment and safe operation; document measurements, photos, assumptions and limitations; analyze and prepare prioritized report; quality review and deliver; discuss measures/program/referral and follow-up. BPI’s Energy Auditor competency includes energy, health and safety evaluation; DOE program may require assessor/partner QA. Blower-door testing can interact with combustion appliances and indoor conditions, so qualified procedure and safety screening matter. Stop/referral for suspected combustion spillage, hazardous materials, mold, electrical/structural issue, unsafe attic or inaccessible system. Do not certify a retrofit or savings based solely on quick visual visit. DOE notes tax-credit auditor-report certification and identifiers; that page itself flags statutory changes, so auditor/client should check current IRS rules and not rely on stale marketing. No universal “green consultant” license established.

## Customer communication and self-service

Explain service level, tests, required bills/access, fee or subsidy, report delivery time, score/rating purpose, uncertainty/utility-bill variability, referral relationships and next steps. Customer portal can securely collect bills/photos, schedule, release report and track upgrades. Include utility data privacy and limited access. Make clear that recommendations are not contractor quotes or guaranteed savings; client may choose independent contractors. If report is used for a federal/state incentive, communicate current eligibility/credential requirement with authoritative source and updated date rather than promise credit.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Assessment may use blower door/fan, pressure gauges, duct leakage tester, infrared camera (only if part of energy assessment), combustion analyzer, moisture/temperature meters, ladder, PPE, tablet/report software, utility data authorization and transport cases. Equipment calibration and assessor certification/program account are important. Credentials include BPI BA-T/BA-P or HEP Energy Auditor and DOE Home Energy Score assessor program, depending report/service. Do not imply every consultant requires every certification. No inventory beyond equipment/consumables; workforce often single auditor plus analyst/report QA; utility programs may have contractors/trade allies.

## Reporting and operating measures

Track leads by source/program, assessment scope and payment source, scheduling/access failure, field hours, report turnaround, testing completeness, measures by home/system, client acceptance, rebate referral conversion (without conflating with work quality), follow-up after upgrades and QA corrections. Performance savings should be measured against a defined baseline/weather normalization if claimed. No operator KPI benchmark found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Consultants use audit/rating calculation tools, utility bills/data, portable test equipment, report templates, secure photo/data storage, scheduling and invoicing; this common stack is inferred. DOE offers a national standardized Home Energy Score/report workflow and partner QA; BPI publishes assessor credentials and scope. Utility programs may fund audits and structure referrals. No typical field CRM software or subscription/WTP was evidenced. Integration choices depend on whether the consultant operates independently or within utility/HVAC/inspection business. Thermal imaging may be one measurement tool but the candidate is not general home inspection.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** DOE standards a score/report and partner pathway; BPI credentials define collection and analysis; paid independent and subsidized utility service both exist. **Inference:** willingness to pay depends on report depth, rebate access, confidence and whether client plans work; CRM value likely lies in report/client lifecycle. **Uncertain:** “green home consultation” could cover sustainability topics beyond home energy; representative solo consultant software choices and WTP are missing. Federal tax credits and qualified auditor rules have changed and should be verified at service time. Confidence high for energy-assessment branch, medium for product fit, low for full semantic scope.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask whether the firm performs energy audits, DOE scores, sustainability/material advising, post-retrofit verification, or installs upgrades; identify credential/program, report deliverable, payor and testing scope. Prototype property/system profile, secure utility data and photos, assessment form, field readings, report and follow-up. Keep technical calculation external/configurable and avoid presenting CRM reports as certified score/audit outputs. Verify current program, DOE/IRS, utility and state rules before setting defaults.
