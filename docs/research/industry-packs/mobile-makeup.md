## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Mobile makeup artists travel to weddings, events, photography/production, and private clients with a portable kit. Wedding work adds trials, event date reservation, party headcount, per-face timing, travel/parking, early start, touch-ups, assistant artists, cancellation terms, and a ready-by timeline. Artist pages show retainers/contracts and travel fees; an operator/manager discussion notes that bridal specialists may have seasonal peak weekends and often cannot do more than one wedding in a day. Texas cosmetology inspection guidance requires fresh makeup brushes/disposables when they contact client skin/product (Texas-specific regulation). FDA eye-cosmetic advice warns against sharing cosmetics and says instruments near eyes should be clean.

**Inference:** The profile should support event/project-style bookings and single-appointment makeup work, linked trials, headcount/timeline and assigned artists, rather than recurring household-style scheduling. Capture allergy/sensitivity disclosures and product/skin preferences with consent; never diagnose or promise a medical outcome. Makeup application licensing varies (California includes makeup among licensed cosmetology acts; other jurisdictions differ), and FDA MoCRA principally applies to product manufacturers/responsible persons, not as a substitute for local practitioner rules.

## Business model and customer segments

Clients include brides/wedding parties, event attendees, performers/production, photographers/editorial clients, and consumers seeking a lesson or occasion look. Services may be in the artist's studio, client home/hotel, venue, or backstage; mobile booking often centers on a date and venue. Independent artists may work solo, while larger weddings need assistants/artists assigned per person and schedule. Wedding season and Saturday clustering are visible in provider/operator evidence, but seasonality varies by region and event type. Repeat customers may book trials, engagements, family events, or lessons; most event day work is project-based.

## Terminology and durable records

Use client, event/booking, venue/getting-ready location, look, trial, face/service, artist/assistant, ready-by time, touch-up, and retainer. Site details: venue/address, getting-ready room, parking/loading, access/security, lighting, chair, table, electrical outlets, water/handwashing access, setup window, photo schedule, and venue contact. Client profile may retain approved look notes, skin-tone/product shades, allergies/sensitivities, preferred coverage, lash preferences, and reference/consent photos. Treat health disclosures as sensitive; only ask what is necessary to avoid product or application issues. Product batch/expiry, kit sanitation, and shade/products used can be recorded if the operator needs traceability; individual makeup products are not customer-owned assets by default.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Common work: event makeup, bridal day-of, trial/preview, wedding-party faces, lessons, on-location touch-ups, production/editorial day rate, lashes, and travel/destination service. Intake should capture event date, location(s), getting-ready time, ready-by deadline, party headcount and service menu, each person's timing, trial preference/date, artist preference/identity, skin/product concerns/allergies, desired look/photos, travel/parking, venue setup and payment contact. Portfolio photos are optional and require consent. Quote behavior is fixed menu/per face plus travel, minimum booking, early start, assistant, wait/touch-up, parking, lodging, or custom day rate. Date is normally secured by signed agreement and retainer per provider policies; cancellation/reschedule terms vary. A request with timeline that cannot fit available artists needs human review, not overbooking.

## Pricing and commercial model

Price drivers include look/complexity, bride vs party rate, trial, headcount/minimum booking, duration/timeline, number of artists, travel/mileage/parking/lodging, early start, touch-up/second location, lashes, and location/season. A Southern California artist lists bridal application and trials from $300–$550, party hair or makeup $200/person, and travel by zone; other artist pages differ. A Montreal customer shared multiple quotes: bridal makeup around CAD $250 plus travel, bridal hair $350 and trial $200, and combined hair/makeup packages varying widely. These examples illustrate pricing components and geography, not a market rate. **Software willingness-to-pay:** no direct makeup-artist software purchase/WTP evidence identified. HoneyBook currently lists Starter/Essentials/Premium as $29/$49/$109 monthly billed annually or $36/$59/$129 monthly according to its vendor support page; it supports inquiry/project, contract, invoice/payment and portal workflows. This is vendor list price, not observed MUA purchase. GlossGenius lists annual Standard $24/month, Gold $48, Platinum $148 for beauty businesses; also advertising only.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Appointment booking must protect date/time for trials and event, hold a schedule by face and artist, include travel/setup/cleanup, enforce minimums and ready-by deadline, and support multiple venues or touch-up blocks. Trial is often weeks/months before event but timing is policy. Artists may accept one large booking on a peak day rather than several scattered jobs; a geographically distant event may require an overnight or travel day. Intake or timeline changes after booking should trigger a capacity review. Cancellation/rescheduling and deposits/retainers are contract-specific. Everyday occasion clients may book ordinary appointments in wider availability windows.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Pre-event: confirm agreement, date/location, event timetable, party list, style/trial notes, products/allergies, access, setup conditions, travel, and assigned artist. Arrive/setup with clean brushes and disposable applicators; wash hands; use clean palette/decant product rather than double-dipping; use individual applicators for eye/lip products; avoid serving a client with active eye infection or irritated skin and refer/decline as appropriate. Apply agreed look; note product changes or sensitivities; capture optional completion/portfolio photo with separate consent; confirm client acceptance/touch-up and final payment. Texas rules give one jurisdiction example for single-use makeup brushes/tools per client; do not generalize its exact rule to every state. FDA advises clean eye-area instruments and not sharing eye cosmetics. MoCRA cosmetic-product adverse-event and labeling duties are manufacturer/responsible-party matters; a freelance artist should use products according to labels and report safety problems through appropriate channels. Noncompletion/rework: illness/allergy reaction, client/artist late, no setup space/light, missing party member, timeline slippage, unavailable artist, product shade mismatch, weather/venue access, or client requests material look change. Record agreed correction/reaction without promising results beyond skill/product capability.

## Customer communication and self-service

Inquiry response should clarify date/location/headcount and turnaround timeline before quoting. Send proposal, per-face schedule, contract/retainer, trial prep and result notes, reminders, travel/arrival instructions, changes, ready-by confirmation, balance invoice, and optional review/photo request. A portal can maintain contract, invoice, trial details, look references, and per-person schedule. Obtain explicit permission before posting faces/portfolio content. Human review is required for allergy/sensitivity disclosures, event timeline feasibility, assignment changes, refund/cancellation, product reactions, or requests outside practice scope.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Portable light/mirror, chair, makeup kit/shades, clean brushes, spatulas/palettes, single-use mascara/lip applicators/sponges, hand sanitizer, brush cleaner/disinfectant as appropriate, tissues, disposables, and touch-up kit. Kit inventory matters more when multiple artists, skin tones, destination events, or product continuity are involved. Product expiry/storage and hot-car exposure matter; FDA warns cosmetics can degrade at high temperatures. Roles include lead artist, assistant artist, makeup artist per party member, and coordinator; capture skills, assignment, travel time, service time, and optional tip/commission/payroll export. Avoid recording medical diagnoses or unneeded health detail.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred measures: inquiries-to-booking conversion by lead source/date, event revenue per artist-hour, average party size, travel/setup share, on-time ready-by completion, trial-to-wedding conversion, cancellations/retainers retained/refunded, rework/satisfaction, artist utilization, and seasonal/date occupancy. These are operational inferences; provider pages do not establish standard sector KPIs.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Event freelancers commonly need CRM/project pipeline, proposals/contracts, retainers/invoices, questionnaires, portals, and calendar; HoneyBook documents these features across plans. Beauty apps such as GlossGenius/Vagaro add service menus, booking, reminders, client profiles, forms, team calendars, and POS. A combined bridal provider showcases packages, trial services, group rates, add-ons, multi-day/destination booking, travel, and lead time. Operator/consumer discussions show friction over high travel/trial fees, event minimums, reliability/artist identity, tight schedules, and weekend capacity, but are individual anecdotes. A common stack may combine Instagram/website leads, forms/contracts, beauty scheduler, payment, spreadsheet run-of-show, and group text; prevalence is unverified. Software list pricing is not proof of mobile makeup artist WTP.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

On-location bridal work is well evidenced; makeup alone also serves events, everyday clients, and production, but research here skews wedding-oriented. Sanitation guidance is jurisdiction/product-specific, and FDA consumer eye-cosmetic precautions do not constitute a complete makeup-artist code. California/Texas licensing examples differ; confirm local rules for makeup-only practice and any hair/skin services. Prices and booking terms vary widely by geography, experience and party size; anecdotes should not become defaults. There is little direct software spend evidence among mobile makeup artists.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask whether the owner works weddings, general events, production, or everyday appointments; jurisdiction and service/license scope; event volume/headcount; trial policy; travel radius; lead time, retainer and cancellation terms; portfolio/photo consent; solo or multi-artist model; and booking/payment tools. Prototype inquiry-to-event quote, trial linked to the event, person-by-person schedule and artist assignment, location readiness, clean-kit closeout, allergy/sensitivity note with restricted visibility, retainer/balance notices, and explicit photo permission. Leave sanitation method, licenses, products, pricing and contract terms configurable pending local review.
