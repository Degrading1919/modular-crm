## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Boat repair is a diagnostic and work-order business anchored to a vessel, engine/system and service location that can be a slip, marina/boatyard, dry stack, trailer/driveway or shop. Repair categories include propulsion, electrical/electronics, fuel, steering, plumbing/bilge, fiberglass/gelcoat, rigging and accessories. A diagnostic visit identifies cause before the owner approves estimate/parts/work; boats may require haul-out, special access, OEM parts, model/engine-hour records, sea trial and warranty documentation. Marina restrictions, technician skills, parts lead time and seasonal launch backlog affect schedule and customer communication.

**Inference:** Use vessel/HIN and engine/component records, hours/serials, symptom and diagnostic results, approval boundary, parts, technical procedure, labor, tests and warranty as connected work history. Manage jobs as quote/diagnose/authorize/repair/test/handoff projects, not as a generic fixed-duration home service call. Keep winterizing and cleaning separate despite their possible presence in a marine shop’s overall menu.

## Business model and customer segments

Recreational owners, fishing/charter operators, marinas, boat dealers, fleets, yacht owners and buyers needing pre-purchase inspections hire mobile mechanics, independent repair shops, authorized engine dealers, marine electricians, fiberglass shops and full-service boatyards. One shop may handle multiple systems or outsource specialized work. Mobile techs serve dockside/driveway/trailer work when possible; heavy structural/fiberglass jobs may require haul-out and yard access. Repair may be customer-pay, warranty, insurance or pre-purchase; each has different authorization/records.

Repair is irregular, condition-driven and often urgent near launch/peak season. Preventive service is driven by engine hours/manufacturer service intervals and owner plans; breakdowns can occur at any time and may affect safety or ability to use the boat. Spring demand competes for tech/yard/parts capacity. One vessel may have multiple engines/systems, each with distinct serial, hours, diagnostic and warranty history. Mobile, shop, dealership and boatyard operations should not share one fixed routing/scope assumption.

## Terminology and durable records

Use customer/owner, vessel/boat, HIN, make/model/year, engine, serial, engine hours, work order, service writer/advisor, mechanic/technician, diagnostic, estimate, authorization, parts, operation, sea trial, warranty claim and service record. Vessel asset fields: name, HIN, registration/state, make/model/year, length/type, hull material, engine/drive model and serials, port/starboard association, engine hours at service, onboard system/equipment, marina/yard/home location, key/access state, service history, manuals, warranty, photo/damage history. HIN, registration and marina access details are sensitive; restrict and retain only as necessary.

Location needs current marina/slip/berth or trailer address, haul-out/launch yard, dry stack, gate/escort/vendor approval, vessel availability/access, parking, crane/lift/shore-power requirements, time windows, owner/harbor contact and environmental control requirements. Assets/components might include engine, drive, prop, batteries, bilge pump, electrical panel, fuel system, steering, trailer and navigation electronics; distinguish these from disposable job inputs. Store technician-only safety notes separately from customer-facing messages.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Possible work: engine and propulsion diagnostics, scheduled engine service, fuel system, cooling/impeller, lower unit/drive, battery/charging/electrical, navigation/audio, steering/controls, pumps/plumbing/bilge, fiberglass/hull/gelcoat, rigging, electronics installation, trailer, inspection, corrosion/wiring repair and warranty work. Seasonal preparation/winterizing is separately listed and should remain disabled unless tenant selects it; cleaning is another workflow. Emergency dockside callout, pre-purchase inspection, repair and maintenance plan are materially different service jobs.

Intake captures customer authorization; boat HIN/make/model/year/length; engine/drive make/model/serial, count and hours; symptoms/error/alarm, onset and conditions; service history; photos/video; parts/modifications; location/slip/yard/haul-out/access; operating safety impact; warranty/insurance; preferred timing; and customer cap/urgency. Ask whether boat can be moved, launched or tested. Diagnostic estimate may be separate from repair; show labor, parts, shop supplies/environmental fee/tax, travel/mobile callout, haul-out/storage and subcontractor separately. Set an approved limit; any newly discovered scope/parts requires customer approval before additional spend. Complex structural, fuel, electrical, or warranty work needs a qualified technician and documented review. Self-service should request symptoms/records and appointment, not diagnose or promise repair remotely.

## Pricing and commercial model

Prices may be hourly or flat-rate by engine, operation, boat length/system, parts and supplies, diagnostics, shop/mobile site, trip distance, haul-out, urgency, weather access, subcontractor and warranty status. Full Throttle Marine (Concord, CA) publicly lists $140/hour in-shop, $300 mobile callout within its county, $300 minimum shop diagnostic and written repair estimates; scope is its local, owner-operated shop. Juanito’s Boat Repair (Southwest FL) lists $135/hour drop-off and $165/hour mobile, diagnostics from $150, with maintenance/repair item prices and trip fee by zone; its page says price starts for typical single-outboard boats and excludes parts/tax/environmental fees. Manitowoc Marina’s 2024 mobile service guide charges travel time per mile and time/materials, demonstrating that access/travel may be separate. Reddit owner reports in NYC cite $150–$180 hourly labor and difficulty getting mechanic responses during busy periods; those are anecdotal and vary by work/region.

Deposits may be required for parts or large projects; authorization, diagnostic fees, parts availability, storage and warranty depend on shop contract. Customers may be billed for inspection/diagnosis even if repair is declined. Include due date only as estimate with parts/yard dependencies. **Software willingness-to-pay:** DockMaster and other marine systems generally require demo/contact sales; a third-party marketplace lists DockMaster “from $165/month,” but the vendor’s own service page does not publish list pricing and the aggregator’s packaging basis is unclear. A ShopSynq competitor table advertises $90–$259/mo for its own product and DockMaster quote-only. These are list-price signals, not observed purchases. No direct operator WTP/purchase receipt was found.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Preventive maintenance can recur by engine hours, manufacturer schedule or annual use; repair and diagnosis are one-time/problem-driven. Do not equate seasonal winterization (separate candidate) with regular boat repair, though service shops may cross-sell it. Schedule by technician skill/OEM authorization, engine/system, shop bay/haul-out/yard availability, parts lead time, owner presence, marina vendor window, mobile zone and sea-trial/weather conditions. Keep separate stages for waiting diagnosis authorization, parts ordered/received, subcontract/haul-out, work, test, customer pickup/launch and warranty follow-up.

Dispatch needs current boat location; a boat may shift slips or be stored ashore. Service techs may work remote from the office with intermittent dock connectivity. Urgent no-start/safety issue should be triaged; schedule not guaranteed before boat/parts are available. Seasonal backlogs, supplier delays, wrong parts, hidden corrosion/damage, marina access limits and launch windows cause late work. Record customer-initiated delays and estimate approval timing rather than attributing all lateness to technician.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm vessel identity, service request and owner authorization, yard/vendor access, safety status, keys and engine hours. Document pre-existing condition and diagnostic findings (codes/readings/tests/photos). Prepare estimate with diagnostics, labor, parts, shop/travel/environmental charges and approval cap; obtain explicit authorization. Confirm correct part/serial and procedure, perform work within skill/standards, log technician/time/parts/consumables, capture test results and customer-approved change orders, run required dock/sea trial when safe and authorized, inspect for leaks/alarms/operation, return boat/keys and provide written service record/warranty invoice.

For electrical, fuel, propulsion, lift/haul-out, paint/sanding and fiberglass work, hazards include fuel/vapor, battery/shore power, moving machinery, confined spaces, falls/drowning, chemicals, dust, heavy lifts and open-water testing. ABYC certification covers standards-based training for systems such as electrical, engines/fuel, corrosion and composites; ABYC says technician designation is a professional credential and has experience/exam requirements. Do not imply all repairers are certified or that ABYC credential is a law. EPA’s Sector R ship/boat repair-yard stormwater guidance recommends pressure washing only in designated washwater containment areas and containing sanding/paint waste; its industrial repair-yard context must not be applied blindly to every private mobile repair job, but local permit/yard policies matter. Exceptions: owner/no access, diagnostic inconclusive, unsafe fuel/electrical/weather, incompatible/missing part, hidden damage, customer declines, haul-out required, warranty/insurance review, subcontract delay, failed test, or repeat defect. Warranty claims require work history, installed part, manufacturer, procedure and evidence.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need intake confirmation, diagnostic findings and not-to-exceed quote, approval/change order, parts ETA, status, yard/launch access, test/sea-trial result, completed service record, invoice/payment, warranty information and pickup readiness. Portal can retain vessel/engine history, documents, estimates and approvals. Avoid automated diagnosis, sea-trial promise, or “safe to operate” message absent technician sign-off. Repair approval and safety-related service notices must remain attributable to named customer and employee.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Tools depend on service: OEM/engine diagnostics, multimeter, compression tester, torque tools, lifting equipment, pumps, electrical crimp tools, fuel-safe equipment, marine-grade wiring/heat shrink, prop pullers, fiberglass tools, PPE, spill kit, service vehicle/trailer and shop bay. Parts can be engine/serial-specific, proprietary and special-order; tie ordered part/vendor/ETA/cost to approved operation and record returns/core exchange. Yard may supply lift, stands, travelift, crane, storage and subcontract vendors. Roles may include service writer, certified/experienced mechanic, electrician, fiberglass tech, rigger, yard crew and parts specialist; staffing and payroll/time vary. Workforce credentials/certifications and scope should be recorded only where actually applicable. The repair work itself is distinct from winterization stock and cleaning chemicals.

## Reporting and operating measures

Marine service software advertises estimates-to-workorders, standard vs actual time, service scheduler, warranty claims, parts and technician productivity. Useful reports: active work orders by approved/waiting diagnosis/waiting part/repair/test; quote-to-approval; parts wait; promised vs actual turnaround; first-time fix/callback; labor/parts margin; travel/haul-out utilization; warranty recovery; hours by engine/operation/tech; safety/test exceptions; and seasonal backlog. A job closed before customer pickup/warranty paperwork is complete can misstate results; define “complete,” “ready” and “released” separately. Avoid a universal target absent industry evidence.

## Existing software and operator evidence

DockMaster markets vessel/customer history, digital estimate approval, deposits, work order conversion, special order parts, inventory, mobile time tracking, photos/videos, offline entry and warranty claims; it targets marinas/dealers/boat service operations, not necessarily a solo technician. Repair shops also use OEM diagnostic/parts tools, dealer portals, accounting, calendar, paper work orders, email/text, photos and spreadsheets. Operator-facing rate pages reveal the need to disclose trip fees, diagnostic fee, customer approval and parts/tax/environmental extras. Reddit repair-bill threads show debate about diagnostics, shop supplies and parts markup; other boat owners complain about delayed boatyard communication and difficulty finding an available marine mechanic. These individual reports don't establish prevalence, but reinforce explicit authorizations, diagnosis evidence, status and parts ETA. One-person mobile shops may have little need for marina ERP; large yards need parts/warranty/yard operations. No representative software adoption survey or operator WTP is established.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Marine standards/certification emphasize specific technical competency; shops vary in what they work on, mobile vs yard and authorized brands. Published prices show separate diagnostic, hourly, travel, parts and environmental charges; consumers report mixed expectations about shop supplies, diagnostic labor and availability. **Inference:** explicit estimate approval, asset history and stage/parts status are primary product needs. **Uncertain:** many systems and work scopes have different licensing/insurance requirements; this research did not establish one national boat-mechanic license. ABYC standards/certification are respected industry evidence but not interchangeable with law. Federal/state environmental permits apply to some yard activities, not every dockside repair. Vendor list price and feature marketing do not establish software adoption/WTP. Confidence high for work-order model, medium for feature fit and low for nationwide regulatory summary.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype vessel and component records, diagnostic-first quote, explicit customer authorization, parts wait, repair operation, test result and release. Ask what systems/brands are serviced, shop/mobile/yard mix, warranty handling, service intervals, parts stock, subcontract/haul-out and customer access policy. Keep shop labor/travel, diagnostic, parts, environmental surcharge, warranty and customer approval threshold tenant-specific. Require a technician to record diagnosis/test and approve safety communication; do not auto-diagnose or infer a boat is safe. Do not fold winterizing or cleaning into repair defaults.
