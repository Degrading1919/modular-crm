## Executive summary

Pipe fitting work assembles, fabricates, installs, tests and sometimes maintains piping systems. Scope ranges from commercial mechanical/HVAC piping to industrial process piping in chemical, energy, pharmaceutical, food, paper, semiconductor and other facilities. Shop prefabrication/spools and field erection form a linked workflow; material traceability, weld/fitter qualification, inspection and testing can be part of project quality documentation.

**Observed evidence:** ASME B31.3 covers process-piping materials/components, design, fabrication, assembly, erection, examination, inspection and testing. UA trade discussion describes variation between construction fitters, service fitters, pipe welders and local division boundaries. **Uncertain:** “pipe fitting” does not identify fluid system, pressure/code, shop vs field, union/contract structure or whether it means plumbing installation. Profile is partial until the candidate's segment is known.

## Business model and customer segments

- Buyers include mechanical contractors/GCs, industrial plant owners, EPCs, utilities and facilities teams. Work is bid subcontract/project work, time-and-material service/maintenance, prefabricated spool production, shutdown/turnaround, or a combination.
- Systems may carry water, steam, air, refrigerant, gas, chemical, petroleum or process fluids. New construction, plant modification, equipment tie-in, service outage and repair are common triggers (source-backed by ASME scope and trade descriptions).
- Firms can be union or nonunion, fabrication shop, field installation contractor, service contractor, or integrated mechanical contractor. These structures change labor assignment, certifications, timekeeping, access and billing.
- Do not use pipe fitting as a proxy for a plumber, sprinkler fitter, welder or excavation contractor. Boundary depends on the system, jurisdiction and trade agreement.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms include pipe/fitting, valve, spool, isometric, line number, weld map, WPS/PQR, welder qualification, NDE/NDT, hydrotest/leak test, pressure class, material test report (MTR), heat/lot traceability, hanger/support, tie-in, punch list and turnover dossier.
- **Service-location data:** facility/unit/line/system, hazardous/controlled area, owner permit/access process, isolation/lockout and hot-work/confined-space controls, drawings/isometrics, test boundaries, tie-in/outage window and site contact.
- **Persistent customer assets:** system and line/component history, drawings, installed valves/equipment, inspection/test records and turnover documents. A project also creates temporary spool/weld/material traceability records and daily progress/quantities.
- Safety-critical and regulated data may include pressure/service design, isolation, hot-work permits, confined space, qualified weld procedures/personnel, material traceability, NDE and test acceptance. Requirements are code/contract-specific; CRM status is not a compliance approval.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Work may include shop spool fabrication, field layout/fit-up/erection, pipe support/hangers, system connection, weld/braze/thread/groove work, pressure test, commissioning and maintenance. Job scope must distinguish process piping from building plumbing and HVAC piping.
- Intake: drawings/isometrics/spec, service/fluid/material/pressure, quantities/line classes, site survey, access/permit windows, owner QA/QC and inspection/NDE requirements, prefabrication opportunity, material procurement/traceability, outage/tie-in plan, labor agreement and schedule.
- Quotes may be drawing takeoff/unit/labor hour/project bid, time-and-material or service-call estimates; materials, shop/field labor, lifts/rigging, NDE, hydrotest, prefabrication, permits and outage work are distinct costs.
- Booking is a construction sequence tied to drawings, other trades, material availability, weld/NDE/test/inspection points and plant/site outages. Emergency repair and planned shutdown work are different dispatch modes.

## Pricing and commercial model

- **Observed evidence:** ASME code scope covers fabrication through testing, while fabrication workflow vendors track material receipt through field installation; these indicate project/quality dependencies but do not specify common pricing.
- Price may be lump sum from drawings, unit-based, labor/material, time-and-material service, or contract rates. Inputs include material/alloy, diameter/schedule, weld count/position, access, pre-fab, NDE/testing, pressure/service class, qualified labor, shutdown/overtime and documentation burden.
- Progress billing, retainage, change orders, service agreements and warranty depend on GC/owner contract. No direct representative rate or operator software WTP evidence found.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- New fabrication/install is project and milestone based. Maintenance/service and repeat shutdowns are conditional on facilities contracts and assets.
- Capacity depends on qualified fitters/welders, spool/shop capacity, material/heat traceability, lifts/rigging, other-trade readiness, permit windows and inspection/NDE availability. Prefab can reduce field work but requires drawing/design maturity.
- Daily dispatch must coordinate shop, delivery and field crew; technician route optimization is usually secondary to project workforce and work-package planning.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Review contract documents, line list/isometrics, materials, code/spec and quality plan; site-walk and resolve drawing/interface questions.
2. Procure/receive traceable material; prefabricate spools and identify them; plan fit-up/erection sequence and lift/access.
3. Verify permits, isolations, hot work, confined space and qualified workforce as applicable; install and record work/weld identifiers.
4. Perform required visual/NDE/examination, pressure/leak tests and inspection; remediate punch items and retest where required.
5. Deliver turnover dossier/as-builts, MTRs, weld/NDE/test records and customer acceptance; update system history for service work.
- **Observed evidence:** ASME outlines process piping fabrication, erection, examination and testing; project specifications and PypeServer describe weld/material/field status evidence. Actual governing edition and scope are specified by contract/jurisdiction.
- Rework can result from drawing revision, material substitution/traceability gap, fit-up, weld rejection, inaccessible tie-in, test failure, late owner witness or shutdown window change. Safety permit and QA signoff require human authority.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Customers/GCs need drawing and submittal clarifications, material approvals, delivery/shop status, access/outage coordination, inspection and test notices, RFI/change-order approval, progress, punch status and complete turnover package.
- Website intake can request scope/drawings and service location, but estimating and system suitability require technical review. Avoid self-service claims of code compliance or “approved” pressure testing.
- Daily reports may support customer and GC updates. Sensitive plant drawings and system info need tenant-defined access controls.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Pipe, fittings, valves, supports, consumables/electrodes, spool pieces, tools, weld machines, test equipment, lifts/rigging, shop machines, PPE and traceability certificates. Stocking needs vary by material and service.
- Crew records may include fitter/welder qualifications, procedures, union classifications/agreements, hours, shop/field assignment and safety training. Do not assume every fitter welds or all jobs use a union workforce.
- Suppliers and fabrication shops feed lead times, substitutions, material test certificates and delivery/spool identifiers into the project. Material traceability can be a quality obligation rather than normal CRM inventory.

## Reporting and operating measures

- **Observed evidence:** PypeServer advertises workflow visibility from material receipt through installation; industry specifications use project quality records such as MTR, weld maps, NDE and hydrotest.
- **Inference:** installed quantities/welds/spools, planned vs actual labor, shop productivity, material issued/waste, inspection acceptance/rework, test status, RFI/change value, milestone and outage schedule performance, safety and project margin are useful. KPIs differ for shop vs field/service firms.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

ASME source describes process piping scope but does not say all “pipe fitting” businesses are process contractors. Trade forums show craft boundaries vary by local union/industry and actual employer. Fabricator software illustrates shop-field traceability but is promotional. Distinctions among plumber, fitter, sprinkler fitter and welder differ by jurisdiction, system and labor agreement. Scope ambiguity and limited independent operator/business evidence justify partial status. No general price or software WTP claims are supported.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

First ask whether the business fabricates process piping, installs building mechanical/HVAC, provides pipe service, or performs another system. Prototype job phases with drawing revision, materials/spool, shop/field crew, test/inspection and turnover documents only for tenants who need them. Keep code and QA requirements, labor terms, rates, credentials, inventory, test forms and outage process configurable. Consider integrations to specialist fabrication/ERP/project systems; do not represent weld compliance or pressure-test acceptance as generic CRM validation.
