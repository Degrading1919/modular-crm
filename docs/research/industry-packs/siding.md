## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Siding contractors sell exterior cladding replacement/installation and related trim, weather-barrier, repair, and finish work. Material system instructions, local conditions, product availability, access, and substrate repairs affect schedule and quality. James Hardie reports a typical seven-to-fourteen-day installation for its own average-sized-home product projects, explicitly dependent on home size and required repairs; do not generalize that duration to all siding.

**Inference:** The core product opportunity is a project sales-to-install loop: qualify property and material goals, estimate exterior area and scope, approve a material/appearance proposal, schedule crews and deliveries, document concealed substrate/weather-barrier stages, and hand over warranty/care information. Siding differs from a general contractor because a siding company may control one building-envelope trade and product system rather than the full construction project.

## Business model and customer segments

- Residential homeowners, landlords/property managers, builders, remodelers, and commercial owners/GCs. Work ranges from repairs and partial elevations to whole-building replacement/new construction.
- Triggers include deterioration, water intrusion, storm damage, renovation/resale, energy/aesthetic upgrades, and new construction; urgency and seasonality vary by climate and storm events.
- Operators may be owner-led sales crews, subcontract installation teams, product-certified specialists, or larger multi-crew exterior remodelers. Manufacturer certification is product/program-specific, not universal trade licensing.
- **Uncertain:** relative mix of residential replacement, new construction, insurance work, and commercial packages is geography/company dependent.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms include cladding/siding, elevation, lap, panel, shingle, soffit, fascia, trim, starter strip, flashing, water-resistive barrier/housewrap, rainscreen, fastener schedule, reveal, tear-off, and punch list.
- **Service-location data:** building/elevations, stories/height, access/landscaping, existing cladding, substrate, window/door/roof interfaces, moisture/rot, staging, utilities, weather exposure and jurisdiction.
- **Persistent customer assets:** property/building, installed cladding product/color/profile, warranty/documentation, and project photos/history. One-time records include measured area, current defects, product lot/delivery and scaffold/lift plan.
- Safety/customer-visible: silica dust for fiber-cement cutting, work at height, weather barrier, flashing and manufacturer installation requirements. A photo checklist can document work but cannot certify code compliance.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Services may include inspection/repair, tear-off, new siding, replacement, trim/soffit/fascia, housewrap/rainscreen, flashing coordination, painting/finish where offered, and concealed-damage repair/change orders.
- Intake: property/elevations/photos, material preference, approximate dimensions/stories, existing substrate, access, desired appearance, urgency, budget range if customer supplies it, and HOA/permit/insurance constraints.
- Quotes are measured/project bids with separate removal, substrate repair, weatherproofing, material system, trim, access equipment, disposal and options. Unknown rot or sheathing damage requires a defined allowance/change-order path.
- Booking is a multiday crew project with delivery, weather, cure/finish, and material sequencing; same-day small repairs are a distinct flow.

## Pricing and commercial model

- **Observed evidence:** Provider estimating pages organize bids by square footage/material type, tear-off, housewrap, trim and repairs. Price depends on measured area, stories/access, material/system, removal, substrate condition, trim/openings, disposal, region and crew scope.
- Residential proposals may offer material/finish options; commercial/new-build jobs may be bid to plans or GC schedule. Deposits, progress/final billing, allowances, warranty and cancellation policy vary by company/contract.
- Vendor-published example prices are not treated as market averages. No direct owner evidence established software willingness-to-pay; trade-specific software pricing pages indicate product supply, not representative buyer demand.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Installation/replacement is project-based; inspection or maintenance reminders may be appropriate only where the contractor offers them. No routine recurrence is established.
- Capacity depends on trained crew availability, lift/scaffold needs, material delivery and color/lot consistency, weather, permit/access and concealed repairs. Large homes and multiple elevations can span days; production pauses may be needed for inspections or weather.
- Dispatch is crew/project sequencing, not dense daily route work. Emergency weather-damage response is conditional and can displace planned work.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Qualify and measure elevations; inspect existing cladding and risk areas; select product and clarify scope.
2. Obtain approvals/materials and stage lift/scaffold, disposal and weather protection.
3. Protect site; remove old material as scoped; inspect/document substrate and escalate newly discovered rot or damage.
4. Install weather barrier, flashing and cladding to product-specific instructions and applicable code; document concealed interfaces and fastener/spacing details as needed.
5. Finish trim/punch list, remove waste, customer walkthrough, closeout photos and product warranty/care documents.
- **Observed evidence:** James Hardie emphasizes product-specific fastening/installation instructions, qualified experience and that local code may change method; its guidance also includes silica safety. Other manufacturers and jurisdictions differ.
- Rework can follow water intrusion, omitted flashing, incorrect fastening/clearance, visible finish mismatch, concealed damage, weather or material substitution. Human review is needed for scope changes and safety/code exceptions.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Customers need a clear material/appearance choice, measured proposal, start window, delivery and property-preparation instructions, daily delay updates, change-order approval for concealed damage, completion photos and warranty documents.
- A website should collect property photos/address and request an estimate; final scope generally needs review/measurement. Automated messages must not promise a product warranty or code result.
- Email/SMS/phone usage is plausible from incumbent software materials; direct operator evidence on channel/consent norms was limited.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Material procurement (cladding, trim, wrap, flashing, fasteners, sealants), color/profile/lot, waste, disposal, and access equipment are job relevant. Exact inventory complexity depends on whether the company sells material or uses GC/customer procurement.
- Crew members need product and height/silica safety training appropriate to work. Subcontracting is possible; do not presume a single employment model or licensing standard nationwide.
- Record product documentation and material substitutions. Supplier orders and delivery confirmations are useful; real-time inventory is conditional on business model.

## Reporting and operating measures

- Incumbent trade pages mention sales pipeline, estimates, crew dispatch, job photos/daily logs and review capture; these are marketed workflows, not independently proven universal operator KPIs.
- **Inference:** lead-to-estimate time, estimate approval, sold material/system, estimated vs actual area/labor/material, change-order recovery, on-time start/close, callback/warranty rates, margin by job, and lead source can help owners.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- James Hardie's Pros Hub provides product guidance, dealer/product discovery, and order tracking for dealers. Siding-specific software vendors advertise measured estimating, pipeline, crew/project scheduling, photos and proposal/invoice/payment workflows.
- First-person Reddit contractor discussions describe disputes about adherence to changing manufacturer details and warranty expectations; these posts are anecdotal, not frequency estimates. They show why versioned product instructions and documented customer decisions matter.
- Siding CRM pricing observed: Subcrewz lists Growth at $149/month for up to 10 users and Business at $299/month with unlimited users (accessed 2026-09-24); vendor list prices do not demonstrate adoption or WTP. Company size and software stack remain uncertain.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree that siding is product-specific and installation quality is tied to site conditions and instructions. Marketing pages emphasize speed and close rates; manufacturer guidance stresses codes, installation methods and trained contractors. The seven-to-fourteen-day duration applies only to James Hardie's average-sized-home projects. Fibercement handling and wind/code methods vary by product and locality. No representative industry-wide pricing, software stack, operator complaint rate or CRM WTP was established. Confidence is medium for project workflow, low/medium for software fit.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype property/elevation intake, material-system quote options, site-measure follow-up, crew/delivery scheduling, concealed-damage change orders, photo milestones, punch list and warranty/care handoff. Keep unit pricing, material catalog, product installation checklists, permitting, warranty, insurance claim, crew qualifications and weather buffers tenant-controlled. Ask repair vs replacement, cladding type, supplier responsibility, number of stories, access and expected permit/HOA review. Research deeper regional material/code variation before enforcing defaults.
