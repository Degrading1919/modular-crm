## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** “Hurricane prep service” appears to describe a seasonal/property service that executes a customer's readiness plan: secure outdoor items, close or coordinate existing shutters, clear drainage/debris, document property condition, and sometimes visit/report after landfall or coordinate licensed vendors. South Florida providers target snowbirds, vacant homes and property investors, documenting work with photos ([Palm Beach Property Services](https://pbcpropertyservices.com/hurricane-prep/), [NeverAbsent](https://neverabsent.com/services/hurricane-storm-prep/), [Seaside Solutions](https://www.seasidesolutionsfl.com/services/hurricane-preparation)). FEMA's homeowner checklist supports preparedness tasks but does not define a contractor industry ([FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/plan/prevent/nhp/hurdam.pdf)).

**Uncertain:** The candidate label does not specify geographic scope, whether the operator is a home-watch concierge, shutter deployment crew, property manager, landscaper, or emergency contractor. These models overlap and may have different licensing/insurance/liability. Post-storm assessment is not structural inspection, mitigation, insurance adjustment, or restoration. Profile is partial because operator model and season economics lack independent evidence; keep candidate distinct from installation and remediation.

## Business model and customer segments

- Likely customer: absent seasonal homeowner, second-home owner, vacation-rental host/property manager, HOA, or business owner. Operator may act as local trusted contact and execute/checklist/report rather than sell repair construction.
- Possible tasks include closing existing approved shutters/removable panels, storing outdoor furniture, securing loose items, clearing gutters/drains, moving items indoors, generator/system checks, photos, water shutoff by authorization, and post-storm visual walk-through. Each operator sets scope; several South Florida provider pages list overlapping but non-identical menus.
- Pre-storm demand is weather-triggered and capacity-constrained; home-watch subscription may provide year-round relationship/priority. Operators state advance relationship improves priority when forecasts drive demand ([Palm Beach Property Services](https://pbcpropertyservices.com/hurricane-prep/)).
- Company structures vary from one-person home-watch/concierge to insured property services firm with employees, partner trades and vendor coordination. Geography limits operations; storm surge travel, access closures and evacuation orders affect safe service.
- Avoid unlicensed structural, electrical, roofing, generator repair, mold remediation or insurance adjusting claims. Verify jurisdictional rules, trade licenses, contract authority and safety.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms: storm plan, readiness checklist, named-storm trigger, shutter deployment, property watch, absentee owner, pre-storm photos, post-storm walk-through, vendor coordination, emergency access and safe return.
- **Service-location data:** property address, owner/authorized contact, access/key procedure, existing shutter type/operation, exterior items, drainage, generator/shutoff, known hazards, HOA/condo rules, storm plan and local evacuation/access information.
- **Persistent customer assets:** property, owner authorization, keys/access credentials, shutter/window inventory, emergency contacts, generator or critical systems, vendor network, recurring home-watch plan and history. Keep key codes highly restricted.
- Safety-critical records: owner authority for shutoffs/moving property, approved safe shutter operation, evacuation/road closure, staff check-in, scope limits, pre/post photos, and limits of visual inspection.
- Job inputs: storm name/forecast trigger, property-specific checklist, owner go/no-go, task completion, conditions found and timestamped photo evidence.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Work may include seasonal preparedness visit, named-storm activation, shutter closure/removal/staging, outdoor property securing, drainage/yard clearing, generator check, pre-storm photo inventory, post-storm visual condition report, vendor coordination and minor maintenance. Separate permanent shutter installation and licensed repair.
- Intake needs property/address, owner and emergency contact, authorization limits, absence dates, shutter type/training, service checklist, equipment/site access, pets/occupants, high-risk systems, trigger policy, spending cap, vendor permissions and communication preference.
- Quote may be per property/checklist or visit, annual home-watch membership plus event activation, travel/emergency premium or hourly task. Public provider prices are mostly request-a-quote; one Florida concierge publishes $50/hour for shopping, not hurricane prep, so do not borrow it as price evidence ([Royal Tides](https://www.royaltidesconcierge.com/services)).
- Booking may be planned in advance, then activated by forecast/owner approval. Self-service should record preference and authorization, not promise exact pre-landfall attendance.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Inputs likely include property size/number of openings, shutter operation/removable panels, distance, labor, stairs/access, furniture volume, drain/gutter scope, standby duration, post-storm visit, emergency priority and vendor coordination. These are inference from menus and customer needs.
- Could be annual recurring home-watch contract plus per-event service, or one-off pre-storm service. Providers offer home watch/home management and hurricane prep as adjacent offerings; no universal pattern established.
- No verified hurricane-prep service price card or direct operator WTP for software found. Customer service rates/software prices remain unsupported. Never infer software budget from property value.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Home watch may recur weekly/biweekly/monthly; hurricane prep is seasonal/forecast-triggered; post-storm inspection depends on safe access. FEMA guidance stresses preparing before the storm, and operator sites tell owners to plan before forecast pressure ([FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/plan/prevent/nhp/hurdam.pdf), [Palm Beach Property Services](https://pbcpropertyservices.com/hurricane-prep/)).
- Assign staff by property familiarity, shutter skill, authorization, service radius and safety. One storm creates a sharp workload surge and route/capacity conflicts.
- Dispatch needs trigger/approval state, priority by contract, estimated arrival, access logistics, evacuation/safety stop conditions, completion evidence, and truthful “unable to reach” status. Local road closure and evacuation orders override service plans.
- Return visit can be post-storm condition check or deferred work; never dispatch staff into unsafe conditions.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Preseason property inventory/owner authorization → create checklist and access plan → monitor official alerts/owner trigger → triage requests/capacity → staff check-in → perform authorized tasks → time-stamped photos/checklist → notify owner → cease work/evacuate as conditions require → after safe access, inspect visible conditions/report → refer repairs to appropriately licensed vendor.
- Record before/after photos, shutter status, outdoor items secured, drains clear, utility status only if authorized, exceptions, access attempts, time and staff. Do not claim the property is “hurricane-proof.”
- Safety includes no work under unsafe winds, flooding, lightning or official evacuation restriction; ladder/shutter/electrical hazards; personal protective equipment and check-in/out. FEMA guidance is for homeowner preparation, not contractor authorization.
- Noncompletion: owner did not authorize, property inaccessible, storm arrived before dispatch, staff evacuation/safety, shutters jammed/unsafe, missing key, HOA restriction or task requires licensed trade. Define charge/billability transparently.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Absentee owners need advance plan, service confirmation, priority rules, access notices, photos, no-entry exceptions, storm-event status, post-storm visit and vendor estimates/contact. Include honest limits if area becomes inaccessible.
- Portal could retain plan, contacts, property photos, invoices and preferences. Customer may activate checklist or approve extra work; sensitive access and vendor authority must not be exposed.
- Automated messages should communicate verified status, not disaster predictions, safe condition, insurance outcome, or guaranteed response.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Staff may use ladders, panel storage/labels, hand tools, tie-downs, yard bags, radios/phones, flashlights and property-specific supplies. Equipment list depends on whether tasks include shutters, landscape cleanup, generators or vendor coordination.
- Small operators may have owner plus vetted subcontractors. Larger providers need home-watch staff, storm-response crews, dispatch, licensed partner vendors, on-call policy, safety training, keys/vehicles and incident reporting.
- Keep vendor credentials and service authorization separate; storm restoration contractors are not automatically part of the prep provider.

## Reporting and operating measures

- Candidate KPIs: properties with current plans, seasonal renewal, forecast-trigger response time, tasks completed before deadline, unable-to-access rate, checklist/photo completeness, staff safety stop, emergency vendor coordination and customer complaint/claim rate.
- No common reporting standard found. Distinguish property visual observations from engineering/insurance assessments.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- Providers describe property-watch or concierge operations and some use proprietary software for dated/photo/GPS inspection reports; home-watch company Serenity says each visit produces report with photos, timestamps and GPS coordinates ([Serenity](https://www.serenitypropertiesfl.com/)). Other vendors advertise owner portal or request forms. This is direct evidence of at least one tool claim, not a market stack.
- Operator pages agree on shutters, securing items and photo documentation; they differ on post-storm checks, drainage, water shutoff, generator, repair coordination and geographic promise ([NeverAbsent](https://neverabsent.com/services/hurricane-storm-prep/), [Seaside Solutions](https://www.seasidesolutionsfl.com/services/hurricane-preparation), [1st Integrity](https://1stintegrityps.com/storm-prep/)).
- Customer voices ask for shutter installation/deployment and post-event service; no robust operator forum complaint or software price/WTP evidence found ([South Florida request](https://www.reddit.com/r/SouthFlorida/comments/1nb1qev/service_to_put_up_take_down_hurricane_shutters/)).
- Current stack may include home-watch inspection software, scheduling/dispatch, maps, phone/text, photo storage, accounting and contractor referral network; mostly inference.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Provider sources agree on advance planning, securing property and documenting status, but scope varies materially. Some offer post-storm observation and vendor coordination; others limit to pre-storm checklist. FEMA provides homeowner preparedness guidance, not proof of an outsourced market. Profile is partial because customer/operator segment and independent evidence for business economics and tooling are thin. Geographic evidence is South Florida-heavy; do not imply service need in non-hurricane regions. Confidence medium for the observed service menu, low for prevalence and software needs.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a property-specific plan and authorization record, named-storm activation queue, checklist/photos, access/no-entry states, staff safety stop and verified customer report. Keep tasks, trigger, prioritization, post-storm scope, vendor permissions, shutter types, membership and geography configurable. Ask operators whether they are home-watch firms, shutter crews, property managers or contractors; what trigger authorizes mobilization; and what they will not do. Keep repair/restoration/referral work in separate service boundaries.
