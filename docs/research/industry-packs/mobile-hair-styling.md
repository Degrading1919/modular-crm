## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Mobile hair styling includes blowouts, curls, updos, bridal/event looks, trials, wedding-party services, and touch-ups delivered at a home, hotel, venue, or production site. Bridal providers publish service menus with separate trials, per-person rates, travel, destination packages, early calls, additional artists, and booking retainers; a Reddit wedding-party discussion reports same-day group demand, quote variability, and that multiple artists may be needed. Texas' mobile-establishment rules require services to occur inside the licensed vehicle and specify water/restroom/itinerary requirements, while California provides mobile unit and personal-service permit pathways; off-site house calls cannot be assumed lawful everywhere.

**Inference:** The pack should treat event readiness and group timeline as core for bridal work while allowing routine on-location styling/occasion appointments. It should separate styling menu/time from haircut, color, chemical treatment, and makeup services. The important CRM loop is inquiry/date hold → style/trial → event schedule/artist allocation → venue-ready setup → service/touch-up → settlement and photos with consent.

## Business model and customer segments

Customers include wedding parties, formal-event clients, photo/film productions, performers, destination/event coordinators, and individuals preferring service at home/hotel. Revenue may be per style, bridal package, per-party member, minimum booking, or custom day rate. Solo stylists may stay close to one event/day; teams/assistants increase group capacity. Demand clusters around weddings/proms/holiday and peak weekend dates, but seasonality differs by climate and event market. Hair styling is labor and skill dependent: curly/textured hair, extensions, accessories, and long-wear bridal styles may require specialist experience.

## Terminology and durable records

Use client, event/venue, look/style, trial, service/person, lead stylist/assistant, ready-by schedule, touch-up, and booking/retainer. Site fields: getting-ready room, outlet/power, lighting/mirror/chair, parking/load-in, access restrictions, venue schedule, travel, water/shampoo availability where relevant, and styling stations. Persistent client preference/history may include hair length/texture, style reference/photo, extensions/accessories, product sensitivities, and prior trial notes, with consent and restricted sensitivity fields. Per-appointment hair condition, washing status, styling product and requested modifications are job inputs. Do not store medical/scalp diagnoses or equate styling with haircut/color work.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Possible services: blowout, curls/waves, updo, braids, half-up/half-down, bridal day-of, trial, wedding-party styling, extension/accessory placement, second look, on-site touch-up, and production day rate. Intake captures occasion/date, location(s), ready-by/photo/ceremony time, headcount/service per person, hair length/texture/desired look, inspiration photos, extensions/accessories, wash/prep requirements, trial preference, setup needs, travel/parking, and lead contact. Provider may ask for trials weeks/months ahead. Quote can be per person/service plus travel/early start/additional artist; group minimum or date minimum may make a one-person peak Saturday uneconomic. Include exact scope and identity/number of stylists; a group timeline should be reviewed manually before contract/retainer. A request combining haircut/color/chemical treatment should route to licensed menu or separate provider.

## Pricing and commercial model

Price drivers include style complexity and duration, bridal vs party rate, trial, party size/minimum, hair length/texture/extensions, travel/multiple locations, early call, assistant count, touch-up hours, destination days and parking. One Southern California bridal artist lists bridal styling from $550, hair-only wedding-party service at $200/person, and a bridal trial at $300; a Montreal customer thread reports separate local quotes of approximately CAD $350 bridal hair and $200 trial plus possible travel. A client discussion of mobile bridal styling describes a two-artist/group quote around $400 and a $250 trial, another local anecdote; not comparable rates. **Software willingness-to-pay:** no direct stylist software purchase/WTP was found. Vagaro (US list $23.99/month base plus $10 per extra bookable calendar, vendor pricing page) and GlossGenius ($24/$48/$148 billed annually) are incumbent list-price signals for salons/beauty teams, not evidence of mobile bridal stylist spending.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Trials and event day are distinct appointments linked to one booking. Event plan needs service duration by guest, artist assignment, setup time, travel, order of service, ready-by deadline, venue schedule, contingency, and possible second location/touch-up. Multiple stylists may work concurrently but may need different stations/outlets and coordination with makeup/photo teams. A single in-demand stylist may reserve only one wedding/day. For ordinary blowouts/styling, exact appointments and travel buffers apply. Weather, traffic, early start, wedding party lateness, missing participants, room access and timeline changes can break dispatch; preserve manual edit/approval rather than silently reflow promised service times.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm booking, look/trial notes, guest list, prep/wash expectation, venue access, setup equipment, outlet/light, timeline, and assigned artist. Arrive/setup, verify client/look, use clean tools/capes and appropriate products, provide styling, record product/accessory changes and time deviations, obtain client acceptance, complete touch-up/checklist and leave work area clear. Capture optional result photo only with permission. Texas sanitation guidance calls for clean/disinfected combs, brushes, scissors and clean towels/capes, and excludes service on contagious conditions; requirements differ by state and service. Mobile establishment vs service in client's home follows local law, not the business's generic booking status. Noncompletion reasons: no legal/off-site permission, venue/setup inaccessible, client unprepared/late, timeline infeasible, missing artist, unsuitable hair condition/product reaction, equipment/power failure, weather/traffic, out-of-scope haircut/color, or cancellation. Re-style/correction, refund, and delay remedy depend on agreement.

## Customer communication and self-service

Messages should clarify booking hold/retainer, trial booking, hair prep, desired look/photo references, guest schedule, arrival/setup, ready-by time, artist roster, travel fees, delay/change, final balance, and review/photo opt-in. Portal can hold contract, per-person style preference, trial notes, event schedule and invoice. Avoid sending private event venue or getting-ready room/security details outside assigned team. Human review is needed when party size/timing changes, style/material expectations change, or makeup/hair bundling changes who is responsible for which service.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Styling kit may include dryer, hot tools, brushes, combs, clips, pins, elastics, extension tools, products, heat protection, sanitation supplies, extension/accessory storage, portable chair/mirror/light, extension cords, and event touch-up kit. Track kit/assets for larger teams, not as a default inventory burden for solo stylists. Workforce can include lead/associate artists, assistants, contractors, and event coordinator; skills by texture/style/extension and travel/time should inform assignments. Tips/commission and payroll export depend on business model. Setup should meet electrical load and venue rules; stylist should not promise chemical treatments or haircut services beyond qualifications.

## Reporting and operating measures

Inferred metrics: inquiry/date conversion, wedding-party size, revenue/style and artist-hour, travel/setup share, on-time ready-by completion, average time by style/texture/length, trial-to-event conversion, cancellation/retainer, rework, peak-date occupancy, and source/referrals. No reviewed incumbent exposed a canonical mobile-hair-styling KPI standard.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Vagaro and GlossGenius manage calendars, online booking, client records/forms, notifications, payment, and team resources; Booksy and general event CRM HoneyBook can support booking/client/project contracts. Bridal artist websites show custom proposals, contracts, trials, travel fees and event planning rather than simple self-booking. A Reddit event-client thread reports being unable to find availability and seeing differing trial/bridal quotes; team replies explain capacity, seasonality, specialization and why multiple styles require multiple artists. These are anecdotes. Common stack may combine Instagram/portfolio website, project/contract CRM, beauty calendar, payment and spreadsheet event run-of-show; no representative operator survey found. Plan prices are vendor list prices, not user-reported software purchases.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Evidence is strongest for bridal/event packages; everyday mobile styling may have shorter bookings and different pricing. Some providers charge per person, some custom package/minimum; local travel-fee expectations differ. State rules can distinguish mobile vehicles, permitted home service, and salon-based work. Validate local rules for styling-only, cutting and chemical services separately. Anonymous wedding discussions do not establish sector-wide demand, price tolerance, or software pain. Product WTP is not evidenced.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask which styling menu is offered, whether the business also cuts/color/styles chemically, event vs everyday mix, date/headcount/deadline, trial and retainer policy, service setting/jurisdiction, travel range, texture/extension expertise, and whether a team is available. Prototype event inquiry, trial-to-event association, client/party schedule and artist assignment, travel/minimum pricing, setup checklist, payment milestones, arrival and final sign-off. Keep licensing, sanitation process, artist roster, rates, photo consent and re-style policy configurable.
