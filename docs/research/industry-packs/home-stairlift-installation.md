## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Residential stairlift installation is a home-access equipment sale and fit job built around a survey of the particular stairs/user, selection of straight or curved rail/equipment, installation, safety checks, and post-install service. Manufacturers say in-home assessment determines fit and quote, installation can be completed in hours for some models, and authorized dealers handle curved installations/service ([Stannah](https://www.stannah.com/en-us/stairlifts/installation), [Bruno](https://www.bruno.com/stairlifts/guide)). ASME A18.1 covers chairlift design, construction, installation, operation, inspection, testing, maintenance, and repair; local adoption and product instructions matter.

**Inference:** The high-value workflow is an accessible, careful sales/survey-to-install-to-reliable-aftercare record, with home/stair dimensions, user needs/preferences, model/rail configuration, equipment serial/warranty, accessibility, and urgent breakdown history. Poor fit, installation fault, or delayed response can have serious mobility consequences. The exact scope excludes elevators and platform lifts. Evidence includes manufacturer claims and user experiences but little independent installer-operator software evidence, so status is partial.

## Business model and customer segments

- Buyers are homeowners/families supporting an older adult or person with mobility limitations; referral channels may include caregivers, occupational therapists, aging-in-place professionals, home-access dealers, and discharge planners. Referral channel mix not established.
- Typical product variants include straight or curved indoor stairlifts and outdoor models. Services may include survey, quote, installation, annual service, repair, warranty, rental/reconditioned equipment, removal/relocation, and battery/rail checks, depending on provider.
- Triggers include difficulty using stairs, post-injury/disability, aging in place, and caregiver safety concerns. Urgency can be high when a person cannot safely access a bedroom or bathroom. Geography depends on dealer service territory and local after-sales support.
- Providers range from manufacturer networks and specialist accessibility dealers to independent mobility/home-improvement firms. Curved stairs and unusual landings may require custom rail configuration and experienced survey/install teams; model coverage varies.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms: stairlift/stair lift, stairway chairlift, carriage, rail/track, straight/curved stair, landing, folding seat/footrest/arms, swivel seat, obstruction sensor, charging point, call/send remote, service call, and authorized dealer.
- Location data: home address, stair type/turns/landings, stair width/clearance/headroom, step/rail dimensions, power outlet/charging position, indoor/outdoor exposure, installation access, and household/landlord/building approval when relevant.
- Durable asset is the installed stairlift: manufacturer/model, serial, straight/curved rail details, install date, warranty and service-plan dates, safety test/inspection records, charger/battery, customer manual, and fault/repair history. User mobility diagnosis is not required to service the equipment; collect only accommodations necessary for fit/safe use.
- Sensitive information includes home layout/access photos, disability/health details, and emergency contact; minimize collection and restrict technician access. Safety-critical fit/operation records and customer training should be retained.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Common jobs: in-home survey/fit assessment, straight/curved equipment installation, outdoor lift install, commissioning/user instruction, preventive service, emergency repair, reconditioned unit fit, removal, and warranty/parts work.
- Intake should ask stair type/shape and floors/landings, approximate dimensions/photos, indoor/outdoor, intended rider access needs/transfer considerations, preferred contact/caregiver, home access, existing lift/model/symptoms, power availability, urgency, and landlord/HOA/building permission if relevant. Avoid requesting diagnosis details unless needed by a trained assessor and the customer chooses to share.
- Quote is tailored after survey; manufacturer pages explicitly avoid one-size-fits-all pricing. Inputs include straight vs curved/rail length, turns/landings, model/options, installation complexity, used/new unit, travel, aftercare plan, and removal/repair. No current public customer service price confirmed.
- Booking is usually survey appointment followed by installation window and later service call. Provider says installations can take several hours for some products; curved stairs/complex site or construction modifications change timing. Public quote form/photo intake should lead to in-home survey rather than automatic final quote.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Product+installation package, custom rail/configuration, reconditioned equipment, warranty, and optional service plan are possible; public pricing varies by model and geometry. Stannah says quote follows in-home survey. Manufacturer warranty terms differ by model and region.
- Customer may pay directly or via third-party funding/benefit arrangements, but source set does not establish prevalence. Deposits, financing, cancellation, warranty, and annual service plan terms vary by provider.
- No software price or verified stairlift installer software WTP evidence found. Consumer lift price, warranty, or repair cost cannot imply software willingness to pay.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Installation is one-time; maintenance is periodic/condition-based and breakdown repair is reactive. Bruno recommends annual inspection/clean/lubrication for a curved model, while straight model guidance describes basic user maintenance; model instructions differ, so no universal cadence.
- Capacity depends on trained dealer availability, surveyor/installer travel, rail/product readiness, customer/caregiver schedule, and need for urgent restoration of access. Install may require two-person handling or model-specific experience; evidence is insufficient for standard crew size.
- Rescheduled surveys/installations may create accessibility hardship. A nonfunctional lift may require priority response and safe alternative guidance, but do not advise transfers or emergency evacuation automatically; involve the customer/caregiver and emergency services where appropriate.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Workflow: listen to access goal → survey stairs and user/transfer needs → validate model/rail fit and quote → obtain permissions/site readiness → deliver/fit rail and carriage per manufacturer instructions → connect/test charging and safety features → test full travel and obstruction devices → instruct customer/caregiver and hand over manual/service details → record installation and warranty → schedule or respond to service.
- Records may include stair measurements, rail configuration, installed serial/model, before/after photos, safety test/commissioning, customer training/acceptance, service plan, and installed condition. Customer may have mobility limitations affecting how user training is delivered; assess respectfully.
- ASME A18.1 covers chairlift lifecycle safety. Installation/service may require trained or authorized dealer personnel per manufacturer (Bruno says only authorized dealers install curved indoor lift). Electrical and building permissions are jurisdiction-specific. ADA accessibility guidance addresses platform lifts/stairway chairlifts for public facilities; a private-home stairlift should not be treated as the same compliance pathway as public building access equipment.
- Noncompletion reasons: rail/model not suitable for staircase, insufficient clearance, electrical/power issue, unsafe stair condition, permissions absent, part delay, or customer not ready. A faulty fit, obstruction sensor, emergency call function, or service delay can be high-impact; escalate promptly and document remedy.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Customer needs a clear survey appointment and preparation list, explanation of fit and price options, installer identity/ETA, household access/safety preparation, operating instructions, warranty/maintenance schedule, and emergency/repair contact.
- Website photos/virtual assessment may start qualification, but manufacturer and dealer sources favor in-home survey for accurate fit. Caregiver can coordinate at customer's direction. Provide accessible language and avoid pressure sales, given mobility need.
- Automated service reminders can be useful if model guidance calls for inspection; urgent fault reports need human triage. Do not claim lift is safe to operate before technician checks; protect personal health details.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Equipment includes model-specific rail/track, seat/carriage, remotes, batteries/charger, footrest/arms, switches/sensors, and repair components. Curved rail may be configured to staircase; stock and lead time vary.
- Personnel may include assessor/sales consultant, installer, service technician, and manufacturer-authorized dealer. Training/authorization is manufacturer- and product-specific. A caregiver/occupational therapist may advise but is not the installer.
- Track product/rail configuration and serial-specific warranty/parts; maintenance supplies and common components may be regional. No common payroll/time-pay model is evidenced.

## Reporting and operating measures

- Incumbents do not publish a common KPI set. Candidate measures (inference): survey-to-quote time, install completion on first visit, fit/safety commissioning pass, response time on mobility-critical outage, repeat failure, warranty callback, service-plan retention, and customer training completion.
- Segment by straight/curved/outdoor, manufacturer/model, geography, lead source, install/repair, urgency, and warranty status. Do not use patient health diagnoses as ordinary performance dimensions.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- Stannah promotes a customer/service portal with equipment details, service contracts, invoices, service records, and parts. Manufacturer/dealer networks likely use order/service tools, but the source set cannot establish common software across independent dealers.
- First-person forum and review evidence describes stair geometry that defeated an initial installer assessment, varied response/service experiences, reliability and fit concerns, and costly/pressured quote experiences. A 2026 consumer forum report describes failures and weak follow-up; a 2012 UK MS Society post reports an installer unable to fit a curved rail after quoting. These are serious but anecdotal and not representative rates.
- No verified installer-owner testimony about business software complaints, workarounds, switching, or software budget found. No product software pricing found.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree on tailored in-home survey, model-specific fit, installation, safety, and aftercare. User sources report contrasting outcomes: a successful installation/rapid response versus misfit, repeated breakdown, and poor follow-up. User experiences are regional, dated, and product-specific; they identify failure modes but do not measure frequency. A18.1 and ADA apply in distinct ways: the standards guide chairlift design/maintenance, while ADA platform-lift scoping for public access is not equivalent to an individual's home purchase. Funding, regulation and service-plan practices are unresolved by geography.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype respectful survey intake and scheduling, a staircase configuration, product/serial/warranty asset history, installation and safety-checklist, customer/caregiver instruction and signed handoff, and priority repair handling. Keep product family, fit criteria, authorization, pricing, service interval, warranty plan, financing, permits, and consent/preferences configurable. Ask whether the business sells, installs, services, or rents lifts; whether it handles straight, curved, platform, or other access systems; and its response coverage. Preserve personal health data minimization and human confirmation for safety-critical status.
