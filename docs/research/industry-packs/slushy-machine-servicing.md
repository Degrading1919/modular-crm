## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Specialized and general foodservice equipment companies service slush/frozen beverage machines with preventive maintenance, sanitation/cleaning, diagnostics, repair, calibration, installation, and parts. Equipment configurations and models matter. Food-contact cleaning may be frequent, while deeper disassembly intervals are model/product-specific; refrigeration/sealed-system work can require specialized certification in the US ([Beverage Medic](https://beveragemedic.com/frozen-beverage-machine-service/), [Taylor UK service](https://www.taylor-company.co.uk/service/), [ADRIUM](https://adriumservice.com/services/commercial-slush-machine-repair/)).

**Inference:** The distinct product loop is customer/site + machine model/serial/history → triage/urgency → qualified technician, model-specific parts and service instructions → on-site repair/maintenance → verification and record. The candidate is a narrow equipment service line that may be one part of a wider beverage/food-equipment business. Evidence supports workflows, but not service prevalence, software complaints, typical WTP, or a single maintenance interval; status is partial.

## Business model and customer segments

- Customers include convenience stores, bars, cafes, restaurants/QSR, resorts, entertainment venues, dessert shops, and operators leasing equipment. Service providers may be independent technicians, beverage distributors, manufacturer-authorized networks, or multi-category refrigeration/equipment repair businesses.
- Job types include corrective service (not freezing, auger/drive failure, leak, temperature/product consistency issue), preventative maintenance, cleaning/sanitization, installation, calibration, and replacement of seals/blades/drive parts. Not all providers perform sealed-system repairs.
- Purchase triggers include breakdown during service, hygiene/inspection concerns, seasonal/high-volume use, preventive upkeep, new installation, and lease coverage. A bar-owner discussion notes bars sometimes rent machines with supplier repair responsibility, but this is anecdotal ([Reddit bar owners](https://www.reddit.com/r/bartenders/comments/133yjgm/frozen_drink_machines/)).
- Geography matters for response times and service coverage; model/brand specialization and technician credentials matter. Operators span local one-person service to authorized distributor networks. No market-size evidence found.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms include slush/slushy, frozen beverage, granita, frozen carbonated beverage (FCB), barrel, hopper, auger/beater, scraper blade, seal kit, gearmotor, coupling, dispense valve, condenser, refrigeration sealed system, cleaning/sanitizing, and preventive maintenance.
- Site data: business name/location, contact/access, service window/after-hours availability, machine position/utilities, foodservice operating conditions, and any access constraints.
- A durable customer asset is each machine: make/model, serial number, barrel/tank count, refrigeration/electrical specs when documented, install date, lease/warranty status, service history, parts changed, and maintenance interval from manufacturer guidance. Product mix and symptoms are job inputs, not persistent equipment identities.
- Food-contact and refrigeration safety records may be important: cleaning/sanitizing evidence, temperature/performance verification, refrigerant handling, certification, and parts/warranty. Sensitivity and required documents vary by jurisdiction/contract; follow exact model manual.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Service examples: scheduled maintenance, cleaning/sanitizing, emergency breakdown, diagnosis/repair, model-specific component replacement, calibration, installation, and lease-supplied service. Restaurant deep cleaning is separate and does not service machine mechanics by default.
- Intake should request equipment brand/model/serial or photo, number of barrels, symptom, error code, when issue began, product/mix context, downtime/business impact, last cleaning/service, location, and preferred access window. Soda Medic explicitly invites the caller to describe symptoms and what changed if exact issue is unknown.
- Quotes may be diagnostic/dispatch fee plus labor/parts, scheduled contract, manufacturer/distributor plan, or lease-included service. ADRIUM posts a $99 diagnostic credited toward repair and best-effort response; Liquid Assets lists $135 for a service offering. These are individual 2026 observed customer-service prices, US providers, and not industry averages.
- Same/next-day or after-hours work may be offered because lost product sales/downtime create urgency; advertised targets are provider-specific. Self-service should collect model and symptom, then route uncertain or potentially unsafe repair to a technician.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Evidence-backed price inputs include diagnostic/travel, urgency/time, labor, machine type/brand/model, fault, parts, sealed-system versus mechanical work, PM/cleaning scope, and whether service is included in a lease or plan. Market/regional differences are likely but not measured.
- Preventative maintenance plans and aftercare packages are offered by some providers. Manufacturer UK servicing lists quarterly health checks and replacement kits; this does not establish US cadence or all machine families.
- Recorded customer service prices: ADRIUM $99 diagnostic credited to repair (Bay Area, accessed 2026-09-24); Liquid Assets lists $135 on a repair/maintenance service page (Las Vegas, accessed 2026-09-24). These may be outdated/offer-specific and are not software pricing.
- **Software WTP:** No verified service-provider software price or operator purchasing statement found. Do not infer software WTP from repair fees, lost sales, or machine cost.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Food-contact touch-point cleaning may be daily/through shift while full disassembly/brush cleaning intervals depend on machine, product, and manual. Taylor guidance gives model/product-specific intervals; Taylor UK offers a technician cleaning service at a stated interval for one product line. Do not impose this on all slush machines.
- Preventive visits may be quarterly or contract-based; breakdown dispatch is urgent and may be after-hours. Client scheduling should consider service area, technician certification/model experience, parts availability, machine operating hours, and urgency.
- A Montreal-area specialist says office staff route technicians based on calls received. This is direct operator description but one firm, not standard practice ([Servislush+](https://www.servislush.ca/en/)).
- Reschedules may need a temporary workaround or escalation; avoid implying a machine is food-safe/ready without technician verification.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Lifecycle: collect machine/symptom details → confirm coverage/model and urgency → triage and assign competent technician → arrive, isolate power as needed, inspect and diagnose → repair/clean according to manufacturer instructions → test temperature, freezing/drive/dispense/leaks → document parts and next maintenance → advise operator and close work order.
- Documentation may include photos, model/serial, readings, repair actions, parts, cleaning steps, and return-to-service verification. Taylor manufacturer materials offer operator manuals and training; model-specific instructions prevail.
- Hazards include electricity, moving augers, hot/cold components, food contamination, refrigerant/sealed systems, chemicals, and heavy equipment. US EPA certification requirements apply to technicians handling regulated refrigerants in covered work; exact rules and state/local food sanitation rules need jurisdiction-specific review. Do not make restaurant cleaning staff responsible for mechanical or sealed-system repair by default.
- Noncompletion might be no access, incorrect model/part, no fault reproduced, unsafe condition, or specialist/part required. This is a proposed set, not a source-established taxonomy. Return trips depend on diagnosis/parts/contract.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Customers need a clear symptom intake and response window, ETA/delay, parts approval/estimate, expected downtime, safe-use guidance, test result, invoice/plan reminder, and maintenance due notice.
- Phone/text remain useful for urgent calls. Service request forms can capture model/photo and symptom without requiring the customer to diagnose. Machine history and service reports help multi-site facility teams.
- Human review is required before safety, sanitation, or readiness claims and when sealed-system work/food-contact concerns arise. Do not automate a “fixed” status until technician verification.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Technician van stock may include model-specific seals, gaskets, blades, o-rings, cleaning brushes/sanitizer, valves, gear components, and diagnostics; compressor/refrigeration parts may require sourcing. Operators advertise common spares but stocking patterns vary.
- Workforce may include trained appliance/refrigeration technicians, authorized distributor technicians, and beverage equipment specialists. EPA credentials apply where regulated refrigerant work requires; factory authorization, state contractor rules, and food safety requirements are equipment/region-specific.
- Tie parts to make/model compatibility and technician skill. Track replaced parts, warranty, and service interval. No broad evidence on technician pay, payroll export, mileage, or subcontracting.

## Reporting and operating measures

- No shared KPI standard found. Candidate metrics (inference): first-visit resolution, response time by severity, downtime duration, repeat/callback rate, PM completion, asset availability, parts usage, service contract renewal, and machine count by model/location.
- Multi-site customers may need machine-level history and site-level service coverage. Segment by brand/model, call type, technician, geography, contract/lease, and downtime severity.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a machine asset record and model/photo-led intake; diagnostic/repair/PM job types; technician qualification and urgency routing; model-specific checklist attachments; parts/warranty, readings, before/after, function verification, and service history. Keep maintenance intervals, covered brands, repair scope, contract/warranty handling, after-hours service, sanitation instructions, and jurisdictional credentials tenant-configurable. Ask whether the company is independent, distributor-authorized, or equipment lessor; which brands it serves; and what repair/cleaning it performs. Do not fold premises cleaning into this pack behavior.
