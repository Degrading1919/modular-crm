## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Fireplace projects include new factory-built gas/wood/pellet appliances and venting, inserts/replacements in existing masonry fireplaces, surrounds/mantels, hearths and masonry construction. Selection and installation depend on fuel, appliance listing, vent/chimney compatibility, clearances, floor protection, gas/electrical service, combustion air and finish. A fireplace retailer/installer may conduct site review and sales, while a mason, gas fitter, electrician, framer or GC owns parts of the project. Installation errors can create fire, gas, carbon monoxide and property-loss exposure; model manual and local code are central.

CRM opportunity: link customer/site to appliance make/model/serial, manual, approved venting/clearance plan, responsible trades, inspection, startup, owner instructions and warranty. Keep fireplace construction distinct from chimney cleaning/inspection, which has a separate recurring service loop. Sources support safety/workflow well; no fireplace contractor software stack, complaints or actual CRM WTP was verified.

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners, custom-home builders, remodelers, hearth retailers/dealers and property owners buy new installs, inserts, appliance replacement or surround/masonry upgrades. Residential dominates these sources; commercial/public projects may introduce engineered plans and formal procurement. Fuel options include gas, wood, pellet and electric; vented/direct-vent, factory-built and masonry configurations differ. Work may be a standalone dealer install, subcontract to GC, or larger custom masonry/renovation. Repair/service contracts can recur, but routine chimney inspection/sweep should remain distinguishable.

## Terminology and durable records

Records: property/location, firebox/opening, chimney/flue/vent, fuel, appliance model/serial/listing, vent kit, clearances, hearth/floor protection, gas/electric supply, combustion air, mantel/surround, drawings, installation manual, permit/inspection, installer certification, startup/test, warranty and service history. The appliance and venting are durable assets; opening measurements and concealed framing/firestop photos are project-specific but useful for later servicing. Store manuals and product-specific clearances by model/revision, not universal text.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Ask new build vs replacement/insert, fuel and preferred model, existing chimney/vent condition, opening and room dimensions, construction type, vent route/termination, gas/electric availability, hearth/mantel finish, home age/occupancy, access, permit/inspection and whether GC/gas fitter/electrician is engaged. A site review/inspection and product selection are often prerequisites for price. Quotes may split appliance, vent kit, hearth/surround, demolition/disposal, framing/masonry, fuel connection, electrical, permits, delivery, install and startup. Approval must identify included trades, model, finish and exclusions; change after rough opening or vent routing should trigger revised scope.

Scheduling: site review → model/plan approval → permit/material → framing/masonry and utility work → appliance/vent install → inspection/startup → finish/punch and handover. Web forms can qualify type/location/photos; avoid self-diagnosing unsafe chimneys.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

New builds align with framing, chimney, gas and finish sequence; retrofit scheduling depends on site inspection, vent materials, trade availability and household heat needs. Weather affects exterior chimney/roof work. New installation can lead to annual inspection/sweep or service, but that is a separate service workflow and may be outsourced. Noncompletion: unsuitable existing flue, clearance conflict, wrong model/vent, failed inspection, missing gas/electric, damaged surround or owner selection change. Record hold cause and responsible party.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Survey opening/chimney/utility → choose listed appliance and compatible vent → drawings/permit and trade plan → protect home/demolish as agreed → frame/hearth/clearances and vent/chimney work → install appliance and connections to exact manual/code → inspection and startup/leak/draft/operation tests by qualified parties → install finish/mantel with required clearances → photos, serial/manual, owner instruction, acceptance and warranty. NFI identifies gas/wood/pellet/hearth-design certifications and stresses qualified planning/install; local licensing can differ. CSIA's inspection protocols document the need to inspect existing fireplace/chimney when use or appliance changes. Hazards include fire, combustion gases, electrical/gas, masonry/silica, roof/fall and lifting. Never infer pass status from a checklist alone. Noncompletion/rework includes poor draft/venting, gas leak, inadequate clearances, smoke/soot, improper hearth, cracked masonry and failed inspection.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need model/fuel choices, vent route/appearance, scope/finish, access and outage, permits, arrival, test results, controls/care, warranty and annual maintenance advice/referral. Portal can collect photos and make/model preference, approve finish and store manual/service record. Safety issue/damaged chimney should be reviewed by qualified person, not an automated recommendation.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Inventory includes appliance, vent/liner, termination, firestop, hearth, mantel, masonry/finish, gas controls and accessories. Supplier/dealer compatibility and serial/lot data matter. Roles may include estimator, hearth designer/dealer, NFI-certified planner/installer, mason, gas fitter, electrician and GC. NFI certification is a recognized industry credential, not a blanket substitute for local license/permit. Store credential type/expiry only if business needs it.
