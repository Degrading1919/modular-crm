## Executive summary

**Observed evidence.** Home staging is a design and installation service bought by a seller or listing agent to prepare a property for market. Work ranges from occupied-home consultation/editing to partial or vacant staging. In a vacant inventory-heavy model, a stager sources and installs furniture/accessories, then removes them at sale or end of term. The core operational record is a one-time listing project: consult, photos/measures, room plan, proposal, delivery/install, rental term/extensions, destage and invoice. Inventory-based operators also track furniture/accessories, warehouse locations, condition and utilization across projects.

This is distinct from tenant/owner property operations and STR guest reservations. It is a transaction-triggered creative and physical-logistics project; inventory may be rented and reused. RESA submissions span occupied, vacant, partial and consult-only projects, but association ROI snapshots do not prove causation. Dedicated software packages project, inventory, proposal, invoice and crew workflows. The reported software list prices are signals, not general CRM WTP.

## Business model and customer segments

- Payers include seller/homeowner, listing agent/team, builder/developer or investor. RESA 2025 quarterly submissions show payer mix varies; NAR 2025 says agents value design quality and price in choosing a stager.
- Services: design consult, occupied declutter/rearrangement/accessorizing, partial staging, full vacant staging, furniture/accessory rental, install/destage, photo styling, extension, and sometimes virtual staging. Digital virtual staging has no physical inventory/crew loop and should remain a distinct service.
- Triggers include listing/photo/open-house date, seller move-out, property condition/layout, local buyer expectations, price tier and expected listing duration. Dates may shift after a sale or extension.
- Solo consultants can work with client-owned items; larger firms have warehouse stock, designers, movers, trucks and installation crews, with multiple concurrent projects.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms: client (seller/agent/builder), listing/property, staging project, consult, room plan, proposal, inventory item, pull list, install, rental term/extension, destage, warehouse, crew/mover and condition/damage.
- Location facts: address, property type/size/rooms, occupancy, access/loading restrictions, parking, listing/photo/install/destage dates, seller/agent, room priorities/style, measurements/photos and building/HOA requirements.
- Durable business-owned assets: furniture, rugs, lamps, artwork, decor and accessories with tag/ID, photo, dimensions, cost, warehouse/bin, availability, project reservation, condition/repair, utilization and replacement/sale state. Client-owned furnishings can be noted without becoming company stock.
- The listing project is not a permanently managed property record after close; room plans, scope, inventory assignment and site inputs are job records. Protect access details, private photos, pricing and internal item costs by role.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Intake asks source/decision-maker, address, occupied/vacant, listing/photo date, rooms, measurements/photos, style/audience, seller possessions, budget, access/loading, timeline, available furnishings, pets/occupants and agent coordination.
- Consult/occupied scope may be fixed-price or time-based. Partial/vacant estimates require a visit/photo review and depend on rooms/size, design, inventory, distance, labor, rental term, stairs/elevator and mover/truck needs. Proposal defines spaces/items, fee, term/extension, damage/loss, access, taxes and destage.
- Calendar commitments are consultation, install, photo-ready/open-house, extension and removal. Date changes are supported by vendor workflows, but frequency is unquantified. Review unknown dimensions, unavailable items, rush work, difficult access and out-of-area leads.
- Website should explain consult/occupied/vacant services, show portfolio, area and request form; photos are valuable intake. Avoid firm price promises from images alone.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Service price inputs: rooms/square footage, inventory volume/quality, design time, delivery/install/removal labor, distance, stairs/elevator, truck/movers, access, term/extensions, urgency and damage risk. Consult-only and vacant inventory models have different capital and labor economics.
- RESA's quarterly submitted-project average investment: **$3,588 Q1 2025**, **$4,387 Q2**, **$3,813 Q3**. These are different quarterly submission samples, not median prices, universal rates or individual operator revenue. Its 2025 vacant report page says 120 anonymized company responses and studies fees, margins, volume, inventory and storage; full data is paid.
- Billing may combine design/install fee and furniture rental by term, extension, transport/destage, deposit/authorization and damage/loss charges. Consult-only may be fixed/hourly. No universal fee or cancellation formula established.
- **Software WTP evidence:** Stageforce advertises Project/CRM Essentials **$49/month**, Inventory Essentials **$49/month starting**, Professional **$89/month starting**, with inventory count affecting price. Stager Tech lists **$79/month** inventory/projects and **$149/month** complete studio; its page says checkout is not enabled and setup is manual. Accessed 2026-09-24; software price is distinct from staging service fees.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Projects/milestones drive scheduling, not recurring customer routes. A stage may rent monthly and extend while the listing remains active; concurrent jobs reserve shared inventory.
- Dependencies: consult/measure → plan/proposal → approval → inventory reservation → warehouse pull/pack → crew/truck assignment → install → photo-ready → term end/extension → destage → condition check/warehouse return → invoice.
- Dispatch constraints include room plan, item dimensions, warehouse/bin, truck load, crew size, property access, loading windows, stairs/elevator and hard photo/open-house date. Routing matters for multiple installs/removals per day; solo consulting may need only calendar appointments.
- Seller access/date changes, sale contract, delayed photos, extension, cancellation, damage, missing inventory, mover delay and weather need explicit replan. Log extension revenue and item release date.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Consult: confirm authorized scope → photograph/measure → assess rooms/current furnishings → prepare concept/plan → send quote/term → obtain approval.
- Vacant install: reserve items → pull list/condition check → load → deliver and place → room checklist/photos/client signoff → mark item/project state and rental term. Destage: extend or schedule removal → inspect → note damage/missing item → return/restock/repair → final invoice.
- Occupied work needs consent before moving possessions. Crew safety includes lifting/loading, stairs, artwork/wall attachment and clear pathways. OSHA warehouse guidance identifies heavy lifting, awkward posture, pushing/pulling, repetitive work and storage/material-handling hazards; apply local worker requirements and company training.
- Rework includes fit/design issue, incomplete install, item damage/loss, missed photo deadline, unauthorized movement or access failure. Record corrective visit, responsibility, approval and billability.
- Useful proof: itemized pull/return list, room checklist, timestamped condition photos, crew notes, client acceptance and extension approval. Virtual imagery rights/disclosure are a separate workflow.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Client needs portfolio/process, consult availability, quote/plan approval, access window, install and photo-ready confirmation, extension/removal choice, invoice/payment and damage resolution. Realtor relationships/referrals matter, though prevalence was not quantified.
- Client portal can show proposal, dates, room plan, photos, extension and invoice. Homeowners do not need internal item cost/bin/crew routing.
- Crew needs day sheet, site/access, room placements, pull list, handling instructions, deadline and photo/issue capture. Human review before changing photo/listing deadlines.
- Do not auto-extend rental, authorize damage charges, choose design or publish private property photos without client approval/rights.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Vacant staging needs tagged stock, warehouse/bays, reservations, pull/return lists, condition/repair, sourcing, storage, truck and supplies. Occupied consult-only may own little inventory.
- RESA's 2025 vacant report explicitly covers inventory investment, storage, labor and sourcing. Stageforce/Stager Tech describe inventory states, warehouse, pull lists, condition/utilization, crew access, schedule and day sheets.
- Workforce may include designer, staging assistant, movers, install crew, warehouse manager and photographer. Third-party movers, payroll, time and subcontractor records are conditional.
- RESA operator interviews describe finance visibility, logistics, buying inventory, storage and staffing as growth concerns. These illustrate but do not measure prevalence.

## Reporting and operating measures

- Useful operator measures: project margin, quoted-to-won, install/destage capacity, inventory utilization/idle stock, item repair/loss, labor/delivery cost, on-time photo-ready rate, extension days, crew productivity, deposit/AR and referral repeat.
- RESA publishes staging investment, sale-to-list, days-on-market, ROI and payer type for submitted projects; these are not operator KPIs or causal proof.
- Report by project, agent/client, region, service type (consult/occupied/partial/vacant/virtual), room/item category, crew and period. Separate billed project revenue from inventory capital expense and rental-term revenue.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- Stageforce markets project/client relationships, inventory, proposals, invoice/billing and inventory-scaled tiers. Stager Tech markets projects, warehouse states, pull lists, contracts, invoices, crew day sheets, photos/timeline, client portal, booking and reports. This demonstrates an existing vertical software category, not feature adoption.
- Alternative stack can include general CRM/project tools, accounting, calendar, Drive/photos, inventory spreadsheet/barcode, design boards, e-sign/payment, email/text, movers and warehouse logs. This is a hypothesis from vendor scope and operator narratives; validate with interviews.
- Operator friction evidence: Sarah Lewis (RESA podcast) describes financial reporting/bookkeeper and warehouse/logistics challenges as she grew; Betsy Boughner recounts early inventory investment, storage constraints and reinvestment; a Boston stager job post sought a more integrated inventory system. These are first-person examples, not prevalence studies.
- A consumer Houzz discussion questions the value and storage/rental charges, contrasting professional association ROI claims. This illustrates buyer skepticism, not representative customer sentiment. RESA/NAR survey figures are submitted/self-reported and not causal evidence.
- Public software pricing accessed 2026-09-24: Stageforce $49/$49/$89 starts (inventory-scaled); Stager Tech $79/$149 monthly, with disclosed manual setup because checkout is not live.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Evidence supports one-time projects and a clear occupied/consult vs vacant inventory split. RESA quarterly self-reported investment averages vary ($3,588 Q1, $4,387 Q2, $3,813 Q3 2025), demonstrating sample/quarter variance. NAR/RESA agent and stager survey outcomes are not causal sales evidence. NAR notes quality and price matter to vendor choice; a consumer Houzz anecdote disputes perceived value/fees. Vendor pricing/features and operator stories show available workflows but not market share. Validate city, service type, occupied vs vacant, solo vs warehouse operator, digital vs physical staging, and whether adjacent event-rental firms share inventory needs.

Confidence is high for project lifecycle/inventory split; medium for capability fit/economics; low for generalized ROI, stack prevalence and buyer WTP.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype one-time project lifecycle: inquiry, consult/measures/photos, proposal, install date, crew day sheet, room checklist/photos, term extension, destage, item condition/return and project reconciliation. Ask if occupied-consult, vacant inventory rental, mixed or virtual; inventory ownership, concurrent capacity, warehouse/movers, deposit/quote policy and payer. Keep inventory/categories/quantities, rental term, damage clause, photo rights, service area and staffing configurable. Interview event/furniture/AV rental operators before turning inventory reservation into a shared core capability.
