# Furniture building (dorm, office, hospital, hotel, etc)

## Profile metadata

- **Industry key:** `commercial-furniture-installation`
- **Source name:** Furniture building (dorm, office, hospital, hotel, etc)
- **Source category:** Business Services
- **Candidate source:** [Businesses I Love](https://www.sweatystartup.com/blog/businesses-i-love), `Business Services`, position 14
- **Other exact/near duplicate labels:** `Carpenter` (`Trades/construction`, position 3) and niche carpentry entries are adjacent skills, not duplicates. This profile means commercial/institutional furniture fabrication and/or FF&E assembly/installation; it excludes generic residential carpentry and furniture retail.
- **Research date:** `2026-09-24`
- **Geographic scope:** US examples in Arizona, Texas, Georgia, and multi-region commercial vendor/manufacturer material. Healthcare, hospitality, education, and office requirements vary by facility and contract.
- **Research status:** partial

## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Commercial furniture work ranges from fabrication/custom build to receiving, inspecting, warehousing, staging, delivering, assembling/placing, reconfiguring, documenting damage/shortage, correcting punch items, and signing off. Larger FF&E projects coordinate room/floor sequencing, dock/freight-elevator windows, occupied operations, drawings, power/data, and move-in deadlines ([USSI](https://www.ussinstall.com/), [Flood Brothers](https://www.floodbrothers.com/office-furniture-installation/), [Pettus](https://pettusinteriors.com/delivery-installation-support/)).

**Inference:** A profile should start with project execution rather than assume one-man carpentry: approved scope/drawings, materials/freight, site readiness, crew skills, phased work, actual labor, punch/acceptance, and warranty/follow-up. Small fabrication/install shops and larger dealer/FF&E subcontractors differ materially. A specialized software product exists, but evidence about typical usage outside that vendor's claims is thin, so status is partial. The source label's “building” leaves fabrication-versus-installation scope unresolved; onboarding must distinguish it.

## Business model and customer segments

- Buyers include furniture dealers/manufacturers, general contractors, architects/designers, corporate facilities teams, schools/universities, hospitals/senior living, hotels, and public agencies. Contractors may be hired directly by end customer or subcontracted by a dealer/project manager.
- Jobs may be custom fabrication, modular/system furniture installation, FF&E room setup, delivery/assembly, office relocation/reconfiguration, decommissioning, or punch/warranty work. Project work and repeat changes coexist; evidence does not establish contract shares.
- Purchase triggers include new build/renovation, relocation, opening or room turnover, replacement, reconfiguration, and damage/shortage closeout. Site readiness and delivery windows can be gating factors.
- Small local assembly teams may perform a few jobs per day; institutional projects can involve warehouse receiving, large crews, multiple floors/rooms, subcontractors, phased/night/weekend work. This scale contrast is supported by operator offerings, not industry census.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms include FF&E (furniture, fixtures, and equipment), systems furniture, workstation/cubicle, casegoods, receiving, staging, installation plan/drawings, field verification, punch list, turnover, and closeout.
- Location data: building/site, floor/room/zone, dock and freight-elevator booking, access/security, staging/storage area, floor protection, work hours, occupancy status, and power/data coordination.
- Persistent customer assets can include installed furniture, serialized/tagged units, systems/components, room sets, and warranty records; custom shop inputs may be one-off materials and dimensions rather than customer-owned durable assets. Do not turn each carton into a lasting service asset without a tracking need.
- Customer-visible records include approved layout/drawings, delivery condition, shortages/damage photos, progress, punch items, signoff, warranties, and change approvals. Site access/security and healthcare/workplace protocols may be sensitive; requirements are facility-specific.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Service types include custom furniture build/fabrication, office/system furniture assembly, institutional room/guestroom FF&E, delivery/receiving, warehouse storage/staging, installation, relocation/reconfiguration, decommissioning, and punch/warranty corrections. Not all firms provide all types.
- Intake should capture customer/project contact, building and rooms/quantity, furniture make/model or shop drawings, approved plan, dimensions/field condition, freight source and dates, site readiness, access, occupancy, install deadlines, labor/skills, disposal, insurance/site requirements, and photos/documents.
- Pricing may be project quote, labor/time, unit/item count, crew/day rates, or hourly when site survey/scope is uncertain. One FF&E operator explicitly says binding quotes follow free site survey, otherwise work is hourly ([Gorilla Movers](https://gorillamovers.com/ffe-installation-san-diego)); this is one provider's practice, not an industry rule.
- Booking is often a delivery and installation window, phased schedule, after-hours/weekend sequence, or room-by-room deadline. Public self-booking is a poor fit for complex installations; quote forms should hand off drawings and site review to a coordinator.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Evidence-backed price drivers include item/room count, system complexity, freight receiving/storage, site distance/access, floor/elevator/dock logistics, phasing, after-hours work, assembly scope, disposal, labor uncertainty, and corrections/change orders. Scope boundaries and site readiness affect add-on/return visits (Steelcase terms, regional operators).
- Project contracts can be tied to a dealer/manufacturer sale or billed separately by an installer. Warranty and freight claims have deadlines; terms vary. A Steelcase UK sale document distinguishes normal-hours and overtime installation, but geography and contract conditions limit generalization.
- One purpose-built app advertises $249/month starting price with trial, not proof that operators buy it or what they will pay ([Fieldworks Pro](https://www.fieldworkspro.com/)). Treat as current advertised software price observed 2026-09-24; no operator WTP statements were found. Service labor percentages or installation prices must not be used to infer software WTP.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Most large installs are project-based, with follow-up punch, warranty, move/reconfiguration, or repeat dealer work. Scheduled work may be phased by floor/room and after-hours/weekend windows around occupied sites.
- Constraints: delivery arrival, warehouse readiness, freight elevator/dock appointments, room/site availability, other trades, crew size/skill, drawings, technology/power coordination, and building rules. Dispatch needs project/phase/zone structure more than route density for large jobs; small multi-stop assembly operators may need conventional routing.
- Changes in materials, ready dates, site access, and missing items should show downstream effects on crews and commitments. Schedule coordination is evidenced by commercial operator sources; common schedule-change policies are not.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Typical supported lifecycle: scope and survey → review drawings/site readiness → receive/count/inspect freight → document damage/shortage → warehouse or site stage by phase/room → protect/access site → assemble/install per plan → capture issues → punch/walkthrough → client acceptance, cleanup/debris removal, and closeout. Sources consistently describe these steps.
- Field evidence may include drawings, packing lists, condition photos, measurements, labor time, install checklists, punch items, customer signoff, debris/removal, and warranty or carrier claims. Exact records differ by dealer, manufacturer, facility, and scope.
- Safety covers lifting/handling, tools, occupied sites, ladders, access rules, floor protection, and coordination with electrical/data work. Healthcare infection-control, public purchasing, and building-specific credentials may apply locally; not universal defaults.
- Common return-work drivers suggested by operator descriptions include freight damage/shortages, wrong/missing components, site not ready, field dimensions differing from plans, and punch corrections. Billability and responsibility should follow contract and cause.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Customers need pre-install readiness lists and delivery/access confirmation; during work, progress, delays, missing-item/change approval, and room/floor updates; after, punch status, photos, signoff, invoice, and warranty follow-through.
- Dealer/facility project portal or shared record may help enterprise buyers; smaller jobs can use email/phone and signed forms. No cross-market adoption data found.
- Customer should upload plans/photos and provide site details; coordinator reviews before committing crew/date. Automated “complete” notices need human review when punch items remain or acceptance is disputed.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Job-specific resources include furniture/components, tools, dollies/lifts, floor-protection material, vehicles, warehouse/storage, packing supplies, and disposal. Receiving/inventory/staging matters for projects that warehouse product; direct-to-site jobs may not need inventory management.
- Crew roles can include project manager/coordinator, warehouse receiver, lead installer, installers, drivers, and subcontract labor. Furniture/system-specific drawing literacy and skill mix affect productivity. A commercial installer platform advertises crew/labor tracking; job postings and provider sites provide little consistent evidence on payroll, pay model, or certifications.
- Supplier/dealer/manufacturer relations govern drawings, freight, replacements, warranty parts, and claims. Track item identifiers only where the contract/site requires it.

## Reporting and operating measures

- No common KPI set was established in sources. Candidate measures (inference): estimated vs actual crew hours, install completion by phase/room, freight damage/shortage, punch aging/revisit, on-time turnover, crew utilization, warehouse dwell, change-order margin, and warranty callbacks.
- Segment by client/dealer, project/site, building/floor/room, manufacturer, service type, crew, phase, and date. Enterprise project reporting needs more phase and closeout views; small installers may only need estimates, calendar, and invoice.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- Fieldworks Pro advertises purpose-built quoting, project/crew/labor management, documents/photos, punch lists, client signoff, and invoicing, with starting price $249/month and 30-day trial. Steelcase describes an end-to-end service workflow view of quotes, PO status, delivery schedules, punch status, invoices, and historical spend. Furniture Wizard is a retail system with receiving/inventory and delivery routing, not evidence of installer adoption.
- Installers/dealers also rely on manufacturer/dealer systems, drawings, email, warehouse/inventory systems, accounting, and phone; this is a plausible tool mix, not a measured “common stack.” Do not claim spreadsheets dominate absent evidence.
- Operator-authored service pages emphasize coordination risk around cartons, field conditions, freight claims, staging, building schedules, punch items, and signoff. These are promotional pages. Direct independent owner complaints about incumbent software, switching, support, or willingness to pay were not found. A prospective commercial carpenter voice discusses uncertainty in separating install bids from build work, but is adjacent and anecdotal ([Reddit cabinetry thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/cabinetry/comments/1v9d4y1/install_questions_for_pros/)).
- Fieldworks Pro is the only current explicit software list price located: $249/month starting price, accessed 2026-09-24. Packages above Starter are not quoted in the cited output. This is advertised price, not observed operator purchase.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Operator sources broadly agree on project planning, receiving/staging, site access, installation, punch, and final handoff. Scope differs: some handle warehouse and full FF&E; others provide install-only crews; fabrication is not adequately evidenced. Software product claims establish an available package and its advertised price but not actual usage, complaints, or willingness to pay. Facility safety and medical/hospital procedures cannot be generalized.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype project intake, site survey/readiness, phase/room scheduling, freight receipt/condition, drawings and attachments, crew labor, punch items, signoff, and closeout. Keep fabrication, direct-ship vs warehouse, employee vs subcontract crews, billing, after-hours, acceptance, insurance, and facility protocol configurable. Ask whether the business fabricates furniture, installs customer-supplied goods, or does both; whether it warehouses products; and which markets/building types it serves. Research custom fabrication separately before applying these defaults to a shop-built-furniture business.
