## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A gate installation may be a new or retrofitted physical gate, motor/operator, safety sensors, power/conduit, keypad/intercom/card reader, vehicle detection, programming, and commissioning. Residential, multi-family, commercial and industrial use classes have different equipment and access needs. ASTM F2200 addresses automated vehicular gate construction; UL 325 addresses operator safety/entrapment protection. Manufacturers state that compatible and functioning safety devices are required for their systems. Operators describe site assessment, gate/operator compatibility, fire access, access provisioning, functional tests and maintenance.

**Inference:** This is a quote-led installation/project workflow with safety-critical commissioning, not a generic keypad or locksmith call. CRM records should connect site/gate class and geometry, operator/device compatibility, power and access, entrapment-zone protection, credential/access design, permits/inspection, test results, training and maintenance. Shared project/estimate/scheduling/field capabilities appear suitable. A fit-for-purpose configuration should not be treated as a replacement for a certified safety evaluation.

**Uncertain:** Candidate wording does not clarify whether the operator builds fences/gates or installs keypads on existing gates, or focuses on residential vs commercial/self-storage. Gate fabrication, electrical work, access control and software connection may be divided among contractors. Keep these project boundaries explicit.

## Business model and customer segments

Buyers include homeowners/HOAs, apartment managers, self-storage operators, parking facilities, warehouses, industrial sites, general contractors and security/access integrators. Projects may include new gate/fence fabrication, operator retrofit, keypad/intercom installation, credential system integration, repair, safety upgrade and scheduled maintenance. Residential jobs often have one gate and simple credential needs; commercial sites require multiple entry modes, visitor/contractor access, audit history, fire access and integration with a property/storage platform.

Triggers include new construction, perimeter/security changes, tenant access updates, failed operator, traffic flow, delivery/fire access, replacement or safety deficiency. Quote intake should capture gate type (swing/slide/barrier/etc.), dimensions/weight/usage frequency, existing operator/model, photos, power availability, mounting/ground condition, safety devices, access modes, users, emergency requirements, property class, control software and desired schedule. Site assessment should precede hard pricing or online scheduling when safety compatibility is unknown.

## Terminology and durable records

- **Site:** property/entrance/driveway, gate opening and approach, pedestrian path, grade, power, access restrictions, traffic/vehicle types, emergency entry, network/cellular coverage, adjacent public space and service access.
- **Persistent assets:** gate leaf/frame, operator/controller, keypad/intercom/reader, sensors/photo eyes/safety edges, loops, battery, fire department access hardware, network node and firmware. Record model/serial, class, zones, install/test date, settings/config backup, credential scheme (never plaintext PIN list), warranty and maintenance.
- **Job inputs:** gate dimensions/material/weight/cycle, handing/direction, safety zones, operator compatibility, civil/electrical readiness, conduit, existing code/credentials, number of users, remote/cloud expectations, fire authority instructions, use class, site drawings and photos.
- **Sensitive data:** door/gate codes, user names/access groups, security drawings and alarm interface should have restrictive access and secure credential handling. Customer-facing closeout may show test results without exposing credentials.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services include gate/fence fabrication and installation; operator install/retrofit; keypad/fob/card/intercom/mobile access; loop/safety sensors; wiring/conduit/power; software interface; programming/credential migration; repair; preventive maintenance and upgrades. Clarify who provides fence/gate, electrical service, access-control platform and monitoring; make construction, trenching, concrete, fire department connection, network, permit and user-enrollment boundaries explicit.

Quotes are site-specific based on gate size/type/cycle, usage class, operator capacity, safety hardware and number of entrapment zones, access devices/users, trench/concrete/wiring, network/power, integration, permit, commissioning, travel and after-hours work. An operator publicly lists an up-front service-call amount and hourly follow-on labor; that single regional repair price is not a new-install benchmark. Quote and change approval should account for hidden incompatible equipment or safety deficiencies discovered onsite.

Bookings usually require site walk, proposal/approval, parts/fabrication, civil/electrical readiness, install, commissioning, owner/admin training, and inspection where required. Urgent gate failure can disrupt site access but must not bypass safety checks. For commercial work coordinate with GC, electrician, fire authority, access software vendor and property operations.

## Pricing and commercial model

Pricing is a project quote or service call, often with material, gate/operator, access hardware, labor, trenching/concrete/electric, integration, permits, programming and warranty separated. A local operator's $200 first-hour service-call amount and $100/hour additional labor plus parts was visible on its page as of access date; it is a regional service-call price, not an installation/software price or market average. No general install rate or independently evidenced operator CRM WTP was found. Keep quoted amounts tenant-entered and distinguish monitoring/access platform recurring fees from installation labor.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

New installation is milestone-driven; maintenance and repair can recur. Capacity depends on gate/operator type, crew qualifications, fabrication, lift/trenching tools, parts and coordination with other trades. Route planning matters for regional maintenance portfolios; otherwise project scheduling dominates. Record blocked access, power/network failure, missing safety parts, concrete cure, permit inspection, owner credential roster, and vendor integration as dependencies. For emergency repair, confirm safe state/temporary access and communication before promising a restore time.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

1. Verify approved scope, site, gate type/use class, access hours, affected users and emergency plan.
2. Inspect gate structure/movement, pinch/entrapment zones, operator rating, existing control wiring, power and network; pause and escalate incompatible/unsafe condition.
3. Install gate/operator and appropriate monitored primary/secondary entrapment protection per the applicable standard and manufacturer instructions; wire electrical components as authorized.
4. Install/program keypad/readers/intercom/loops; configure only approved access roles and document credential handoff securely.
5. Test travel limits, sensing/reversal, all access methods, battery backup, fire/emergency access and integration where in scope; record test results/configuration.
6. Train property owner/admin, provide manuals and maintenance schedule, obtain customer acceptance, and close permits/inspection as applicable.

ASTM F2200-24 states automated vehicular gate systems should also comply with UL 325; UL's educational material explains entrapment devices and system compatibility. Local permits and rules must be checked. Operator sources emphasize testing access methods and avoiding incompatible equipment; this operational advice does not replace standards. Noncompletion reasons include unsafe/existing gate, inadequate structure/power, site excavation/permit delay, missing compatible hardware, weather, network/vendor issue, no credential approval or failed safety test. Never mark an unsafe gate commissioned because keypad access works.

## Customer communication and self-service

Communicate site survey, construction prep, outage/temporary access, permit/inspection, parts delay, access roster deadline, install arrival, test/commissioning, user training, credential setup, closeout and maintenance. Self-service can gather gate photos and approved users, but user identity/authorization and sensitive credential distribution require controlled review. Provide emergency/access instructions and clear escalation if a gate is left in manual or fail-safe mode.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Equipment/materials can include gate/operator, controller, posts/concrete/track/hinges, conduit, wire, keypad/intercom/readers, photo eyes, edge sensors, loops, battery, network connection, test equipment, lifts/welders/trenching tools and signs. Track project-ordered hardware by vendor/model and compatibility; stock standard service parts only when the business does. Crew skills may include gate fabrication/welding, low-voltage access controls, licensed electrical scope, concrete/trenching, programming and safety commissioning. Roles and certifications depend on local law/contract.

Possible measures: estimate variance; installation milestones; first-pass inspection; commissioning pass rate; access-device compatibility callbacks; time to resolve gate-down call; recurring maintenance compliance; warranty/callback by component; and margin by residential/commercial system. These are suggested measures, not verified standard KPIs.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree gate construction, operator class and entrapment protection are safety-critical; access control is a separate but integrated scope. Some operators install/configure the full stack; other buyer/operator discussions describe split gate and software vendors. **High confidence:** compatibility, site survey, safety testing and credential handoff are distinct closeout requirements. **Medium:** service/maintenance recurrence depends on customer segment. **Low:** universal software stack/WTP. ## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask whether physical gate work, wiring, keypad/access, cloud platform, and maintenance are sold together. Prototype a safety-gated commissioning workflow with site/gate details, approved parts, access-device list, test results and owner handoff. Keep code, licensing, vendor compatibility, credential access and emergency procedures jurisdiction- and tenant-specific; never let a generic completed checkbox replace verified safety testing.
