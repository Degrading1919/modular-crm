## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** This niche spans a built-in cabinet/bar counter installed by a cabinet shop through a fully coordinated basement entertainment build with framing, plumbing, electrical, stone, lighting and finish work. The buyer is usually a homeowner, interior designer or remodeler; the purchase is one-time, design-led and referral/portfolio driven. Site measure, appliance and sink selections, drawings, trade rough-ins, fabrication, delivery, installation and punch are distinct stages.

The pack opportunity is an estimate and project record that ties approved dimensions/selections to procurement, trade dependencies, installation evidence and approved changes. **Inference:** Solo cabinet installers may need a lighter quote/calendar workflow than design-build firms. Public sources document service scope and warranties but do not establish CRM software adoption or actual software willingness to pay (WTP).

## Business model and customer segments

Homeowners commission basement, dining-room, den or outdoor entertaining bars; custom cabinetmakers and millwork firms sell design/fabrication/install; kitchen and bath remodelers add bar cabinetry; interior designers and general contractors refer or coordinate larger jobs. Products range from a dry beverage cabinet to a wet bar with sink, refrigerator, ice maker, dishwasher, stone top, display lighting, shelving and seating. The scope may be cabinetry-only or room renovation. The source label does not establish a liquor-service business; this profile concerns residential construction.

Jobs are generally one-time, sometimes part of a larger remodel or new home. A small shop may measure, fabricate and install with an owner and helper. Larger firms separate design, shop, installation and trade coordination. Geography is local because measurements, delivery and installation require access; larger design firms may sell remotely and use local installers.

## Terminology and durable records

Records include customer, property, room, project, elevation/shop drawing, revision, cabinet run, finish/hardware selection, appliance specification, countertop template, rough-in, installer, punch and warranty. Capture room dimensions, ceiling/soffit, wall/floor condition, access/stairs/elevator, occupancy, parking/loading, building rules, and existing utilities. Persistent records may include installed cabinetry/appliances and warranty; room measurements, cut sheets, selections, and concealed-wall photos are project-version data. Restrict access codes, personal photos, and internal costs.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Lead intake should ask dry/wet/outdoor use, room and approximate dimensions, inspiration/photos/plans, desired sink/appliances, storage/display needs, material/finish, budget band if volunteered, building access, and whether a GC/designer is involved. An in-person measure and trade review are needed before a binding custom quote. Quote line items may distinguish design, cabinets, hardware, delivery, demo, countertop, plumbing/electrical, appliances, installation, tax/permit and allowances. Drawings/selections should be approved before production; changes after fabrication begins need a dated change order and revised schedule.

Booking is milestone-based: measure/design, client approval, trade rough-in, fabrication, countertop template, delivery/install, final connections and punch. Public web forms are useful for lead qualification and consultation requests, not exact custom pricing.

## Pricing and commercial model

Price varies with linear footage, cabinet construction, material/finish, custom curves, hardware, shelving/glass, countertop, appliances, sink/plumbing, electrical/lighting, demolition, delivery/access, field fit, trade scope and finish. A design/build contractor may quote a full project; a cabinet shop may quote cabinetry separately. Deposits, progress payments, allowances, final payment, warranty and cancellation terms depend on contract and local law. Trade Mark advertises cabinetry warranty and design process; Magna Cabinetry reports a five-year limited warranty and projects “as little as four weeks,” both vendor claims, not benchmarks.

**Software WTP:** no operator purchase statement or verified purchase amount found for this niche. Jobber publicly advertises a range of plans and pricing/payment options; the advertised service-business list price is not evidence this niche buys it or what a bar builder will pay. Do not infer CRM budget from project cost.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Scheduling depends on designer and customer approvals, appliance lead times, cabinet-shop queue, material availability, countertop template/fabrication, electrician/plumber/GC readiness, building hours and elevator/loading rules. Weather matters for outdoor bars. A single installer may schedule by calendar and calls; multi-crew businesses need shared milestones and responsibility. Rework triggers include wrong dimensions, appliance model changes, out-of-square walls, concealed utilities, finish mismatch, delayed trades or countertop fit. Track dependencies and who owns the next action.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Qualify → consult/measure → layout and selections → estimate/drawing approval → trade coordination/rough-in → order/fabricate → delivery check → site protection and install → countertop template/install and connections → verify doors/drawers, level, appliance clearances, plumbing/electrical by qualified trades → walkthrough, photos, punch, warranty and invoice. Evidence includes signed selections/drawing revision, site-condition photos, delivery damage, installed appliance models and punch acceptance. OSHA and local construction/electrical/plumbing requirements depend on employer and tasks; a CRM checklist cannot establish code compliance. Noncompletion may be missing selection/material, unready rough-in/site, access restrictions, damage, failed inspection or customer change.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need portfolio examples, consultation booking, design/finish approvals, appliance cut sheets, site-prep instructions, delivery/install updates, change pricing, punch and warranty contacts. Portal approval is useful for drawings and selections; final field dimensions and trade suitability need human confirmation. Do not auto-confirm completion where a trade inspection or appliance commissioning is outstanding.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Shop/field equipment includes measuring/laser tools, saws, drills, dust collection, lifts/carts, templates and vehicles. Inputs include casework, panels, hardware, tops, glass, lighting, appliances and plumbing/electrical materials. Custom shops may track sheet goods/hardware and capacity; installers often buy per project. Roles include designer/estimator, cabinetmaker, installer/helper, countertop fabricator, electrician, plumber and GC. Subcontract assignment and insurance/licensing requirements vary locally.

## Reporting and operating measures

Suggested, not standardized, measures: inquiry-to-consult and quote-to-approval, design revisions, approval-to-delivery time, supplier delay, labor/material variance, first-install completion, punch/callback rate, change-order capture and margin by job. Segment dry/wet/outdoor, cabinet-only/full remodel, in-house vs subcontracted trades and lead source.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Cabinet design/CAD and vendor catalogs may handle drawings/cut lists; general contractor or field-service software may handle quote, schedule, communication, invoice and job photos. Public design/build pages from DesignLab, Trade Mark and Mr.Countertops describe in-house/managed design, sourcing, fabrication and installation. They are promotional pages, not independent proof of actual software stack. No direct home-bar operator software complaint or verified CRM WTP signal was located. Jobber advertises publicly available plans, but this is an incumbent package signal only.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Cabinetry-only and full basement bar projects have very different trade coordination and scheduling needs. Vendor process pages are useful descriptions of offered workflows but promotional. No representative bar-builder operator survey, WTP, or complaint source was found; software-fit confidence is therefore medium/low. No evidence supports a niche-specific core gap.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype editable project variants for cabinetry-only and wet/full-room bars. Ask if plumbing, electrical, appliances, countertop, design and fabrication are included or subcontracted. Store approved drawing/selections, appliance cut sheets, site readiness, milestone ownership and punch. Keep rates, deposit and warranty editable. Validate actual operator software, stack and WTP before any niche-specific capability decision.
