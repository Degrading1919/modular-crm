## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** A home-cinema installer sells consultation, room/system design, AV equipment integration, wiring, acoustic and lighting coordination, installation, configuration/calibration, user handover, and aftercare. Scope can range from a living-room surround upgrade to a dedicated multi-week room build with construction trades. CEDIA research discusses charging for design/process and project/change management, while local operators describe room-specific quotes, equipment, acoustics, calibration, and warranties ([CEDIA](https://cedia.org/smart-home-professionals/news/change-order-management-for-smart-homes/), [Cam's Custom AV](https://camscustomav.com/services/home-theater/), [Mr Secured](https://www.mrsecured.com.au/home-cinema/)).

**Inference:** This is a high-consideration project pipeline where customer budget/options, room constraints, equipment compatibility, builder coordination and change orders matter. A system may require support visits after install, but frequency and managed-service model vary. This is not equivalent to broad smart-home installation: whole-home automation can be an add-on, while cinema design has specialized room/performance deliverables.

## Business model and customer segments

- Residential homeowners build a dedicated theater or upgrade an existing media/family room; home builders/designers may refer prewire/new construction jobs. Commercial or hospitality cinemas are adjacent and may need different contract/project handling.
- Buyer may be homeowner, interior designer, architect, builder, or AV/technology integrator. Purchases include equipment plus design/install labor and sometimes construction/acoustic treatment, shades/lighting/control integration and ongoing support.
- Typical trigger: new build/remodel, equipment refresh, poor sound/visual setup, or dedicated entertainment room. Project timeline may span weeks, involve customer decisions and other trades, and vary greatly by budget.
- Solo AV technicians may install purchased components; CEDIA integrators can run a multi-person design/sales/engineering/installation operation. Larger dedicated room builds coordinate general contractors, electricians, drywall, acoustic treatment, seating and control systems.

## Terminology and durable records

- Terms: media room, dedicated theater, prewire, AV rack, source, projector/display, screen, channel layout, surround/Atmos, acoustic treatment, room calibration, control scene, commissioning, handover, punch list and change order.
- **Service-location data:** room dimensions, wall/ceiling construction, seating/throw distance, ambient light, window/shade, electrical/network, rack location, HVAC noise, wiring paths, access and construction stage.
- **Persistent customer assets:** room/system configuration, installed devices, serial/warranty details, wiring/rack maps, control programming, calibration results and service history. Product inventory is installed/customer-owned after closeout.
- Sensitive records include home plans, network details, security/access codes, device credentials, photos and family schedules. Secrets should not be stored in ordinary technician notes.
- One-time inputs: budget, desired use, equipment choices, measurements, acoustic plan, cable runs, site conditions and room drawings.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Services: consultation/design, prewire, display/projection/screen, speaker/subwoofer installation, acoustic treatment, AV rack and networking, control programming, lighting/shades, construction coordination, calibration, user training, warranty/callback and upgrades.
- Intake needs room photos/plans/dimensions, new vs existing construction, equipment owned, seating, number of listeners, content sources, sound isolation expectations, lighting, control preference, budget, builder schedule, access and decision-makers.
- Quotes may have design/consultation fees, itemized equipment and labor, packages/good-better-best, allowances and conditional scope for hidden conditions. Local provider example lists $100/hour for labor and project range, while another gives staged installation pricing; these are provider-specific customer service prices, not market averages ([Cam's Custom AV](https://camscustomav.com/services/home-theater/), [Triumph AV](https://triumphav.com/home-theater-installation/)).
- Complex dedicated rooms require site consultation and human design. Simple TV/speaker mounting can sometimes be booked from a menu.

## Pricing and commercial model

- Price inputs: room complexity/size, device count/quality, cable concealment and wall access, prewire/new build, sound isolation/treatment, projector/screen geometry, control programming, calibration, builder schedule, permits/electrician subcontract, freight, and warranty coverage.
- Project billing may use deposit for equipment procurement, progress/final milestones, approved change orders and separate service calls. Project and warranty terms are operator-specific.
- Observed local service prices: Cam's Custom AV says its projects commonly land $15,000–$60,000 and labor is $100/hour; another Dallas provider publishes $549 starting setup and dedicated rooms $8,500–$35,000+. Geography, equipment and inclusions differ; accessed 2026-09-24 ([Cam's pricing](https://camscustomav.com/services/home-theater/), [Cognito Media](https://www.cognitomediaservice.com/home-theater-installation-dallas)). These are customer project prices, not software WTP.
- No directly evidenced purchase rates or software subscription WTP for cinema installers found. Do not derive CRM budget from installation ticket size.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Project schedule may include consult, design approval, prewire, rough-in, construction dependencies, equipment delivery, installation, programming/calibration, walkthrough and service follow-up. Small service calls are separate appointment work.
- Match installer skills (mounting, wiring, network, control programming, acoustics), equipment, room access and construction phase; coordinate with builder and trades. Parts/device availability and customer approval can block sequence.
- Schedule delays arise from construction readiness, equipment backorder, change orders, hidden conditions and customer decisions. Multi-day jobs need crew continuity and room/site protection; route planning is secondary except for service calls.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Inquiry/budget → discovery/site survey → system/room design → itemized proposal and equipment approval → prewire/construction coordination → install wiring/rack/display/speakers → integrate control/lighting → calibrate/test → customer training/walkthrough → warranty registration and support.
- Checklist includes measurements, approved system drawing, circuit/network readiness, cable/device labels, configuration backup, test/calibration, room cleanup, customer signoff and device/warranty handover.
- Safety: ladder/ceiling work, electrical isolation, wall cavity hazards and construction permits/licensed subcontractors. Licensing rules depend on electrical/low-voltage scope and state/province. Never treat AV installer as electrician without qualifications.
- Rework includes compatibility/firmware issues, acoustics/placement dissatisfaction, concealed wiring faults, missed scope, builder changes or failed hardware. Track cause, warranty/vendor responsibility and callback separately.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Customer needs expectation setting, package comparison, design approvals, equipment lead times, site readiness instructions, schedule updates, change authorizations, install status, user training and support path.
- Website can show systems, portfolio, capability/budget ranges, consultation request and service areas. Self-booking appropriate for defined mounting/service calls, not for bespoke dedicated-room design.
- Avoid unattended automated promises about system performance or “cinema quality”; actual room constraints and options require human explanation.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Equipment is high-value and order-specific: displays/projectors, screens, speakers, receivers/processors, amplifiers, mounts, racks, cabling, connectors, control devices, acoustic materials, shades and networking gear. Serial, SKU, vendor, ETA, warranty and job allocation help.
- Team may include salesperson/designer, project manager, AV tech/programmer, installer and subcontract electricians/builders/acoustic trades. Track labor skills/time and vendor purchasing; commission may apply but evidence does not establish common policy.
- Some integrators are labor-only with customer-supplied devices; others resell/integrate products. Keep purchasing/inventory behavior conditional.

## Reporting and operating measures

- Candidate inferred KPIs: lead conversion, design fee recovery, estimate vs actual labor/equipment margin, equipment lead time, schedule variance, change-order revenue, install callback rate, warranty cost, customer acceptance/reviews, referral source and recurring support revenue.
- CEDIA material describes monetizing design/ongoing user experience and change-order impacts on cost/schedule ([CEDIA business model](https://cedia.org/smart-home-professionals/news/integrator-2027/), [change orders](https://cedia.org/smart-home-professionals/news/change-order-management-for-smart-homes/)). No common dashboard standard established.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- AV integrators may use vertical integration/project tools, inventory/quoting platforms, design/calibration tools, accounting, vendor ordering, calendars and communication; this research did not verify a typical stack or software brand usage rates.
- CEDIA guidance notes challenges commercializing design/experience and managing change orders/schedule/cost. First-person practitioners emphasize careful room design and customer expectation setting; homeowner forums show buyers comparing multiple quotes and disputing line-item value ([CEDIA](https://cedia.org/smart-home-professionals/news/generating-recurring-revenue-in-the-smart-home-industry/), [Wavetrain interview](https://www.techradar.com/televisions/home-theater/nothing-can-replace-good-room-design-how-one-award-winning-home-theater-designer-approaches-custom-installs), [homeowner quote thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/hometheater/comments/1rfxczk/homeowner_needing_help_choosing_between_6_home/)). Complaints are anecdotal and not necessarily installers' complaints.
- Local operators advertise consultation, fixed/project/hourly pricing, warranty and calibration. No direct evidence of CRM subscription payment or software-price friction; mark WTP unsupported.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree that room design, equipment integration, calibration and customer handover distinguish professional cinema projects; scope ranges widely from simple install to construction. Price examples differ greatly because included equipment, room construction and geography differ. Residential AV integrators can expand into broader smart-home or light commercial work, but that does not make the workflows identical. Direct operator evidence on CRM tooling/pricing remains thin. Confidence is high for project phases, medium for software fit and low for prevalent stacks.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype consultation/design, quote options, room-specific records, equipment ordering, install phases, calibration/testing, user training and warranty follow-up. Keep equipment brands, AV/control stacks, acoustic approach, budget thresholds, service plan, subcontract scope and payment milestones configurable. Ask whether the firm designs rooms or installs only, supports new builds, supplies equipment, programs control systems, offers recurring support and manages subcontractors. Avoid assuming service calls alone represent the work.
