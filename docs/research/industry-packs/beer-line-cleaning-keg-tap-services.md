## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Specialist technicians serve bars, restaurants, breweries and venues by cleaning beverage lines and related components, inspecting taps/couplers, and sometimes repairing/installing dispense systems, chillers or gas equipment. Accounts generally recur on a defined cycle. The Brewers Association's US draught-quality manual recommends line/faucet/coupler cleaning every 14 days, quarterly acid de-stoning and semiannual detail cleaning; UK guidance says at least every seven days. These are different regional guidance regimes, not one global rule.

**Inference:** The core CRM record is an account/site connected to its draft system and line inventory (tap/faucet, coupler, line length/size, beverage, cleaning cycle, chiller/glycol/gas), recurring route stop, chemical/process checklist, readings/repairs, and proof of completed service. A niche SaaS advertises $49/month early access and a local operator lists service prices; those are separate software/service prices and do not establish WTP broadly.

## Business model and customer segments

- Recurring line cleaning and tap maintenance serve bars, pubs, restaurants, breweries, stadiums, hotels and event venues. Related services include install/design, coupler/faucet/tower repair, glycol chiller maintenance, CO2/gas delivery, wine/cold brew/kombucha line care.
- Poor pours/off-flavor and scheduled hygiene/quality cycles trigger work; equipment failure can create urgent calls. Accounts may be one venue or multi-site groups with differing line counts and systems.
- Operators range from a solo route technician to install/service firms with equipment sales and multiple techs. Basic cleaning-only and technical system commissioning require different skills/tools.

## Terminology and durable records

- Durable records: account, venue, draft system, line/faucet/tap, coupler, line length/diameter, beverage, cleaning recipe/chemical, service interval, prior clean, readings/pressure/temperature, repair, and keg/tap setup.
- Site data: bar/keg room access, hours of operation, basement/cooler location, line path, glycol/chiller location, water/drain, shutoff and contact. Persistent draft system assets and components should have identifiers and service history.
- Chemical concentration/contact time/rinse condition and food-contact surfaces are safety-critical. Store chemical/SDS/training references and job evidence; do not claim a given CRM record proves sanitation compliance.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

- Intake: venue type, address/access/hours, number and length of lines, faucet/coupler count, product/beverage types, direct-draw vs long-draw/glycol, existing maintenance, preferred service window, site contact, and emergency symptoms. A site survey may be needed to quote complex systems.
- Quote may be per account, location, first line plus incremental lines, tap count, frequency, travel, install/repair materials, emergency response, and service plan. A local Pennsylvania operator advertises service starting $55/month; another public service price lists first line $60 CAD and lower additional-line pricing. These are geographically/provider-specific examples.
- Distinguish recurring clean from diagnosis/repair, installation, part replacement and gas delivery; approval for repair parts may be a change order.

## Pricing and commercial model

- **Observed service pricing:** Tap So Clean lists draft cleaning and repair starting at $55/month in Western Pennsylvania (accessed 2026-09-24), not universal market pricing. The ART-of-Draft lists CAD $60 for the first line, $18 second and $15 subsequent lines in Ontario. Different scope/currency; do not compare as equivalent.
- **Observed software pricing:** DraftTrack, a niche software vendor claiming to be built by a working line cleaner, currently advertises early access at $49/month, no setup fee, cancel any time. This is a software subscription/list offer, not an operator's service charge; verify availability/terms before relying on it. It directly signals at least one vendor's chosen entry price but no broad WTP or paid conversion.
- Route frequency, line count, travel density, access complexity, installation, materials, emergency call and agreement term affect economics. A two-week service cycle is cited by Brewers Association guidance; UK guidance uses weekly minimum; customer practice may be different.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

- Recurring visits by line count and cycle; cluster bars/venues geographically. Track last completed service and next due date per system/account, not only a generic account recurrence.
- Schedule before service window or quiet periods to avoid interrupting service. Emergency tap/gas/chiller failures require triage and a parts/tool-capable technician.
- Route density, venue open hours, basement access, line count and parking affect stop length. Missed clean should remain due/overdue and trigger rescheduling, not silently reset the recurrence.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

- Review system map and prior issues → confirm beverage/lines and venue access → isolate/label service where needed → prepare chemical/pump and PPE → circulate cleaning solution for specified process/contact time → disassemble/clean faucets and couplers → rinse/purge, restore system, verify pour and document completion → note defects/recommend repair and set next due date.
- Brewers Association's Draught Beer Quality Manual recommends cleaning lines every 14 days with caustic solution, faucets/couplers/FOB details, quarterly de-stoning and semiannual device detail; it calls for suitable PPE. UK Brewers and Licensed Retailers guidance says food-grade cleaner at least every seven days. Local laws, beverage systems, manufacturer instructions and service chemistry may supersede; never encode one global interval.
- Customer complaints/failures: off-taste, foam, leaks, poorly maintained taps, missed cleaning, chemical residue/rinse concerns, and service interruption are plausible from operator marketing, but claims of common complaint prevalence are not established. Log chemistry used, cycle/contact time, rinse verification, post-service checks and any noncompletion.

## Customer communication and self-service

- Account needs due-service reminders, appointment window, access request, notice of outage/line unavailable, completed cleaning record, observed defect, part quote and invoice. Bar manager/owner is likely operational contact; brewery may set quality protocols.
- Automated reminders suit recurring tasks; technician must control service completion and safety/chemical signoff. Do not state “certified clean” without a verified standard/process and supporting evidence.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

- Cleaning pump, hoses/adapters, line-cleaning chemical, measuring/concentration tools, PPE, faucet/coupler brushes, wrenches, replacement seals/couplers/faucets, test strips, and possibly glycol/gas diagnostic tools. Inventory management is relevant for repair parts and chemicals; lot/SDS/expiry tracking is tenant-dependent.
- Tech capability differs between routine clean, long-draw/glycol systems, installation, gas, and refrigeration repair. Record skills, training, PPE, vehicle/tools, and supplier/parts purchase as operator-specific.

## Reporting and operating measures

- **Observed incumbent software features:** DraftTrack markets route schedule, stop times/mileage, line-build documentation, client management, quotes, and profits. Useful counts include lines, taps, visits, quotes, and routes.
- Useful inferred measures: due/overdue clean by line, on-time completion, service margin by account/line, route miles/time, chemical/parts usage, repeat defect, complaint/callback, quote conversion, and account retention.

## Existing software and operator evidence

- DraftTrack is an early-access product that claims scheduling, estimate, line-build documentation and client management; it advertises $49/month. This is niche software list pricing, not independent review or proof of market adoption.
- Local service pages show subscription-like customer cleaning plans and component repair. Brewers Association and UK trade association publish operational guidance. No repeated first-person operator complaint corpus was found. DraftTrack's feature choices suggest, but do not prove, that line system documentation, route scheduling and estimate margin are pains.
- Hardware/dispense vendors, accounting, maps, forms, SMS/email and spreadsheets may appear in stacks; no operator voice verifies a common set. No direct SaaS willingness-to-pay beyond the vendor's own announced entry price.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Evidence is strong for recurring cleaning, line-level asset records and differing US/UK intervals; medium for task sequence and public service pricing; low for operator complaint prevalence and platform adoption. Brewers Association and UK association recommendations differ (14-day vs at least 7-day guidance), demonstrating that schedules should be configurable and jurisdiction/product standards checked.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask for service geography, segment/beverage, system complexity, number of lines/taps, line-level identification, contract cycle, chemical/process policy, repair/install scope, crew skills and required evidence. Prototype a venue's draft system with per-line/coupler history and recurring service schedule, then checklist and repair quote. Keep cleaning frequency, chemical, PPE and compliance configurable by jurisdiction and system; confirm whether a specialist product or connected tools already own line history.
