## Executive summary

This candidate covers installation/replacement of windows and doors, including opening measurement, product selection/order, site preparation, setting, flashing/weather sealing, hardware, finish and customer acceptance. Installation may be sold by a specialty installer, remodeler, dealer or general contractor; some businesses sell the product and labor while subcontract installers receive work from a dealer/GC. The defining workflow is measure → quote/product specification → order and lead-time tracking → site readiness → install → inspection/photos/punchlist → warranty.

FGIA describes InstallationMasters training for new construction and replacement window/exterior glass-door installers in residential and light commercial markets. Operator discussion from a small commercial millwork installation company shows QuickBooks estimates/change orders, Clockify time, Google Calendar/Drive for plans, plus GC-provided Procore/PlanGrid; the owner complained specialty subs can find general-contractor software mismatched to their work. This is a useful workflow example, not representative prevalence. A pack should model opening/product/order/install records and revision-safe documents, while manufacturer-specific installation and code rules remain job-specific.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential customers include homeowners, landlords and remodelers replacing units or installing new construction. Commercial buyers include GCs, developers, property managers and door/window dealers; larger contracts may be a specialty subcontract. Work includes replacement windows, new-construction windows, exterior doors, interior/commercial door packages, storefronts and hardware; these scopes differ in codes, product sourcing, crews and commissioning. This profile excludes window washing, glazing fabrication and general carpentry except where an installer performs trim/rough-opening repair.

Replacement work often starts with a service call or in-home sales visit, then a detailed measurement and product order. New-build/subcontract work starts with drawings, specifications, bid invitation and schedule. The site may have multiple openings, each with unit size, handing, grid/glass, rating, finish, hardware, location and rough-opening condition. Companies range from a solo installer/repair shop to dealer-led sales/measure crews plus installation teams, or commercial subs coordinating many openings and GCs.

## Terminology and durable records

Use customer, property/site, project, opening/unit, rough opening, product schedule, manufacturer/model, order, finish/hardware, measurement, installation record, punch item and warranty/callback. Capture site/contact/access, building age/type, floor/level, opening dimensions, wall assembly, existing unit condition, water management details, interior/exterior finish, access/lift needs, occupant constraints and applicable permit/inspection. Persistent window/door records can support warranty/maintenance history, but individual measurements and product orders belong to the job and should not be forced into permanent asset records for every small replacement.

Maintain dated measurement sheets, sketches/photos, signed product specification, estimate revisions, purchase/order confirmation, manufacturer installation instructions, permit/inspection details, field checklist, before/after pictures, customer/GC sign-off and warranty. Confirm which party owns product defects, installation defects and trim/repair scope.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake needs address, number and type of openings, new construction vs replacement, rough sizes or request for measurement, preferred product/material, performance/rating needs, finish/hardware, age/lead concern, access, occupancy and desired time. An exact quote usually needs field measurement and product costs; online forms can qualify and schedule measurement but should not guarantee a final price from rough dimensions. Estimates distinguish products, labor, removal/disposal, flashing/sealants, trim/repair, lift/scaffold, permits, tax, delivery, protection and exclusions. Commercial bids tie unit counts/specification to drawing revision, alternates and schedule.

Track survey/measure, quote and customer selections, approval/deposit, order submitted/confirmed, ship/receipt/inspection, site readiness, install appointment(s), inspection/punch and final payment. Supplier lead times, backorders, special finishes, glass/hardware, weather, access and GC sequencing affect schedule. Scope additions for rot or nonconforming openings require documented price/change approval before repair where practicable.

## Pricing and commercial model

Common price inputs include unit count/size/type, product grade, glass, frame, hardware, custom dimensions/finish, labor/access, demolition/disposal, flashing and trim, repair/rot, travel, minimum visit, warranty and overhead. Residential proposals can use deposit/order milestone/final balance; commercial work may use contract schedule-of-values and progress billing, retainage, backcharges and GC paperwork. Cancellation/restocking and product-specific warranties need clear terms.

No reliable independent software WTP was found for this exact niche. A door-contractor discussion includes a small millwork installer who reports QuickBooks estimates/change orders and low-cost Clockify/Google tools; the same thread reports PlanGrid at a remembered $50 per user/month and Procore access free through the GC. That is one dated operator account, not current market pricing or a broad willingness-to-pay result. FGIA certification is a training credential, not a software requirement.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Most installs are one-time, with return visits for punch, adjustment or warranty. Measurement visits, material arrival and installation dates are separate events. Match installer skill to unit type, crew size, lifting equipment and project access. Confirm units are delivered and checked, openings are ready, and other trades/GC clear the work area. Delays may result from custom manufacturing/backorder, damaged/missing units, weather, unsafe access, concealed rot, customer/occupant availability or predecessor work; retain original promise, revised date and cause.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Preflight confirms approved product/revision, unit labels/condition, site protection, opening/access and manufacturer instruction. Installation then removes/sets/plumbs/fastens/seals/flashes per product and approved detail, reinstalls trim, tests operation/locks and leaves the area clean. Checklist/evidence can record opening/unit ID, rough-opening condition, dimensions, shim/fastener/flashing/seal steps, photos, operation test, water-management detail, product serial/label, punch items and customer acceptance. FGIA states installation practice affects air-leakage performance and offers installer education; product-specific details remain controlling.

OSHA fall protection/ladder rules and silica/lead hazards may apply to site conditions; pre-1978 renovation can trigger EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting requirements when covered paint disturbance occurs. Local code and product approvals vary. Noncompletion/callback reasons include missing/damaged product, wrong measurement/handing, site not ready, hidden rot, unsafe height/access, failed operation/seal, water intrusion or customer change. Track responsibility and warranty status to prevent an installer callback being confused with product/manufacturer defect.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customers need measurement appointment, selection/spec approval, order/lead-time updates, delivery readiness, arrival window, access/preparation instructions, change approval, installation proof, punch closure, invoice and warranty records. A portal can collect photos and approvals, but final measurement, building-condition assessment and product selection should be confirmed by trained staff. Send schedule updates without promising a date before supplier confirmation.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Materials include units/glass, frames, hardware, flashing, fasteners, sealants, insulation, trim and disposal materials. Track special-order status, units by opening and damage/shortage on receipt. Tools include measurement devices, drills/fasteners, sealant gear, lifts/scaffolds and glass handling tools. Crew roles vary from salesperson/measure technician to installer/helper/lead and punch tech; commercial subs may need GC orientations and site credentials. Time capture and travel may be useful for multi-crew operators; payroll, piece rates and certification requirements vary.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful operational measures (mostly inferred) include quote-to-sale, measure-to-order time, supplier lead-time variance, first-visit completion, installed units/openings per crew day, callback/rework rate by product/installer, damage/shortage rate, punch aging, gross margin per job and warranty cost. Segment by product, project, crew, supplier, GC/dealer and replacement/new construction. FGIA training describes the goal of reducing installation deficiencies and callbacks but does not publish a CRM KPI standard.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Small specialty operator evidence shows an accounting app for estimates/change orders, a time app, shared calendar/drive for specs and GC-provided project platforms. The operator describes general construction platforms as primarily built for GCs, with difficulty handling specialty-sub workflows such as drawing revisions and takeoff. FGIA's training and documentation represent a primary installation practice source; vendors such as Cinderblock market product specs, orders and install scheduling in one job record, but vendor positioning is not evidence of adoption or WTP. Current incumbent subscription prices specific to this niche were not independently verified; public evidence supports only the operator's historical remembered price and free GC access.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a measurement-to-order-to-install workflow, per-opening product notes, document revisions, readiness checks and punch/warranty follow-up. Ask whether the business sells product, works direct or as a sub, handles replacement or new construction, and whether it tracks many custom orders. Keep manufacturer instructions, code, lead controls, product warranties and prime-contractor portals configurable. Validate willingness to pay and data granularity with small installers and commercial subs.
