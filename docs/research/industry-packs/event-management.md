## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** “Event management” spans independent planners/coordinators, corporate meeting and conference teams, venues, nonprofit/festival organizers, and production firms. Work may be a one-off project, a recurring program, or an event sold by a venue. A durable event record coordinates the buyer, venue/site/rooms, vendors and their contacts, guest or attendee experience, staffing, assets, schedule, budget, approvals, and closeout. Cvent documents a lifecycle spanning venue sourcing, registration, attendee engagement, onsite execution, and reporting; smaller venue systems emphasize inquiry-to-booking, proposals/contracts, BEOs, floor plans, invoices, and payments.

**Inference:** The strongest common product opportunity is a shared project/event workspace that ties client decisions and commercial terms to a versioned run of show, work owners, suppliers, venue logistics, and post-event outcomes. Attendee registration, ticketing, sponsor/exhibitor management, floor plans, production safety, and multi-day programs should be conditional modules; they do not apply to every independent planner or small private event.

**Uncertain:** The inventory label is underspecified. Do not make weddings, corporate conferences, concerts, festivals, or venue operations the universal workflow. Intake should establish whether the operator plans third-party client events, runs its own recurring events, sells venue space, or produces events onsite.

## Business model and customer segments

Planners may sell full planning, partial planning, day-of coordination, production management, venue sourcing, registration/marketing, or consulting. Buyers include couples/families, corporate marketing/HR teams, associations, nonprofits, schools, public bodies, and venues. Revenue may be a fixed planning fee, package, hourly/project fee, percentage of budget, venue/F&B/rental revenue, ticket/registration revenue, or a recurring corporate program; sources do not establish a universal pricing convention. Leads come from referrals, venue/vendor networks, web inquiries, RFPs, and prior clients. Season, event date, venue availability, attendee count, and lead time matter. Solo planners may use one client workspace per event; enterprise teams coordinate portfolios, approvals, registration, suppliers, and ROI. Public or high-attendance events add access, weather, crowd, emergency, and permit considerations.

## Terminology and durable records

Terms vary: **client/host**, **event/program**, **venue/site/room**, **attendee/guest**, **vendor/supplier**, **speaker/exhibitor/sponsor**, **run of show / event timeline**, **BEO / event order**, **floor plan / seating chart**, **RFP**, and **closeout / post-event report**. Store client organization and contacts with roles; event date/time zone, format, objectives, audience, venue and room/site details, load-in/out, capacity, access, accessibility, parking, power/network, permitted use, venue contacts, and contingency plan. A venue/room, reusable equipment, vendor, and repeat program can be persistent records; event-specific attendee lists, seating assignments, menu choices, floor plans, agenda, and deliverables are event inputs/snapshots. Keep private attendee, payment, accessibility/accommodation, emergency-contact, and security details permission-controlled. Preserve who approved each schedule/contract change and the version sent to vendors.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Service scope ranges from planning and vendor procurement to coordination, registration, venue operations, and onsite production. Intake should capture event type/objective, date flexibility, estimated headcount, format/site count, audience, budget range, scope sought, decision-maker, venue status, vendors already contracted, accessibility needs, registration/ticketing, and deadlines. Photos, venue plans, RFPs, proposals, insurance/permits, and contracts can matter. Fees may be packaged, scoped after discovery, or quoted against a client budget; large corporate/public projects can use an RFP/procurement process. A signed agreement and retainer/deposit commonly reserve planner capacity, but rates and cancellation schedules are contract-specific. Booking a venue or vendor may create separate contracts and deposit dates, not one universal event deposit.

## Pricing and commercial model

Drivers include event count/scale, planning scope, attendee volume, event length, number of venues/rooms, production/registration complexity, travel, staffing, vendor pass-throughs, and deadline/change burden. Client budgets, planner fee, supplier commitments, and event revenue should remain separate. Contracts may stage fees by booking, milestones, and completion and set rescheduling, cancellation, scope-change, overtime, and force-majeure terms; no single pattern is established across weddings, corporate work, venue sales, and public festivals. **Software WTP evidence:** Planning Pod publicly says most single-location venues fall around **$199–$319/month** and prices based on active event volume; this is the vendor’s venue-specific advertised range, not a planner-wide budget. Cvent requires a quote for its principal event-management platform. No direct small-planner software-spend statement was found in the cited operator sample.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

An event is usually date-bound, but preparation is a dependency schedule: discovery/brief -> scope/budget approval -> venue and vendor holds/contracts -> registration/design/content -> production plan -> final confirmations -> load-in/setup -> doors/program -> strike -> reconciliation/report. Lead times and gates vary. Dependencies include venue access and room turns, permits/insurance, supplier delivery, speaker/content, registration deadlines, attendee count, staffing, AV tests, accessibility, and client approvals. Use milestones and owners with due dates, critical-path/blocked status, date/time-zone clarity, change log, and a consolidated timeline. Day-of schedules are usually exact; flexible-date sourcing and annual programs need calendar/availability views. Multi-event teams need resource conflicts and capacity; transport/routing is conditional for offsite production or attendee shuttles. Event changes may need a human to approve downstream vendor notifications, cost, or attendee messages.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Suggested configurable lifecycle: confirm scope and latest approved plan; check venue access, permits/insurance, vendor arrivals and safety roles; load in; inspect setup against floor plan/AV requirements; brief staff; check in attendees or guests when relevant; run program against the schedule; log incidents and authorized changes; strike/return rentals; reconcile supplier invoices, attendance, budget, and client feedback. Checklists need owners and completion evidence (sign-off, photos, permit, insurance certificate, test result), with versioned run sheets. FEMA’s special-event contingency materials are relevant for public-safety planning but are not a universal checklist for a private dinner. Local fire/building/assembly, noise, alcohol, food, street-closure, and crowd rules depend on venue, event, and jurisdiction. Noncompletion/incident categories may include weather, venue loss, supplier no-show, unsafe conditions, low attendance, client change, technical failure, and cancellation; effects and insurance vary. Rework includes rescheduling, reprinting/reissuing event orders, supplier replacement, settlement, and claims.

## Customer communication and self-service

Clients need inquiry response, proposal and contract, payment schedule, planning tasks/approvals, vendor confirmations, schedule changes, event-week briefing, day-of contact/escalation, attendee invitations/registration/reminders where applicable, and post-event reconciliation/feedback. A portal can expose client-owned approvals, documents, budget commitments, guest counts, and decisions; supplier/attendee access should be role-limited. Avoid automatically sending unapproved scope, cost, cancellation, safety, or attendee changes. Registration accessibility, consent, privacy, and opt-outs depend on audience and local rules.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Persistent records can include venues/rooms, AV/lighting/staging, furniture, radios, signage, vehicles, and reusable decor when operator-owned; rental inventory and vendor-owned equipment should remain distinguishable. Many planners own few event assets and broker suppliers; inventory is conditional. Roles can include planner/producer, venue manager, coordinator, registration/check-in, security/medical, AV, catering, decorators, volunteers, and contractors. Skills, shift times, access passes, emergency roles, breaks, and post-event hours matter for larger programs. Do not assume all planners employ staff or own rentals.

## Reporting and operating measures

Incumbents expose attendee/registration and event performance reporting; Cvent markets engagement and ROI dashboards. Candidate measures: inquiry conversion, event count/value, budget variance, deposits/receivables, registration vs attendance/no-show, cost per attendee, vendor reliability, milestone lateness, incidents, post-event satisfaction, and repeat booking. Definitions should be tenant-selected: event ROI is not equivalent to a planner’s operating margin. Segment by client, event format, venue, lead source, program, owner, and time.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Cvent is an enterprise event suite for venue sourcing, registration, attendee engagement, onsite check-in, diagrams, speakers/exhibitors, budget, and reporting; core pricing is sales-quoted. Planning Pod serves planners and venues; it describes active-event-based pricing and a venue-first focus, with online lead capture, contracts, BEOs, diagrams, invoices, payments, and migration help. Tripleseat targets venue/restaurant/hotel event sales and operations with custom pricing. Small operators also use spreadsheets, Google Calendar, email, project tools, HoneyBook/Aisle Planner, and specialist registration/diagramming apps. A wedding-planner discussion describes a shared spreadsheet containing checklist/due dates, timeline, vendor and wedding-party contacts, ceremony order, songs, packing and photo lists; another event-production discussion says planners reuse RFP templates and value tracking multiple venue bids. These are examples, not prevalence estimates. Complaint/evaluation signals include scattered tools or manual copies, expensive/complex enterprise pricing, setup/migration work, and poor fit when no product spans planning, venue operations, guest engagement, and client CRM. These are corroborated as positioning by vendors and anecdotal operator posts, not quantified market-wide defect rates.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

Sources agree that event execution joins an inquiry/brief to venue/supplier logistics, schedules, attendee or guest experience, onsite execution, and closeout. They disagree because product scope is segment-dependent: Cvent covers enterprise attendee programs; Planning Pod/Tripleseat emphasize venue-side sales and BEOs; wedding planners maintain client planning workspaces and vendor/timeline artifacts. Public/private scale, venue ownership, ticketing, and attendee count change what is required. Confidence is **high** that the event record and timeline need client/vendor/location links; **medium** for milestones, CRM and reporting defaults; **low** for one standard price/deposit/cancellation formula or universal event-management profile. Unanswered: prevalence of independent planners vs venue operators in this candidate label; common small-company stack; and how often run-of-show changes require formal acknowledgement.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a configurable client-event workspace: define the operator model first; gather date/headcount/scope/budget/site; create linked venue, vendor, and contact records; track approvals, milestones, and payment terms; then generate a current event plan with owners, load-in, run-of-show, and closeout. Ask whether the business is a planner, venue, corporate team, or event owner; event mix and volume; registration/ticketing needs; attendee scale; owned equipment/staff; and public-safety/permitting context. Keep pricing, deposits, cancellation, registration fields, safety plans, and plan templates tenant-defined. Keep Event DJ and Catering profiles distinct because their day-of deliverables and asset/safety workflows differ.
