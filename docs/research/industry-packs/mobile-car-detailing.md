## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Mobile auto detailing brings a technician, cleaning products, tools and sometimes self-contained water/power to a customer’s home, workplace or parking location. Work spans maintenance hand wash/vacuum through interior extraction, paint decontamination, correction, sealant/wax and ceramic coating. Quotes vary by vehicle body size, condition, service scope, pet hair/stains, travel/access and whether the operator must supply power/water. Before/after photos and condition records help establish what was present and what service was done. Repeat maintenance packages can coexist with one-off correction/coating jobs.

**Inference:** The useful asset anchor is the specific vehicle (VIN where available, make/model/year, size, finish/material concerns and service history) plus a service location that may change from visit to visit. The mobile workflow needs service-area, vehicle/site readiness, water-power and wastewater confirmation, routing/buffers and evidence. Do not conflate this with mobile mechanics or assume all businesses are fully self-contained; vendors differ.

## Business model and customer segments

Individual vehicle owners buy convenience at a residence, office, apartment lot or fleet/employee amenity. Mobile operators include solo detailers and multi-van crews, with higher-skill work such as paint correction and coatings alongside recurring washes. Fleet or workplace wash contracts add batch scheduling, site access and per-vehicle lists; their workflow is not the default single-car customer. Purchase triggers include routine cleanliness, sale/lease return, spills, pet hair, seasonal grime, road salt, paint protection, or correction of oxidation/swirls. Climate and pollen/salt seasons may affect demand, but the cadence varies by region and owner.

Service is performed at a mobile site, unlike a fixed detail bay. Some providers bring fresh water, generator and reclaimed-water setup; others rely on customer water/power or use low-water methods. No universal self-contained rig configuration was evidenced. Mobile service can be limited by apartment rules, parking/staging, shade, secure access, weather, water source and storm drains.

## Terminology and durable records

Use customer/vehicle owner, service address/site, vehicle, detail package, technician, estimate, add-on, condition report and completion photos. Vehicle asset fields: VIN (sensitive enough to limit exposure), plate if needed, make/model/year/trim, body size/class, color/finish, paint/interior material, mileage where relevant, prior coating/protection, known damage, recurring package, preferences and dated service/photo history. VIN scanner and vehicle damage diagrams are advertised by Mobile Tech RX; not every solo detailer needs VIN capture, but correct vehicle association and pre-existing damage documentation matter.

Location/site fields: residential/office/fleet/apartment, parking bay/height restriction, safe vehicle access, shade, water/power availability, permission to work, water recovery/drain restrictions, service area/zone, gate/access instructions and site contact. Avoid retaining access codes longer than necessary. Condition, damage and customer notes should be job-specific until confirmed as durable vehicle history. Do not store payment data in notes.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Service menu may include exterior hand wash/dry, wheels/tires/glass, interior vacuum/wipe, seat/carpet shampoo/extraction, pet-hair or odor treatment, engine bay cleaning (if offered), clay/decontamination, polish/paint correction, wax/sealant, ceramic coating, headlight restoration and maintenance wash. Coatings/correction need paint condition review and are skill/equipment intensive; no generic “detail” label should imply one fixed scope. The profile excludes repair and fleet washing as defaults.

Intake asks for vehicle make/model/size, service requested, condition and photos, pet hair/stains/sand/salt, existing coating or delicate/aftermarket surface, location and parking/height access, shade, water/power supply, drain/discharge restrictions, fleet count if applicable, preferred time, and customer expectations. Offer menu pricing for defined packages but route heavy soil, oversized vehicle, paint correction/coating, sensitive surfaces, no suitable space, or environmental uncertainty to inspection/manual quote. Record customer acknowledgment of pre-existing scratches/dents and excluded work. Online booking may select package/vehicle and submit photos; final suitability and scope can require technician approval.

## Pricing and commercial model

Observed prices vary by vehicle size and package. Perfect Touch Mobile Detailing (San Diego County) lists basic car wash at $40–$50 for cars and $50–$60 for SUVs/trucks, with deluxe and deeper services priced higher. Fresh Layer (San Diego) lists wash-and-wax from $99, priced by vehicle size, and says final packages rise with condition; its vans bring a 100-gallon water tank and generator. These dated public offers are individual local prices, not a national average. Add-ons, paint condition, extra labor, travel, fleet volume and service location can change quote. Recurring wash plans may charge per visit: a San Diego operator’s Club Clean lists $99/$119/$149 by vehicle size for weekly, biweekly or monthly cadence. Deposits/cancellation and maintenance plan terms vary by operator.

**Software willingness-to-pay:** Detail Bookie lists $99/month for up to five users; Mobile Tech RX lists $39/admin/month + $15/additional user for Getting Started, $99/$199 admin monthly for higher plans (accessed 2026-09-24). These are advertised list prices. Mobile Tech RX includes vehicle records/VIN, estimates/invoices, photos, schedule, text alerts and QuickBooks features by tier; Detail Bookie advertises calendar, online booking, vehicle profiles, deposits and Square payments. One operator-thread response reports testing Mobile Tech RX and finding customer self-booking/automatic recurring texts insufficient for their workflow; this is one user’s experience, not broad adoption evidence. Price pages do not prove actual purchases or WTP.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Maintenance washes may be weekly, biweekly or monthly; restoration/correction/coating is one-time or periodic condition-driven work. Route planning helps a multi-stop mobile day, but solo operators may schedule by customer window and zone. Account for drive time, wash/extraction/polishing duration, drying/cure time, shade/weather, water refill and wastewater disposal. Crew/service skill and vehicle class affect duration. Apartment/fleet access windows may require a list, key handling and customer-site coordination. Do not let recurring booking fill a route if the location lacks permission or lawful discharge arrangement.

Noncompletion causes include rain/temperature, unsafe/unsuitable surface, vehicle absent, no water/power when required, inadequate parking/height clearance, locked site, water recovery unavailable, heavy condition beyond booked package and customer scope change. Have manual reschedule and revised quote; mobile work does not guarantee same-day service in all weather.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm vehicle/customer, package, site permission, access, water/power and wash-water handling before setup. Photograph vehicle condition and any visible damage. Protect adjacent vehicles and property, use manufacturer/product directions and surface-appropriate chemicals, keep wet-process runoff contained or otherwise compliant with local rule, clean and dry specified areas, and inspect against the purchased package. Capture timestamped before/after photos, add-on approvals, exceptions, product/coating details where useful, customer sign-off and invoice/payment. Record missed panels, stains that remain, paint defects found, customer-supplied water issue, damage claim, weather interruption and rework separately.

EPA guidance explains that storm drains often discharge directly to waterways and local laws can prohibit commercial wash water from entering storm drains. Rancho Santa Margarita’s mobile-detailer BMP makes capture/containment a local compliance requirement. Rules differ by municipality; do not encode one city’s exact process universally. California mobile operator AMD advertises de-ionized water and compliance with its wastewater-reclaiming requirements, but that is a vendor assertion, not regulator verification. Worker hazards include wet/slippery surfaces, chemical handling, electrical equipment near water, heat, lifting extractors/tanks and traffic; specific safety controls depend on operator and locality. Never represent paint correction or coating as guaranteed defect removal.

## Customer communication and self-service

Send appointment confirmation, prep instructions (remove valuables/personal effects, disclose child seats/pet hair/known damage), arrival/delay, scope-change quote, completion photos, care/cure instructions for coatings, invoice and next maintenance reminder. Portal/booking should support vehicle profiles, package selection, image upload and quote approval. Do not automatically promise that paint marks, odor or stains will be removed before inspection. Obtain permission before photographing customer property/plate or publishing photos. Customer notification consent and SMS rules should use shared communication preferences.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Mobile rig may carry water tank, generator, vacuum/extractor, pressure washer/foam system, hoses/reels, microfiber, brushes, polishers, lighting, chemicals, PPE, water recovery mats/vacuum and waste containers. Some operators require customer hose/outlet; others advertise self-contained capacity. Inventory tracking is conditional on multiple vans, chemical volume or coating stock. Crew skills vary from wash/detail to paint correction/coating; certification is voluntary/training evidence, not a universal state license. IDA offers detailer certification and specialty certifications, including RV and watercraft; do not present it as required law. Record chemical product/SDS availability and staff training per the business’s safety program.

## Reporting and operating measures

Vertical detail software exposes package menus, service/vehicle records, booking and revenue reports. Useful reports include bookings by package/vehicle size, estimate-to-final adjustment, revenue per labor hour, travel time/miles, return interval, coating follow-up, callbacks/rework, cancellation/no-show, chemical use, fleet account completion and water-recovery exceptions. These measures are proposed unless vendor-reported; do not imply a known industry benchmark.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Mobile Tech RX and Detail Bookie are direct incumbents; detailers also use Square, QuickBooks, calendar/text, generic FSM, forms/photos and spreadsheets. Vendor pages emphasize vehicle/condition photos, estimates, online booking, reminders, service packages and payments. An operator who trialed Mobile Tech RX reports lack of customer self-booking and automatic scheduled texts as a mismatch; another Reddit operator thread centers on package pricing, vehicle condition and how to estimate before seeing the car. These are individual reports. Growth from a solo operator to multi-van crew can make centralized scheduling, employee roles, QA and accounting sync more valuable, but direct purchase/WTP data remain weak.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Vendors differ on bringing water/power and on price/model; size- and condition-based prices appear repeatedly. Local stormwater controls can prohibit washwater discharge to drains and may require capture. **Inference:** location qualification and vehicle history belong in one booking/job record with condition proof. **Uncertain:** Mobile-detailing work is not nationally regulated under a single washwater rule; exact permit, containment, water use, HOA/apartment access and wastewater disposal requirements must be confirmed at each work location. IDA certification is voluntary. Software list prices are not paid-use evidence; one user report is not prevalence. Confidence high for the workflow, medium for field/asset defaults, low for universal regulatory or software adoption statements.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Prototype a vehicle profile, condition check-in/photos, package estimate and mobile-site qualification, with optional maintenance cadence. Ask whether the operator brings water/power, serves apartments/fleets, uses washwater recovery, offers coatings, and employs a crew. Keep service amounts, vehicle classes, site restrictions, consent, environmental method and recurrence tenant-configurable. Route paint correction, heavy condition, uncertain discharge permission, and fleet/property access to review. Confirm shared vehicle/asset and media support before proposing a core gap.
