## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Interior designers sell professional advice and project services to residential and commercial clients. A project may contract separately for discovery/programming, concept design, merchandise selection/specification, purchasing, project administration and installation coordination; ASID's contract packages expose these as selectable scopes and fee options. Work therefore follows a lead/brief → site and needs record → proposal/contract → design/selection approvals → purchasing/coordination → installation/punch/closeout loop, not simply an appointment.

**Inference:** A CRM/pack can help with scoped proposals, room/project records, decisions and revision history, selections, procurement, milestones, invoices and client communication. It should distinguish e-design/decorating from construction administration and licensed work. **Uncertain:** software adoption and project pricing vary substantially by solo designer, studio and commercial firm. Houzz Pro lists $249/month US Pro pricing at access date, but no sampled operator purchase establishes general willingness to pay.

## Business model and customer segments

Residential homeowners, property developers, hospitality/retail businesses and other organizations hire independent designers or studios for a single room, whole-home furnishing, renovation, workplace or hospitality project. Some sell consultations/e-design packages; others provide full-service selections, purchasing and site administration. Fees may be hourly, fixed, phase-based, retainer, percentage/markup, purchasing-related or combinations; merchandise may be billed separately and third-party construction contracts remain distinct. Buyers may be owner, facilities/procurement lead or architect/contractor. Projects are mostly project-based with referral/repeat work; scope and coordination intensity vary by services contracted. Designers are not automatically architects, general contractors or licensed professionals for every regulated task.

## Terminology and durable records

Keep client/organization, project, site/building, room/zone, design brief, concept, finish/FF&E selection, vendor, purchase order, approval, revision, installation and punch item as separate records or linked objects. Durable project data: address/site use, room dimensions and photos, occupants/accessibility needs, style preferences, functional requirements, budget, decision-makers, existing furnishings/constraints, jurisdiction, approved drawings/specifications, finishes, products, vendor/model/SKU, price/lead time, sample/selection decision and warranty/care docs. Version and timestamp client approvals and scope changes. Construction details, hazardous materials, structure, fire/life safety and MEP work need qualified consultants/contractors; do not present a generic CRM record as professional plans or permit verification.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Service menus range from consultation, space planning, concept boards and sourcing to drawings/specifications, purchasing and project administration. Intake should capture property type, rooms, photos/measurements, desired style/function, household/business users, budget, target date, existing items to keep, access, decision makers, and whether construction or permit-bound work is expected. Designer may need discovery call and site visit before proposal. Written agreement should define services, deliverables, fee/compensation, payment, purchase authority, allowances, number of revisions, schedule, termination and dispute terms. ASID separates concept, merchandise selection, purchasing, project administration and compensation schedules. E-design may use remote photos/measurements and limited implementation support; do not promise full-service site outcomes. Booking is typically consultation or milestone meeting; project start is after scope, contract, deposit and inputs.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service/commercial evidence:** ASID's US residential contract package provides nine designer-compensation choices and distinct schedules for merchandise selection, purchasing and project administration; its non-member package price is $698, which is the price of a professional contract resource, not a design service fee. This directly supports flexible compensation and contracted scope, not a universal rate card. No dependable cross-market service-price sample was located; quote inputs include project/room count, deliverables, site visits, purchasing volume, installation/admin involvement, revision rounds, consultants, schedule and complexity. Geography and procurement model affect fee and merchandise handling.

**Software pricing/WTP:** Houzz Pro's US public pricing page lists Pro at $249/month after a 30-day free trial and custom/team plans by sales discussion, plus separate advertising starting at $499/month. These are advertised list prices, not evidence of a specific designer's purchase or general WTP. A designer forum post reports concern that an advertising package change would bundle/double their cost; other posts mention frustration with lead conversion. These are anecdotal complaints, not representative adoption data. Do not infer CRM budget from design-project fees or Houzz list price.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Projects use milestone and dependency scheduling rather than route-based recurring service: discovery, survey/measure, concept, client approval, sourcing, lead-time management, procurement/delivery, site coordination, installation, punch and closeout. Dates depend on client decisions, vendor stock, construction readiness, permit/contractor dependencies and delivery. Appointment booking can support consultations, measures and site meetings, but each project schedule needs a named owner and approved scope. Revisions, backorders, shipping damage, client delays and change orders require rescheduling and customer updates. Repeat work comes from later rooms/properties and referrals, not a default cadence.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Confirm signed scope, budget and decision authority; collect brief, dimensions and site conditions; document concept and options; record approval/version; specify and confirm product/finish; issue purchase or coordinate vendor order under contract; monitor delivery/site readiness; document installation and unresolved defects; complete walk-through/punch and archive client-approved handoff/care documents. Client selection approval is a meaningful hold point before purchasing. Measure/site visit accuracy and installed condition affect outcomes. On commercial or permit work, confirm licensed scope and coordinate qualified architect/engineer/contractor. CIDQ documents regulated jurisdictions, and requirements vary; verify directly with board before representing title, filing plans or offering regulated practice. No universal technical compliance checklist can be inferred. Noncompletion/rework may arise from client scope/revision, stock/backorder, measurement error, vendor defect, site not ready, damaged delivery or construction change. Track responsibility and change approval without implying the designer controls every vendor.

## Customer communication and self-service

Clients need scope/fee/contract, meeting schedule, concept and selection presentations, approval/decline, budget/allowance visibility, order/status, delivery, change orders, installation calendar, punch list and care/warranty documents. A branded portal can help remote projects share selections and decisions. Automatic purchase or schedule actions should wait for explicit approval and budget authority; prompt human review for ambiguous changes and substitutions. Self-book a discovery consultation only; construction scope, detailed estimates and procurement need a review gate.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Tools may include measuring/laser scan, camera, CAD/3D/floor-planning and rendering software, sample library, specification sheets, product catalogs, project plans and client portal. Designers source from showrooms, manufacturers, distributors, trades and artisans; distinguish designer-owned display samples from client-purchased goods and supplier-managed stock. Roles can include principal/designer, design assistant, procurement/project coordinator, installer, architect/engineer and general contractor. Track purchasing responsibility and vendor/consultant contacts; physical inventory and payroll are not universal. Confidential client photos, building plans and household preferences require appropriate access and sharing controls.

## Reporting and operating measures

Useful measures (mostly inferred from workflow): lead source and conversion, proposals accepted, fee/margin by project type, time by project/phase, scope/revision count, selections awaiting client approval, budget versus selections/purchases, vendor lead-time/late/damaged orders, change-order value, on-time milestone, punch completion and referral/repeat work. Houzz advertises project/financial reporting in higher tiers; no association-wide benchmark was found. Separate merchandise pass-through from design fee and revenue recognition policy with accountant.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Houzz Pro promotes estimates/proposals, CRM, floor planning, mood boards, selections, project schedule, client dashboard, purchase orders, invoicing, payments, project documents and QuickBooks integration. This shows incumbent coverage, not that every interior designer uses it. ASID provides contracts and scope/fee resources rather than operational software. Anecdotal Houzz community and Reddit posts include complaint themes about lead-generation value, advertising/software bundling, usability and migration/product data, while Houzz hosts feature/practice discussions. A solo e-designer may use calendar, email, spreadsheets and design applications; bigger studios need procurement, team roles, accounting integration and project finance. That stack segmentation is an inference; no representative operator survey or software purchase panel was found.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** ASID contract materials clearly separate concept, merchandise selection, purchasing, administration and compensation. Houzz sells bundled design/business workflow, with advertised Pro price and separate advertising; individual designers report concerns about price/lead outcomes, but these are anecdotal. **Inference:** configuration must ask firm scope and authority before enabling procurement reminders or client portal. **Uncertain:** public project-service rates and representative software adoption/WTP are not available in this sample. Regulation differs by state/province and project function; jurisdiction-level verification is necessary. Confidence high for project contract phases, medium for product fit, low for general WTP.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask residential/commercial, e-design/full service, contract scope, purchasing authority, billing model, project/room count, permit-related work and whether the firm holds samples or manages client goods. Prototype project/room records, contract/fee options, staged client approvals, selections, vendor/order links, milestone notifications and punch closeout. Keep jurisdiction and consultant scope tenant-specific; do not replace CAD/design or accounting systems. Validate exact software migration and WTP with solo and multi-person studios.
