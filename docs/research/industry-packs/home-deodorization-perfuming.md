# Home deodorization / perfuming

## Profile metadata

- **Industry key:** `home-deodorization-perfuming`
- **Source name:** Home deodorization / perfuming
- **Source category:** Home services
- **Candidate source:** [Businesses I Love](https://www.sweatystartup.com/blog/businesses-i-love), Home services, position 29 (after “Fire damage remediation”; before “Interior painting”).
- **Other exact/near duplicate labels:** Fire/smoke restoration and mold remediation remain separate. This candidate name covers two distinct offers: source-based odor removal/deodorization (diagnose, clean/remove source, then possibly treat) and elective fragrance/perfuming (apply scent to a space). Evidence for fragrance-only home service is sparse; do not assume it is a remediation service or safe for every occupant.

## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Odor remediation operators assess the likely source and surface/air exposure, then combine source removal/cleaning with treatments such as hydroxyl, thermal fogging or ozone according to occupancy and contamination; restoration may require removing porous material. IICRC S700 frames fire/smoke work around thorough source removal and ongoing odor evaluation. EPA cautions ozone is a lung irritant, ineffective for many pollutants at safe concentrations, and high-concentration treatment requires an unoccupied environment and is not a general substitute for cleaning/source correction.

**Inference:** A safe CRM workflow must screen odor/source and occupancy, distinguish remediation from scent application, and require human review before chemical/device selection or claims of elimination. **Uncertain:** “perfuming” may refer to fragrance service, odor masking, or commercial scent marketing; credible consumer/operator evidence for standalone home perfuming is limited. Those service modes should be independently enabled rather than merged by default.

## Business model and customer segments

Odor-removal customers include homeowners/tenants, landlords/property managers, real-estate turnover teams, restoration/insurance clients, vehicle owners and property professionals dealing with smoke, pets, cooking, mustiness or other persistent odors. Some are a one-time remediation tied to a source event and others request treatment after ordinary cleaning. A separate ambient scent/perfuming business might sell a chosen fragrance or recurring diffuser refill to homeowners/property managers; this customer model is not well evidenced. Structural fire/smoke restoration, sewage/mold remediation, trauma cleanup, and source repair may require specialists outside a deodorization-only operator’s scope.

## Terminology and durable records

Record customer/property and rooms; occupancy/tenant status, pets, children, asthma/sensitivity disclosure if voluntarily given, HVAC/ventilation, source type/history, affected area/materials, prior cleaning/treatment, visible damage/water source, product and device used, technician, treatment duration, evacuation/re-entry time, and acceptance/return check. Store fragrance preference/refusal and allergies only when needed and consensually; sensitive health notes should be minimized. Durable property/room treatment history matters; odor intensity, airflow and occupancy are job-visit observations. Keep scent preference separate from contamination/odor diagnosis.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Intake asks what odor, onset/source event, affected rooms/materials/HVAC, previous attempts, source known or ongoing, occupancy/pets/sensitivities, insurance/event context, requested removal vs masking/fragrance, and photos/inspection access. Triage for suspected gas/sewer/chemical/biological hazard, active leak, fire/soot, mold/water damage, drug residues or unknown chemical—stop and refer or scope under qualified specialty work. Service options may include source inspection, physical cleaning, porous item removal, odor neutralization, HEPA/air scrubbing, hydroxyl or fogging; ozone only by appropriately trained operator with controlled vacant-space protocol, not for occupied home. Fragrance application is separate, optional and consent-based. Quote by affected area/rooms, contamination severity, material removal/cleaning, treatment time/device days, return visit, PPE, disposal and source access; site inspection commonly needed for smoke/structural odor. Never promise “permanent” or guaranteed elimination before source and materials are understood.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed price evidence:** GoGreen Restoration publishes service-specific examples: chlorine dioxide gas at $0.99/sq ft, hydroxyl at $175/day, and a smoke-cleaning example of HEPA/dry chemical sponge cleaning at $2.50/sq ft followed by hydroxyl at $175/day ([operator pricing article](https://www.gogreenrestoration.com/blog/odor-removal-cost-connecticut), accessed 2026-09-24). These are one regional operator’s own rates, not universal price benchmarks. Another regional provider describes inspect-and-tailor scope but posts no flat consumer price ([operator](https://www.gogreenrestoration.com/odor-removal), accessed 2026-09-24). Inputs include room/structure area, odor/source and severity, contents/surfaces, source removal, treatment method/device duration, PPE, occupancy/re-entry planning, equipment, travel, return tests and insurance documentation. Fragrance-only jobs may use per-room/visit/refill pricing but this is unverified.

Software WTP: no direct odor-remediation/fragrance operator software subscription evidence located. Restoration firms may have project/insurance estimating and documentation tools; fragrance operators may use scheduling/POS. This is an inference, not an observed stack. No software list price or stated WTP for this candidate was found; remediation ticket prices do not evidence software budget.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Schedule assessment, source cleaning/removal, treatment time, ventilation/re-entry and follow-up evaluation; one job can take multiple visits and require the structure to be vacant. Coordinate tenant/owner, fire restoration, cleaning, HVAC and contents vendors. Route planning applies to mobile crews, but equipment capacity and safe transport/storage of devices/chemicals matter. A scent service may use recurring refill visits, which should be separately opt-in and adjustable. Do not schedule unattended ozone or equivalent treatment with occupants in place; confirm vacancy, access control and return-to-occupancy plan.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Triage hazards and establish scope; inspect/document source and affected materials; isolate affected work area; remove/correct source and clean where within scope; choose product/device per label and professional standard; log application, concentration/duration/location, ventilation and exclusion; notify occupants and secure access; allow required purge/re-entry; reevaluate with client under comparable conditions; record residual odor/source, recommendations, photos and closeout. For fire/smoke projects, IICRC S700 calls for source removal and ongoing/final odor evaluation. EPA says ozone generators are not EPA approved for occupied-space use and can create harmful exposures; do not market them as broad safe/healthy purification. Other chemicals are product-specific; SDS/PPE, ventilation and label directions are required. OSHA respiratory protection rules apply when respirators are required. Suspected mold needs moisture control/source repair and appropriate remediation, not perfume. Noncompletion: source inaccessible/active, hazard, occupied space, inadequate ventilation, refusal to remove contaminated porous contents, weather/utility failure, failed odor acceptance, or specialist referral. Rework follows source investigation rather than simply repeating scent treatment.

## Customer communication and self-service

Explain service mode, method and limits; what occupants/pets must do; treatment/vacancy/re-entry instructions; scent preference or no-scent option; source repairs/contents removal; treatment time; follow-up and outcome criteria. Obtain explicit approval for fragrance and disclose product/scent; do not default fragrances for sensitive occupants. Document no claim of medical disinfection/health benefit unless supported/authorized. Quote approval and tenant/owner contact must be attributable. A portal can show room/visit history and instructions, but hazard diagnosis and re-entry clearance need trained human accountability.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Odor inspection equipment, PPE/respirators where required, HEPA air scrubber, hydroxyl generator, fogger, ozone equipment in vacant-space specialized context, cleaning/extraction supplies, odor counteractants, containment materials, filters, disposal containers, ventilation fans and vehicle. Track device serial/calibration/maintenance, product/SDS/lot, filter and treatment days. Staff need hazard recognition, source-based cleaning, chemical safety, customer communication and device-specific competency; fire/smoke/mold/trauma specialists require distinct training/scope. No universal licensing claim is supported. Fragrance-only staff must understand product instructions and consent but should not imply remediation skill.

## Reporting and operating measures

Track source/type and severity, inspection-to-quote, treatment duration, source-removal scope, device-days, re-entry completed, odor acceptance/residual complaints, return visits, referral/exclusion reason, insurance documentation and margin by treatment method. For scent service, opt-in/renewal and complaint/refusal rates are more relevant than remediation success metrics. No shared benchmark found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Regional restoration operators publish method pages/prices and emphasize tailored source/occupancy evaluation. Broader restoration software and job-management tools may support photos, estimates, schedules, insurance paperwork and work orders; actual stack prevalence is unverified. IICRC offers fire/smoke technician certification and odor management standards. EPA safety/effectiveness guidance conflicts with broad vendor claims for ozone; this is a material disagreement in the market and must be represented in safety intake, not automated claims. No representative software price/WTP or first-person business software complaint was found. Home perfuming-specific business evidence is the largest source gap.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

First ask whether this business removes an identified odor source or applies optional fragrance. For remediation, prototype hazard/source screening, affected-room/occupancy record, estimate, approved cleaning/treatment, vacancy/re-entry instructions and client outcome review. Keep method, products, PPE, qualifications and claims tenant-configurable and compliant with current labels/local rules. Validate fragrance-only demand and actual buyer software/WTP before treating that side as a pack default.
