## Executive summary

**Observed evidence:** Custom refinishing begins with photos, dimensions, piece type, condition and finish direction, then an assessment of veneer, hardware, drawers/repairs and pickup/delivery constraints before a firm quote. Work proceeds through intake/custody, stripping or prep, repair, stain/color sample, finishing and cure, inspection and return. Published operator prices vary by piece type and condition; another shop reports 5–6 weeks standard turnaround. Operator software exists in the furniture restoration niche, but its plan price is vendor list pricing, not proof of adoption.

**Inference:** A furniture-as-asset record and custody workflow are distinctive: each table, chair, cabinet or set has unique provenance, materials, condition/photos, finish sample and chain-of-custody notes. Quote acceptance, customer-owned item transport, damage photos and finish approval should be explicit. **Uncertain:** small shops vary between purely shop-based, in-home touch-up, refinishing, upholstery and commercial contract; customer service price can exceed replacement value and is condition-sensitive.

## Business model and customer segments

Households restore heirloom, antique, vintage or quality furniture; designers, antique dealers/resellers, property managers, hospitality/commercial clients and institutions may commission multiple pieces. Services can be sold by workshop that receives customer-owned pieces, mobile repair/touch-up, or shop plus pickup/delivery. Typical work is project-based with long, multiweek cycle time; seasonal demand and schedule are local. Some operators reject low-value or unsuitable particleboard/veneer pieces or recommend paint/repair instead. Upholstery, caning, structural joinery, hardware and shipping may be subcontracted/scope add-ons, not automatic.

## Terminology and durable records

Persistent asset: individual item ID, owner/provenance, maker/era if known, dimensions/weight, construction/material/veneer, finish/paint, hardware, drawers/doors, existing damage/repairs, photos from all sides, requested result, approved sample, service history and customer pickup/delivery address. Chain of custody: receipt condition, tags, keys/hardware bag, transport crew/vehicle, custody dates, customer signature, storage location and release proof. Job fields: labor steps, product/lot, subcontractor, color sample and cure time. Sets have a linked item list to prevent missing a chair/leaf.

## Services, intake, quoting, and booking

Services may include strip/re-stain/refinish, repair, veneer repair/replacement, touch-up, custom paint, antique conservation, upholstery/caning, in-home work and pickup/delivery. Intake by photos/text or in-person inspection needs dimensions, construction/material, condition close-ups, desired finish/color, previous treatment, use/location, timeline, pickup access and whether customer wants preserve/repair vs redesign. Shop quotes are piece-by-piece and condition-based; photo estimates can be provisional pending inspection. Approve finish/sample before full treatment; a sample or finish board mitigates color mismatch. Quote includes labor/repair complexity, stripping, finish/material, veneer/hardware, pickup/delivery, special packing, outsourced upholstery, storage and tax. Establish scope boundary for hidden damage, brittle veneer or prior coatings.

## Pricing and commercial model

**Observed service prices:** Dale’s Furniture Refinishing (Twin Cities) lists dining chairs $90–$200, coffee/end table $150–$400, dining table full refinish $450–$900+, dresser/chest $300–$800 and antique restoration quoted per piece ([operator price guide](https://dalesfurniturerefinishing.com/pricing/), accessed 2026-09-24). Weathersby Guild (TX) lists a single chair $250–$450 and small tables/dressers $400–$800, with final quote depending on material, size and current workload ([operator](https://weathersbyguildsa-aus.com/services/furniture-refinishing), accessed 2026-09-24). Andrews Refinishing states photo estimate, size/condition/repair factors and 5–6 weeks standard shop turnaround ([operator](https://andrewsrefinishing.com/furniture-refinishing-richardson-tx/), accessed 2026-09-24). These regional list prices show custom pricing by piece and condition, not national average.

Commercial inputs: piece type/size, construction and veneer, finish type, repair/stripping/detail, sample/color match, upholstery/caning, shop labor/capacity, pickup/delivery, storage and complexity. Some shops take deposit before pickup/materials, balance on completion; payment, cancellation, abandoned item and storage policy must be explicit per contract. Software WTP: QuantumRepair advertises furniture restoration software Growth at $66/mo billed annually or $79 monthly and Scale $83/$99 ([vendor pricing](https://quantumrepair.app/software/furniture), accessed 2026-09-24). This is current vendor list price only; not verified customer purchase, market share or operator WTP. No public evidence of actual refinisher subscription purchase was located.

## Recurrence, scheduling, dispatch, and routing

Workshop schedule is a queue by promised ready date, process stage, cure/dry time, subcontract/parts, available booth/bench and customer approval. Intake and final delivery have pickup windows, vehicle capacity, protective wrap and customer/site access. Multi-piece lots may share pickup, estimate and delivery but retain item-level condition/finish status. Onsite touch-up is a separate appointment with travel and room protection. Quoted lead times may extend with repair complexity/workload; communicate status and revised date. Not a recurring service by default, though designers/antique dealers can repeat.

## Field workflow, safety, completion, and rework

Tag and receipt-check each piece; capture all-side condition photos and note existing damage; document owner approval, estimated scope and transport condition; assess construction/finish and hidden repair risks; disassemble/tag hardware if required; strip/sand/repair, prepare and test stain/finish sample; obtain approval; apply finish and allow product-specific cure; reassemble, inspect, photograph and compare; pack/protect, schedule release/delivery, obtain receipt and final payment. Wood stains vary by substrate and topcoat; manufacturer instructs test samples and ventilation. Spray finishing may trigger OSHA requirements for booth/ventilation, grounding and flammable-liquid handling; applicable workplace rule depends on shop process. Solvent/chemical SDS, PPE, ignition control and waste-rag disposal matter. Historic lead paint or unknown coatings call for assessment before sanding; don't assume ordinary refinishing is safe. Noncompletion: unstable/unsuitable substrate, hidden damage, veneer failure, customer declines change, sample rejection, unrepairable value tradeoff, missing piece/hardware, delayed subcontract/finish cure, pickup damage or inaccessible property. Callback/dispute around color/finish should be compared to signed sample under consistent light; record care instructions and limits rather than promising exact match across dissimilar wood.

## Customer communication and self-service

Customer needs photo-estimate request instructions, pieces accepted/rejected, inspection result, scope/price/change approval, sample choice, pickup date/condition, stage/ETA, delay, ready notice, delivery proof, invoice and maintenance/cure instructions. Portal should show each piece and its photos, not one undifferentiated “furniture job.” Automate stage updates but obtain approval before scope change, replacement/repair, sample choice, subcontract work or disposal/storage. Avoid claim that a restoration preserves monetary value.

## Equipment, inventory, suppliers, and workforce

Shop: workbenches, clamps, hand tools, sanding/stripping equipment, dust collection, spray booth/ventilation if spraying, PPE, compatible stains/sealers/topcoats, solvents/cleaners, sample boards, drying/cure space, moving blankets/dollies, packing materials and storage racks. Inventory includes finish chemicals by compatibility/color/lot, hardware, veneer, adhesives and common repair stock; source/finish uniqueness reduces standard stock. Roles: estimator/restorer/finisher, repair/cabinetmaker, upholstery/caning subcontractor, driver/mover, customer-service coordinator. Skill depth varies by antique conservation vs cosmetic refinishing; record specialty capabilities and vendor/contractor rather than assume one tech does all.

## Reporting and operating measures

Track estimate conversion by type, pieces accepted/rejected, promised vs actual cycle time, stage aging, sample approval, labor/material/outsourcing per piece, pickup/delivery cost, damage incidents, rework/color callback, storage age/unclaimed pieces and contribution margin. Each item should have completion status and service history. No representative shop KPI benchmark found.

## Existing software and operator evidence

Operators use photo/text estimates, website intake, shop work orders, tags, finish samples, pickup/delivery scheduling and progress communications. Small shop stack may include calendar, accounting/payments, spreadsheets, photo storage, texts and subcontract tracking; prevalence uncertain. Furniture-specific software vendor QuantumRepair publishes quote/status, item tracking, SMS and list pricing, while small operators’ direct use is not established. Shop price pages show variation and quote-by-piece; operator forum posts describe the challenge of color matching and skilled labor. There is no broad independent user review data in sources reviewed, so no repeated product complaint should be asserted.

## Evidence, disagreements, and uncertainty

**Observed evidence:** Shops publish piece-based price examples; intake sources request photos/dimensions and inspect veneer/hardware/logistics; turnaround can span weeks. Manufacturer instructions highlight test samples and drying. **Inference:** item-level identity/condition and stage tracking are more suitable than one generic project description. **Uncertain:** exact service blend, software adoption/WTP, unclaimed property rules, transport insurance and lead/chemical obligations vary and were not comprehensively surveyed. Confidence high for intake/asset/workflow, medium for pricing, low for adoption/market share.

## Handoff to a future pack implementer

Ask whether work is shop, mobile or pickup/delivery; which materials/eras/services; size/set handling; sample process; repairs/upholstery; capacity and transport scope. Prototype photo-led estimate intake, item-level tags and condition records, customer finish approval, production queue/cure stages, pickup custody and ready/delivery confirmation. Keep prices, accepted materials, finish practices, deposits, storage and subcontractors configurable. Verify current asset/job relationships can represent multiple customer-owned pieces before declaring a core gap.
